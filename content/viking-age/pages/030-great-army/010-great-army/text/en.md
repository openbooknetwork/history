In 865 a large Danish army lands in East Anglia. The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle calls it the *mycel hæþen here*, the great heathen army, and later sources name Ivar the Boneless, Ubba and Halfdan among its leaders. The East Angles make peace and supply the army with horses, and it winters in their kingdom. Modern estimates of its size range from under a thousand men to a few thousand.

In 866 the army crosses the Humber into Northumbria, where Osberht and Ælla are fighting over the kingship, and takes York on 1 November. On 21 March 867 the two rivals attack the city together. The Northumbrians break into York, but the battle turns against them and both kings are killed. The survivors make peace, and the Danes set up Ecgberht as king of Northumbria under their control.

Later in 867 the army moves south into Mercia and winters at Nottingham. King Burgred of Mercia calls on his brothers-in-law, King Æthelred of Wessex and Alfred, and in 868 their armies besiege the Danes in the town. No great battle is fought, the Mercians make peace with the Danes, and the army returns to York for the winter of 868–869.

In 869 the army rides across Mercia into East Anglia and takes winter quarters at Thetford. King Edmund fights it that winter, and the Danes win, kill him on 20 November and take his kingdom. The Chronicle leaves open whether he dies in the battle or after it. Abbo of Fleury, writing about 986, tells of his martyrdom, and a later tradition places his death at Hoxne in Suffolk.
