Murad, sixteen years old, is proclaimed sultan at [Bursa](@bursa-1421) in June 1421. At [Constantinople](@constantinople) Manuel II has left the conduct of affairs to his son and co-emperor John VIII, and the Byzantine court recognizes Bayezid’s son Mustafa as the lawful heir to the throne. Mustafa promises to hand [Gallipoli](@gallipoli-1421) over to Byzantium, and Byzantine galleys bring him and Cüneyd, the former bey of Aydın, from [Lemnos](@lemnos-1421) to Gallipoli, where the town goes over to him. Ottoman chroniclers call him Düzmece Mustafa, the false Mustafa.

Many of the beys and akıncı leaders of Rumelia join Mustafa. Murad’s grand vizier Bayezid Pasha leads the army at [Edirne](@edirne-1421) against him, and at Sazlıdere near the Gulf of Saros his troops and their commanders go over to Mustafa. Bayezid Pasha surrenders and is beheaded at Cüneyd’s urging, while his brother Hamza Bey is spared. Bayezid Pasha’s grave is at Sazlıdere.

Mustafa enters Edirne to the cheers of its people. He has the Friday sermon read in his name, strikes coins and rules the Ottoman lands in Europe, and silver akçes struck in his name at Edirne in 1421 survive, as do copper coins from Serres. When the Byzantines ask for Gallipoli, Mustafa refuses, saying that Islamic law forbids handing land won by conquest back to unbelievers.
