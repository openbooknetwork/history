In the spring of 335 BC Alexander leads his army north from [Amphipolis](@amphipolis-335). The peoples on Macedon’s northern frontier are thinking of revolt, Arrian writes, and Alexander will not leave them unbeaten at his back when he goes to Asia. He crosses the river Nessus and the Rhodope mountains, and on the tenth day reaches a pass over [Mount Haemus](@haemus-335).\* The free Thracians hold the summit and send waggons rolling down on the phalanx, but Alexander’s men open their ranks or lie under their locked shields. In Arrian’s account the waggons kill no one, and the Thracians are driven off.

Syrmus, king of the Triballi, the people between the Haemus and the Danube, has taken refuge on Peuce, an island in the river. His warriors circle behind the Macedonians and make a stand in a wooded glen by the river [Lyginus](@lyginus-335). Alexander’s archers and slingers draw them out, and his cavalry and phalanx break them. Arrian gives 3,000 Triballi dead and, following Ptolemy, one of Alexander’s officers, 11 Macedonian horsemen and about 40 foot.

Three days later the army reaches the [Danube](@danube-335), where warships from [Byzantium](@byzantium-335) meet him. They cannot land troops on Peuce: the banks are steep and the current fast. On the far bank wait the Getae, by Arrian’s count about 4,000 horse and more than 10,000 foot. In one night Alexander takes 1,500 horse and 4,000 foot across on the ships, on log canoes and on hides stuffed with hay. At dawn the Getae flee to their [town](@getic-town-335) and then leave it, and Alexander razes it and recrosses the river the same day. Syrmus and the other peoples by the Danube send envoys and submit.

The sites of the Lyginus battle, Peuce and the Getic town are not known.

### Notes

\* Which pass is disputed. Bosworth thinks it most likely to be the Trojan pass, the more direct route from the plain of Philippopolis, today’s Plovdiv. Hammond takes it to be probably the Shipka pass.
