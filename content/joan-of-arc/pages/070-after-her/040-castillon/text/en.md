In 1451 Charles VII's armies take Gascony, the English duchy of the south-west. [Bordeaux](@bordeaux-1453) agrees terms on 12 June and is handed over to the French commander Dunois on 30 June. [Bayonne](@bayonne-1451), besieged from 6 or 7 August, is entered on 20 August. On the mainland the English keep only Calais.

Bordeaux calls them back. John Talbot, Earl of Shrewsbury, a veteran captain whom Henry VI made his lieutenant in Aquitaine on 2 September 1452, lands in the Médoc on 17 October. The country round Bordeaux rises for him, and in October Bordeaux opens its gates and takes the French seneschal, Coëtivy, prisoner. This English Gascony of 1452 and 1453 runs from Bordeaux and the Médoc up the Dordogne to Castillon.

On 13 or 14 July 1453 the French lay siege to Castillon. Jean Bureau and his brother, who direct the French artillery, keep the guns in a camp ringed with ditches beside the Lidoire, a stream east of the town, while the Bretons hold the Mont Horable, a height to the north-east.

Talbot marches from Bordeaux to relieve the town and arrives at daybreak on 17 July, probably crossing the Dordogne at Branne. He drives French archers out of an abbey west of the town and attacks the camp. For about an hour the English fight at the palisades while the French guns fire into them. Then the Breton men-at-arms under the lord of Montauban and the lord of La Hunaudaye come down on the English left, and the French charge out of the camp. Talbot is killed, and his son Viscount Lisle with him. Four or five hundred English dead are buried.

Castillon surrenders the next day. Bordeaux, blockaded from 1 August, agrees terms early in October, and on 19 October 1453 Charles VII's officers take possession of the city. The Hundred Years' War ends here. Of all the lands the English held in France, Calais is left.
