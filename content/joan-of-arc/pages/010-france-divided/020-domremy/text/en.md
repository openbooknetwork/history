Joan is born about 1412 at [Domrémy](@domremy), a village on the upper Meuse that makes one parish with its neighbour Greux. When her judges at Rouen asked her age on 21 February 1431, she said she thought she was nineteen, and Lowell and Lang both say that the exact year cannot be known. Only one source gives her a birthday: a letter that Perceval de Boulainvilliers wrote to the Duke of Milan in 1429, which puts it on the night of the Epiphany, 6 January.

At the same hearing she gave her parents' names, Jacques d'Arc and Isabelle, and said she had been baptised at Domrémy. In 1455, questioned in the village for the retrial, her godfather Jean Morel said the same and named the church as Saint-Remy. Jacques's birthplace was Ceffonds, in Champagne; Isabelle, known as Romée, came from Vouthon. Beatrix Estellin, a widow of the village, remembered them in 1455 as people who worked the land, devout and respected, but not rich.

Joan told the court that her mother taught her the Paternoster, the Ave Maria and the Credo, and that at sewing and spinning she "feared no woman in Rouen". Whether she also kept the animals in the fields is told two ways.\*

Down the river stands [Vaucouleurs](@vaucouleurs-1412), a walled town and castle held for the king's party. Lang calls its district, with Domrémy and Greux, "a kind of island of loyalty" in country that was Anglo-Burgundian or outside France altogether. Across the Meuse the people of [Maxey](@maxey-1412) were Burgundians, Joan said, and in Domrémy she knew only one. Its boys and the boys of Domrémy fought as Burgundians against Armagnacs, a rivalry that Lowell thinks was more likely an old grudge between villages than politics.

The map shows the upper Meuse, where the kingdom, with Vaucouleurs, meets the duchies of Bar and Lorraine, with the county of Burgundy to the south. Domrémy lies on the edge of the kingdom and Bar, and who was its lord is disputed.\*\* The line drawn through the village, along the Three Fountains brook, is approximate.

### Notes

\* On 22 February 1431 Joan told the court that while she lived at home she did the work of the house and did not take the sheep or the other beasts out to the fields. Jean Morel told the inquiry of 1455 that up to the day she left home she worked behind the plough and at times watched the cattle. Thurston, writing in 1910, calls the popular picture of a child alone in the pastures with the flocks unfounded.

\*\* Lowell puts the part of Domrémy south of the Three Fountains brook in the duchy of Bar, so that Joan was born a subject of Bar and only at one remove of the King of France. Lang has her father's house divided from Lorraine only by a little stream, or even on the Lorraine side of the border, and notes that the villagers spoke of "going into France".
