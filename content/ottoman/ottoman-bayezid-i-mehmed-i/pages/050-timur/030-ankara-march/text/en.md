In March 1402 Timur holds a council of his princes and emirs in Karabagh, where many of the emirs speak against a war between two Muslim rulers. A comet seen that spring is read by his astrologers as a sign of victory in the west. He waits two months at Avnik, then gathers his army at [Erzurum](@erzurum-1402), and his grandson Muhammad Sultan takes Kemah after a siege of about ten days and hands it back to Taharten. At Sivas Timur receives Bayezid’s envoys and has his army parade before them from dawn to noon.

Bayezid lifts the blockade of Constantinople and gathers his army at [Bursa](@bursa), İznik and İzmit, with troops from Rumelia and Anatolia and those of his Christian vassals. It marches in two columns, by İzmit, Bolu and Gerede and by Geyve and Beypazarı, and camps around [Ankara](@ankara-1402) at the end of June. Against the advice of the grand vizier Çandarlı Ali Pasha, who wants to wait there, Bayezid leaves his baggage at Ankara, crosses the Kızılırmak and camps at [Tokat](@tokat-1402) to hold the mountain roads west of Sivas.

Timur avoids the mountain roads and turns south down the Kızılırmak valley. He reaches [Kayseri](@kayseri-1398) in about six days, spends four days there gathering the new harvest for his army and marches on by [Kırşehir](@kirsehir-1402), where his scouts meet the Ottoman outposts. Three days later he reaches Ankara and besieges the castle. Bayezid turns back and marches for eight days in the summer heat, by Yozgat and Kalecik in Ömer Halis Bıyıktay’s reconstruction, while Halil Çetin has him follow Timur’s road by Kırşehir. The Ottoman army reaches the plain north-east of Ankara tired and short of water.
