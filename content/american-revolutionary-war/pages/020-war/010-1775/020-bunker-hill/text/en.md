On 10 May Ethan Allen’s Green Mountain Boys and Benedict Arnold take Fort Ticonderoga on Lake Champlain, with its artillery, without a fight. The same day the Second Continental Congress meets in Philadelphia. In June it adopts the New England army outside Boston as the Continental Army and names George Washington of Virginia its commander.

Before he arrives, Gage moves against the militia entrenched on the Charlestown peninsula. On 17 June the British take Breed’s Hill, remembered as Bunker Hill, at the third assault. They lose over a thousand men, about 40 percent of the attacking force. Washington takes command at Cambridge on 3 July. In August the king declares the colonies in open rebellion.
