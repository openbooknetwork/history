Joan is born about 1412 at [Domrémy](@domremy), a village on the upper Meuse that makes one parish with its neighbour Greux. The exact year cannot be known: asked her age in 1431, she thinks she is nineteen. Only one source gives her a birthday: a letter that Perceval de Boulainvilliers writes to the Duke of Milan in 1429, which sets it on the night of the Epiphany, 6 January.

Her parents are Jacques d'Arc and Isabelle, and she is baptised at Domrémy, in the church of Saint-Remy. Jacques's birthplace is Ceffonds, in Champagne; Isabelle, known as Romée, comes from Vouthon. They are people who work the land, devout and respected, but not rich.

Her mother teaches her the Paternoster, the Ave Maria and the Credo, and to sew and spin so well that she "feared no woman in Rouen".\*

Down the river stands [Vaucouleurs](@vaucouleurs-1412), a walled town and castle held for the king's party. Its district, with Domrémy and Greux, stands alone for the king in country that is Anglo-Burgundian or outside France altogether. Across the Meuse the people of [Maxey](@maxey-1412) are Burgundians, and in Domrémy Joan knows only one. The boys of Maxey and of Domrémy fight as Burgundians against Armagnacs, a rivalry that may be an old grudge between villages more than politics.

On the upper Meuse the kingdom, with Vaucouleurs, meets the duchies of Bar and Lorraine, with the county of Burgundy to the south. Domrémy lies on the edge of the kingdom and Bar, and who is its lord is disputed.
