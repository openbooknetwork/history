Early in 238 BC the rebels send most of their army, about 50,000 men under [Spendius](@mercenary-war-trap-spendius), Autaritus and Zarzas, to stop [Hamilcar’s](@mercenary-war-trap-hamilcar) raids. For months the armies march and countermarch through the uplands, the rebels keeping to rough ground where the Carthaginian elephants and cavalry are of little use. Hamilcar has his prisoners trampled to death by elephants, which only makes the rebels fight harder.

In the end Hamilcar, with three divisions under himself, his deputy [Hannibal](@mercenary-war-trap-hannibal) and [Naravas](@mercenary-war-trap-naravas), traps the rebels in a pass or against a range of hills called the Saw for its jagged outline. The site is unknown. Before the rebels grasp their situation the Carthaginians fortify positions on ground that makes any attack hopeless, and more than 40,000 rebels are left with water but no food.
