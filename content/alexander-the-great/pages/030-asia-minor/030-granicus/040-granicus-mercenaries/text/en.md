Only the [Greek mercenaries](@granicus-mercenaries-mercenaries) are left. They have not moved from the high ground behind the river where they were posted. Arrian thinks shock at the sudden rout held them there, not any firm decision to fight. Plutarch says they ask for quarter, and that [Alexander](@granicus-mercenaries-alexander), in anger, charges them at the head of his men and has his horse killed under him.

Alexander leads the phalanx against their front and sends the cavalry against them from all sides, his own horse and [Parmenion's](@granicus-mercenaries-parmenion). Most of the Macedonians killed or wounded that day, Plutarch writes, fall here. The mercenaries are cut down where they stand, and the survivors are taken prisoner.\* No source says which cavalry fell on which flank.

The Persians lose far more men than the Macedonians, though the figures differ from one author to the next.\*\* Arsites flees into Phrygia and, Arrian reports, kills himself. Alexander sends 300 Persian suits of armour to Athens as an offering to Athena.

### Notes

\* Arrian says about 2,000 mercenaries are taken prisoner. Diodorus has more than 20,000 Persians taken alive.

\*\* Arrian gives the Macedonian dead as about 25 Companions, more than 60 other horsemen and about 30 foot soldiers; Aristobulus, as Plutarch cites him, gives 34 in all. For the Persians, Arrian gives only about 1,000 horsemen killed, since Alexander did not pursue far, besides the mercenaries. Plutarch puts the Persian dead at 20,000 foot and 2,500 horse, and Diodorus at more than 10,000 foot and 2,000 horse.
