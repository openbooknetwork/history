Charles VII stays at Reims until Thursday 21 July. Then he rides to the shrine of Saint-Marcoul at [Corbeny](@corbeny-1429) and touches the sick for the King's Evil, as kings do after their anointing. There the keys or the deputies of [Laon](@laon-1429) are brought to him. He goes on by Vailly on 22 July, and on Saturday 23 July he enters [Soissons](@soissons-1429), which has sent him its keys.

The road to Paris lies open, but the army turns south instead. [Château-Thierry](@chateau-thierry-1429), held for Burgundy by the lords of Châtillon, Croy and Brimeu, gives itself up: its townsmen lean to the king, and its lords expect no help soon. The king enters on the evening of 29 July and stays until 1 August. There on 31 July, at Joan's request, he frees Domrémy and Greux, her home villages, from taxes. Crécy and [Coulommiers](@coulommiers-1429) declare for him as well. On Tuesday 2 August he reaches [Provins](@provins-1429), where he stays until Friday 5 August.

Bedford, regent of France for Henry VI, has meanwhile had fresh troops from England, brought by Henry VI's great-uncle, Cardinal Beaufort: some 2,750 to 4,000 fighting men, who enter Paris late in July. On the last day of July the poet Christine de Pizan finishes her Ditié, a poem in praise of Joan.

Bedford will name Laon, Provins and [Sens](@sens-1429) among the cities lost after Orléans, though Sens still holds out against the king in September 1429.

The towns on the king's road from Reims to Provins are now his. Paris stays English, and whether the king will march on it is still undecided.
