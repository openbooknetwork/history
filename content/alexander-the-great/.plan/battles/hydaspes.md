# Battle of the Hydaspes, May 326 BC

Section 070 (india). Card on `hydaspes` (the campaign page `040-hydaspes/010-hydaspes`); three phase pages,
`hydaspes-crossing`, `hydaspes-cavalry`, `hydaspes-elephants`, after Arrian's sequence (claims 070.6–070.8). The
ground is Stein's (base camp at Haranpur, crossing at Jalalpur), drawn as a reconstruction by the cloud ruling of
decisions.md; Smith's and Breloer's Jhelum and Bosworth's plain of Gujrat are told in the text (070.9). Sides:
`macedon` (Alexander's army), `taxila` (Taxiles and his 5,000 Indians, with Craterus at the camp) and `porus`, the
story's families, as the section's plan names them.

## Sources

- **Arrian 5.8–19**, primary, from Ptolemy and Aristobulus. The only full account, and "the only basis for
  reconstructing the battle" (Bosworth, Comm. on 5.8–18). Gives the camps facing each other across the river
  (5.9, 5.15.3), the headland and island 150 stades from the camp (5.11.1–2), Craterus's force at the camp
  (5.11.3), the mercenaries between (5.12.1), the units of the striking force (5.12.2), the crossing, the second
  island and the ford (5.12.3–13.3), Alexander's order after the ford (5.13.4), the advance of the cavalry ahead
  of the foot (5.14.1), the two versions of the fight with Porus's son (5.14.3–15.2), Porus's order of battle on
  the sandy ground (5.15.3–7), Alexander's plan and the cavalry action (5.16–17.2), the elephants and the
  encirclement (5.17.3–7), the pursuit, losses and surrender (5.18), the cities (5.19).
- **Bosworth, Comm. on 5.8–19**, scholarly. The three readings of the site (Stein; Smith and Breloer; the river
  further east, the plain of Gujrat) and their weaknesses; the river's breadth at high water; whether the
  crossing was upstream; the islands; the place of the fight with Porus's son (twenty stades from the crossing,
  after the letter in Plutarch); the frontage of the Macedonian foot (700 m at most) and what it means for the
  Indian line; Coenus on the Macedonian left and his ride behind the Indian line; the light infantry on the
  wings; the letter-D encirclement (Fig. 4a, 4b).
- **Bosworth, C&E ch. 2.XV**, scholarly. The same reading in narrative: the camp backed by the Salt Range, the
  "clear sandy plain", Alexander taking the cavalry "across the front of the phalanx", the Indian horse filtered
  across the rear of the elephant line, Craterus's men "supplemented by the mercenary forces stationed along the
  bank upstream". Stein's Haranpur "176 km from Taxila" and Jalalpur "28 km upstream" (note 339). He puts
  Bucephala at the base camp, against Arrian (see Report).
- **Bosworth, CAH**, scholarly. Repeats C&E; the Indian cavalry "forced back into Porus' infantry column"; the
  cavalry "virtually enveloped the rear of the Indian line".
- **Curtius 8.13–14**, primary, the other tradition. The river four stades wide at the camp (8.13.8); the island
  "larger than the rest" and the ravine (8.13.17); Ptolemy's feints "farther down the river" (8.13.23), which
  puts the crossing upstream; Porus's advance force of 100 chariots and 4,000 horse under "Spitaces, his brother"
  (8.14.2); the chariots stuck in the mud (8.14.4); the Scythians and Dahae attacking first (8.14.5); Alexander
  against the left with Ptolemy, Perdiccas and Hephaestion, Coenus against the right, Antigenes, Leonnatus and
  Tauron against the centre (8.14.15); the Agrianians and Thracians sent at the elephants (8.14.24); the fight
  "until late in the day" (8.14.28); Porus's surrender (8.14.31–40).
- **Diodorus 17.87–89**, primary, short. Porus's army (87.2); the elephants "in a single line at equal intervals"
  with infantry between and cavalry on both flanks (87.4–5); the chariots "put out of action by Alexander's
  cavalry" (88.1); Porus with forty elephants (88.4); losses (89.1–3); the two cities (89.6).
- **Plutarch 60–62**, primary, from "Alexander's letter" (which Bosworth thinks a later elaboration). The small
  island and the breach; the cavalry led "twenty furlongs in advance of his infantry"; 1,000 horse and 60 chariots
  with 400 horse killed; Alexander against the left, Coenus against the right; the routed wings falling back on
  the elephants; the eighth hour (60). Porus's 20,000 foot and 2,000 horse (62).
- **McCrindle**, scholarly (1896), with Cunningham: the camp at Jalalpur and the crossing 17 miles upstream near
  Dilawar, a reading not named in 070.9; the Hydaspes "about a mile and a quarter" wide at Jalalpur; Nicaea at
  Mong. Polyaenus 4.3.22 in translation: Porus "at the head of his left wing", the elephants "within fifty yards
  of each other".
- **Dodge**, scholarly (1890), on Cunningham's ground. Gives Alexander's right flank leaning "upon the river or
  the river bottom-lands", Porus's front "about four miles" from Arrian's figures, and halves Arrian's horse and
  chariots per wing. Used for the facing and as the source of the four-mile figure; his own diagram is not drawn.
- **Tarn II, App. 6**, scholarly. The Jhelum–Jalalpur controversy; Arrian's "notable" bend "suits Jhelum, but will
  not suit Jalalpur". No positions.
- **WP**, for the date and the strengths' range; its coordinates (32.828, 73.639) are not Stein's site and are not
  used. Its battle narrative follows Arrian and modern writers and adds nothing to the positions.

The positions of both sides are given: Arrian places each Indian and Macedonian body relative to the others and
to the river. Only the site on the ground is a reconstruction.

## Ground

**The river.** The Hydaspes is the Jhelum (names.md). On Stein's ground it comes out of the gap between the
Salt Range and the Pabbi hills at Jalalpur and runs west-south-west along the foot of the Salt Range to Haranpur:
"The final bottleneck is the six-kilo- metre gap between the eastern outlier of the Salt Range and the western
flank of the Pabbi range ... In more recent times the Jhelum has skirted its north-western extremities, describing
a wide bow close to the Salt Range" (Bosworth, Comm. on 9.1). `reconstruction`: Bosworth holds the ancient river
"ran more to the east" and the battle site "should be located in the middle of the plain of Gujrat" (Comm. on
9.1); the ruling keeps Stein. The implementor draws the river as `water` along its modern course (no source file
gives it point by point; the base map's relief shows the Salt Range it follows).

- Width at high water: "According to Curtius (8. 13. 8) the river was four stades wide in the area of
  Alexander’s base camp. That corresponds to modern estimates of its breadth at high water, between 600 m. and
  1.6 km." (Bosworth, Comm. on 9.4); "Extending to a width of four stadia" (Curtius 8.13.8); "The breadth of the
  Hydaspes at Jalalpur is about a mile and a quarter" (McCrindle, Note J, after Cunningham). `sourced` within
  600 m–2 km; drawn 1.5 km at the crossing.
- Alexander's base camp, on the right (north-west) bank near Haranpur, with Craterus: "The base camp was pitched
  near modern Haranpur, almost exactly 120 Roman miles from Taxila" (Bosworth, Comm. on 9.1, after Stein).
  `reconstruction` (070.9). About lon 73.13, lat 32.62: take the campaign page's marker `hydaspes-camp-326`.
  About 22 km downstream of the phase frame's origin, so off the phase pages.
- Porus's camp, on the left bank opposite: "the great camp which was directly opposite his position" (Arrian
  5.15.3). `sourced` for "opposite", `reconstruction` for the place (marker `porus-camp-326`). Off the phase pages.
- The crossing: "There was in the bank of the Hydaspes, a projecting headland, where the river makes a remarkable
  bend. It was densely covered by a grove ... over against it in the river was an island full of trees and
  without a foot-track ... The headland and island were 150 stades distant from his great camp" (Arrian 5.11.1–2);
  "the crossing was made at Jalalpur nearly 30 km. (150 stades) upstream, where a significant ravine (the Kandar
  Kas) joins the river" (Bosworth, Comm. on 9.1, after Stein); "Stein's location of the crossing-point by
  Jalalpur where a spur of the Salt range does abut the river" (Comm. on 11.1). `sourced` for the 150 stades and
  the features, `reconstruction` for Jalalpur (Jalalpur Sharif, about lon 73.40, lat 32.65; take the campaign
  page's marker `bucephala-326`). Bosworth adds that Stein "was unable to find a suitable bend in the river"
  (Comm. on 9.1) and Tarn that the bend "will not suit Jalalpur" (Tarn II, App. 6): the text's clause that the
  site is approximate covers it.
- Upstream or downstream: "Frontinus (Siral. 1. 4. 9) states explicitly that Alexander crossed the river per
  superiorem partem, while Curtius (8. 13. 23) claims that, when Alexander crossed, the enemy's attention was
  focused on Ptolemy on the lower reaches of the river ... But the conclusion is insecure." (Bosworth, Comm. on
  11.2); "those who, with Ptolemy, had occupied a part of the bank farther down the river" (Curtius 8.13.23).
  `sourced` as the majority reading, which Stein's ground follows: the crossing lies upstream (north-east) of the
  camp.
- The two islands and the ford: the first island "full of trees" before the headland (Arrian 5.11.1), "larger
  than the rest, wooded also and suitable for concealing an ambuscade" (Curtius 8.13.17); the landing "on ground
  which was not a part of the mainland, but an island, a large one indeed ... It was cut off from the rest of the
  land by a part of the river where the water was shallow" (Arrian 5.13.2); "The first was clearly the larger"
  (Bosworth, Comm. on 13.2); "crossed over to a small island" (Plutarch 60). `sourced` for the order (headland,
  island 1, channel, island 2, shallow channel, left bank); their sizes and places are `reconstruction`: "The
  islands will certainly not be discovered, for they appear and disappear with each major flood" (Bosworth,
  Comm. on 9.1).
- The landing ground: clay. "useless in the action itself on account of the clayey ground" (Arrian 5.15.2); "the
  heavy and almost immovable chariots stuck fast in the muck and mudholes" (Curtius 8.14.4). `sourced`.
- The field: sand. "When he found a place where he saw there was no clay, but that on account of the sand the
  ground was all level and hard, and thus fit for the advance and retreat of horses, he there drew up his army"
  (Arrian 5.15.4); "chose a clear sandy plain to mount his defence" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced` for the ground;
  its place is `reconstruction`: on the left bank downstream of the landing, between the crossing and Porus's
  camp, a few kilometres from the river. It rests on three things: Porus marches from his camp "against
  Alexander" (Arrian 5.15.4); the fight with his son took place "some twenty stades from the crossing" (Bosworth,
  Comm. on 14.6, after Plutarch 60.7) and Curtius and Ptolemy place that skirmish "relatively close to the site
  of the main battle" (Bosworth, C&E, note); and Nicaea stands on the field (Arrian 5.19.4), which the campaign
  page puts between the crossing and Porus's camp. The plan puts the centre of the field 5.5 km downstream of the
  landing and 2.5 km in from the left bank, off the riverside clay.

**Facing.** Alexander, coming downstream from the crossing, faces south-west; Porus, coming upstream from his
camp, faces north-east. `reconstruction` from the crossing upstream of the camps (above) and Porus marching from
his camp to meet him (Arrian 5.15.4). So the battle line runs across the plain from the river (north-west) inland
(south-east), and the Indian left and the Macedonian right are the river ends: "Alexander had advanced so as to be
able to lean his right flank constantly upon the river or the river bottom-lands" (Dodge, ch. XLI, on
Cunningham's ground, which has the same orientation). `reconstruction`.

**Width of the front.** Two sourced estimates disagree:
- The Macedonian foot: "The Macedonian phalanx, no more than 6,000 strong ... cannot have occupied a frontage
  wider than 700 m., if it was arranged eight deep with files a metre apart (cf. Devine 101)" (Bosworth, Comm. on
  15.4). `sourced` as Bosworth's estimate; drawn 700 m.
- The Indian line: Arrian's 200 elephants "not less than a plethrum apart" (5.15.4–5) make a line of about 6 km;
  Dodge's reading of it is "a front of about four miles" (ch. XLI). Bosworth rejects it: "That would allow for
  less than fifty elephants in the Indian line, if it was comparable in length to the Macedonian array ... it
  does rule out Arrian's figure for the elephants as a blatant exaggeration" (Comm. on 15.4), since Alexander
  "was able to envelop Porus’ entire phalanx" (15.4n, after Arrian 5.17.7). `disputed`. The plan follows
  Bosworth: elephants and centre infantry 1,000 m, wing infantry and cavalry outside that, the whole Indian line
  about 2.8 km. The 1,000 m is a size picked for legibility and appears in no text. The elephant spacing is
  `disputed` too: a plethrum, 100 ft (Arrian 5.15.4; Chinnock's note 683); "Polyaenus gives a more credible
  variant of fifty feet" (Bosworth, Comm. on 15.5); "within fifty yards of each other" (Polyaenus in Shepherd's
  translation, McCrindle Note K).

## Order of battle

### Macedon, crossing force, from the right (river end) to the left

"He then picked the select body-guard called the Companions, as well as the cavalry regiments of Hephaestion,
Perdiccas, and Demetrius, the cavalry from Bactria, Sogdiana, and Scythia, and the Daan horse-archers; and from
the phalanx of infantry the shield-bearing guards, the brigades of Clitus and Coenus, with the archers and
Agrianians" (Arrian 5.12.2). Totals: "he ordered the infantry to follow at a slow pace and in regular order,
numbering as it did not much under 6,000 men; and because he thought he was superior in cavalry, he took only his
horse-soldiers, who were 5,000 in number" (Arrian 5.14.1). No source gives a strength per unit except the horse
archers; the unit names leave numbers out except where shown.

### Porus, from the left (river end, opposite Alexander) to the right

"First he placed the elephants in the front, each animal being not less than a plethrum apart, so that they might
be extended in the front before the whole of the phalanx of infantry ... Near these he had posted the infantry,
not occupying a line on a level with the beasts, but in a second line behind them, only so far distant that the
companies of foot might be pushed forward a short distance into the spaces between them. He had also bodies of
infantry standing beyond the elephants on the wings; and on both sides of the infantry he had posted the cavalry,
in front of which were placed the chariots on both wings of his army" (Arrian 5.15.4–7). "He stationed his cavalry
upon both flanks, and arranged his elephants ... in a single line ... at equal intervals along his front. Between
these beasts he placed the rest of his infantry" (Diodorus 17.87.4). "The Indian infantry formed a line with the
rear of the elephants: the front of the beasts projected outwards" (Bosworth, Comm. on 15.6). All `sourced`.

Totals on the field: "He then took all his cavalry to the number of 4,000 men, all his chariots to the number of
300, with 200 of his elephants and 30,000 choice infantry" (Arrian 5.15.4), `disputed` (070.10; see Card). No
source divides them between the wings; Dodge's "Two thousand Indian cavalry was on each extreme flank, and in the
front of the cavalry of each flank one hundred and fifty chariots" (ch. XLI) halves Arrian and is not drawn as a
figure. The unit names carry no numbers.

## Phases

### Phase 1: `hydaspes-crossing`, "The crossing in the storm"

**Opens.** Night, in the storm, before dawn. The striking force (M1–M6) is in the wood behind the headland, with
the boats and the skin rafts: "made a secret march, keeping far away from the bank of the river ... There the
skins were filled in the night with the hay ... Most of the vessels, the thirty-oared galleys included with the
rest, had been cut in pieces by his order and conveyed to this place, where they had been fixed together again and
hidden in the wood" (Arrian 5.12.2–4). M7 on the right bank between the crossing and the camp (Arrian 5.12.1).
Porus's scouts along the left bank: "sent guards to all the other parts of the river which were easily fordable"
(Arrian 5.9.1); "the sentinels posted by Porus" (5.12.4). Porus's army at his camp, off the page. `sourced`.

**Moves.**

1. The crossing on boats and rafts: one `macedon` arrow from the headland wood (−4,250, −5,800) across the main
   channel, round the downstream tip of island 1 (−3,700, −4,750), and across the middle channel onto island 2
   (−2,850, −5,400). "At the approach of daylight, both the wind and the rain calmed down; and the rest of the
   army went over opposite the island, the cavalry mounting upon the skins, and as many of the foot soldiers as
   the boats would receive getting into them. They went so secretly that they were not observed by the sentinels
   posted by Porus, before they had already got beyond the island and were only a little way from the other
   bank" (Arrian 5.12.4); "Alexander himself was the first to land" (5.13.1). `sourced` for the sequence;
   `reconstruction` for the line round the island's downstream tip.
2. The ford and the cavalry's ride ahead: one `macedon` arrow from island 2 (−2,700, −5,500) across the shallow
   channel (−2,450, −5,400) and down the left bank to M2's end place (−1,900, −2,850). "But when at last the ford
   was found, he led his men through it with much difficulty; for where the water was deepest, it reached higher
   than the breasts of the infantry; and of the horses only the heads rose above the river" (Arrian 5.13.3); "he
   took only his horse-soldiers, who were 5,000 in number, and led them forward with speed" (5.14.1); "after he had
   crossed he led his horsemen" ... "twenty furlongs in advance of his infantry" (Plutarch 60). `sourced`; the

3. The foot follows slowly: one `macedon` arrow from island 2 (−2,700, −5,700) across the ford to M4–M5's end

   "Horse archers came in front of the cavalry line, infantry behind" (Bosworth, Comm. on 13.4). `sourced`.
4. Porus's son comes up: one `porus` arrow from the south-west edge (−900, +4,500) to (−1,750, −2,050). "Ptolemy
   says that the son of Porus arrived at the head of 2000 cavalry and 120 chariots; but that Alexander had already
   made even the last passage from the island before he appeared" (Arrian 5.14.6). `sourced` that he comes;
   `reconstruction` that he comes from downstream, where Porus's camp lay (5.15.3). The other versions of his force
   are in the Card.
5. His rout: one `porus` `dashed` arrow from (−1,550, −2,000) back to the south-west edge (−600, +4,500). "Alexander

   rapid charge upon them with the cavalry around him ... they gave way; and 400 of their cavalry, including the
   son of Porus, fell in the contest. The chariots also were captured, horses and all, being heavy and slow in the
   retreat, and useless in the action itself on account of the clayey ground. When the horsemen who had escaped
   from this rout brought news to Porus" (Arrian 5.15.1–3); "The Scythians and the Dahae first of all attacked the
   Indi" (Curtius 8.14.5). `sourced`; the direction, back towards Porus, is `reconstruction` from "brought news to
   Porus".

Clash: (−1,800, −2,050), size 250, the horse archers and Companions against the son's force. Its place,
`reconstruction`: "If ‘Alexander’s letter’ is correct (Plut. Al. 60. 7), the engagement took place some twenty
stades from the crossing" (Bosworth, Comm. on 14.6). Aristobulus put the encounter at the ford itself (Arrian
5.14.3); Arrian follows Ptolemy, and so does the plan.

**End blocks.**

### Phase 3: `hydaspes-elephants`, "The elephants"

The sources give two moments here: the elephant fight and the encirclement (Arrian 5.17.3–7), then the pursuit and
the surrender (5.18). The page shows the end of both.

**Opens.** As phase 2 ends: the Indian cavalry among the elephants and the left of the Indian foot; the
Companions on its flank and Coenus in its rear; the Macedonian foot at w = −450, unengaged.

**Moves.**

1. The elephants turn on the cavalry and the phalanx goes in against them: one `macedon` arrow from the foot's
   centre (0, −450) to (0, +250), with a clash at the elephant line. "Upon this, the drivers of the elephants urged
   forward the beasts against the cavalry; but now the phalanx itself of the Macedonians was advancing against the
   elephants, the men casting darts at the riders and also striking the beasts themselves, standing round them on
   all sides ... wherever the beasts could wheel round, they rushed forth against the ranks of infantry and
   demolished the phalanx of the Macedonians, dense as it was" (Arrian 5.17.3); "Alexander sent against the
   elephants the light-armed Agriani and the other Thracians ... These cast a great shower of weapons both upon the
   elephants and upon those who were guiding them. The phalanx also began steadily to attack the terrified
   animals" (Curtius 8.14.24–25); "They used their long spears to good effect against the Indians stationed beside
   the elephants" (Diodorus 17.88.2). `sourced`. The light troops (M3) move up on both ends of the foot with it;
   their end blocks show it, no separate arrow.
2. The cavalry, now one body, rings the Indian line: one `macedon` arrow from the Indian left flank (−1,100, +700)
   through (−600, +1,250) and (+400, +1,300) to the Indian right flank (+800, +850). "The Indian cavalry also,
   seeing that the infantry were engaged in the action, rallied again and advanced against the Macedonian cavalry.
   But when Alexander’s men ... got the mastery over them the second time, they were again repulsed towards the

   that the infantry should link their shields together so as to form a very densely closed body, and thus advance
   in phalanx" (5.17.7); "The visual effect would have been that of a letter D, with the Indian forces enclosed in
   the centre" (Bosworth, Comm. on 17.7, and Fig. 4b). `sourced`; the arrow's line round the rear is
   `reconstruction`.
3. The Indians break out and flee: one `porus` `dashed` arrow from the Indian rear (+300, +950) through (+1,000,

   cavalry cordon and allowed some of the infantry to escape" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced` that they flee;

4. Craterus crosses and takes up the pursuit: one `macedon` arrow from the south-west edge (−600, +4,500) to

## Card

- **Sides**: `macedon` (Alexander's army), with `taxila` (Taxiles's Indians, at the camp with Craterus), against
  `porus`.
- **Date**: May 326 BC. "fought in the archonship of Hegemon at Athens, in the month Munychion" (Arrian 5.19.3;
  Chinnock: 18 April to 18 May); "probably in the first weeks of May" (Bosworth, Comm. on 9.4). Diodorus dates it
  to the archonship of Chremes (17.87.1), which Welles's note reads, after Fontana, as about July 326; Tarn puts
  the crossing "Shortly after the summer solstice" (vol. I) and McCrindle thinks "the later date has probability
  in its favour". The card keeps May with Arrian (070.6).
- **Commanders**: Macedon: Alexander; Craterus at the base camp (Arrian 5.11.3); Coenus with his own and
  Demetrius's hipparchies (5.16.3); Seleucus, Antigenes and Tauron leading the foot (5.16.3); Meleager, Attalus,
  son of Andromenes, and Gorgias along the bank (5.12.1). Taxila: Taxiles (5.8.5, 5.18.6). Porus: Porus; his son,
  who leads the force at the landing and is killed (5.14–15). Curtius names the leader of that force "Spitaces,
  his brother" (8.14.2), and Arrian has a Spitaces, "the governor of the Indians of that district", among the dead
  (5.18.2); Bosworth: "The advance force was known to have been commanded by a relative of Porus ... but there was
  no unanimity over his identity" (Comm. on 14.4). The card gives "Porus's son" with Arrian.
- **Strengths**:
  - Macedon: the crossing force "not much under 6,000" foot and 5,000 horse (Arrian 5.14.1; 070.11); the whole
    army `unknown`. Craterus's force `unknown` but for Taxiles's 5,000 Indians (Arrian 5.8.5, 5.11.3).
  - Porus, `disputed` (070.10): Arrian 4,000 horse, 300 chariots, 200 elephants and 30,000 foot (5.15.4);
    Diodorus "more than fifty thousand infantry, about three thousand cavalry, more than a thousand chariots of
    war, and one hundred and thirty elephants" (17.87.2); Curtius "eighty-five elephants ... 300 chariots and about
    30,000 foot-soldiers" (8.13.6); Plutarch "twenty thousand infantry and two thousand horse" (62).
  - Porus's son's force, `disputed`: Ptolemy 2,000 horse and 120 chariots; Aristobulus about 60 chariots (both
    Arrian 5.14.3–6); the letter 1,000 horse and 60 chariots (Plutarch 60); Curtius 4,000 horse and 100 chariots

- **Casualties**, `disputed` (070.12):
  - Porus: Arrian "little short of 20,000 infantry and 3,000 cavalry", two sons of Porus, Spitaces and all the
    chariots (5.18.2), and 400 horse with Porus's son at the landing (5.15.2); Diodorus "more than twelve
    thousand", the two sons of Porus among them, and "Above nine thousand men were taken alive, together with eighty
    elephants" (17.89.1–2).
  - Macedon: Arrian "about 80 of the 6,000 foot-soldiers", 10 horse archers, "about 20 of the Companion cavalry,
    and about 200 of the other horsemen" (5.18.3); Diodorus "two hundred and eighty cavalry and more than seven
    hundred infantry" (17.89.3).
- **Outcome**: Macedonian victory. Porus is wounded, surrenders and is confirmed as king with more land: "not only
  granted him the rule over his own Indians, but also added another country to that which he had before, of
  larger extent than the former" (Arrian 5.19.3; 070.6).
