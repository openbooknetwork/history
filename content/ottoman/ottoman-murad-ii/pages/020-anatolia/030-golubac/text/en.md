Despot Stefan Lazarević of Serbia dies on 19 July 1427. By the agreement that he made with King Sigismund at Tata in May 1426, his nephew Đurađ Branković succeeds him, and Belgrade, Mačva and Golubac go back to Hungary. Sigismund stays at [Belgrade](@belgrade-1427) from 17 September to 19 November 1427. The commander of Golubac, Jeremija, asks 12,000 ducats for the fortress, and when Sigismund refuses, Jeremija hands it to the Ottomans.

Murad answers the Hungarian advance with an army that takes [Niš](@nis-1427) and [Kruševac](@krusevac-1427) and besieges [Novo Brdo](@novo-brdo-1427), and in the autumn a Hungarian and Serbian force destroys an Ottoman detachment near Ravanica. In the spring of 1428 Sigismund besieges Golubac from the land and from the Danube with Dan II’s Wallachians and Polish horsemen under the knight Zawisza the Black. Sigismund’s guns, firing from ships and from the new fort of Lászlóvár on the north bank, break the walls.

Murad arrives with his main army in late May, and Sigismund, who does not want to fight it, agrees a truce. When the Hungarians begin to cross the Danube in early June, the Ottoman commander Sinan Bey falls on their rear. Zawisza is killed with the men who cover the crossing, and Sigismund escapes with the help of Cecília Rozgonyi, who commands one of his ships. Under the peace, Đurađ rules Serbia as a vassal of both the sultan and the king, and in the autumn of 1428 he begins a new capital at [Smederevo](@smederevo) on the Danube, whose inner city is finished in 1430.
