In the 10th century the Dál gCais, until then a small kingdom in what is now County Clare, rise to rule Munster. In 964 Mathgamain, Brian’s elder brother, takes Cashel, the seat of the kings of Munster. In 968 he defeats the Norse of Limerick under Ivar at Sulcoit and burns Limerick the next day. Mathgamain is killed in 976, and Brian kills Ivar on Scattery Island in 977 and defeats his Munster rival Máel Muad at Belach Lechta in 978.

In 980 Máel Sechnaill, king of Tara, defeats the Norse of Dublin and the Isles at Tara. Amlaíb Cuarán, king of Dublin, goes into religious retirement on Iona, and Máel Sechnaill besieges Dublin for three days and takes away the hostages held there, 2,000 by the Annals of the Four Masters. In 989 he besieges the town again for twenty nights and takes it.

From 982 Brian and Máel Sechnaill fight each other, mostly in Leinster, until they meet at Clonfert in 997 and divide Ireland, the south to Brian and the north to Máel Sechnaill. Late in 999 Leinster and Dublin revolt against Brian. On 30 December Brian and Máel Sechnaill defeat Máel Mórda, king of Leinster, and Sigtrygg Silkbeard, king of Dublin, at Glenmama, probably near Lyons Hill. On 1 January 1000 Brian’s army burns Dublin and the grove of Thor beside it.

Brian lets Sigtrygg keep Dublin after he submits and gives him a daughter in marriage. Dublin strikes its first coins about 995, in Sigtrygg’s name. In 1000 Brian turns against Máel Sechnaill, and in 1002 at Athlone Máel Sechnaill acknowledges him as high king of Ireland.
