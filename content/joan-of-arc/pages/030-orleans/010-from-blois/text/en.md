On 27 or 28 April 1429 the convoy for besieged Orléans, a long train of wagons and a drove of cattle, crosses the bridge at [Blois](@blois), with the town's priests walking ahead and singing the Veni Creator. Anatole France gives the 27th, noting that the German chronicler Windecke has the 28th; Lowell gives the 28th, and Ramsay leaves it at one or the other. The escort is led by the marshals Gilles de Rais and de Boussac, with the admiral de Culant, La Hire and Ambroise de Loré.\* Joan rides with them. Raoul de Gaucourt, governor of Orléans, has brought her here from Tours.

She wants to march straight through the Beauce, on the north bank, where the English are. The captains take the south bank through the Sologne instead, because the English are strongest in the Beauce, and they do not tell her. On 28 April, by Lang, France and Ramsay, the convoy comes past Olivet, across the river from the city.\*\* The Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois), who commands there for the king, crosses to meet her. In his own account of 1456 she asks him at once whether it was he who had her brought this way, and not straight to Talbot and the English.

The army is too weak to force a way in, so the supplies go by water. Boats sent up from the city are loaded upstream, before [Chécy](@checy), at the Île-aux-Bourdons, says the Orléans chronicle of the feast of 8 May.\*\*\* The wind, against them until then, turns, and the laden boats sail down past the English bastilles while the garrison skirmishes before the English fort of Saint-Loup.

Joan crosses with about 200 lances, the figure of Simon Beaucroix, a squire of her company, and of Ramsay. The rest of the escort goes back to Blois, the nearest bridge, to come again by the north bank, and her confessor Jean Pasquerel goes with it, with the priests and her banner. The Journal of the Siege, kept in the city, has her company lie a night at Chécy.\*\*\*\* The captains agree that she will wait for dark, to spare her the crowds.

At about eight in the evening of Friday 29 April she rides into [Orléans](@orleans-entry), armed, on a white horse, with the Bastard on her left and townspeople carrying torches; the English do nothing to stop her. She lodges with Jacques Boucher, the Duke of Orléans's treasurer, by the Porte Regnart.

The map shows the convoy's road up the south bank, the boats coming down the river and her ride into the city. These lines, like the places of the English forts on the pages after this one, are approximate: no plan of the bastilles survives in the sources.

### Notes

\* Lowell puts the army at about 3,000, and Lang finds the same number in Windecke and the Chronique de Tournai. Monstrelet gives 7,000, and Joan told her judges in 1431 that the king gave her 10,000 or 12,000 men. Lang calls the large figures impossible: such an army would soon have eaten the supplies it brought.

\*\* Lowell has her reach the heights of Olivet about noon on Friday 29 April.

\*\*\* Dunois remembered the convoy coming to the river facing the church of Saint-Loup, and Ramsay follows him. Lowell has the boats taken five miles up the river from the city.

\*\*\*\* Lang and France have her sleep at Reuilly, the house of Guy de Cailly, and Ramsay at Rully near Chécy. Lowell takes the Journal's Chécy for a slip of the pen and reads Cléry, on the march.
