# India

## Header
- Years: late spring 327 BC to summer 325 BC
- Opens: Alexander holds the former Persian Empire to the Jaxartes and the Hindu Kush (`macedon-329b`). The
  risings in Bactria and Sogdiana have been put down after nearly two years, and Amyntas, son of Nicolaus, stays
  in Bactria with 10,000 foot and 3,500 horse (060.15). The reader has been told of Roxana's marriage, the
  proskynesis quarrel and the pages' conspiracy (060.14, 060.16, 060.17). Beyond the Hindu Kush lie the Cophen
  valley and the Indian princes, none of them under Alexander. Earlier pages have named the Indians in Darius's
  army (050.6), Bucephalus (010.12), Craterus, Coenus and Stein.
- Tells:
  - late spring 327 BC: Alexander crosses the Hindu Kush again and marches down the Cophen valley. Taxiles and
    the chiefs west of the Indus come to meet him, and Hephaestion and Perdiccas go ahead to bridge the Indus
    (070.1)
  - 327 BC: the siege of Massaga; the Indian mercenaries surrender on terms and are cut down (070.2)
  - winter 327–326 BC: the rock of Aornos is taken (070.3)
  - spring 326 BC: the Indus is crossed on a bridge of boats, Taxila receives Alexander, and Taxiles's army
    joins him (070.4); Porus holds the far bank of the Hydaspes (070.5)
  - May 326 BC: the battle of the Hydaspes. Porus is beaten, captured and confirmed as king (070.6–070.12).
    Nicaea and Bucephala are founded, and Bucephalus dies (070.13)
  - summer 326 BC: at the Hyphasis the army refuses to go on. Alexander raises twelve altars, gives the land up
    to the Hyphasis to Porus and turns back (070.14)
  - from November 326 BC: the fleet carries the army down the Hydaspes and the Acesines (070.15)
  - winter 326–325 BC: the Mallian town; the arrow in Alexander's chest (070.16)
  - 325 BC: Craterus's column leaves for Carmania by Arachosia and Drangiana (070.17)
  - summer 325 BC: Patala, and the Ocean reached (070.17)
- Introduces: people: Hephaestion, Taxiles, Porus, Seleucus, Tauron (on his marker), Meleager, Attalus, son of Andromenes, Gorgias,
  Demetrius, son of Althaemenes, Peucestas, Leonnatus, Abreas, Antigenes, Onesicritus, and the scholars Smith and
  Breloer. States and peoples: the kingdom of Taxiles (Taxila), the kingdom of Porus, the Malli. Places: the
  Cophen, Bajaur, Swat, Massaga, Aornos, the Indus, Taxila, the Hydaspes, Nicaea, Bucephala, the Acesines, the
  Hydraotes, the Hyphasis, the Mallian town, Patala, the Indian Ocean and Carmania.
- Battles: Aornos (card, no phase pages); the Hydaspes (card and 3 phase pages, drawn as a reconstruction since
  the site is disputed, 070.9); the Mallian town (card, no phase pages). Massaga is told without a card.
- Hands on: Alexander holds the Indus valley down to Patala and the Ocean. Taxiles and Porus rule as his vassal
  kings, Porus up to the Hyphasis. Craterus's column is marching west through Arachosia and Drangiana towards
  Carmania. The fleet waits at Patala to sail along an unknown coast, and Alexander is about to march west by
  land. Still unsettled: whether the fleet can make the voyage, the desert road ahead (080), and how long a
  conquest held by terror will last in India (Bosworth, ch. 2.XIV and 2.XVI).

## Pages

**cophen**, 327 BC. Alexander leaves Bactra at the end of spring 327, crosses the Hindu Kush in ten days and
marches down the Cophen (Kabul) valley. Taxiles and the chiefs west of the Indus come to meet him. He divides the
army: Hephaestion, Alexander's closest friend, and Perdiccas, a Macedonian general first met in 020 (a few words
on who he is), with about half the Macedonians and all the mercenaries, take the main road down
the Cophen to the Indus to bridge it. Alexander takes the picked troops into the hills north of the river, Bajaur
and Swat, storms the strongholds and besieges Massaga. He is wounded in the ankle there. The 7,000 Indian
mercenaries surrender on terms and are cut down. Why: no digest line carries the cause, so the text gives it at
most in one clause naming Bosworth: in his reading Taxiles had offered his help in Sogdiana, and Alexander meant
to restore Persian rule in the Kabul valley and carry it to the Ocean (ch. 2.XIV). Map change: the army
enters the Indian lands in two columns, Hephaestion's along the Cophen to the Indus and Alexander's through the
hills to Massaga.
`Rests on:` 070.1 (single, Arrian: told as Arrian's account). 070.2: the siege is agreed; the massacre is
`disputed`, and the text gives both accounts, each with its author. 070.19 (single, Bosworth: the site of
Massaga, given as his placing).
The massacre: Arrian says the mercenaries meant to slip away by night rather than fight other Indians, Diodorus
has them leave under the truce unsuspecting, and Plutarch calls the act a stain on Alexander's career.

**aornos**, winter 327–326 BC. The people of the region flee to the rock of Aornos, which a local story said
had defied Heracles. Arrian gives it a circuit of about 200 stades and one ascent, and he doubts the Heracles
story; Diodorus gives 100 furlongs. Alexander garrisons the towns behind him. Ptolemy seizes a ridge with local
guides, the army builds a mound across the ravine, and the defenders give way. Arrian has Alexander let them start
away by night and then fall on them with 700 men; Diodorus has him open the path and let them leave. Meanwhile Hephaestion and Perdiccas reach the Indus and bridge it,
and the land west of the Indus is put under a Macedonian satrap. Why: the rock was the last refuge of the
Assacenians, and Arrian says the Heracles legend made Alexander want it all the more (Arrian 4.28). Map change:
the Cophen valley and the land west of the Indus become Macedonian (`macedon-326`); Aornos above the Indus is taken
and the bridge stands ready.
`Rests on:` 070.3: the taking is agreed and told plainly; its measures and its ending are `disputed`, and the
text gives Arrian's and Diodorus's versions, each with its author; the Heracles story is a tale Arrian doubts.
070.18 is `disputed`: the text names Stein's Pir Sar, which Bosworth holds decisive, and Tucci's and Eggermont's
Mt Ilam, and says the map draws Pir Sar as a reconstruction. 070.1 for the bridge.

**taxila**, spring 326 BC. Alexander crosses the Indus on Hephaestion's bridge of boats, probably near Ohind, and
sacrifices. Taxiles receives him at Taxila, the largest city between the Indus and the Hydaspes, and is confirmed
in his kingdom. Taxiles's 5,000 Indians join the army. Porus, king between the Hydaspes and the Acesines, refuses
to submit and holds the far bank of the Hydaspes. Why: no digest line carries the cause, so the text gives it at
most in one clause naming Bosworth: Taxiles hoped to use Alexander's army against his neighbours, Porus above all
(ch. 2.XV). Map change: the army crosses the Indus into the
kingdom of Taxiles, its first ally east of the river, and faces Porus's kingdom across the Hydaspes.
`Rests on:` 070.4 (agreed; the 5,000 are Arrian's figure). 070.5 (agreed). 070.1 for Taxiles's first meeting.

**hydaspes** (campaign page of the group `040-hydaspes`), May 326 BC. Porus lines the far bank with his
elephants. Alexander keeps him guessing with noise along the river by night. He leaves Craterus at the main camp
and Meleager, Attalus, son of Andromenes, and Gorgias along the bank between, and crosses in a storm at a wooded
headland and island 150 stades upstream. He routs Porus's son at the landing, draws Porus out against him, and
beats him in a battle of cavalry and elephants. Porus is wounded and captured, and Alexander confirms him as king.
Nicaea is founded on the battlefield, and Bucephala, named for Bucephalus, who dies there about thirty years old,
at the crossing by Arrian's account or at the base camp by Bosworth's. The text gives the month as Arrian's, May
326 BC (the Athenian month Munychion), and adds that Diodorus implies a date about July. Why: Porus holds the far bank to stop the crossing (070.5); Arrian adds, in a clause naming him,
that the river was rising with the snows and the rains. Map change: the army crosses the Hydaspes upstream of Porus's camp and
beats him on his own bank; Nicaea and Bucephala appear on the river.
`Rests on:` 070.5, 070.6, 070.7, 070.8 (agreed). 070.9 is `disputed`: the text names Stein's camp at Haranpur
and crossing at Jalalpur, Smith's and Breloer's Jhelum and Fort Mangla, and Bosworth's view that the river ran
further east and the battle lay in the plain of Gujrat. It also says the map draws Stein's sites as a
reconstruction. 070.10 and 070.12 are `disputed`: the card gives each figure with its author, and a figure in the
text names Arrian or Diodorus. 070.11 (single, Arrian). 070.6 for the date, going `disputed` in the digest: May
is told as Arrian's, with Diodorus implying about July. 070.13: the founding of the cities is agreed; Bucephala's
site, going `disputed`, is told as at the crossing (Arrian) or the base camp (Bosworth). The cause of
Bucephalus's death is `disputed`: Arrian and Onesicritus say toil and old age, while most writers in Plutarch say
wounds.
Minor commanders (Alcetas, Polyperchon, Spitaces, Meroes, the battalion commander Cleitus, Curtius's Leonnatus)
stay out of the text and the markers.

- phase **hydaspes-crossing**, May 326 BC: the night crossing at the headland, the landing on the second island,
  the ford, and the rout of Porus's son (070.7, 070.8).
- phase **hydaspes-cavalry**, May 326 BC: Porus's line on the sandy plain. Alexander strikes the Indian left with
  the horse archers and the Companions while Coenus rides round behind, and the Indian cavalry, caught both ways,
  falls back on the elephants (070.8).
- phase **hydaspes-elephants**, May 326 BC: the phalanx against the elephants, the cavalry closing round, the
  locked shields, the Indian flight, Craterus crossing to take up the pursuit, and Porus's surrender (070.8,
  070.6).

The battle plan (`.plan/battles/hydaspes.md`) keeps these 3 phase pages; there is no deployment page.

**hyphasis**, summer 326 BC. Alexander marches east across the Acesines and the Hydraotes in the monsoon rains
to the Hyphasis (Beas). There the army refuses to go on. Coenus speaks for the men, Alexander shuts himself in his
tent for three days, the sacrifices for the crossing turn out unfavourable, and he turns back. He raises twelve
altars on the river and gives the land up to the Hyphasis to Porus. He marches back to the Hydraotes and on to the
Hydaspes, where the fleet is being built. Why: seventy days of monsoon rain, worn-out arms and clothing, and the
prospect of more rivers to cross and of armies with elephants beyond (070.20, Diodorus, read by Bosworth). Map
change: the march reaches its furthest east and turns back,
and Porus's kingdom grows to the Hyphasis (`porus-326b`).
`Rests on:` 070.14 (agreed). 070.20 (agreed): the causes told plainly.

**malli**, November 326 BC to winter 326–325 BC. The fleet sets out down the Hydaspes from Nicaea and Bucephala,
with Craterus and Hephaestion marching along the banks. At the junction with the Acesines it turns south towards
the Malli, the most warlike people of the lower Punjab. Alexander crosses the desert to the Hydraotes and drives
the Malli into their strongest town. At the citadel wall the ladders break behind him. He leaps down inside with
Peucestas, Leonnatus and Abreas, and an arrow pierces his breastplate into his chest, while Peucestas covers him
with the shield from Troy. The Macedonians break in and kill everyone in the citadel. Why: Alexander meant to
sail down to the Ocean (070.15, Diodorus); that the Malli were preparing to resist him has no digest line, so it
is at most a clause naming Bosworth. Map
change: the fleet and the columns move down the Hydaspes and the Acesines into the land of the Malli, and the
Mallian town falls.
`Rests on:` 070.15 (agreed). 070.16 (agreed).
The killing in the citadel is in Arrian (book 6, chapter 11) and Diodorus (book 17, chapter 99), not in a digest
line: told with Arrian's name.

**ocean**, summer 325 BC. The fleet goes on down the Acesines into the Indus and subdues the peoples of the lower
river. Before it reaches the delta, Alexander divides the army for the return. Craterus takes the battalions of
Attalus, son of Andromenes, Meleager and Antigenes, the elephants and the men unfit for service west by Arachosia
and Drangiana towards Carmania. Alexander sails on to Patala at the head of the delta, then down the Indus and out
into the Ocean, where he sacrifices to Poseidon. Why: no digest line carries the cause, so the text gives it at
most in one clause naming Bosworth: Alexander meant to take the harder coastal route himself and could spare the
men who no longer wanted hardship (ch. 2.XVI). Map change: the Indus valley down to Patala and the Ocean becomes
Macedonian (`macedon-325a`);
Craterus's column sets off west, and the fleet reaches the Ocean.
`Rests on:` 070.17 (single, Arrian: told as Arrian's account; Craterus leaves before the Ocean is reached, as
the brief has it). Bosworth's "at this point, if not earlier" for the date of Craterus's departure is told with
his name if used. Bosworth's mid-July 325 for the arrival at Patala, after Aristobulus, is not a digest line; the
page's date stays "summer 325 BC".

## Left out
None: every "Event" line of the digest's 070 section (070.1–070.17) is on a page.

These events are in the source files but not in the digest, so they get at most a clause, with no marker and no
figure: Nysa and its Dionysus legend (Arrian 5.1–2); Hephaestion's thirty-day siege of Peucelaotis (Arrian 4.22);
Bazira and Ora in Swat (Arrian 4.27); Abisares's embassies (Arrian 5.8, 5.20); the Glausae (Arrian 5.20); the

Oxycanus and Sambus (Arrian 6.15–17). Each is left out because no digest line carries it and no page's map change
needs it.

## Battles

### aornos (card)
- Sides: `macedon` against `indians` (the Assacenians and the other refugees of the region).
- Commanders: Alexander, with Ptolemy (son of Lagus) leading the party that seizes the ridge (Arrian 4.29), and
  Craterus holding the base below the rock (Arrian 4.28). The Indian commander is `unknown`; no source read names
  him.
- Strengths: the Macedonian assault force has no total, so it is `unknown`. Arrian 4.28 lists its units: the
  archers, the Agrianians, Coenus's battalion, picked phalanx men, 200 Companion cavalry and 100 horse archers.
  The card may list the units without a total. Indian strength is `unknown`.
- Casualties: `unknown` on both sides. Arrian says many were killed in the flight or fell from the cliffs, but
  gives no figure.
- Outcome: the rock is taken and garrisoned. Arrian: the defenders seek a truce to slip away by night, and
  Alexander climbs the rock with 700 bodyguards and hypaspists and cuts them down. Diodorus: he lets them go and
  takes the rock without a fight (070.3, disputed for the ending). The site is disputed (070.18): Stein's Pir
  Sar, which Bosworth holds decisive, against Tucci's and Eggermont's Mt Ilam.

### hydaspes (card and 3 phase pages)
- Sides: `macedon` (with Taxiles's Indians, `taxila`) against `porus`.
- Commanders: Alexander; Craterus at the main camp; Meleager, Attalus, son of Andromenes, and Gorgias along the
  bank; Coenus with his own and Demetrius's cavalry regiments; Antigenes, with Seleucus and Tauron, leading the
  phalanx (Arrian 5.11–16). Seleucus is first named here and takes a few words: a Companion who commands the
  royal hypaspists at the crossing, "afterwards king" in Arrian's words (5.13; names.md). Porus, and his son, who leads the force at the landing and is killed (Arrian 5.14–15).
- Strengths: Alexander's crossing force is about 6,000 foot and 5,000 horse (Arrian, 070.11); his whole army is
  `unknown`. Taxiles's Indians number 5,000 (Arrian 5.8, 070.4); they stay at the base camp with Craterus (Arrian
5.11.3; Bosworth's Commentary on 11.3), so they are not in the crossing force. Porus's army is disputed (070.10). Arrian: 4,000
  horse, 300 chariots, 200 elephants and 30,000 foot. Diodorus: more than 50,000 foot, about 3,000 horse, more
  than 1,000 chariots and 130 elephants. Curtius: 85 elephants, 300 chariots and about 30,000 foot. Plutarch:
  20,000 foot and 2,000 horse. Porus's son's force is 2,000 horse and 120 chariots by Ptolemy, or about 60
  chariots by Aristobulus, both in Arrian 5.14.
- Casualties: disputed (070.12). Arrian: nearly 20,000 Indian foot and 3,000 horse killed, two sons of Porus
  among them; of Alexander's men about 80 foot, 10 horse archers, about 20 Companions and about 200 other horse.
  Diodorus: more than 12,000 Indians killed and over 9,000 taken with 80 elephants; 280 Macedonian horse and more
  than 700 foot dead. Arrian has 400 horse killed at the landing, Porus's son among them (5.15).
- Outcome: Alexander wins. Porus is wounded and captured, and Alexander confirms him as king with more land
  (070.6).
- Phases: 3, as proposed under Pages. The positions and movements of both sides are in Arrian 5.9–18
  (`archive.org-download-theanabasisofale46976gut-46976-0.txt.txt`), which Bosworth calls "the only basis for
  reconstructing the battle". With it go Bosworth's Commentary vol. 2 on 5.8–18 (the "Topography and tactics"
  notes), Bosworth, Conquest and Empire ch. 2.XV, Diodorus 17.87–89 for the elephant line, Curtius 8.13–14 and
  Plutarch 60.
- Ground: drawn as a reconstruction since the site is disputed (070.9; cloud ruling of step 4). The campaign page
  draws Stein's sites (base camp at Haranpur, crossing at Jalalpur, 150 stades upstream). The phase pages should
  stand on the same ground so the two agree; a battle plan that chooses other ground says so, and the campaign
  page's markers follow it.

### mallian-town (card)
- Sides: `macedon` against `indians` (the Malli).
- Commanders: Alexander, with Perdiccas leading the other half of the assault (Arrian 6.9); Peucestas, Leonnatus
  and Abreas inside the citadel with him (070.16). The Mallian commander is `unknown`; Arrian has Alexander kill
  their leader without naming him (6.9).
- Strengths: the Malli at the Hydraotes before they fled into the town number about 50,000 (Arrian 6.8).
  Diodorus has the Oxydracae (Sydracae) and the Malli mobilise 80,000 foot, 10,000 horse and 700 chariots
  (17.98.1). The Macedonian force is `unknown`.
- Casualties: the defenders of the citadel are all killed, women and children too (Arrian 6.11); the figure is
  `unknown`. On the Macedonian side Abreas is killed, and Alexander, Peucestas and Leonnatus are wounded (Arrian
  6.10); the total is `unknown`.
- Outcome: the town is stormed. Alexander is gravely wounded, recovers, and the Malli submit (Bosworth ch. 2.XVI).
- The figures beyond 070.16 are not digest lines. The implementor reads them in Arrian 6.8–11 and Diodorus
  17.98–99.

## Contract

Folders sit in `content/alexander-the-great/pages/070-india/`. Every page leaves out `when`: BC years cannot go
in it (story.yaml), so the folder numbers set the order. No page has a tally. Every zone is a `reconstruction`,
and the chapter's text says once that the borders are approximate. Where a source names a river (the Indus,
Hydaspes, Acesines, Hydraotes or Hyphasis), the zone or route follows it. Rivers are drawn on their modern
courses, and Bosworth warns that the Punjab rivers have moved (ch. 2.XVI; Commentary vol. 2 on 5.9.1). A marker's
colour is the family that holds the place on that page.

### 010-cophen
- Title: "Down the Cophen valley"
- `date`: "327 BC"; `when`: none
- Edges: Bactra in the north-west, the Hydaspes in the east, the plain south of the Cophen near Peshawar in the
  south, the Hindu Kush passes in the north. About `[66.0, 33.0, 74.0, 37.3]`.
- Zones: `macedon-327` (entry on macedon-329b), `indians-327` (whole, uncut), `taxila-327`, `porus-326`.
- Routes, in the order travelled:
  1. `hephaestion-327`, macedon: from the Cophen valley below the Hindu Kush, down the Cophen by the main road to
     the Indus near Ohind. Reconstruction from Arrian 4.22 (down the Cophen towards the Indus, into Peucelaotis)
     and Bosworth ch. 2.XIV (the main road into India; the bridge near Ohind, ch. 2.XV).
  2. `alexander-327b`, macedon: from Bactra over the Hindu Kush, down the Cophen valley, then north of the river
     into the hills of Bajaur and Swat, to Massaga. Reconstruction from Arrian 4.22–26 (Bactra, the Caucasus in
     ten days, the Cophen, the river Choes, the Guraeus) and Bosworth ch. 2.XIV (Bajaur and Swat; Massaga north of
     the Swat near the Katgala pass).
- Markers:
  - `bactra-327b`, "Bactra" (Balkh), macedon: where Alexander sets out at the end of spring 327. Sourced (Arrian
    4.22). Its own id, since `bactra-327` is 060's marker.
  - `hindu-kush-327`, "Hindu Kush", macedon: the passes crossed in ten days. Reconstruction (Arrian 4.22; the
    pass is not named).
  - `cophen-327`, "Cophen" (Kabul River), indians: the river the army follows. Sourced.
  - `bajaur-327`, "Bajaur", indians, and `swat-327`, "Swat", indians: the hill country of Alexander's column.
    Sourced as regions (Bosworth ch. 2.XIV).
  - `massaga-327`, "Massaga", indians: the siege, and the mercenaries killed after the surrender. Reconstruction from
    070.19: Bosworth places it "somewhere to the north of the Swat in the vicinity of the Katgala pass"; the note
    says the site is unknown.
  - `indus-bridge-327`, "Bridge on the Indus", macedon: Hephaestion and Perdiccas go ahead to bridge the river.
    Reconstruction: Bosworth puts the crossing "probably in the vicinity of Ohind" (ch. 2.XV).

- Tally: none.

## Choices
- The Cophen campaign gets two pages, cophen and aornos, although Massaga and Aornos lie in one region within a
  few months. The first page's change is the army entering India in two columns. The second's is the Cophen valley
  becoming Macedonian, with its own card. The brief's line "The Cophen campaign and the rock of Aornos" is split
  this way.
- The fleet's departure and the Mallian town share one page (malli): one region within three months, the fleet's
  route leading straight into the Mallian campaign. The return from the Hyphasis to the Hydaspes ends the hyphasis
  page.
- Taxila keeps a page of its own, as the brief has it. Its map change, the crossing into an allied kingdom, comes
  before the Hydaspes campaign and in another place.
- The Hydaspes campaign page draws Stein's sites (Haranpur, Jalalpur) as the reconstruction. They are the only
  reading in the sources with places that fit Arrian's 150 stades; the text names the other two readings.
- The Hydaspes gets 3 phase pages after Arrian's sequence, which the battle plan keeps (cloud ruling: no
  deployment page).
- `porus-326` stands on the pages of 327 BC, because `indians-327` leaves Porus's land out and the map would
  otherwise show a hole.
- The malli page keeps `macedon-326`; the annexation of the Malli and the lower Indus shows on ocean with
  `macedon-325a`.
- The ocean page uses `macedon-325a` rather than `macedon-325`, which adds Gedrosia and Ora, conquered only in
  080 (both are now in the Map part).
- Craterus's route takes the Bolan Pass, the first of Bosworth's two passes, and runs on to Carmania so the
  column's destination is on the map. The ocean page is therefore wide (56.5–72.5 E).
- Aornos (070.18) and Massaga (070.19) are drawn on Bosworth's placings; the Mallian town and Patala on the
  placings names.md takes from Bosworth, which are not digest lines. Each note says the site is unknown or
  uncertain.
- The page dates are the digest's seasons ("327 BC" for the whole Cophen march). Bosworth's mid-July 325 for
  Patala is left to the text.
- The Hydaspes battle sits in a group folder with the campaign page first, as the Punic Wars story nests its
  battles.
