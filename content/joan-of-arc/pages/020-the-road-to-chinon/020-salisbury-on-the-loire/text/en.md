In July 1428 Thomas Montagu, Earl of Salisbury, Henry VI's lieutenant in France, comes over from England with fresh troops, and men of Paris, Chartres and Normandy join him: four or five thousand in all by August.

Janville is stormed on 29 August. [Meung](@meung-1428) surrenders, the bridge and castle of [Beaugency](@beaugency-1428) fall on 25 September, and on 5 October Sir John de la Pole takes [Jargeau](@jargeau-1428). [Sully](@sully-1428), upstream, surrenders too. Of the four bridges on the Loire between Sully and Blois, the English now hold all but the one at Orléans.

On 12 October Salisbury camps by the Portereau, the suburb across the river, and lays siege to [Orléans](@orleans-besieged). On 24 October the English take [the Tourelles](@tourelles-1428), the fort at the bridge's southern end, and the bridge behind it is broken. Looking at the city from a window of the Tourelles, Salisbury is struck by a cannon shot, and he dies at Meung. William de la Pole, Earl of Suffolk, takes command. Inside, the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois) holds the city for the king. Through the winter the English ring it with forts called bastilles, leaving the north-east open.

On 12 February 1429 Sir John Fastolf is bringing stores from Paris to the siege, among them herrings for the fasting season. At Rouvray, two leagues from Janville, French and Scots under Charles, Count of Clermont, come out against him, and he makes a park of his carts and stakes. The French have agreed to fight mounted, but the Scots of Sir John Stewart of Darnley dismount and attack. The English sally out and break them, and Clermont's horsemen ride away. Darnley is among the dead. After the Battle of the Herrings, as the fight is called, Clermont goes home, and the king's council talks of withdrawing to Dauphiné.
