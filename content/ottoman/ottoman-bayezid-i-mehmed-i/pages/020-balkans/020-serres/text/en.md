In the winter of 1393–1394 Bayezid summons his Christian vassals to [Serres](@serres): Manuel II and John VII, Manuel’s brother Theodore, the Despot of the Morea, Konstantin Dragaš and Stefan Lazarević. By Manuel’s own account the sultan orders their deaths and then relents. Theodore has to follow Bayezid south and promise him Argos and Monemvasia, but he escapes to [Mistra](@mistra). Manuel returns to Constantinople and does not answer the sultan’s later summons.

Bayezid marches into Thessaly, which the Ottomans annex with [Larissa](@larissa). The widowed countess Helena Kantakouzene surrenders [Salona](@salona-1394) to him, and her daughter Maria enters his harem. In April 1394 the Ottomans take direct control of [Thessalonica](@thessalonica-1394), which has governed itself under Ottoman overlordship since 1387, and its church and people keep most of their property.

@image salona-castle-f5e022

In southern Greece Venice gains ground. Theodore hands [Argos](@argos-1394) over to Venice in 1394, and Venice has held Nauplia since 1389, bought from Maria of Enghien together with Argos. Nerio I Acciaioli dies on 25 September 1394, and when Ottoman raiders attack [Athens](@athens-1394), the castellan of the Acropolis offers the town to Venice, which sends troops before the end of the year.
