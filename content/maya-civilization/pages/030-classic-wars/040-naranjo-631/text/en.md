Aj Wosal, installed at Naranjo by the Kaanul king Tuun K'ab Hix in AD 546, rules for decades. Around 600 he has a building with a large stucco frieze raised at nearby Holmul. After his death Naranjo breaks away from the Kaanul kings.

At Caracol, K'an II, a son of Yajaw Te' K'inich II, takes the throne in 618. His monuments record the accession of the Kaanul king Tajoom Uk'ab K'ahk' in 622 and a gift from him in 627. Caracol defeats Naranjo in May 626 and again in 627.

Tajoom Uk'ab K'ahk' dies in 630, and in 631 his successor Yuknoom Head brings Naranjo back under Kaanul rule. Caracol records this as a "star war" won together with Yuknoom Head. The texts say that the king of Naranjo is already a captive at Calakmul on the day his city is taken, and that he is punished the same day. The word used, k'uxaj, means "tortured" or "eaten".

A fourth war follows in 636, and Caracol prospers under K'an II, who reigns for forty years: its causeways are extended, its population grows and a building boom begins. A hieroglyphic stairway on Structure B-18 at Naranjo tells the story of these wars. By the older reading K'an II sets it up in the defeated city. Alexandre Tokovinine reads it as a monument first built at Caracol and carried to Naranjo later.
