From [Krujë](@kruje) Skanderbeg takes the smaller castles around it, Petrela, Prezë and Guri i Bardhë among them, and comes to hold more than the lands of his father Gjon Kastrioti. He gives up Islam for Christianity and, according to the historian Kristo Frashëri, raises a red flag with a black double-headed eagle over Krujë. Gjergj Arianiti has been in revolt against the Ottomans in central Albania again since August 1443.

On 2 March 1444 Skanderbeg calls the Albanian lords to [Lezhë](@lezhe), a town held by Venice. Gjergj Arianiti, Pal Dukagjini, Andrea Thopia, Lekë Dushmani, Theodor Corona Musachi, Peter Spani, Lekë Zaharia, Pal Strez Balsha and Stefan Crnojević of Zeta form an alliance against the Ottomans there and choose Skanderbeg as its captain. The meeting is known from Marin Barleti, Skanderbeg’s biographer, and no Venetian document of the time mentions it. Later historians call the alliance the League of Lezhë.

In June 1444 Ali Pasha leaves [Skopje](@skopje-1444) for Albania with 25,000 men by Barleti’s account, and other figures reach 40,000. Skanderbeg meets him in Lower Dibra with 15,000 men, 8,000 of them horsemen. On 29 June, on the plain of Torvioll, Skanderbeg’s army feigns a retreat and draws the Ottomans into the gorge, and 3,000 horsemen under Hamza Kastrioti come out of the forest at a signal and fall on their rear. By the Albanian accounts the Ottomans lose 7,000 to 10,000 dead. The historian Kenneth Setton writes that most accounts of Skanderbeg in 1443 and 1444 “owe far more to fancy than to fact”.
