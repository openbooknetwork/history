The crusade fleet sailed from Venice in June 1444 under the papal legate Cardinal Francesco Condulmer, with Venetian galleys under Alvise Loredan and Burgundian galleys under Walerand de Wavrin, and reached Methoni (Modon) by mid-July. Its task is to hold the straits, so that Murad, who has crossed to Anatolia against Karaman, cannot bring his army back to Europe. The main fleet holds the Dardanelles off [Gallipoli](@gallipoli), and six ships, two Burgundian, two Ragusan and two Byzantine, guard the Bosporus by Constantinople.

Murad leaves [Manisa](@manisa) with the troops of Anatolia. Because the fleet holds the Dardanelles, he marches by Balıkesir, [Bursa](@bursa) and Gemlik to the Bosporus, and on 18 or 20 October the army crosses to Europe at the castle of [Anadolu Hisarı](@anadoluhisari-1444) on the Asian shore. Doukas writes that Genoese ships ferry the troops over for a ducat a head, and Halil Pasha has guns set up on the European shore to keep the galleys away. Kenneth Setton writes that contrary winds hold the fleet back, that the Venetians make no real effort to stop the crossing, and that the Genoese are accused of taking Murad’s money.

Murad marches fast to [Edirne](@edirne), spends the night with the army outside the city, and the troops of Rumelia join him there. Mehmed stays at Edirne. The army marches north across the Balkan range and on the evening of 9 November reaches the hills west of [Varna](@varna-1444), where the crusaders are camped.
