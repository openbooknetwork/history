Süleyman reaches [Gallipoli](@gallipoli-1403) on 20 August 1402 and takes over the Ottoman lands in Europe from [Edirne](@edirne-1403). To secure peace in Europe while his brothers contend for Anatolia, he negotiates with Venice, Genoa and John VII, regent in [Constantinople](@constantinople) for Manuel II, who is in Paris. The treaty is concluded at Gallipoli in January or early February 1403 with Byzantium, Venice, Genoa with Chios, the Knights of Rhodes and the Duke of Naxos.

@image suleyman-celebi-tughra-e6a925

Süleyman, who calls the emperor “my father”, gives back [Thessalonica](@thessalonica-1403) with the Chalcidice, the land around Constantinople from Panidos on the Sea of Marmara to [Mesembria](@mesembria-1403) on the Black Sea, and the Northern Sporades. Byzantium no longer pays tribute. Venice receives a strip of the mainland opposite [Negroponte](@negroponte-1403), the Genoese colonies and Naxos are freed of tribute, and Stefan Lazarević keeps his lands with the tribute and service he owed Bayezid. Gallipoli stays Ottoman.

@image thessaloniki-walls-1d3f4e

Manuel returns on 9 June 1403, confirms the treaty and sends John VII to Lemnos. After a quarrel the two emperors come to terms, and John rules Thessalonica as emperor from the end of 1403 until his death in 1408. Stefan Lazarević becomes a vassal of King Sigismund of Hungary at the end of 1403 or early in 1404, receives [Belgrade](@belgrade) and Mačva, and makes Belgrade his capital in 1405.
