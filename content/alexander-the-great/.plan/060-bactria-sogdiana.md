# Bactria and Sogdiana

## Header
- Years: July 330 BC (Darius's death, the end of 050) to the end of spring 327 BC (the army gathered at Bactra for
  India)
- Opens: Darius has been arrested and stabbed by Bessus, Nabarzanes and Barsaentes and has died in Parthia in July
  330 (050.24). Alexander holds the empire from Macedon and Greece through Asia Minor, the Levant, Egypt,
  Mesopotamia, Persis and Media to where Darius died (macedon-330). Antipater holds Europe, and Agis III has fallen
  at Megalopolis (050.15). Parmenion is at Ecbatana, and the Greek allies have been sent home (050.23, disputed for
  place). Bessus has fled to his satrapy of Bactria. Nabarzanes is in hiding in Hyrcania, and Barsaentes is in
  Drangiana and Arachosia.

## Pages

**010-phrada**, July–autumn 330 BC. After Darius's death Alexander crosses the Elburz into Hyrcania, where
Darius's officers and Greek mercenaries surrender at Zadracarta. He then marches east through Parthia to Susia on
the border of Areia. There Satibarzanes submits, and news comes that Bessus has taken the upright tiara in Bactria
and calls himself king. Alexander answers as the Great King's heir: he takes up items of Persian court dress, the
diadem, the white-striped tunic and the girdle, worn with the Macedonian hat and cloak, and gives his Companions the
scarlet robes of Persian courtiers. Then he sets out for Bactria. Satibarzanes kills the small Macedonian force left with him
and declares for Bessus, so Alexander turns back. He covers 600 stades in two days to Artacoana, and Satibarzanes
flees. The army moves south into Drangiana, where Barsaentes flees to India, and reaches Phrada. At Phrada a plot on
Alexander's life comes out. Philotas, son of Parmenion, who heard of it and said nothing, is tried before the
Macedonians and killed. Riders on racing camels carry Alexander's orders across the desert to Ecbatana, where
Cleander, Sitalces and Menidas kill Parmenion. Why it happened: Bessus's claim to the throne made him a usurper to be
put down, and the satraps who had submitted were free to follow him (Bosworth XI). Parmenion could not be left to
outlive his son (Arrian 3.26). Bosworth reads the purge as the removal of the old general's family and of the
opposition to the march east (Bosworth XII). The court dress showed that Alexander took his claim to Darius's
kingship seriously, against Bessus, and angered the more conservative Macedonians (Bosworth XI). Map change: Alexander's march runs from where Darius died through
Hyrcania, Parthia and Areia to Phrada, and these lands come into his empire (macedon-330b). Bessus's
kingdom in Bactria and Sogdiana stands against him (persia-330b), and the order to kill Parmenion crosses
the desert to Ecbatana. The text says once in this chapter, here, that the borders are approximate.
Rests on: 060.18, 060.19, 060.2, 060.1, 060.22, and 060.27.
The court dress is told plainly once the researcher's line stands, after Bosworth XI (at Susia), Plutarch 45 (in
Parthia) and Arrian 4.7 (Arrian's disapproval), with the place given as the line gives it.
Whether Philotas was guilty is not a digest line: the text says only what Arrian and Bosworth agree on, that he did
not report the plot.

**020-hindu-kush**, winter 330–329 to summer 329 BC. From Phrada the army winters among the Ariaspians by the lakes
of the Helmand, then marches through Arachosia in deep snow and short of food. Satibarzanes comes back into Areia
with 2,000 of Bessus's horse and is killed by Erigyius. Alexander founds a city at the foot of the Hindu Kush and
crosses the range into Bactria in spring 329. Bessus lays waste the land below the mountains, then crosses the Oxus,
burns his boats and withdraws to Nautaca in Sogdiana. The Bactrian horse leave him. Bactra falls without a blow. In
the summer heat the army crosses the desert to the Oxus and takes five days to cross it on tent skins stuffed with
chaff. The oldest Macedonians and the Thessalians who had stayed on as volunteers are sent home. Spitamenes and Dataphernes offer to give
Bessus up, and Ptolemy brings him in. Why it happened: Alexander would not leave the usurper free in the far east
(Bosworth XI and XIII). Bessus could not unite the Bactrian nobles, and his Sogdian allies chose to make their peace
by giving him up (Bosworth XIII). Map change: Arachosia and Bactria to the Oxus come into the empire (macedon-329),
and Bessus's kingdom shrinks to Sogdiana (persia-329). The army marches from Phrada round to the Kabul valley, over
the Hindu Kush and across the Oxus, and Bessus withdraws before it.
Rests on: 060.3, 060.21, 060.20, 060.4, 060.5.
060.3 and 060.4 are single and are told as Arrian's. 060.5 is agreed for the surrender. Arrian gives two accounts of
it, Ptolemy's chase and Aristobulus's handover, and Bosworth prefers the second; the text may say so in a clause.

**030-jaxartes**, summer 329 BC. Alexander marches through Sogdiana to its royal seat at Maracanda and on to the
Jaxartes, which he and his staff take for the Tanais. He plans a city on its bank, Alexandria Eschate. The towns
along the river kill their Macedonian garrisons, and most of Sogdiana and part of Bactria rise behind Spitamenes
and Dataphernes. Spitamenes besieges the garrison in the citadel of Maracanda. Alexander takes five of the seven
towns in two days and storms Cyropolis, the largest, through a dry stream bed. He is struck on the head and neck
by a stone. He sends a column to relieve Maracanda, fortifies Alexandria Eschate in twenty days and crosses the
Jaxartes against the Saka who taunt him from the far bank. Catapults clear the bank, the light troops and cavalry
break the horse archers' circling, and the Saka flee. Alexander, sick from bad water, cannot pursue. Why it
happened: the nobles who had given up Bessus had made their peace only for the moment, and they feared the
assembly Alexander had called at Bactra (Arrian 4.1, Bosworth XIII). Alexander meant the city to guard the
frontier against the nomads and to serve for a crossing (Arrian 4.1). Map change: Sogdiana to the Jaxartes comes
into the empire (macedon-329b) and at once rises in revolt (sogdians-329). The army marches from the Oxus to the
Jaxartes, Alexandria Eschate is founded, and the Saka stand beyond the river (scythians-329).
Rests on: 060.6, 060.7, 060.8, 060.24.
060.8 is single and is told as Arrian's. 060.24 is single for each figure: the Saka losses are Arrian's, the
Macedonian losses Curtius's, and the card and the text name each author.

**040-polytimetus**, autumn 329 to winter 329–328 BC. As the relief column nears Maracanda, Spitamenes lifts the
siege and draws back west along the Polytimetus (today the Zeravshan). There Scythian horse join him. Pharnuches
and the officers with him follow him to the edge of the desert, where the horse archers ride round them and shoot
them down. In Ptolemy's account in Arrian the column breaks in panic crossing the river and dies on an island in
it. In Aristobulus's account it falls into an ambush while its commanders argue over who leads. Of 60 Companions,
800 mercenary horse and 1,500 mercenary foot, Arrian says no more than 40 horse and 300 foot escaped. Alexander
covers the road from the Jaxartes to Maracanda at speed, and Spitamenes escapes again into the desert. Alexander
buries the dead, lays waste the Zeravshan valley, and goes into winter quarters at Bactra. There, before a council,
he has Bessus's nose and ears cut off and sends him to Ecbatana to be put to death before the Medes and Persians
(Arrian 4.7 alone; Curtius 7.5.40 has the mutilation at the handover to Oxathres, Diodorus 17.83.9 has him cut to pieces).
Why it happened: the column was small, its horses were worn out, and it pursued into the desert without one
commander (Arrian 4.5–6). Bosworth notes that the blame was later laid on the officers. Alexander punished the
valley because he held that its people had helped Spitamenes (Arrian 4.6). Map change: Spitamenes's withdrawal and
return, the column's march to its end on the Zeravshan, Alexander's relief march, and the army's move south to
Bactra for the winter.
Rests on: 060.9, 060.10, 060.25, 060.5.
060.9 is disputed in its course: the text gives Ptolemy's account (the column harried to the river under a divided
command) and Aristobulus's (an ambush from a park as Pharnuches hands over the command), both in Arrian, and says in a
clause that Curtius has a third (Menedemus besieged on a hill). 060.10 and 060.25 are disputed figures: the text and
the card give Arrian's (60 Companions, 800 mercenary horse and 1,500 foot; 40 horse and 300 foot escape) and
Curtius's (3,000 foot and 800 horse; 2,000 foot and 300 horse fallen), each with its author. 060.5 is disputed for
how and where Bessus died: the text tells the mutilation at Bactra and the sending to Ecbatana as Arrian's and
Curtius's account, with Arrian's condemnation, and Diodorus's, in which Alexander hands Bessus to Darius's brother
and kin, who cut him to pieces.

**050-maracanda**, 328 to winter 328–327 BC. In spring Alexander crosses the Oxus again and sends columns through
Sogdiana to take the strongholds that still hold out. They meet at Maracanda. There, at a banquet in the autumn,
flatterers set Alexander above the Dioscuri and above Philip and mock the generals who died at the Zeravshan.
Cleitus the Black, who saved Alexander's life at the Granicus, praises Philip and taunts the king. Alexander seizes
a spear from a guard and runs him through. Meanwhile Spitamenes, with Massagetic horse, takes a fort in Bactria and
raids up to Bactra. Craterus drives the Massagetae into the desert. Alexander goes into winter quarters at Nautaca
and leaves Coenus to hold Sogdiana from Maracanda. Spitamenes gathers 3,000 Scythian horse at Gabae ("Bagae" in Chinnock's Arrian) on
the edge of the desert and invades. Coenus beats him, and the Sogdians and Bactrians with him go over to Coenus. Spitamenes is
killed: Arrian says the Massagetae cut off his head and sent it to Alexander, and Curtius says his wife did. Why it
happened: the risings were put down stronghold by stronghold, with garrisons and mobile columns that left
Spitamenes no refuge (Arrian 4.17, Bosworth XIII). At Maracanda the older Macedonians resented the king's claim to
all the glory and the turn to Persian ways (Bosworth XIII). Map change: Alexander's columns cross Sogdiana to
Maracanda and then go south to Nautaca. Spitamenes's raid runs into Bactria and back to the desert, and his last
invasion breaks against Coenus in the west of Sogdiana.
Rests on: 060.12, 060.23, 060.11.
060.23 is agreed for the raid and Coenus's victory and single for its figures, which the text names to Arrian.
060.11 is disputed: the text gives Arrian's Massagetae and Curtius's wife, each with its author. The text does not
date the Sogdian Rock to this summer. Bosworth's date is told on the next page.

**060-sogdian-rock**, spring 327 BC. At the first sign of spring Alexander leaves Nautaca for the rock in Sogdiana,
where many Sogdians have fled with Oxyartes's wife and daughters, and which the defenders think no one could take
without wings. Three hundred men who have climbed in sieges scale the cliff by night with tent pegs and ropes. Thirty
fall. At dawn the rest wave linen flags from above the defenders, and the rock surrenders to the "winged men".
Among the captives is Roxana, daughter of Oxyartes, and Alexander marries her. Oxyartes comes over to him. The army
gathers at Bactra. There Alexander tries to bring in proskynesis, the Persian act of homage, and Callisthenes refuses
it. Then the royal pages under Hermolaus plot to kill the king in his sleep. The plot is betrayed, the pages are
stoned, and Callisthenes is arrested. Alexander leaves Amyntas, son of Nicolaus, as satrap of Bactria and Sogdiana
with 10,000 foot and 3,500 horse, and at the end of spring the army sets out for India. Why it happened: the rock
was the last refuge of the rising (Arrian 4.18). Roxana's good treatment won over her father, and a wife from the
conquered land underscored Alexander's claim to rule it (Bosworth XIII). Bosworth reads proskynesis as the formal
introduction of one court protocol for all Alexander's subjects, set in a debate on divine honours (part II, D). Bosworth links the
pages' plot to the court's turn to despotism, with proskynesis as its catalyst (Bosworth XIII). Map change: the
rising is over and Sogdiana is wholly Alexander's (the rebel zone is gone; macedon-327). The march
runs from Nautaca to the rock and south to Bactra, where the army gathers for India.
Rests on: 060.13, 060.26, 060.14, 060.16, 060.17, 060.15.
060.13 is disputed for the date: the text tells the siege in Arrian's place, spring 327, and says that Bosworth
dates it to summer 328, before the death of Cleitus; it also says that the vulgate names the rock's lord as
Ariamazes. 060.26 is disputed for the climbers' losses: 30 in Arrian, 32 in Curtius, each named; Curtius's 30,000
defenders are told as his. 060.14 is disputed for the meeting: Arrian has Roxana taken on the rock, Plutarch has
Alexander see her dancing at a banquet; the text gives both. 060.17 is disputed for Callisthenes's end: Ptolemy,
racked and hanged; Aristobulus and Chares of Mytilene, died in fetters; the text gives the three. 060.15 is single:
"nearly two years" is told as Bosworth's judgement, and the garrison figures are Arrian's as well.

## Battles

**jaxartes-329**, the battle of the Jaxartes, summer 329 BC. Card on 030-jaxartes.
- Sides: `macedon` against `scythians` (the Saka beyond the Jaxartes, whom Arrian calls the Asiatic Scythians).
- Commanders: Alexander. Balacrus leads the light troops mixed with the cavalry (Arrian 4.4). Saka side: Satraces,
  a chief who is killed (Arrian 4.4). No source read names their king.
- Strengths: Macedonians `unknown`: Arrian names the units (the catapults, the archers and slingers, one regiment
  of Greek horse, four squadrons of lancers, three hipparchies of Companions and the mounted javelin men) but no
  number. Saka `unknown`.
- Casualties: Saka 1,000 killed, Satraces among them, and 150 taken, Arrian 4.4 (060.24). Curtius 7.9.16 has them
  lose many killed and more taken, and 1,800 horses driven off (not in the digest; not on the card).
  Macedonians: 60 horse and about 100 foot killed and 1,000 wounded, Curtius 7.9.16 (060.24). Arrian
  gives no Macedonian figure. Bosworth calls the Saka losses "disproportionately large" and gives no number.
- Outcome: Macedonian victory. The Saka king sends envoys to apologise and submit (Arrian 4.5, Bosworth XIII).
- Phase pages: none. 060.8: Arrian gives Alexander's crossing and attack, not the Saka positions.
- Card place: the north bank of the Jaxartes across from Alexandria Eschate, about 40.32 N 69.62 E.
  Reconstruction: Arrian 4.4 (the river narrow there, the army posted by it near the new city) and Bosworth XIII
  (Saka cavalry massing opposite Alexandria Eschate, in the vicinity of Khujand).

**polytimetus-329**, the battle of the Polytimetus, autumn 329 BC. Card on 040-polytimetus.
- Sides: `macedon` (the relief column) against `sogdians` (Spitamenes's horse with the Scythian horse who joined
  him; the brief gives `sogdians` the card).
- Commanders: Pharnuches, the Lycian interpreter, in command, with Andromachus, Menedemus and Caranus (Arrian 4.3).
  Curtius makes Menedemus the commander (7.6.24, 7.7.30–39). Sogdian side: Spitamenes.
- Strengths: the column 60 Companion cavalry, 800 mercenary cavalry and 1,500 mercenary infantry, Arrian 4.3
  (060.10). Curtius gives 3,000 foot and 800 horse (7.6.24; 060.25). Spitamenes `unknown`: Arrian says
  only that 600 Scythian horsemen joined him (4.5).
- Casualties: the column destroyed, with no more than 40 horse and 300 foot escaping, Arrian 4.6 (060.10).
  Curtius gives 2,000 foot and 300 horse killed (7.7.39; 060.25). Spitamenes's losses `unknown`.
- Outcome: Spitamenes's victory, the column destroyed (060.9). How is disputed: Ptolemy in Arrian (Caranus's
  unordered river crossing, panic, the end on an island), Aristobulus in Arrian (an ambush from a park as Pharnuches
  hands over the command), and Curtius (Menedemus besieged on a hill). Bosworth holds that Ptolemy's and
  Aristobulus's may be partial views of one fight.
- Phase pages: none. No source read gives the positions of both sides (research.md, 060 coverage).
- Card place: on the Zeravshan west of Maracanda, towards the desert, about 40.0 N 65.6 E. Reconstruction: Arrian
  4.5 (Spitamenes retreats "towards the frontiers of Sogdiana", the fight is "in a level place near the Scythian
  desert" and ends at the river) and Bosworth XIII (Spitamenes "withdrew westwards along the Zeravshan valley").
  The text and the card say the site is not known.

**sogdian-rock-327**, the siege of the Sogdian Rock, spring 327 BC (Arrian) or summer 328 BC (Bosworth). Card on
060-sogdian-rock.
- Sides: `macedon` against `sogdians`.
- Commanders: Alexander. Sogdian side: Ariamazes, after Curtius 7.11 and Bosworth XIII. Arrian names no commander
  and says that Oxyartes's wife and daughters were on the rock (4.18).
- Strengths: the climbers 300, Arrian 4.19 (060.13); Curtius also has 300 (7.11). The rest of Alexander's army
  `unknown`. Defenders: 30,000 armed men, Curtius 7.11.1 (060.26). Arrian gives no number.
- Casualties: 30 of the climbers fell, Arrian 4.19 (060.13). Curtius gives 32 (7.11.19; 060.26). The defenders surrendered.
  Curtius has Ariamazes and the leading nobles scourged and crucified at the foot of the rock (7.11.28; no digest
  line yet). Arrian has no executions.
- Outcome: Macedonian victory, the rock surrenders to the "winged men" (060.13).
- Phase pages: none. One stratagem, with no positions of either side beyond the cliff.
- Card date: "Spring 327 BC", Arrian's, with the dispute told in the text (decisions.md). Card place: see the
  marker `sogdian-rock-327` below.

## Choices
- Six pages, as the brief expects. Each page is one region and season of the campaign: the road east in 330, the
  crossing to the Oxus in 329, the Jaxartes, the Polytimetus, the 328 campaign, and the end in spring 327.
- Hyrcania, Areia and Phrada share one page (010-phrada). They are one march of about three months, and the brief
  puts them in one line.
- The Philotas affair is told on the march page, not on a page of its own. It changes no border, and its map is the
  couriers' route to Ecbatana.
- 060.5 is split between two pages, following the order of events in Arrian (3.30, 4.7) and Bosworth: the
  surrender in summer 329 on 020-hindu-kush, the mutilation at Bactra and the sending to Ecbatana in winter 329–328
  on 040-polytimetus.
- Proskynesis and the pages' conspiracy are told on 060-sogdian-rock, after the rock, not on the Cleitus page. The
  brief's line groups them with Cleitus in the order of Arrian's digression (4.8–14). Arrian himself (4.22.2) puts
  the pages' plot at Bactra after the rocks, and Bosworth dates proskynesis to late spring 327. The rock keeps
  Arrian's place after Cleitus (decisions.md).
- The Jaxartes and the Polytimetus get pages of their own. A page shows one battle card, and they are fought in
  different places, though in one season.
- `macedon-330b` and `persia-330b` were added to the Map part at this plan's request (Contract). Without them the autumn 330 page has no zone for the
  lands Alexander has just taken, and none for the king who held the east against him.
- `scythians-329` stands on the 330 page under its 329 outline, as the Saka and Dahae were there before Alexander
  met them (Arrian 3.28 has Dahae with Bessus).
- The rising shrinks in stages (map audit, cloud ruling): `sogdians-329` whole on 030-jaxartes, `sogdians-329b`
  without Maracanda's citadel on 040-polytimetus, `sogdians-328` without Maracanda, Nautaca and the columns' land on
  050-maracanda, and none on 060-sogdian-rock. Bosworth says the revolt could be said to have ended by the winter of
  328–327, and the rock is its last stronghold in Arrian.
- Battle sites carry no marker of their own: each card stands at its place (map audit).
- Places the sources leave uncertain are placed after Bosworth where he gives a site (Artacoana near Herat, Susia at
  Tus, Cyropolis at Kurkat, Nautaca at Shakhrisyabz), and after Engels where Bosworth gives none (the Sogdian Rock
  near Derbent, the Khawak pass). Each such label says "site uncertain" or "site unknown".
- The Polytimetus card place and Coenus's victory are set on the western Zeravshan from the sources' general
  descriptions. They are marked "site unknown".
- Routes are drawn as one line per march. The five columns of 328, Ptolemy's dash after Bessus, the round of the
  seven towns, the Jaxartes crossing and the raid on the Mardians are told, not drawn.
- The couriers' route to Ecbatana is drawn straight and dashed, since no source gives its track.
- Card date for the Sogdian Rock: Arrian's spring 327, with Bosworth's 328 told in the text (decisions.md).
