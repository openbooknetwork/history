## Ground

**Site.** Bosworth C&E: Darius "prepared his ground in the plain between the river Bumelus (Gomil) and the Jabal
Maqlub, the highest of the series of hills which intersect the terrain between the Tigris and the foothills of the
Zagros". Bosworth Commentary: "Tell Gomel lies to the north of the Jabal Maqlub, in the plain of Nauqir. The river
Bumelus should be identified with the Gomil, the western arm of the river Khazir". The Wikipedia coordinates
[43.44, 36.56] lie 7.6 km due north of Mount Alfaf (Jabal Maqlub, [43.4414, 36.4917], Wikipedia's coordinates for
that article). So they fall in the plain north of the Jabal, as Bosworth has it. Basis: `sourced` for the plain
between the Gomil and the Jabal Maqlub. The frame's origin is `reconstruction`, set at Wikipedia's point. Tarn,
after Stein, puts the field near Keramlais, south of the Jabal (Karamlesh, [43.41, 36.30], about 29 km south). The
section plan follows Bosworth, and so does this plan.

**Levelled ground.** Arrian 3.8.7: "in a district everywhere level; for whatever ground thereabouts was unlevel ...
had long before been levelled by the Persians". Arrian 3.13.2: Alexander "almost entirely got beyond the ground which
had been cleared and levelled". Its edges are `unknown`, so it is not drawn. The texts may name it.

**The Macedonian approach and facing.** Bosworth C&E: Alexander "moved up his army to a base camp below the northern
outliers of the Jabal Maqlub ... took his fighting force across the intervening hills ... occupy the slopes
overlooking the plain of Gaugamela. ... The Persians were 30 stades away, in full view". Arrian 3.9.3: "when
Alexander was only thirty stades distant from the enemy, and his army was already marching down from the hills ...
he caused his phalanx to halt there". So the Macedonians come down off the Jabal Maqlub hills with the hills at
their backs, and the Persians stand in the plain with the Gomil behind them. Facing: `reconstruction`. The
Macedonians face about north-east (45°) and the Persians about south-west (225°). That is the line from the Jabal
Maqlub towards the Gomil. It also fits three things the sources give. Darius flees from his left towards Arbela,
which lies at bearing 129° from the field (Arrian 3.15.4–5; Curtius 4.16.8–9). Alexander pursues towards the
Lycus, the Great Zab, south-east of the field (Bosworth C&E). Mazaeus leaves from the Persian right "a wide detour
behind the Macedonian army" to the Tigris (Bosworth C&E; Curtius 4.16.7). Read this way, the Macedonian right lies
to the south-east, and Alexander's march to the right runs south-east.

**Frame.** `frame([43.44, 36.56], 315)`. u runs north-west along the lines, towards the Macedonian left and the
Persian right. w runs north-east, from the Macedonians towards the Persians. Macedonian facing `f.face(90)` (45°),
Persian facing `f.face(270)` (225°). Negative u is the Macedonian right and the Persian left.

**Width of the front:** `unknown`. No source gives a width or two fixed places at its ends. What the sources give:

- The Persian line "far outflanking him upon their left" (Arrian 3.13.1).
- Darius and his guard "ranged opposite Alexander himself and his royal squadron" (Arrian 3.13.1).
- "If Darius had originally faced Alexander, the overlap must have been extreme" (Bosworth Commentary on 3.13.1).
- Alexander's formation "compact and relatively narrow" (Bosworth C&E).

The sizes below are drawing sizes for legibility, near the README's scale (a legion's front 500–600 m). No text
gives them:

- Macedonian front about 3.2 km from the right of the Companions to Parmenion's left.
- Persian front about 7 km, centred on Darius opposite Alexander, so its left overlaps the Macedonian right by
  about 3 km and its right overlaps the Macedonian left by a few hundred metres.

**Distances.** Thirty stades between the halted Macedonians and the Persian line (Arrian 3.9.3; Bosworth C&E: "30
stades away"). At Bosworth's own rate ("2,400 stades ... around 440 km", 183 m a stade) that is about 5.5 km. The
Macedonian camp stands 30 stades behind the Persian line, at w ≈ −4,850. Bosworth Commentary: the base camp was "30
stades to the rear ... by now some distance to the left of centre ... some 6 km from the actual hostilities". Basis:
`sourced` for the 30 stades. The camp's place in u is a `reconstruction` (behind the centre at the deployment).
The Lycus is "some 30 km from the battlefield" (Bosworth C&E) and "nearly 32 km as the crow flies" (Bosworth
Commentary). It lies outside the phase bbox.

**Water:** none on the phase pages. No source puts the Bumelus on the ground that was fought over. It lies behind
the Persian camp, and its course is not given.

**Bbox**, the same on all three phases: `f.box([[-6200, -5300], [3600, 3000]], 0)`, about 9.8 × 8.3 km. That suits
`max_zoom: 14`.

## Order of battle

Strengths are `unknown` unless a figure is given with its author. Positions are local (u, w) centres in metres;
width runs along the front. They are guides for the implementor: `reconstruction` from the order the sources give.

### Macedonians (`macedon`), from the right flank to the left

Totals: "The whole of Alexander's army numbered 7,000 cavalry and about 40,000 infantry" (Arrian 3.12.5).

1. **Right flank guard** (`right-flank-guard`; cavalry in front, light infantry inside): (−1950, −1100), width 700,
   depth 250, facing `f.face(180)` (outwards, 135°). Strength `unknown`.
   - Menidas, Greek mercenary cavalry "In front of all" (Arrian 3.12.3).
   - Aretes (the lancers) and Ariston (the Paeonians): "In front of the Agrianians and archers were posted the light
     cavalry used for skirmishing, and the Paeonians, under the command of Aretes and Aristo" (Arrian 3.12.3).
     Bosworth Commentary on 3.12.3: "Aretes must therefore have commanded the πρόδρομοι".
   - Attalus and Brison: "half of the Agrianians, under the command of Attalus, in conjunction with the Macedonian
     archers under Briso's command, were posted angular-wise" (Arrian 3.12.2).
   - Cleander: "Next to the archers were the men called the veteran mercenaries, whose commander was Cleander"
     (Arrian 3.12.2).
   - Placing thrown back: "On each flank there was a formation thrown back en échelon. On the right were light
     troops, Agrianians and archers, and the 'old mercenaries' led by Cleander. These closed the gap between the two
     parallel fronts, and in advance of them was a cavalry screen, first the Scouts and Paeonians and then, at the
     apex of the line, a contingent of mercenary cavalry led by Menidas" (Bosworth C&E).
   - Facing outwards: "consistent with a flank guard facing right, its side exposed to the enemy" (Bosworth
     Commentary on 3.12.2).
2. **Screen against the chariots** (`screen`; light): (−1450, −420), width 600, depth 60. Commanders Balacrus (the
   javelin-men) and Attalus (the Agrianians). "in front of the royal squadron of cavalry and the other Companions had
   been posted half of the Agrianians and archers, and the javelin-men of Balacrus who had been ranged opposite the
   scythe-bearing chariots" (Arrian 3.12.3). Strength `unknown`.
3. **Companion cavalry** (`companions`; cavalry): (−1450, −650), width 600, depth 200. Alexander. Philotas commands
   the eight squadrons. The royal squadron under Cleitus the Black is on the right, then the squadrons of Glaucias,
   Ariston, Sopolis, Heraclides, Demetrius son of Althaemenes, Meleager and Hegelochus. "The right wing was held by
   the cavalry Companions, in front of whom had been posted the royal squadron, commanded by Clitus, son of Dropidas.
   ... All the cavalry Companions were under the supreme command of Philotas, son of Parmenio" (Arrian 3.11.8).
   Strength: about 2,000 (Tarn I: "by Gaugamela were 2,000 strong"; Tarn's estimate, no ancient figure).
4. **Phalanx, right part** (`phalanx-right`; pikes): (−505, −650), width 1250, depth 250.
   - Nicanor, son of Parmenion, with the hypaspists: "nearest to the cavalry had been posted first the select corps
     of shield-bearing guards, and then the rest of the shield-bearing-guards, under the command of Nicanor, son of
     Parmenio".
   - Then the battalions of Coenus, Perdiccas, Meleager and Polyperchon: "Next to these was the brigade of Coenus,
     son of Polemocrates; after these that of Perdiceas, son of Orontes, then that of Meleager, son of Neoptolemus,
     then that of Polysperchon" (Arrian 3.11.9).
   - Strength `unknown`.
5. **Phalanx, left part** (`phalanx-left`; pikes): (+365, −650), width 450, depth 250.
   - Simmias, for his brother Amyntas: "last that of Amyntas, son of Andromenes, under the command of Simmias,
     because Amyntas had been despatched to Macedonia".
   - Craterus: "The brigade of Craterus, son of Alexander, held the left end of the Macedonian phalanx, and this
     general commanded the left wing of the infantry" (Arrian 3.11.9).
   - Diodorus 57.3 and Curtius 4.13.28 give Philip, son of Balacrus, for Simmias. See Bosworth Commentary on 3.11.9.
   - Strength `unknown`.
6. **Left wing cavalry** (`left-cavalry`; cavalry): (+990, −650), width 800, depth 200. Parmenion with the
   Pharsalians at the far left; the Thessalians under Philip, son of Menelaus; the allied Greek horse under
   Erigyius next to Craterus. "Next to him was the allied Grecian cavalry, under the command of Erigyius, son of
   Larichus. Next to these, towards the left wing of the army, were the Thessalian cavalry, under the command of
   Philip, son of Menelaüs. But the whole left wing was led by Parmenio, son of Philotas, round whose person were
   ranged the Pharsalian horsemen" (Arrian 3.11.10). Strength: Thessalians about 2,000 (Tarn I, his estimate);
   otherwise `unknown`.
7. **Left flank guard** (`left-flank-guard`; cavalry, with Thracian infantry): (+1540, −1100), width 700, depth 250,
   facing `f.face(0)` (outwards, 315°). Strength `unknown`.
   - Sitalces (Thracians), Coeranus (allied horse), Agatho (Odrysians), Andromachus (mercenary horse at the head):
     "On the left the Thracians under the command of Sitalces had been posted angular-wise, and near them the cavalry
     of the Grecian allies, under the direction of Coeranus. Next stood the Odrysian cavalry, under the command of
     Agatho, son of Tyrimmas. In this part, in front of all, were posted the auxiliary cavalry of the Grecian
     mercenaries, under the direction of Andromachus, son of Hiero" (Arrian 3.12.4–5).
   - "On the left flank there was a comparable formation, first Thracian infantry closing the gap between the two
     lines and then squadrons of allied mercenary cavalry" (Bosworth C&E).
8. **Second line, the reserve phalanx** (`reserve`; infantry): (−200, −1500), width 2400, depth 200. Commanders
   `unknown`. "he also posted a second array, so that his phalanx might be a double one. Directions had been given to
   the commanders of these men posted in reserve, to wheel round and receive the attack of the foreigners, if they
   should see their own comrades surrounded" (Arrian 3.12.1). Make-up: "Parallel to the front was a second line of
   infantry, the Hellenic troops of the Corinthian League and any mercenaries and troops from the Balkans not deployed
   elsewhere" (Bosworth C&E; by elimination, Bosworth Commentary on 3.12.1). Strength `unknown`.
9. **Camp and baggage**: a marker, `flag`, at (0, −4850) on every phase. "Near the baggage the infantry from Thrace
   were posted as a guard" (Arrian 3.12.5). Place: see Ground. Strength of the guard `unknown`.

The whole formation: "The entire formation was roughly rectangular in shape" (Bosworth C&E). Curtius 4.13.31: "The
hindermost ranks he faced towards the rear, so as to fortify the whole battle-line by a circular formation".
Diodorus 57.5: "On both flanks he kept his wings back".

### Persians (`persia`), from the left flank to the right

Totals: see Card. Darius's line, from Arrian 3.11.3–7 ("the written scheme of arrangement drawn up by Darius was
afterwards captured"). Bosworth C&E gives the same order.

1. **Saka and Bactrian advance guard** (`saka-bactrians`; knights, the mailed Saka): (−2500, +300), width 700,
   depth 200. Mavaces leads the Saka (Arrian 3.8.3: "They were commanded by Mavaces, and were horse-bowmen"), under
   Bessus's command.
   - "On the left, opposite Alexander's right, had been posted the Scythian cavalry, about 1,000 Bactrians and 100
     scythe-bearing chariots" (Arrian 3.11.6).
   - The mail: "the Scythians themselves and their horses were much more completely protected with armour" (Arrian
     3.13.4).
   - Placing in front of the line: "The Scythian and Bactrian advance contingent must then have been placed in front
     of Darius' line" (Bosworth Commentary on 3.11.6).
   - Strength: Bactrians "about 1,000" (Arrian). The Saka are 1,000 if they are Curtius's Dahae ("These are the 1,000
     Dahae whom Curtius places at the extreme left", Bosworth Commentary; Curtius 4.12.6).
2. **100 scythed chariots** (`chariots-left`; chariots): (−1600, +150), width 700, depth 60. "100 scythe-bearing
   chariots" (Arrian 3.11.6). They are placed opposite the Companions because Balacrus's javelin-men were "ranged
   opposite the scythe-bearing chariots" (Arrian 3.12.3; Bosworth Commentary on 3.11.6). Tarn I: "he found that the
   Companions were opposite the scythed chariots". Strength: 100 (Arrian; Curtius 4.12.6 also 100).
3. **Left wing** (`persian-left`; cavalry): (−3600, +800), width 2800, depth 300. Bessus commands: "Mazaeus
   commanded the Persian right wing (iv, 16, 1, 4) and Bessus the left (iv, 15, 2)" (Tarn II, after Curtius).
   - Arrian 3.11.3: "His left wing was held by the Bactrian cavalry, in conjunction with the Daans and Arachosians;
     near these had been posted the Persians, horse and foot mixed together; next to these the Susians, and then the
     Cadusians". The Arachosians are under Barsaentes (Arrian 3.8.4).
   - Strength, after Curtius 4.12.6–7: "Bactrian cavalry, about 1000 in number, the Dahae, of just the same number,
     and the Arachosii and the Susiani, 4000 ... Bessus with 8000 horsemen, who likewise were Bactriani. The
     Massagetae brought up his rear with 2000". Bosworth Commentary: "they amount to 16,000". Curtius's placing
     differs from Arrian's (Bosworth Commentary on 3.11.3). The Cadusians may belong on the right (Curtius 4.12.12;
     Bosworth: Arrian may have "transposed the Cadusians from the inner left to the inner right"). The name leaves
     them out.
4. **Centre** (`persian-centre`; infantry, drawn deep): (−1450, +1050), width 1500, depth 800. Darius.
   - "In the centre where King Darius was, had been posted the king's kinsmen, the Persian guards carrying spears
     with golden apples at the butt end, the Indians, the Carians who had been forcibly removed to Central Asia, and
     the Mardian archers. The Uxians, the Babylonians, the men who dwell near the Red Sea, and the Sitacenians had also
     been drawn up in deep column. ... In front of Darius's royal squadron of cavalry stood the elephants and 50
     chariots. ... The Greek mercenaries ... were stationed right opposite their phalanx, in two divisions close beside
     Darius himself and his Persian attendants, one division on each side" (Arrian 3.11.5–7).
   - Opposite Alexander: Arrian 3.13.1. "In the centre, as usual, was the Great King with his élite squadron of
     Kinsmen and his royal guard of melophoroi ... to the rear, packed in depth, was the bulk of the infantry"
     (Bosworth C&E).
   - Strength: Kinsmen "the whole thousand included in one squadron" (Diodorus 59.2). Apple-bearers "1,000 strong"
     (Bosworth Commentary on 3.11.5). Greek mercenaries: "About 2,000" fled with Darius (Arrian 3.16.2). Elephants
     "about fifteen" (Arrian 3.8.6). Chariots 50 (Arrian 3.11.6). Levies `unknown`.
5. **Right wing** (`persian-right`; cavalry): (+650, +800), width 2700, depth 300. Mazaeus.
   - "On the right had been posted the men from Coele-Syria and Mesopotamia. On the right again were the Medes; next
     to them the Parthians and Sacians; then the Tapurians and Hyrcanians, and last the Albanians and Sacesinians,
     extending as far as the middle of the whole phalanx" (Arrian 3.11.4). The Syrians are at the outer end (Bosworth
     C&E: "To the right were the troops from Syria and Mesopotamia together with the Median levy and peoples from the
     Caspian shore as far east as Parthyaea"; Tarn I: "on the wing the Syrians and Mesopotamians").
   - Mazaeus commands: "Mazaeus was in command of the Persian right wing with the best of the cavalry under him"
     (Diodorus 59.5); Curtius 4.16.1. Mazaeus "led" the Syrians (Arrian 3.8.6).
   - Strength `unknown`.
6. **Armenian and Cappadocian advance guard** (`armenians`; cavalry): (+1500, +350), width 700, depth 200. Orontes and
   Mithraustes (Armenians) and Ariaces (Cappadocians), Arrian 3.8.5. "In front of the right wing the Armenian and
   Cappadocian cavalry with 50 scythe-bearing chariots had been posted" (Arrian 3.11.6). Strength `unknown`; 50
   chariots.

## Phases

The sources give the three phases the section plan proposes. Bosworth's table (Commentary on 3.13–14) has four
stages on each wing. Stages (i)–(iv) on the right and (i)–(iv) on the left fit phases 1–2. Arrian 3.14.4–15 and
Bosworth C&E's last two paragraphs make phase 3.

Arrow and clash coordinates are local (u, w). Each arrow ends on or over its target. Blocks stand where the units are
at the end of each phase. Phase 1 is the one exception: its blocks stand as the lines were drawn up, and its arrows
show the march that begins from there, as on the Cannae deployment page.

### Phase 1: `gaugamela-deployment`, "The armies deploy". 1 October 331 BC, morning

**Where each unit stands.** As in the Order of battle, all 14 blocks: 8 Macedonian, 6 Persian. Basis:
- `sourced` for the order of the units from flank to flank, front and rear (Arrian 3.11–12; Bosworth C&E).
- `sourced` for Darius opposite Alexander and the Persian left overlapping far beyond the Macedonian right (Arrian
  3.13.1).
- `reconstruction` for the distances between the lines, the sizes and the compass facing (see Ground).

**What moves.** Three arrows and one clash.

1. `march-right`, macedon, width 120: from (−1450, −900) by (−2100, −950) to (−2700, −900). Name: "Alexander leads
   his army to the right, towards the edge of the levelled ground". Basis: `sourced`. "Alexander led his own army
   more towards the right" (Arrian 3.13.1). "Accordingly he moved to the right, keeping the Royal squadron at the
   apex of the line, which inevitably became oblique" (Bosworth C&E). Curtius 4.15.1: "ordered his army to charge on
   a slant"; Diodorus 57.6: "advancing obliquely". The length of the move is a `reconstruction`, about 1 km, to
   bring the line to where phase 2 opens.
2. `persian-parallel`, persia, width 120: from (−3600, +1050) by (−4400, +1100) to (−5200, +1050). Name: "The
   Persian left keeps pace, far outflanking him". Basis: `sourced`. "the Persians marched along parallel with him,
   far outflanking him upon their left" (Arrian 3.13.1). Darius's levies are slower: "their forces were too deeply
   massed to be quickly deployed as a whole, and the scythed chariots necessarily remained on their prepared ground"
   (Bosworth C&E). So only the left wing has an arrow, and the chariot block does not move.
3. `saka-ride`, persia, width 100: from (−2500, +200) by (−2450, −300) to (−2150, −900). It ends on the front of the
   right flank guard. Name: "The Saka horse ride along the line and engage Alexander's leading troops". Basis:
   `sourced`. "Then the Scythian cavalry rode along the line, and came into conflict with the front men of
   Alexander's array" (Arrian 3.13.1).
   - Clash at (−2150, −900), size 200.

**Markers (6).**
- `gaugamela-deploy-alexander`, user: (−1450, −1000), "Alexander", "Companions; leads the march right".
- `gaugamela-deploy-darius`, user: (−1450, +1600), "Darius", "Centre, opposite Alexander".
- `gaugamela-deploy-parmenion`, user: (+990, −1000), "Parmenion", "Commands the left".
- `gaugamela-deploy-bessus`, user: (−3600, +1250), "Bessus", "Bactrians and the Persian left".
- `gaugamela-deploy-mazaeus`, user: (+650, +1250), "Mazaeus", "Commands the Persian right".
- `gaugamela-deploy-camp`, flag: (+500, −4850), "Macedonian camp", "Baggage and prisoners, Thracian guard".

Basis for the commanders: Arrian 3.11.8–10; Curtius 4.15.2, 4.16.1; Diodorus 59.5.

**What the phase decides.** Alexander draws the fight away from the levelled ground. Darius has to act before the
Macedonians leave the ground made for his chariots. "Then Darius, fearing that his chariots would become useless, if
the Macedonians advanced into uneven ground, ordered the front ranks of his left wing to ride round the right wing"
(Arrian 3.13.2).

**Share of reconstruction:** about a third (the sizes, the distances, the compass facing and the length of the
march). The units, their order and the movements are sourced.

### Phase 2: `gaugamela-breakthrough`, "The wedge and Darius's flight". 1 October 331 BC

**Where each unit stands as it opens.** As at the end of phase 1. The Macedonian line is about 1 km further right and
oblique, with the right forward and the left held back. The Saka are in contact with the right flank guard. The
Persian left wing has extended to its left.

**What moves**, in the order of Bosworth's reconstruction (Commentary on 3.13–14; C&E). Arrian tells the flank fight
first and then goes back to the chariots.

- The Saka and Bactrians ride round the right. Menidas charges and is routed. Aretes and the Paeonians charge. Bessus
  sends in the rest of the Bactrians, and the cavalry fight sways to and fro.
  - Arrian 3.13.2–4: "Alexander ordered the cavalry of the Grecian mercenaries under the command of Menidas to
    attack them. But the Scythian cavalry and the Bactrians ... being much more numerous they put the small body of
    Greeks to rout. Alexander then ordered Aristo at the head of the Paeonians and Grecian auxiliaries to attack the
    Scythians; and the barbarians gave way. But the rest of the Bactrians ... caused their own comrades who were
    already in flight to turn and renew the battle ... Notwithstanding this, the Macedonians sustained their
    assaults".
  - Bosworth Commentary reads "Aretes and the Paeonians" here and allows "only two cavalry waves". Chinnock's text
    has "Aristo".
- The chariots charge Alexander. They are speared, pulled down or let through, and finished off behind the line.
  "the foreigners launched their scythe-bearing chariots against Alexander himself ... the Agrianians and the
  javelin-men with Balacrus ... hurled their javelins at them ... Yet some rolled right through the ranks; for the
  men stood apart and opened their ranks ... these also were afterwards overpowered by the grooms of Alexander's
  army and by the royal shield-bearing guards" (Arrian 3.13.5–6). "the chariot attack took place in the course of
  the cavalry engagement" (Bosworth Commentary on 3.13.5).
- Persian horse leave the line to help the force riding round the right, and a gap opens. Alexander wheels into it
  in a wedge of Companions and the nearest phalanx battalions, straight at Darius.
  - "when the Persians had made a break in the front line of their army, in consequence of the cavalry sallying forth
    to assist those who were surrounding the right wing, Alexander wheeled round towards the gap, and forming a wedge
    as it were of the Companion cavalry and of the part of the phalanx which was posted here, he led them with a quick
    charge and loud battle-cry straight towards Darius himself" (Arrian 3.14.2).
  - "a gap developed between the Persian left and rest of the line. ... The formation appeared like a flattened
    lambda, with the Royal squadron at its point. This apex now drove into the gap" (Bosworth C&E).
  - Curtius 4.15.20 has the same move, "Alexander therefore attacked their" loosened ranks. He calls it the Persian
    "right wing", in a battle where he puts Darius on his own left (4.14.8; Welles's note 87).
- The phalanx attacks the centre. Darius flees. "when the Macedonian phalanx in dense array and bristling with long
  pikes had also made an attack upon them, all things at once appeared full of terror to Darius ... so that he was
  the first to turn and flee" (Arrian 3.14.3). This is disputed:
  - Bosworth: "He was hardly the first to take to his heels, as Arrian claims ... but equally he cannot have waited
    until Alexander came within spear range".
  - Diodorus 60.3–4: the guard flees first, thinking the king has fallen.
  - Diary: "The king, his troops deserted him".
  - So the arrow's name says only that Darius flees.
- At the same time the left is under pressure. "It is clear that the left was under severe pressure from the
  beginning. Mazaeus ... had fewer chariots but he used them in conjunction with cavalry" (Bosworth C&E). Diodorus
  58.2: "First the scythed chariots swung into action at full gallop ... especially since Mazaeus in command of the
  cavalry made their attack more frightening by supporting with his dense squadrons of horse".

### Phase 3: `gaugamela-left-wing`, "Parmenion's wing and the pursuit". 1 October 331 BC, afternoon and evening

**Where each unit stands as it opens.** As at the end of phase 2. Alexander and the Companions are going in pursuit,
and the right part of the phalanx is pressing through the centre.

**What moves.**

- Simmias halts, and a gap opens in the phalanx. Persian and Indian horse break through to the baggage. The
  prisoners rise. The reserve turns about and cuts them down.
  - "Simmias and his brigade were not yet able to start with Alexander in pursuit, but causing the phalanx to halt
    there, he took part in the struggle, because the left wing of the Macedonians was reported to be hard pressed. In
    this part of the field, their line being broken, some of the Indians and of the Persian cavalry burst through the
    gap towards the baggage ... the foreign prisoners also assisted them ... But the commanders of the men who had
    been posted as a reserve to the first phalanx ... coming upon the Persians in the rear, killed many of them there
    collected round the baggage" (Arrian 3.14.4–6).
  - "The two leftmost battalions stood their ground to preserve continuity with the rest of the Macedonian front
    while the rest pressed onwards diagonally through the centre. The gap was penetrated by a few units of Persian
    and Indian cavalry who were stationed in the vicinity of Darius, but it was a limited irruption, soon neutralised
    by the reserve phalanx in the rear" (Bosworth C&E).
- Mazaeus's horse ride round the Macedonian left and attack Parmenion in the flank. A detachment rides on to the
  camp.
  - "The Persians on the right wing, who had not yet become aware of the flight of Darius, rode round Alexander's
    left wing and attacked Parmenio in flank" (Arrian 3.14.6). Curtius 4.16.1–2: "with his superior numbers he had
    begun to encircle their line".
  - The raid on the camp: "sent off two thousand Cadusii and a thousand picked Scythian horsemen with orders to ride
    around the enemy's flank and to continue on to their camp" (Diodorus 59.5); Curtius 4.15.5 ("his 1000 cavalry");
    Plutarch 32.5. Bosworth C&E: "its core, the Persian attack on the camp, is surely historical".
  - Diodorus and Bosworth put the raid early in the battle. It is drawn here with the other attack on the camp, and
    the text says when it came.
- Parmenion's message. This is disputed and is not drawn as a movement:
  - Arrian 3.15.1: Alexander "immediately turned back again from the pursuit".
  - Diodorus 60.7: the riders find him "already in full pursuit at a great distance from the battlefield" and return.
    Bosworth C&E: "That is surely true".
  - Plutarch 33.9–11 and Curtius 4.16.2–3 have yet other versions.
- The Companions come back and meet the fleeing Persian, Parthian and Indian horse in the hardest cavalry fight of
  the day.
  - "he assaulted the fleeing cavalry of the enemy, the Parthians, some of the Indians, and the most numerous and the
    bravest division of the Persians. Then ensued the most obstinately contested cavalry fight in the whole
    engagement ... Here about sixty of Alexander's Companions fell" (Arrian 3.15.1–2).
  - "As the Companions returned from the pursuit they crossed the path of a large body of stragglers ... Persians,
    Parthyaeans and Indians ... They were riding in deep formation and clashed frontally with the returning
    Companions, who barred their line of retreat" (Bosworth C&E).
  - The arrow comes back from the direction of the pursuit, which fits both accounts.
- The Thessalians throw back the Persian right as news of Darius's flight reaches it.
  - "the Thessalian cavalry in a splendid struggle, had not fallen short of Alexander's success ... For the
    foreigners on the right wing were already beginning to fly when he came on the scene" (Arrian 3.15.3).
  - "Gradually the news of the rout of the centre and the flight of the Great King penetrated to the Persian right,
    and the assault flagged. The Thessalian cavalry ... rallied and threw the enemy into retreat there also"
    (Bosworth C&E). Curtius 4.16.4–6; Diodorus 60.8.
  - Mazaeus later "made a wide detour behind the Macedonian army, crossed the Tigris and took refuge in Babylon"
    (Bosworth C&E; Curtius 4.16.7). That route belongs to `babylon-and-susa` (`mazaeus-babylon-331`).
- The pursuit to the Lycus and Parmenion's capture of the Persian camp: "Alexander crossed the river Lycus and pitched
  his camp there ... while Parmenio seized the Persian camp with their baggage, elephants, and camels" (Arrian
  3.15.4). The Lycus is about 32 km off (Bosworth Commentary), beyond the bbox. It gets a marker here and a route on
  `babylon-and-susa`. The Persian camp's place is `unknown`, so it gets no marker.

## Card

- **Date:** 1 October 331 BC (Diary: "The twenty-fourth, in the morning"; Lendering's note: "1 October"). Arrian
  3.15.7 gives the month Pyanepsion.
- **Place:** Gaugamela, by the Bumelus, about 600 stades from Arbela (Arrian 3.8.7). Bosworth Commentary: "a slight
  exaggeration; modern estimates vary between 80 and 95 km".
- **Commanders.** `macedon`: Alexander; Parmenion commanding the left (Arrian 3.11.10). `persia`: Darius III; Bessus
  on the left (Curtius 4.15.2, as Tarn II reads it; Arrian 3.8.3 has him leading the Bactrians); Mazaeus on the
  right (Diodorus 59.5, 60.5; Curtius 4.16.1).
- **Strength, Macedonian:** 7,000 cavalry and about 40,000 infantry (Arrian 3.12.5).
- **Strength, Persian:**
  - Arrian 3.8.6: 40,000 cavalry, 1,000,000 infantry, 200 scythed chariots and about 15 elephants. Chinnock's
    translation reads "40,000 cavalry". Bosworth's Commentary on the passage reads the same: "Arrian's figure for the
    cavalry is in rough agreement with that of Curtius" (45,000). Only Bosworth C&E prints "1,000,000 infantry and
    400,000 cavalry (Arrian)", which goes against his own Commentary and the translation. The card and texts use
    40,000.
  - Diodorus 53.3: 800,000 infantry and "no less than two hundred thousand cavalry"; 200 chariots (53.1).
  - Curtius 4.12.13: 45,000 cavalry and 200,000 infantry.
  - Bosworth C&E: the advantage "cannot be quantified".
- **Casualties, Macedonian:**
  - Arrian 3.15.6: about 100 killed, more than 1,000 horses lost.
  - Diodorus 61.3: about 500 killed, "very many wounded".
  - Curtius 4.16.26: "less than 300".
- **Casualties, Persian:**
  - Arrian 3.15.6: "about 300,000 slain, and far more were taken prisoners".
  - Diodorus 61.3: "more than ninety thousand".
  - Curtius 4.16.26: 40,000.
  - Bosworth C&E: "It is impossible to estimate the losses on either side".
- **Outcome:** Alexander's victory; Darius flees by Arbela into Media (Arrian 3.15.5, 3.16.1).
