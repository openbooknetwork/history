Kantakouzenos’s son Matthew holds eastern Thrace around [Adrianople](@adrianople), and John V the western part. In 1352 John V attacks Matthew with Serbian help and besieges Adrianople. Kantakouzenos calls on Orhan, whose son Süleyman crosses with Ottoman horsemen, retakes the towns that have gone over to John V and plunders them, Adrianople among them.

In October 1352 about 10,000 Ottoman horsemen under Süleyman meet 4,000 to 6,000 Serbian horsemen, sent to John V by Stefan Dušan, near Didymoteicho (Demotika). The Serbs are routed, with 7,000 dead by Kantakouzenos’s count and 4,000 by Gregoras’s. John V flees to the Venetian island of Tenedos. The battle is the first large Ottoman victory in Europe.

Venice and Genoa are at war in these years. On 13 February 1352 their fleets fight in the Bosporus, and Orhan, at odds with Venice, sends troops to the Genoese at [Galata](@galata). After Demotika Süleyman holds [Tzympe (Çimpe)](@tzympe-1352) on the Gallipoli peninsula, which Kantakouzenos grants to the Ottomans about 1352, their first base in Europe. By Ottoman tradition Süleyman takes it in 1356 in a night attack with 39 men.
