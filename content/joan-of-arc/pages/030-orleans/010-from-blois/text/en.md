On 27 or 28 April 1429 the convoy for besieged Orléans sets out from [Blois](@blois), its priests walking ahead and singing hymns. Gilles de Rais and Boussac, both marshals of France, lead the escort, with the admiral de Culant, the Gascon captain La Hire and Ambroise de Loré. Joan rides with them, brought here by Raoul de Gaucourt, governor of Orléans.

She wants to go straight through the Beauce, on the north bank, but the English are strongest there, so the captains take the south bank through the Sologne without telling her. On 28 April the convoy passes Olivet, opposite the city. The Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois), commanding in the city, crosses to meet her. She asks at once whether it was he who had her brought this way.

The supplies go by water: boats from the city are loaded upstream, before [Chécy](@checy), at the Île-aux-Bourdons. The contrary wind turns, and the laden boats sail down past the bastilles, the English siege forts, while the garrison skirmishes before the one at Saint-Loup.

Joan crosses the river with about 200 lances. The rest of the escort goes back to cross at Blois, with her confessor Jean Pasquerel and her banner. Her company lies a night at Chécy.

At about eight in the evening of Friday 29 April she rides into [Orléans](@orleans-entry) armed, on a white horse, with the Bastard beside her and townspeople carrying torches. The English do nothing to stop her. She lodges with Jacques Boucher, the Duke of Orléans's treasurer, by the Porte Regnart.
