Joan is about thirteen when she first hears a voice she takes to be from God.\* She is in her father's garden at [Domrémy](@domremy-voices) about midday in summer; the voice reaches her from the side of the church, on her right, and a light nearly always comes with it. The year is 1424 or 1425.

Later the voice tells her to leave home and go into France, and that she will raise the siege of Orléans. If she goes to Robert de Baudricourt, the squire in command at [Vaucouleurs](@vaucouleurs-1428), he will find men to take her. Her father knows nothing of it.

Vaucouleurs is a walled town on the Meuse north of Domrémy, some twelve or thirteen miles off. By midsummer 1428 no other town of eastern France still stands for Charles VII, the uncrowned king who holds the lands south of the Loire.

In May 1428 Joan comes to Baudricourt and is sent home in the company of Durand Laxart, her cousin's husband, whom she calls uncle. The captain's advice to Laxart, given more than once, is to slap the girl and bring her home to her father.

In July Antoine de Vergy, governor of Champagne for the English, reviews his troops at [Saint-Urbain](@vergy-review-1428) and marches on Vaucouleurs. Within days the force is gone and Baudricourt still holds the town; how he keeps it is not known. For dread of the Burgundians Joan goes to [Neufchâteau](@neufchateau), a walled town of the Duke of Lorraine a few miles south, and stays about a fortnight with an innkeeper called La Rousse. Her family and the other villagers go too, with their cattle.

Champagne, west of Vaucouleurs, is in Anglo-Burgundian hands, Troyes and Reims with it, and the Duke of Lorraine is an ally of the English.

### Notes

\* As Joan told her judges at Rouen in 1431.
