In spring 1348 the plague reaches the lands of King Peter IV of Aragon. It comes south from [Perpignan](@perpignan) along the coast road, and by sea to the islands and the harbours. [Palma](@palma) on Mallorca has it in the spring, and Barcelona and [Valencia](@valencia) by May. At Valencia the nobles of the Union, who are in revolt, hold the king, and when the plague breaks out he uses the confusion to get away.

That spring brings the first killings of Jews. At [Toulon](@burning-toulon) in Provence, on the night of Palm Sunday, 12 to 13 April 1348, townsmen break into the Jewish street, plunder the houses and kill 40 people. On 17 May a crowd in [Barcelona](@burning-barcelona) attacks the Call, the Jewish quarter, and kills about 20 people. King Peter orders the attackers arrested a few days later. In July the killing spreads inland to [Tàrrega](@burning-tarrega), where about 300 Jews die. Archaeologists found their mass graves outside the town in 2007.

At Lleida, in April 1348, [Jacme d’Agramont](@agramont), a master of medicine at the town’s university, writes a short guide for the town councillors before the plague arrives. His Regiment de preservació, a rule for protection against epidemics and pestilence, is the first known tract on the plague of 1348 and the first medical work written in Catalan. It explains the pestilence by corrupted air and the stars, and it urges the town to keep its streets clean. When the plague reaches Lleida, Agramont is among the first to die of it.
