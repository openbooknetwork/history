At [Orșova](@orsova-1396), where the Danube narrows at the Iron Gates, the army crosses to the right bank on pontoons and boats over eight days. Ivan Sratsimir opens the gates of [Vidin](@vidin) and hands over its Ottoman garrison, whose officers are put to death. The French reach [Rahova](@rahova-1396) by a night march ahead of their allies and fight for the bridge over the moat until Sigismund arrives. The next morning the town surrenders to Sigismund on the promise that its people will be spared, but the French sack and burn it, kill many of its people and take 1,000 Turkish and Bulgarian inhabitants hostage.

The army comes in sight of [Nicopolis](@nicopolis-1396) (Nikopol) on 12 September. The fortress stands on a limestone cliff above the Danube, a larger walled town on the heights and a smaller one below, and its governor Doğan Bey prepares for a long siege. The crusaders have brought no siege engines. They block the town by land while the ships close the river, and wait to starve it out.

Bayezid leaves the blockade of [Constantinople](@constantinople). His army gathers between [Edirne](@edirne) and [Philippopolis](@philippopolis-1396), where Stefan Lazarević joins him with Serbian heavy horsemen from [Kruševac](@krusevac). The army crosses the Shipka Pass and reaches [Tarnovo](@tarnovo-1396), about 110 km south of Nicopolis, where 500 horsemen sent by Sigismund find it. Coucy rides south with 500 knights and 500 mounted archers and ambushes an Ottoman detachment in a pass.
