In winter 1428–1429 Durand Laxart, her cousin's husband, brings Joan from his village of [Burey](@burey-1429) back to [Vaucouleurs](@vaucouleurs-1429). She lodges with Henri Le Royer, a cartwright, and his wife Catherine, about three weeks in all.

The captain, Robert de Baudricourt, sends her away twice before he hears her out and promises an escort, just as her voice has foretold. He comes to the house with the curé, Jean Fournier, and the priest exorcises her.

Charles II, Duke of Lorraine, sends her a safe-conduct and has her brought to [Nancy](@nancy-1429), his capital. On the way Laxart and Jacques Alain, a man of Vaucouleurs, take her on pilgrimage to the shrine of [Saint-Nicolas-de-Port](@saint-nicolas-de-port). The duke asks about his health; she asks for his son, most likely his son-in-law René of Anjou, and for men to take her into France. He gives her four francs, and she goes back.

Before she leaves for Nancy, Jean de Metz, a squire, has sworn at Vaucouleurs to take her to the king, and he and another squire, Bertrand de Poulengy, have offered to escort her at their own cost, which the king's treasury later repays. The townspeople buy her men's clothes for the road, and her horse costs twelve or sixteen francs.

Baudricourt gives way, though with doubt. He gives her a sword, makes her escort swear to take her well and safely, and says, "Go, come what may." His reasons are not known. One story has her know of the French defeat near Orléans at the Battle of the Herrings on the very day, 12 February 1429, and tell Baudricourt.
