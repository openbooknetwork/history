Joan rides out of [Vaucouleurs](@vaucouleurs-departure) by the Gate of France in men's clothes, with six companions: the squires Jean de Metz and Bertrand de Poulengy, their servants Julien and Jean de Honnecourt, a king's messenger named Colet de Vienne, and Richard the Archer. In her own words she went with a knight, a squire and four servants.\*

The day she left is told three ways. Lowell, following Boismarmin, puts it at or about 12 February 1429, the day of the Battle of the Herrings near Orléans. Jean de Metz remembered that they came back from Nancy about Sunday 13 February and then set out. Lang gives 23 February, though he says the critical historians make it the 13th.

As far as the Loire the road runs through country that owns Henry VI as king. They travel mostly by night, Jean de Metz said, to stay clear of the Burgundian and English troops on the roads, and hear mass only twice. They sleep the first night at the abbey of [Saint-Urbain](@saint-urbain) on the Marne. At [Auxerre](@auxerre-1429), a Burgundian city, she hears mass in the principal church. At [Gien](@gien-1429), on the Loire about forty miles above Orléans, they reach a town that holds for Charles VII, and word of her goes ahead to Orléans. Then, Lowell writes, they ride on south of the river across the Sologne.

At [Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois](@fierbois-letter) she hears mass three times in one day and writes to the king for leave to come. She has ridden a hundred and fifty leagues to help him, she says. Her two escorts later said the road took eleven days.\*\* She reaches [Chinon](@chinon-arrival) towards noon: on 23 February by Lowell's reckoning, or on 6 March by the French Continuator of Guillaume de Nangis, the one chronicle that gives the day, whom Quicherat, Lang and Thurston follow.\*\*\*

The map shows her road across English and Burgundian land into the king's, through the towns the witnesses name. Where she crossed the Loire is not told.

### Notes

\* Thurston gives her an escort of three men-at-arms, and Monstrelet one of four to six men.

\*\* Lang notes that Jean de Metz's day of leaving, with the day of arrival Lowell follows, gives ten days rather than eleven.

\*\*\* Beaucourt has the news of her coming reach the castle about noon on 8 March.
