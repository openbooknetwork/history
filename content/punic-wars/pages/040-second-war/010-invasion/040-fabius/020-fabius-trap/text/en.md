Of eight roads out of the Ager Falernus, only three are open to an army north of the Volturnus, whose bridges the Romans hold. Fabius reinforces the garrisons of Casilinum and Cales, posts Minucius to the north to watch the Via Latina and the Via Appia, and camps with the main army near Mount Massicus. A detachment of 4,000 men holds the pass beside Mount Callicula in the east, by which Hannibal had come in.

Fabius waits for the Carthaginians to run short of supplies. When Lucius Hostilius Mancinus, sent out with 400 horsemen to scout, engages the Numidians, Carthalo’s cavalry kills him and destroys his whole force. In autumn Hannibal moves east towards the Callicula pass. Fabius camps with his army on a hill above it, and Minucius joins him there.
