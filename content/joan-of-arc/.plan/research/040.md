## 040 the-loire-and-reims

Coverage: primary and scholarly; no line rests on wikipedia. The Loire week rests on four French chronicles read in Quicherat 4 (Cagny, who rode in Alençon's household; Jean Chartier; the Journal du siège; Gruel, Richemont's man), on Alençon's, Dunois's and Louis de Contes's depositions of 1456 (Murray), and for Patay on Wavrin, who rode under Fastolf. Monstrelet's chapter 61 is the same text as Wavrin's chapter 13 (Quicherat 4 heads it so), so the two are one account of Patay, and the Chronique de la Pucelle copies the Journal and Chartier: neither is counted as a second source to its original. The march to Reims has Cagny, the Journal, Monstrelet, Dunois, the letter of the three Angevin gentlemen written at Reims on 17 July (Quicherat 5) and Joan's own answers at her trial; the towns after the coronation have Cagny, the Journal, Monstrelet, Ramsay, Beaucourt 2 and the three lives. The scholarly works read for the section are Ramsay 1 ch. 27, Oman book 14 ch. 2, Lowell ch. 9-12, Lang ch. 14-16, France ch. 15-18 and vol. 2 ch. 1, and Beaucourt 2 ch. 5; Lowell, Lang and France build on Quicherat, so they count as one reading where they cite the same chronicle. Oman's paragraph on the Loire has Talbot come from Paris with the relief and dates Patay 17 June, against every chronicle read; Beaucourt 2 at p. 33 (book 2 ch. 1) prints Vailly and Soissons as 22 and 23 'août', a slip for July that his p. 234-235 (ch. 5) and every other source contradict. No author is used here with two works but Quicherat as editor of volumes 4 and 5, and volume 5 does not withdraw volume 4; it corrects the printed Monstrelet's date for the coronation (040.63). The assault on Jargeau is told in Cagny, Chartier, the Journal and Alençon from the French side only, and none of them places the English within the town, beyond Alençon's word that at the end they fell back to the bridges, where the French pursued them. The numbers on both sides at Jargeau, Beaugency and Patay differ from writer to writer and are given each with its author. Cagny's figures are read from Roman numerals that the OCR garbles: his 'de vu à vmc' at Jargeau is read as 'vii to viii c', 700 to 800, and his 'n à m mille' as 'ii to iii thousand'.
