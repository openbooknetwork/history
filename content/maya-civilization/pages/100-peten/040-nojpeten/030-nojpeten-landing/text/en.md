Under the musket fire and the guns of the galeota the Itza in the canoes and on the shore soon flee. The Spanish land on the island after a short, bloody fight in which many Itza warriors die and the Spanish suffer only minor losses. The bombardment kills many people on the island.

The surviving Itza abandon Nojpetén and swim across to the mainland, and many drown in the water. The defenders who reach the shore disappear into the forest, and the Spanish occupy an empty town. Kan Ek' and the Itza nobles scatter to settlements across Petén.

Ursúa plants his standard on the highest point of the island and names it Nuestra Señora de los Remedios y San Pablo, Laguna del Itza. The Spanish later count 21 temples in the town. The largest, which they call the castillo, has nine stepped levels on a base 16.5 m square and faces north.
