The Ilkhan Ghazan has Sultan Kayqubad III executed in 1302 and restores Mesud II in 1303. Mesud dies around 1308, the last Seljuk sultan of Rûm. Ilkhanid governors still command in central Anatolia, while the beys of the western marches rule their lands as independent princes and strike coins in their own names.

Germiyan at Kütahya is the strongest of the beyliks. Along the Aegean, from north to south, Karesi at Balıkesir, Saruhan, Aydın and Menteşe at Milas take the Byzantine coastlands in these years. Inland lie Hamid at Eğirdir with its branch Teke at Antalya, Eşref at Beyşehir and Karaman at Larende. Candar holds the hills of Paphlagonia and takes Kastamonu from the Chobanids in 1309.

Osman’s land runs from Söğüt and Eskişehir to Bilecik, İnegöl and Yenişehir, at the edge of the plain of Bithynia. It is one of the smaller beyliks, and the one that borders the Byzantine cities of Bithynia. Ottoman tradition takes 1299 as the founding year of the state.
