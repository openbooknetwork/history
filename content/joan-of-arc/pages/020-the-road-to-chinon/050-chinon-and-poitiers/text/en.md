Joan reaches [Chinon](@chinon-audience) in late February or early March 1429 and lodges at an inn. She said she went up to the castle after dinner, but the envoys of the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois) told him that Charles VII made her wait two days. Louis de Bourbon, Count of Vendôme, brings her in. She picks out the king among his courtiers by the counsel of her voice, she said, and tells him she has come to make war on the English. Until he is anointed at Reims she calls him the Dauphin.

The king had a sign before he believed her, she told her judges, and at first she would say no more. Jean Pasquerel, an Augustinian friar, had it from her that she called him the true heir of France. What the sign was is not known.

At [Poitiers](@poitiers-examination) churchmen and doctors, with Regnault de Chartres, Archbishop of Reims and Chancellor of France, examine her; no record survives. They find nothing in her against the faith, and judge that in the kingdom's need the king may use her.\* Yolande of Aragon, the king's mother-in-law, questions her too.\*\* Her letter bidding the English before Orléans go home is dated at Poitiers on 22 March 1429.

From about mid-April, at [Tours](@tours-armour), she is given armour and a household: a squire, Jean d'Aulon, two pages, and Pasquerel as confessor. A painter of Tours is paid 25 livres tournois for her banners.\*\*\* At her word an armourer of Tours fetches a rusty sword marked with five crosses from behind the altar at [Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois](@fierbois-sword). She said her voices told her of it; Lowell thinks she probably saw or heard of the old arms kept there.

After about ten days at Tours she goes up the Loire to [Blois](@blois-gathering) with the Archbishop of Reims and Raoul de Gaucourt, the Grand Steward. There the convoy for Orléans gathers under the Gascon captain La Hire and the marshals Gilles de Rais and Jean de Brosse, lord of Boussac.

The map shows her road to Poitiers, back to Chinon (Lowell), and by Tours to Blois.

### Notes

\* Gaucourt said she was examined for three weeks at Chinon and Poitiers. Lowell has her sent to Poitiers about 10 March and dates the doctors' opinion about 6 April; Lang dates it about 17–20 April.

\*\* Lowell puts this at Poitiers, where Yolande questioned her herself and reported to the council. Lang puts it later, at Tours, among a jury of ladies who vouched that she was a virgin.

\*\*\* The account of the war treasurer, in Murray's note, pays for a great standard and a small one. Joan described a field sown with lilies, the world and two angels. In Pasquerel's memory it showed Christ judging from the clouds, blessing a fleur-de-lys that an angel held. Thurston gives a picture of God with kneeling angels. Lowell thinks she probably had more than one banner.
