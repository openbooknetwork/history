# Issus and the coast

## Header
- Years: summer 333 BC – early 331 BC (from the Cilician Gates to the return from Siwa to Memphis)
- Opens: Alexander is at Gordium in spring 333 BC. He holds Hellespontine Phrygia, Lydia, Ionia, Caria under
  Ada, Lycia, Pamphylia and Greater Phrygia (`macedon-333`). Pisidia stays independent (brief). Darius III
  holds the rest of the Persian Empire. The Persian fleet, now under Pharnabazus and Autophradates, still holds the Aegean. The reader has
  been told about the Achaemenid Empire and its satrapies, the crossing, the Granicus, Sardis, Miletus,
  the siege of Halicarnassus, Memnon's war at sea and the knot at Gordium (030). The citadels of Halicarnassus
  are no thread of this section: no marker or clause here takes them up.
- Tells:
  - summer 333 BC: Alexander passes the Cilician Gates, saves Tarsus from Arsames and falls dangerously ill
    there (040.1)
  - spring to summer 333 BC: Darius musters the royal army at Babylon, crosses the Euphrates at Thapsacus and
    camps at Sochi in the Amik plain by September (040.2, 040.21)
  - autumn 333 BC: Alexander goes south to Myriandrus. Darius crosses the Amanus by the Amanian Gates, comes
    down behind him at Issus and kills the sick left there. Alexander turns back (040.3, 040.21)
  - November 333 BC: the battle of Issus on the Pinarus. Darius flees (040.4–040.9)
  - November 333 BC: Darius's camp is taken with his mother, wife, son and two daughters. Parmenion seizes the
    treasure at Damascus. Darius flees to Thapsacus (040.10, 040.22)
  - winter 333–332 BC: Aradus, Marathus, Byblus and Sidon submit (040.23). Darius's first letter reaches
    Alexander at Marathus and is refused (040.11)
  - February 332 BC: Tyre refuses Alexander a sacrifice in the island temple, and he lays siege (040.12)
  - winter to summer 332 BC: the siege of Tyre. The mole is built, the fleets join at Sidon, and the city is
    stormed in July or August (040.13–040.15)
  - autumn 332 BC: the siege of Gaza, with the engines shipped from Tyre. Batis is put to death (040.16,
    040.17, 040.24)
  - winter 332–331 BC: Egypt submits without a fight at Pelusium and Memphis (040.18, 040.25)
  - early 331 BC: Alexandria is founded and Alexander travels to the oracle of Ammon at Siwa, in Arrian's order
    (040.19, 040.20, 040.26)
- Introduces: people: Sisygambis (Darius's mother), Darius's wife, Mazaces, Batis, Nabarzanes (commander of the Persian right-wing cavalry at Issus, Curtius 3.9.1; names.md
  lists him first in 050, and his first mention moves to 040), Craterus,
  Aristander, Menon, son of Cerdimmas, and Ptolemy, son of Lagus (as officer and as Arrian's source). Writers: Callisthenes, Curtius, Polybius and
  Cleitarchus. Gods: Melqart (named once, as Heracles of Tyre) and Ammon. Places: Cappadocia, Cilicia, the
  Cilician Gates, Tarsus, the Amanus, the Amanian Gates, Myriandrus, Issus, the Pinarus, Sochi, Thapsacus,
  Babylon and Babylonia, the Euphrates, the Halys, Damascus, Phoenicia, Marathus, Aradus, Byblus, Sidon, the Antilibanus, Tyre, Old
  Tyre, Cyprus, Gaza, Egypt, Pelusium, Memphis, Lake Mareotis, Pharos, Alexandria, Paraetonium and Siwa.
  Peoples: Tyrians, Phoenicians, Cypriots and Egyptians.
- Battles: Issus (card and 3 phase pages, drawn as a reconstruction since the Pinarus is not located); Tyre
  (card, no phase pages); Gaza (card, no phase pages)
- Hands on: in spring 331 BC Alexander holds Asia Minor, Cilicia, Syria, Phoenicia, Cyprus and Egypt as far as
  Paraetonium and Siwa (`macedon-331`). The Phoenician and Cypriot ships have left the Persian fleet
  (Bosworth). Darius is beyond the Euphrates, gathering a second army (Bosworth ch. 2.VII). Left open:
  Darius's greatest offer and its refusal (050), Agis III's war (050), and the interior of Cappadocia, still
  troubled after Issus (Bosworth).

## Pages

**010-cilician-gates.** Summer 333 BC. Alexander marches from Gordium by Ancyra and Cappadocia to the Cilician
Gates. Their guards flee, and his advance column reaches Tarsus before Arsames can burn it. There he falls
dangerously ill. Meanwhile Darius has mustered the levies of the central satrapies at Babylon and marches north
by Thapsacus to Sochi. Why: Alexander wanted the Mediterranean coast before the Persian army came (Bosworth).
Darius had chosen to risk everything on one battle (Bosworth). Map change: Cilicia and Cappadocia turn
Macedonian, and the two armies' first routes converge on the Amanus. This page carries the chapter's one clause
that its borders are approximate.
Rests on: 040.1, 040.2 (`single`, Bosworth's reading: the text names Bosworth for the absence of the north-east
cavalry), 040.21 (`single`, Bosworth: the text tells the march by Thapsacus to Sochi as his account and says
the site of Sochi is not known).

**020-issus / 010-issus.** Autumn 333 BC. Alexander moves east along the coast by Mallus and Issus, leaves his
sick at Issus and crosses the Pillar of Jonah to Myriandrus. Darius leaves Sochi, crosses the Amanus by the
Amanian Gates, comes down behind him at Issus, kills the sick, and advances to the Pinarus. Alexander sends a
ship to check the news, then marches his army back north by night. Why: in the Amik plain Darius ran short of
supply and would not wait. His courtiers told him Alexander was shrinking from battle (Arrian 2.6), and he
moved round the Amanus by the north instead of by the road Alexander was watching (Bosworth). Map change: the
Persian army now stands between Alexander and Cilicia. Battle card `issus`.
Rests on: 040.3, 040.4, 040.21 (`single`, Bosworth: the text tells the road through the Bahçe Pass and
Toprakkale as his reconstruction), 040.5 (`open`: the text says the Pinarus has not been found and that its site may
never be known; Bosworth suggests the Kuru Çay), 040.8 (`disputed`: the text says the ancient totals differ
and names Arrian's 600,000, Diodorus's 500,000 and Callisthenes's 30,000 cavalry and 30,000 mercenaries. It
adds that Bosworth holds the totals beyond any educated guess, and the 30,000 mercenaries perhaps near the
truth), 040.27 (`single`: the card and the text give Callisthenes's 40,000 foot and 4,500 horse at the crossing,
with 5,000 foot and 800 horse joining before Cilicia, as his figure, reported through Polybius. They say it is
higher than the counts of the crossing army given by Arrian (not much above 30,000 foot and more than 5,000
horse) and Diodorus (32,000 foot and 4,500 horse), which 030 gave (030.3)).

**020-issus / 020-issus-lines** (phase). November 333 BC. The two lines face each other across the Pinarus.
Darius draws up the Greek mercenaries opposite the phalanx with Cardaces on each side. His cavalry is massed by
the sea against Parmenion, and troops on the hill on his left curve round behind Alexander's right. Alexander
holds the right with the Companions and sends the Thessalians behind the phalanx to the left, and the men on
the hill are driven up it. Why: the narrow plain between sea and hills. Map change: the field, drawn as a
reconstruction.
Rests on: 040.6, 040.5 (`open`: as above).

**020-issus / 030-issus-river** (phase). November 333 BC. Alexander charges through the river on the right and
breaks the Persian left. The phalanx is disordered in the steep stream bed, and the Greek mercenaries strike
the gap. On the left the Persian cavalry crosses against the Thessalians by the sea. Why: the charge outran the
phalanx (Arrian; Bosworth). Map change: the Macedonian line is broken and slanted across the river.
Rests on: 040.7.

**020-issus / 040-issus-rout** (phase). November 333 BC. Darius flees when his left breaks. The right-wing
battalions wheel onto the mercenaries' flank, and the Persian cavalry breaks once his flight is known. The
pursuit lasts until nightfall. Why: the king's flight. Map change: the Persian army in flight through the
ravines.
Rests on: 040.7, 040.9 (`disputed` for the Macedonian losses. The text gives Diodorus's 300 foot and 150 horse
and Curtius's 302 foot missing, 150 horse killed and about 4,500 wounded, each with its author. It gives the
Persian dead by author: Arrian about 100,000 with more than 10,000 horse among them, Diodorus more than 100,000
foot and at least 10,000 horse, Curtius 100,000 foot and 10,000 horse; Bosworth calls the figures propaganda).

**030-darius-letter.** Winter 333–332 BC. Darius's camp by the Pinarus is taken with his mother, his wife, his
infant son and two daughters, and Parmenion seizes the treasure and the Persian women at Damascus. Darius flees
over the Amanus to Thapsacus on the Euphrates. Alexander goes south into Phoenicia: Aradus and Marathus
surrender, and at Marathus Darius's letter asks for his family and offers friendship. Alexander answers that
Darius must come to him as the lord of Asia. Byblus capitulates and Sidon invites him in. Why: Darius sought his family
and a peace; Alexander claimed the kingship of Asia (Arrian 2.14; Bosworth). Map change: Syria and Phoenicia
as far as Sidon turn Macedonian (`macedon-333c`, new), and the Persian king's route leads away east.
Rests on: 040.10, 040.22, 040.23 (`single`, Arrian: told plainly as his account), 040.11 (`disputed`. The text tells the letter at Marathus as Arrian gives it: the family,
friendship and no land, a version Bosworth follows. It says that Diodorus has this first letter offer Asia
west of the Halys, and that Bosworth places a Halys offer during the siege of Tyre. It says that the number
and order of the letters are disputed, and that the greatest offer comes in 331 BC, told in its place in 050).

**040-siege-of-tyre.** Winter to summer 332 BC. Tyre offers submission but will not let Alexander sacrifice to
Melqart (Heracles) in the island temple. He lays siege and builds a mole from Old Tyre across the four-stade
strait. Tyrian raids and a fire ship burn the towers at its head. In early summer about 80 Phoenician and about
120 Cypriot ships join him at Sidon and pen the Tyrian fleet in its two harbours. Rams on ships breach the
south wall, and the hypaspists and Coenus's battalion storm it while the fleets force the harbours, in July or
August. Why: Tyre's answer amounted to a declaration of neutrality, which Alexander would not allow (Bosworth).
In Arrian's speech, Persian sea power and the road to Egypt made Tyre necessary. Map change: the fleets of
Phoenicia and Cyprus change sides and converge on Tyre. The coast to opposite Tyre and Cyprus turn Macedonian
(`macedon-332a`), and the island city stays out. Battle card `tyre`.
Rests on: 040.12, 040.13, 040.14 (`single`: the ship numbers name Arrian), 040.15 (`disputed`. Arrian gives
about 8,000 Tyrians killed, about 30,000 sold and about 400 Macedonians dead. Diodorus gives more than 7,000
killed and 2,000 crucified. Bosworth calls Arrian's 400 the merest propaganda. Each figure carries its
author).

**050-gaza.** Autumn 332 BC. Alexander marches down the coast from Tyre to Gaza, where Batis holds the city on
its mound with Arab mercenaries. A sortie nearly drives the Macedonians off their siege mound, and Alexander is
wounded in the shoulder. The engines from Tyre come by sea, the walls are mined and battered, and after two
months the fourth assault takes the city. Curtius tells that Batis was dragged to his death behind a chariot.
Why: Gaza was the last resistance on the road to Egypt (Arrian 2.25; Bosworth). Map change: Tyre and the coast
south to Gaza turn Macedonian (`macedon-332`). Battle card `gaza`.
Rests on: 040.16, 040.24, 040.17 (`single`. The text tells it as Curtius's story, probably from Cleitarchus, says that
Arrian is silent, and gives Bosworth's view that it cannot be proved false).

**060-egypt-submits.** Winter 332–331 BC. In seven days Alexander marches from Gaza to Pelusium, where his fleet
is already in harbour and the Egyptians welcome him. The fleet sails up the Nile. The army goes through the
desert to Heliopolis and across the river to Memphis, where the satrap Mazaces surrenders the city and its
treasury and Alexander sacrifices to Apis. Why: Mazaces had no Persian force left after Issus (Arrian 3.1).
Map change: Egypt turns Macedonian (`macedon-331`).
Rests on: 040.18, 040.25.

**070-alexandria-and-siwa.** Early 331 BC. In Arrian's order, Alexander sails down the Nile to Canopus and round
Lake Mareotis, and chooses and marks the site of Alexandria between the lake and the sea, opposite Pharos. He
then goes along the coast to Paraetonium and across the desert to the oracle of Ammon at Siwa, where he
consults the god alone, and returns to Memphis. Why: a site he foresaw would prosper (Arrian 3.1). At Siwa he
wanted to consult the god and to follow Perseus and Heracles (Arrian 3.3; Bosworth). Map change: the desert
road to Siwa enters the story, and the first Alexandria is marked on the map.
Rests on: 040.19 (`disputed` for the order. The page follows Arrian, who founds the city before Siwa. The text
says that Diodorus puts the founding after Siwa, and that Bosworth has the site chosen before and the
boundaries marked after the return), 040.20 (`open` for the questions. The text says that what Alexander asked
is not known: Ptolemy and Aristobulus say only that he was satisfied, and in Plutarch Alexander writes to his
mother of secret answers), 040.25 (the voyage from Memphis down to Canopus), 040.26 (`disputed`: the text gives
Aristobulus's return by the same coastal road and Ptolemy's road straight to Memphis, each with its author).

## Battles

**Issus** (`issus`), November 333 BC (the Athenian month Maimacterion, Arrian 2.11).
- Sides: `macedon` against `persia`. On the phase pages the Thessalian cavalry of the left takes `thessaly`.
- Commanders: Alexander, on the right with the Companions; Parmenion, over the whole left, with Craterus
  over its infantry (Arrian 2.8). Darius III, in the centre. Nabarzanes, over the
  cavalry of the Persian right by the sea (Curtius 3.9.1; Bosworth's Commentary on 2.8.5). Other Persian
  commanders: `unknown` in the digest (Arrian 2.11 names Arsames and other dead nobles).
- Strengths: Macedonian army: 40,000 foot and 4,500 horse at the crossing to Asia, joined by 5,000 foot and 800
  horse before Cilicia (Callisthenes, in Polybius 12.19; `single`, 040.27). The card names it as Callisthenes's
  figure and says it is higher than Arrian's and Diodorus's counts of the army that crossed, given in 030
  (030.3: Arrian, not much above 30,000 foot and more than 5,000 horse; Diodorus, 32,000 foot and 4,500 horse).
  Polybius reckons about 42,000 foot and 5,000 horse present. Arrian gives no figure for Issus. Persian army
  (`disputed`, 040.8): about 600,000 fighting men (Arrian); 600,000 (Plutarch); 400,000 foot and 100,000 horse
  (Diodorus); 30,000 cavalry and 30,000 mercenaries (Callisthenes, in Polybius). Bosworth holds the 30,000
  Greek mercenaries perhaps not much exaggerated and the totals beyond any educated guess.
- Casualties (040.9): Persian (`disputed`): about 100,000 with more than 10,000 cavalry among them (Arrian);
  more than 100,000 foot and at least 10,000 horse (Diodorus); 100,000 foot and 10,000 horse (Curtius). Macedonian (`disputed`): 300 foot and 150 horse (Diodorus); 302 foot missing, 150 horse killed and
  about 4,500 wounded (Curtius); Ptolemy, son of Seleucus, and about 120 Macedonians of note killed in the centre
  (Arrian). Bosworth calls the figures propaganda.
- Outcome: Alexander wins. Darius flees, and his camp is taken with his family (040.10).
- Phase pages: 3. Positions of both sides: Arrian 2.8–11 (`archive.org-download-theanabasisofale46976gut-46976-0.txt.txt`),
  Bosworth, Conquest and Empire ch. 2 "VI The campaign of Issus", Bosworth's Commentary under the running heads
  "THE BATTLE OF ISSUS" (on Arrian 2.6–2.11), Polybius 12.17–22 (Callisthenes's account criticised), and
  Curtius 3.8–11. The ground is a reconstruction: the Pinarus is not located (040.5). The plan places it at the
  Kuru Çay, Bosworth's suggestion, 3 km north of the Payas and about 15 km north of the Pillar of Jonah, with a
  plain of under 14 stades from sea to foothills (Callisthenes). The `battle-coordinator` plans the phases.

**Tyre** (`tyre`), winter to July or August 332 BC (Hecatombaion, Arrian 2.24), seven months (Diodorus 17.46).
- Sides: `macedon` against `tyre`.
- Commanders: Alexander. Tyrian commander: `unknown`. The king, Azemilcus, was with the Persian fleet when the
  siege began and was taken in the temple at the end (Arrian 2.15, 2.24). He is not in names.md.
- Strengths: Macedonian army `unknown`. Ships: about 80 Phoenician and about 120 Cypriot (Arrian, 040.14),
  with 10 from Rhodes, 3 from Soli and Mallus, 10 from Lycia and one from Macedon (Arrian 2.20). Tyrian:
  `unknown`.
- Casualties (`disputed`, 040.15): about 8,000 Tyrians killed, about 30,000 sold and about 400 Macedonians dead
  in the whole siege (Arrian); more than 7,000 killed and 2,000 crucified (Diodorus). Bosworth calls the 400 the
  merest propaganda.
- Outcome: the city is stormed through the breach in the south wall while the fleets force the harbours.
- Card only (decisions, step 4).

**Gaza** (`gaza`), autumn 332 BC, about two months (Diodorus 17.48).
- Sides: `macedon` against `persia` (the garrison).
- Commanders: Alexander; Batis.
- Strengths: `unknown`. Batis had hired Arab mercenaries (Arrian 2.25), but no source gives a figure.
- Casualties: `unknown`. Arrian says the defenders all died fighting and the women and children were sold
  (2.27). Alexander was wounded in the shoulder (040.16).
- Outcome: the city is stormed at the fourth assault and refounded as a fortress.
- Card only.

## Choices
- The brief's first Tells line (Cilicia; Darius cuts the line) is split into two pages, and its "Gaza taken;
  Egypt submits" line is split too. Page 010 shows the strategic convergence at the scale of Anatolia and
  Mesopotamia, and 020-issus/010-issus the manoeuvre round the Amanus at the scale of Cilicia. Gaza (a siege
  on the Levant coast) and Egypt (Pelusium to Memphis) are different ground with different zones. The count
  stays at the brief's 7.
- Memphis with Egypt's submission (060) and Alexandria with Siwa (070) are both in Egypt within months, but they
  get a page each. 070 brings a new region onto the map, the Libyan desert road and the oracle, and the first
  Alexandria, and the brief keeps Arrian's order there as its own line.
- The Issus card sits on the campaign page (020-issus/010-issus), before the phase pages, as the Punic Wars
  pages do. The aftermath (the family, Damascus and Darius's flight) goes on 030, so no page tells the outcome
  before the phases show it.
- `macedon-333c` (Syria and Phoenicia to Sidon, winter 333–332 BC) was proposed by this plan so that Sidon,
  the fleets' base during the siege, does not show as Persian. It is now in the Map part, and so is
  `macedon-333b`'s date of summer 333 BC.
- On page 030 the Halys offer is told inside the dispute of 040.11 (Diodorus puts it in the first letter,
  Bosworth during Tyre). Arrian's Euphrates offer is left to 050.
- The Pinarus marker and the phase pages stand at the Kuru Çay, Bosworth's suggestion. The text says that the
  river is not located (040.5). The Payas and the Deli Çay are the other candidates (Bosworth's Commentary).
- On 070 the return from Siwa follows Aristobulus's road, which Bosworth prefers. Ptolemy's direct road is
  told, not drawn.
- These are left off the maps as detours that change nothing for the reader: the Soli and Rough Cilicia
  excursion, the Antilibanus raid during the siege, the 8,000 mercenaries who sailed from Tripolis, Darius's
  baggage train sent to Damascus before the battle, and the Cyrenaean embassy. A writer may give them a clause
  from the Articles.
- The battle id `gaza` is free: 090's Gaza of 312 BC has no card (decisions, step 4).
