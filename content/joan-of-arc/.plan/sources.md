## Against the brief

- **010, a battle the brief lacks.** Baugé, 22 March 1421, where the Dauphin's Scots and French beat the English and
  killed Clarence, is told by Wylie 3 ch. 69, Beaucourt 1 ch. 6-7, Ramsay 1 and Battle_of_Baugé.wiki; Cravant, 1423,
  by Oman, Ramsay 1 and Battle_of_Cravant.wiki. "Verneuil breaks the Dauphin's field army" reads without the
  victory that army had won.
- **020, Vaucouleurs.** The sources tell of a first visit in May 1428, when Baudricourt sent her home, and a second
  from January 1429, when he held back and then sent her. "Refuses her twice and yields" is one reading of that.
  Lowell (2171-2245, 2857-2897) sets her leaving on 12 or 13 February and her coming to Chinon on 23 February,
  and says others date them 23 February and 6 March. The eleven days are agreed by her two escorts (Murray 11624,
  11927), and the Herrings, 12 February, fall in that month in either reckoning.
- **030, an event the brief lacks.** The English abandoned the bastille of Saint-Jean-le-Blanc on 6 May, before the
  Augustins were taken (the Journal du siège, Lowell ch. 8). Five events are told on six pages.
- **040, the march.** Auxerre did not open its gates: it gave provisions under a truce and the army passed by.
  Troyes yielded after an assault was readied, and Châlons and Reims opened (France ch. 17-18, Lowell ch. 11).
- **050, the capture.** Monstrelet and Chastellain have her pulled from her horse by an archer, and she surrendered
  to the Bastard of Wandonne, a man of John of Luxembourg. "Her capture by a Burgundian archer" holds if the
  surrender is told too.
- **060, the sale.** Ramsay 1 (ch. 29) says she was sold by the Duke of Burgundy; Lowell (ch. 17) tells the bargain
  with John of Luxembourg, whose prisoner she was. The price is ten thousand pounds (livres) in Lowell and
  ten thousand francs in Ramsay.
- **060, too few map changes for six pages.** Four of the six pages are in Rouen: the court, the questioning,
  Saint-Ouen and the Old Market. The map moves only on the first two, from Beaulieu and Beaurevoir by Arras and
  Le Crotoy to Rouen, and then changes only within one city.
- **070, the retrial.** Murray (8665, 9572) and Beaucourt 5 (book 6 ch. 1) begin the retrial with Charles VII's
  enquiry of 1450, after Rouen was retaken, and the legate d'Estouteville's of 1452. Her mother's petition and the
  papal rescript came in 1455. The brief begins with the petition.
- **070, few map changes.** The retrial and the closing page add no new territory. The four war pages carry the
  section's map changes.
- **The scholars the brief names.** No work of Régine Pernoud, Kelly DeVries, Juliet Barker or Helen Castor could
  be read. Pernoud's The Retrial of Joan of Arc (1955) and DeVries's Joan of Arc: A Military Leader (1999) are on
  archive.org for lending only, and the others are in copyright. Every scholarly work read but Renoux's is from
  1881 to 1929.
