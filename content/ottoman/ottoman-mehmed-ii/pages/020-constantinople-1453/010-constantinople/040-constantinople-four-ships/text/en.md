On 20 April 1453 three Genoese ships sent by the pope, and an imperial transport under Phlatanelas carrying grain from Sicily, come up the Sea of Marmara under a south wind. Mehmed orders Baltaoğlu to stop them, and the Ottoman galleys and boats row out from the [Double Columns](@double-columns). The four ships sail through them until the wind drops close to the city, and about 150 Ottoman boats close round them. Runciman puts the calm off the Acropolis point, Turkish histories off Yeşilköy (then San Stefano).

The Ottoman crews try to board and burn the ships. The ships stand higher than the galleys, and their crews throw stones, arrows and fire down on the boats. [Mehmed](@constantinople-four-ships-mehmed) watches from the shore, rides his horse into the sea and shouts orders at [Baltaoğlu](@constantinople-four-ships-baltaoglu).

@image mehmed-ii-nakkas-osman-6e5ce4

In the evening the wind rises again, and the four ships break out of the ring. After dark the chain is opened, and they enter the Horn with an escort of Venetian galleys. The next day Mehmed has Baltaoğlu, who is wounded in the eye, beaten, takes his property and gives the command of the fleet to Hamza Bey.
