The relief army attacks before noon, up the gentle slope from the brook valley in the north and north-east and from Chabařovice in the north-west. The Hussites open fire as soon as the attackers come within range. On the north side the knights overturn a row of wagons, cut the chains and get into the ring, where the second line of wagons stops them.

Between the two lines the knights are shot at from three sides. The Old Czech Annals write that the Hussites fire from the wagons, the howitzers and the field guns until they cut lanes through the attackers. The Thuringian chronicler Johannes Rothe writes that the Hussites pull the nobles from their horses with long hooks, and that the dust is so thick that one man can hardly see another.
