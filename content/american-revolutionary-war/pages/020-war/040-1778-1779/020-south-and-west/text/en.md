The British now turn to the south, where they expect strong Loyalist support. A force from New York takes Savannah on 29 December 1778, and in July 1779 royal government returns to Georgia under Governor James Wright. In October 1779 a French and American assault on Savannah fails with heavy loss, and d’Estaing sails away.

In the west, George Rogers Clark leads about 170 men, nearly half of them French volunteers from Kaskaskia, across flooded country in February 1779 and takes Vincennes and its British commander. On the Hudson, Anthony Wayne storms Stony Point with the bayonet on 16 July.
