# Research for Joan of Arc

## 010 france-divided

Coverage: primary (the trial record of 1431 in Barrett; the depositions at Domrémy of 1455 in Murray; Monstrelet's Chronicles in Johnes's translation; the Journal d'un bourgeois de Paris; the chaplain's Gesta Henrici Quinti; Perceval de Cagny; the Chronique de la Pucelle; Boulainvilliers's letter in Quicherat 5) and scholarly (Ramsay's Lancaster and York, Wylie's Reign of Henry the Fifth vol. 2, Wylie and Waugh's vol. 3, Oman's Art of War, Beaucourt's Histoire de Charles VII vols. 1-2, Lowell's Joan of Arc, Lang's Maid of France, Thurston's signed article), with Wikipedia for the modern estimates at Agincourt, Cravant and Verneuil. All the scholarly works read are of 1881 to 1929: the modern counts of Agincourt (Curry, Barker, Sumption, Rogers) and the strengths at Cravant are known here only through Wikipedia. No modern study of the civil war of 1407-1419 was read. Wylie and Waugh's ch. 69 gives the ground of Baugé; Oman, Ramsay and Wylie give the ground and the movements of both sides at Agincourt; Ramsay, Oman, the Bourgeois and the Chronique de la Pucelle give both lines at Verneuil; Ramsay gives both banks of the Yonne at Cravant.
