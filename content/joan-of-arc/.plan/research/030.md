# Research for Joan of Arc

## 030 orleans

Coverage: primary, scholarly and wikipedia. The ten days are told day by day in the Journal du siège (Quicherat 4, 6236-6830, which dates 7 May as the 6th and 8 May as the 7th, as Quicherat's notes correct) and the Chronique de la Pucelle (Cousinot's edition, 11880-12530, where the paragraphs marked as the Geste share their text with the Journal: the standard touching the boulevard is one account in both). Three other primary accounts written in 1429 or soon after stand apart from them: Guillaume Girault's notarial note (Quicherat 4, 11277-11329), Charles VII's letter to Narbonne of 10 May 1429 (Quicherat 5, 5466-5600) and the Orléans chronicle of the feast of 8 May (Quicherat 5, 13880-14150). The witnesses of 1456 (Dunois, Luillier, de Contes, Beaucroix, Pasquerel, d'Aulon, in Murray), Monstrelet from the Burgundian side, the Bourgeois of Paris and Joan's own answers at her trial give the rest. Of the scholarly works, Ramsay, Lowell, Lang and France follow these with notes, Beaucourt and Oman in a page each. No source read gives a plan of the bastilles, or the places of the garrisons within the forts: their names, their side of the river and the order of the attacks are in the chronicles, and only some distances are given (Saint-Loup from the city, 030.5; the western bastilles from each other, France alone, 030.79), so a drawing of them is a reconstruction. Lowell's map of Orléans and its vicinity is not in the text read. The name of the island used for the crossing of 6 May differs from source to source (030.51), and only Anatole France names the gate by which Joan entered on 29 April (030.25).
