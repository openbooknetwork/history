The day after the battle of Olintepeque the army reaches Tzakahá, now Salcajá, which submits without a fight. The chaplains Juan Godínez and Juan Díaz say mass there under a makeshift roof, and the site is chosen for the first church in Guatemala, dedicated to Concepción La Conquistadora.

In March 1524 Alvarado reaches Q'umarkaj, which his Nahuatl-speaking allies call Tecpan Utatlan. The city stands on a plateau surrounded by ravines more than 100 m deep. Alvarado fears a trap and camps on the plain outside, since his horsemen could not manoeuvre in the narrow streets. He invites the king Oxib-Keh and the king elect Beleheb-Tzy to his camp and seizes them. The K'iche' warriors attack his allies and kill a Spanish soldier, and Alvarado has the two lords burnt alive and then burns the city.

The Mam king Kayb'il B'alam is received with great honour at Q'umarkaj during Alvarado’s stay, and by Lovell’s account it is he who suggests the plan to trap the Spanish in the city. From Q'umarkaj Alvarado sends to the Kaqchikel at Iximche for help against the remaining K'iche'. He writes that they send 4,000 warriors, while the Kaqchikel annals give 400. His third letter to Cortés, written at Q'umarkaj, is dated 11 April 1524.
