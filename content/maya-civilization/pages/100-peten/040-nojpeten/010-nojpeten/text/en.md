By December 1696 the road from Campeche reaches the shore of Lake Petén Itzá, unfinished and in places hardly passable. The Itza leaders are divided: Kan Ek' has offered submission, while the Kowoj and the Chak'an Itza of the north shore oppose the Spanish. In late December the Chak'an Itza raid the Kejache mission town of Pak'ek'em and burn its church, and the garrison at Chuntuki falls back. Ursúa sends soldiers and workmen down the road with orders to build a galeota, an oared warship, and leaves Campeche himself on 23 January 1697.

He reaches the western shore of the lake on 26 February with about 235 soldiers and 120 native labourers and fortifies a camp at Ch'ich' with heavy guns. In 12 days his men assemble the galeota there: a keel of 14.4 m, 12 oars a side, room for 114 men and at least five guns. From 28 February Itza parties shoot arrows towards the camp, while others mingle with the soldiers and receive trinkets.

On 10 March AjChan and Chamach Xulu, ruler of the Yalain, come to Ch'ich'. A canoe under a white flag then brings the Itza high priest, who offers a peaceful surrender, and Ursúa invites Kan Ek' to his camp in three days. On the appointed day Kan Ek' does not come. Instead warriors gather on the shore and in canoes on the lake.
