Early in June 1429 Charles VII's council gives the army to the Duke of Alençon, with orders to take the English places on the Loire. Joan goes with him, and so do the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois) and other captains. The army gathers at [Selles](@selles-rendezvous) on the Cher. On the evening of Monday 6 June a young lord, Guy de Laval, sees Joan ride out, armed all in white but for her head, with many men, and writes of it two days later.

She is back in [Orléans](@orleans-june-1429) on 9 June. On Saturday 11 June the army marches out for [Jargeau](@jargeau-stormed), twelve miles up the river. William de la Pole, Earl of Suffolk, holds it for the English. That afternoon the common people rush the ditches on their own and are thrown back. In the night the guns are set up.

Suffolk asks for a fortnight's delay, to yield if no help comes. The captains refuse; Joan would let the garrison go in their doublets with their lives. On Sunday 12 June the town is stormed after three or four hours. Joan is on a ladder with her standard when a stone strikes her head. She falls, gets up and urges the men on.

Suffolk yields to a squire, Guillaume Regnault, after knighting him. His brother Alexander de la Pole is killed, and another brother is taken with him. The town and its church are plundered. Prisoners are killed on the road to Orléans in a quarrel among the French.
