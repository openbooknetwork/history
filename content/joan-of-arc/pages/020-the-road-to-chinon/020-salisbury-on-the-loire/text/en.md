In July 1428 Thomas Montagu, Earl of Salisbury, Henry VI's lieutenant in France, comes over from England with fresh troops.\* Ramsay counts 2,700 men from England, joined by men of Paris, Chartres and Normandy; Lowell gives four or five thousand by August, about half of them brought from England.

[Janville](@janville) is stormed on 29 August (Ramsay). [Meung](@meung-1428) surrenders, the bridge and castle of [Beaugency](@beaugency-1428) fall on 25 September, and on 5 October Sir John de la Pole takes [Jargeau](@jargeau-1428), while [Sully](@sully-1428), further up the river, surrenders too. Of the four bridges on the Loire between Sully and Blois, the English now hold all but the one at Orléans.

On 12 October Salisbury camps by the Portereau, the suburb across the river, and lays siege to [Orléans](@orleans-besieged). On 24 October the English take [the Tourelles](@tourelles-1428), the fort at the bridge's southern end, and the bridge behind it is broken: by the English, says the Journal of the Siege, a French chronicle of the siege, or by the French, says Lowell. Looking at the city from a window of the Tourelles, Salisbury is struck by a cannon shot, and he dies at Meung.\*\* The Earl of Suffolk takes command. Inside, the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois) holds the city for the king. Through the winter the English ring it with bastilles, leaving the north-east open.

On 12 February 1429 Sir John Fastolf is bringing stores from Paris to the siege, with herrings for the fasting season, when the French meet him at [Rouvray](@rouvray), two leagues from Janville. He makes a park of his carts and stakes.\*\*\* The French had agreed to fight mounted, but the Scots of Sir John Stewart of Darnley dismount and attack. The English sally out and break them, and the Count of Clermont's horsemen ride away. Darnley is among the dead. The fight is remembered as the Battle of the Herrings.

### Notes

\* Lowell has Salisbury land at Calais about 1 July. Ramsay has him pass his muster at Sandwich on 19 July and sail at once.

\*\* The Journal has him struck on the evening of 24 October and dead on the night of the 27th. Ramsay has him struck on 27 October and dead on 3 November. Monstrelet has him struck on the third day after he came and live eight days.

\*\*\* The Journal gives the park one long, narrow entry. Monstrelet gives it two openings held by archers, with the men-at-arms behind them. Oman puts the archers on the carts and spearmen in the gaps.
