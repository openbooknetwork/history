After his defeat at Issus in November 333 BC, Darius III flees through the night. In [Darius's camp](@darius-camp-333) behind the Pinarus, taken in the rout, the Macedonians find his mother, Sisygambis, his wife, his young son and his two daughters. Alexander lets the women keep their royal rank and their servants. Darius had sent most of his treasure and the wives of his nobles to [Damascus](@damascus-333), and Parmenion, Alexander's senior general, seizes them there. Darius gathers about 4,000 men who escaped the battle and hurries over the mountains to [Thapsacus](@thapsacus-333b), to put the Euphrates between himself and Alexander. The routes on the map are approximate.

Alexander makes Menon, son of Cerdimmas, satrap of Coele-Syria and goes south into Phoenicia. Straton, son of the king of Aradus, meets him on the road and surrenders the island city of Aradus and [Marathus](@marathus-333) on the mainland opposite. At Marathus envoys bring a letter from Darius. In Arrian's version, which the historian A. B. Bosworth follows, Darius asks for his family and offers friendship and alliance, but no land.\* Alexander answers that Darius must come to him as to the lord of all Asia, or else stand and fight for the kingdom. A greater offer follows, and a later chapter tells it.

@image amrit-temple-c79ff2

From Marathus Alexander moves down the coast. [Byblus](@byblus-333) surrenders on terms, and the people of [Sidon](@sidon-333), who hate Persian rule, invite him in. Northern Syria and the coast as far as Sidon are now his.

### Notes

\* How many letters Darius sent, and in what order, is disputed. Diodorus has this first letter offer Alexander all of Asia west of the river Halys. Bosworth writes that Darius apparently offered the land to the Halys later, during the siege of Tyre. Arrian puts Darius's greatest offer, of all the land west of the Euphrates, during that siege too, but Bosworth dates it to summer 331 BC, shortly before the battle of Gaugamela.
