# Greece secured

## Header
- Years: autumn 336 BC (Alexander's accession) to the end of September 335 BC (Thebes razed)
- Opens: Alexander, about twenty, has just been acclaimed king at Aegae after Philip's murder (010.20, 010.22).
  Macedon holds Philip's kingdom with Thrace to the Haemus (macedon-340) and Thessaly through Philip's archonship
  (thessaly-352). The mainland Greeks are bound to Macedon in the League of Corinth, which elected Philip hegemon
  and voted war on Persia (010.18), with Sparta outside it. Macedonian garrisons hold the Cadmeia of Thebes. An
  advance force under Parmenion and Attalus has been in Asia Minor since spring 336 (010.19). Epirus is allied by
  Cleopatra's marriage (010.20). The reader has been told how Philip built the army, how Chaeronea broke Athens and
  Thebes, and that the motive for the murder is disputed (010.21).
- Tells:
  - 336–335 BC: Alexander has his rivals killed: Amyntas, son of Perdiccas, and Attalus, whom Hecataeus murders in
    Asia (020.1)
  - autumn and winter 336–335 BC: Alexander marches into Greece by Tempe and Mount Ossa, Thessaly and Thermopylae
    to Corinth, and the Greeks give him Philip's command of the war against Persia. Sparta refuses. Plutarch's later
    date for the Corinth vote goes in a note (020.2, 020.20, 020.21)
  - spring 335 BC: from Amphipolis over the Nessus to Mount Haemus, where the free Thracians roll waggons down on
    the phalanx, at a pass that is disputed (020.3, 020.19)
  - spring 335 BC: the Triballi are beaten at the Lyginus and take refuge on Peuce. The warships from Byzantium
    fail to land there. Alexander crosses the Danube by night, scatters the Getae and razes their town, and Syrmus
    submits (020.15, 020.16, 020.17, 020.4, 020.5)
  - summer 335 BC: Cleitus, son of Bardylis, revolts and Glaucias of the Taulantians joins him. Alexander turns west
    (020.6)
  - summer 335 BC: Pelium: hemmed in, the phalanx drills in sight of the enemy and breaks out over the river. A few
    days later Alexander routs the Illyrians by night. Cleitus burns Pelium and flees. The site and the interval
    go in notes (020.7, 020.18)
  - late summer 335 BC: Thebes revolts on a report that Alexander is dead and besieges the garrison in the Cadmeia
    (020.8)
  - September 335 BC: Alexander comes from Illyria to Pelinna in seven days and into Boeotia six days later (020.9)
  - late September 335 BC: Thebes is stormed (020.10, 020.11, 020.12)
  - autumn 335 BC: the allies vote to raze Thebes and sell its people, and to rebuild Orchomenus and Plataea. It
    is said that Alexander himself spared the house and descendants of Pindar (Arrian 1.9). Southern Greece is quiet and Alexander goes home to prepare the invasion (020.13,
    020.14)
- Introduces: people: Amyntas, son of Perdiccas; Hecataeus; Cleitus, son of Bardylis; Glaucias; Syrmus;
  Perdiccas; Coenus (at Pelium, Arrian 1.6, if the writer names him); Pindar. States and peoples: Sparta, the
  Thracians (the free Thracians of the Haemus), the Triballi, the Getae, the Taulantians, the Agrianians. Places:
  the Peloponnese, the Nessus, Mount Haemus, the Lyginus, Peuce, the Danube, the Getic town, Pelium,
  Eordaea, Elimiotis, Pelinna, Thermopylae, Onchestus, Boeotia, the Cadmeia, Orchomenus, Plataea
- Battles: Thebes (card, no phase pages). Pelium, the Haemus, the Lyginus and the Danube crossing are told without
  cards
- Hands on: by autumn 335 BC Alexander is back in Macedon preparing to invade Asia. Macedon holds what Philip held
  (macedon-335, on the outline of macedon-340), and the Thracians, Triballi, Getae and Illyrians around it have been beaten. Thebes is gone and a
  Macedonian garrison holds the Cadmeia. Alexander is hegemon of the League of Corinth, Sparta is still outside, and
  Athens has been spared. The advance force in Asia is under Parmenion alone. Left unsettled: the war on Persia has
  been voted but not begun, Sparta's refusal (taken up in 050), and who will hold Europe while Alexander is away
  (Antipater, 030.4).

## Pages

**010-league-renewed**, 336–335 BC. Philip's death leaves rivals at home and unrest in Greece. Alexander has
Amyntas, son of Perdiccas, killed, and sends Hecataeus to Asia, where he kills Attalus. Alexander then marches
south. The Thessalians block the vale of Tempe, and he cuts steps up Mount Ossa to get behind them. The Thessalian
League, the Amphictyons at Thermopylae and the congress at Corinth acknowledge him in turn. The Greeks give him
the command against Persia that they had given Philip, and only Sparta refuses. Why it happened: the new king could not leave Macedon or fight in
the north while there were rivals at home (Bosworth), and the Greeks had to be held to the League before he could
leave. Map change: Alexander's first march, from Aegae to Corinth, through a Greece still bound to the League,
with Sparta outside and the Persian Empire across the Aegean.
Rests on: 020.1, 020.2, 020.20 (the sequence of Thessaly, Thermopylae and Corinth is agreed; Tempe and Ossa are
single, told as Bosworth's account), 020.21 (disputed: the body tells the vote here, after Arrian and Diodorus,
with a note marker, and a note under `### Notes` gives Plutarch's account, which tells it after the fall of
Thebes); opening context from 010.19 (the advance
force in Asia) and 010.22 (the acclamation).

**020-the-danube**, spring 335 BC. Alexander marches from Amphipolis, the Macedonian city on the Strymon
that 010 introduced, over the Nessus and through Rhodope to
Mount Haemus. There the free Thracians hold the pass and roll waggons down on the phalanx, and he forces the pass.
The body does not name the pass and carries a note marker. The Triballi circle behind him and make a stand in a wooded glen by the Lyginus. His archers
and slingers draw them out, and his cavalry and phalanx break them. Arrian gives 3,000 Triballi killed, and,
from Ptolemy, 11 Macedonian horsemen and about 40 foot. Syrmus and the Triballi take refuge on the Danube island
of Peuce, and warships from Byzantium fail to force a landing there. With ships, log canoes and stuffed hides he crosses the Danube by night,
scatters the Getae (Arrian: about 4,000 horse and more than 10,000 foot), razes their town and comes back across
the river. Syrmus and the peoples by the Danube send envoys and submit. Why it happened: the peoples on Macedon's northern frontier were planning to revolt, and Alexander would
not leave them unsubdued behind him while he campaigned in Asia (Arrian 1.1). Map change: the Macedonian army
crosses the Haemus into the lands of the Triballi and the Danube into those of the Getae, the first time it goes
beyond the river. A fleet from Byzantium meets it at the Danube.
Rests on: 020.3 (single, Arrian), 020.19, 020.15, 020.16, 020.17, 020.4, 020.5 (single, figure named to Arrian).
020.19 is disputed: the body names no pass and carries a note marker, and a note gives Bosworth's Trojan pass
and Hammond's Shipka. 020.16 is single, with the figures named to Arrian and the Macedonian losses to Ptolemy.

**030-pelium**, summer 335 BC. On the march home, in the land of the Agrianians and Paeonians, news comes that
Cleitus, son of Bardylis (the Illyrian king 010 introduced), has revolted and that Glaucias, king of the Taulantians, has joined him. Alexander goes
west along the Erigon to Pelium, which Cleitus holds, and shuts him in. Glaucias then arrives with a large army and
holds the heights around the plain. Alexander is hemmed in on a narrow road. He drills the phalanx 120 deep in
sight of the enemy, who give up the ridges, and takes the army over the river. A few days later he crosses back by
night and routs the Illyrians in their camp. Cleitus burns Pelium and flees to Glaucias. Why it happened: the
Illyrians were Macedon's old enemies on its western border, and an invasion through Pelium threatened all of
western Macedon (Bosworth). Map change: the army marches from the upper Strymon to the western border, and the
Taulantians come from the west to Pelium and go back there in flight. The site of Pelium is disputed, and a note says
so.
Rests on: 020.6 (single, Arrian), 020.7, 020.18. 020.7 is disputed for the interval: the body says "a few days later"
with a note marker, and a note gives two days in Bosworth and three in Arrian. 020.18 is disputed: the body names
Pelium with a note marker, and a note gives Hammond's hill-fort of Goricë, on the Illyrian side of the Wolf's
Pass, and Bosworth's site near Florina in Lyncestis. No casualty
figure: none is in the digest.

**040-fall-of-thebes**, September 335 BC. Theban exiles return and the Thebans revolt on the report that
Alexander has been killed in Illyria. They besiege the garrison in the Cadmeia behind a double stockade. Alexander
goes from Pelium through Eordaea and Elimiotis to Pelinna in seven days and into Boeotia six days later, before
the Thebans know he is south of Thermopylae. The city is stormed. The allies who fought with him vote to raze it
and sell its people, except the priests and the guest-friends of the Macedonians, and to rebuild Orchomenus and
Plataea. It is said that Alexander himself spared the house and descendants of Pindar (Arrian 1.9). This is his
own act, not part of the allies' vote. Southern Greece falls quiet, and Alexander returns to Macedon to prepare the
invasion. Why it happened: the false report of his death, and the Theban exiles' call to freedom (Arrian 1.7).
Alexander made the razing a warning to the Greeks he would leave behind (Diodorus 17.9, Plutarch 11). Map change:
Thebes in revolt is cut out of the League's Greece. Alexander's march runs from the Illyrian border to Thebes, and
the city is razed.
Rests on: 020.8, 020.9 (single, Arrian), 020.10 (disputed: the body tells a wording true for both accounts,
that the Macedonians break into the city and the garrison joins them, with a note marker. A note gives both. Arrian
has the Thebans break before the phalanx and the Macedonians follow them through the gates. Diodorus has them hold
until Perdiccas gets in by a postern gate left unguarded. Bosworth calls the battle difficult to reconstruct), 020.11 (the Theban
dead and captives as Diodorus and Plutarch give them, the Macedonian dead as Diodorus's), 020.12 (single, named
to Diodorus), 020.13, 020.14 (single: told as Bosworth's judgement).

## Battles

**thebes-335**, the storming of Thebes, late September 335 BC. Card on 040-fall-of-thebes.
- Sides: `macedon` (Alexander's army, with the allied Phocians, Plataeans, Thespians and Orchomenians, Diodorus
  17.13 and Arrian 1.8) against `thebes`.
- Commanders: Alexander. Perdiccas, whose battalion opens the assault on the stockade in Arrian 1.8 (from
  Ptolemy), and whom Diodorus 17.12 sends through the postern gate. Theban side: `unknown`. No source read names a
  commander in the field. Plutarch 11.7 names Phoenix and Prothytes as the leaders whose surrender Alexander
  demanded. Wikipedia's infobox makes them commanders killed in action, and no source read says so.
- Strengths: Macedonians more than 30,000 foot and at least 3,000 horse, Diodorus 17.9.3 (020.12). Thebans
  `unknown`: Diodorus and Plutarch say only that they were many times outnumbered. Wikipedia's 15,000 rests on no
  source read.
- Casualties: Thebans more than 6,000 killed and more than 30,000 captured, Diodorus 17.14.1 and Plutarch 11.12
  (020.11). Macedonians more than 500 killed, Diodorus 17.14.1 (020.11). Arrian 1.8 gives only about 70 archers
  killed with Eurybotas in one episode, not a total.
- Outcome: Macedonian victory. The city is taken, and the allies vote to raze it and sell its people (020.13).
- Phase pages: none. The brief gives Thebes a card. The course is disputed (020.10), Bosworth calls it difficult
  to reconstruct, and Arrian's and Diodorus's movements cannot both be drawn.

Pelium (020.7) is told on 030-pelium without a card, by the cloud ruling in decisions.md. Arrian gives Alexander's
movements but not the Illyrian positions, and the site is disputed (020.18). The Haemus pass (020.3, 020.19), the
Triballian battle at the Lyginus (020.15, 020.16) and the Danube crossing (020.4) are told in the text of
020-the-danube without cards. 020.15 notes that Arrian gives the positions of both sides at the Lyginus. Even so,
the brief lists no battle in 020 but Thebes, outline.md keeps cards and phases for battles that decided the war,
and the site cannot be located (names.md: "unidentified river"). So the Lyginus stays without a card. See the
report.

## Contract

Section folder: `content/alexander-the-great/pages/020-greece-secured/`, `group.yaml` title "Greece secured".
`when` is left out of every page, because BC years cannot go in it (story.yaml). Tally: none, on every page
(brief). Every zone is a `reconstruction` (brief, "Zones"). The section's text says once, on 020-the-danube where
the sites are least certain, that the borders and sites are approximate.

Marker ids carry the year of the page. Where the 010 pages already define a marker for the same place and the
same event (Corinth, Thermopylae, Byzantium), the cartographer takes 010's id in its place and changes this plan's
id wherever a page lists it. Aegae is the exception. `aegae-336` is 010's marker for Philip's murder, and one id
has one note in `shared/markers.yaml`. So Alexander's acclamation there takes its own id, `aegae-336b`.

## Choices
- Four pages, as the brief expects: one for each region the army works in (Greece in 336, the Danube, Illyria,
  Thebes). The revolt of Thebes, the march south, the storming and the razing share one page: they happen in one
  place within weeks.
- The revolt of Thebes (020.8) is told on 040-fall-of-thebes, not on 030-pelium, though it breaks out while
  Alexander is in Illyria. It is the cause of the march that page draws.
- The renewal of Philip's command (020.2) is placed in winter 336–335 BC, before the Danube campaign, after Arrian
  and Diodorus. Plutarch 14 puts the assembly at the Isthmus after the fall of Thebes (020.21, disputed). The page
  follows the two writers who agree. Plutarch's order goes in a note (rules.md).
- 010-league-renewed and 020-the-danube keep 010's `illyrians-359` (editor's cloud ruling). The switch to
  `illyrians-335` comes on 030-pelium, where Cleitus's revolt is told, and 040-fall-of-thebes keeps it.
- The routes follow Bosworth where Bosworth and Hammond differ: the Trojan pass (020.19), the Lyginus near Pleven
  (names.md says the same), and the inland route to Boeotia. Bosworth is the digest's main scholarly source for
  020. The Pelium marker is the exception (020.18): it follows names.md, which gives Hammond's south-east Albania.
  Each of these points is disputed, and a note on its page gives both accounts (rules.md).
- The Lyginus (020.15) gets no card, though Arrian gives both sides' positions. The brief lists only Thebes for
  020, outline.md keeps cards for the battles of a campaign that the brief names, and the site is unidentified.
- No route is drawn for the night crossing of the Danube, the river crossings at Pelium or Alexander's return from
  Thebes to Macedon. The first two are a few kilometres long at a scale of hundreds. The return has no place in the
  digest beyond "returns to Macedon".
- The fleet from Byzantium is drawn only to the Danube's mouth, because a route keeps to sea or to land.
- Page dates: "Summer 335 BC" for Pelium is inferred, not given. The Danube is spring (020.3), Thebes fell late in
  September (020.10), and the march from Pelium to Boeotia took about thirteen days (020.9).
