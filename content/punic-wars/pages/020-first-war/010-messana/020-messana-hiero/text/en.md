Appius Claudius crosses the strait with two legions, about 9,000 men. Despite the strength of the Carthaginian navy the crossing meets little opposition, and Polybius writes that it is made at night. The Romans enter Messana, which the Mamertines hold for them.

The besiegers lie in two separate camps. Polybius places the Carthaginian army north of the city and its fleet at Cape Pelorus, and Hiero’s army on a hill outside the city. The Romans march out and attack the Syracusans first. They defeat them, and Hiero withdraws his army to Syracuse.
