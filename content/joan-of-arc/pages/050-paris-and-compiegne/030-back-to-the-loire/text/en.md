The attack on Paris has failed, and in September 1429 the king turns south. Before she leaves [Saint-Denis](@saint-denis-abbey), north of Paris, Joan offers her whole armour before the image of Our Lady and the relics of the abbey. At her trial in 1431 she said it was a suit of white armour, given with a sword she had won before Paris. Wounded soldiers made such offerings, she said, and she gave hers to the abbey's saint because his name was the war cry of France. She also told her judges that her voice had bidden her stay at Saint-Denis, and that the lords took her away against her will.

Charles VII leaves governors in the towns he holds north of the Seine, among them Guillaume de Flavy at Compiègne and Ambroise de Loré at Lagny. On Tuesday 13 September\* the king leaves Saint-Denis after dinner. He crosses the Marne at [Lagny](@lagny-sep-1429) and goes by [Provins](@provins-sep-1429) to [Bray](@bray-1429) on the Seine, which shut him out in August and now opens to him. [Sens](@sens-sep-1429) will not let him in, so he fords the Yonne a little below it and rides on by [Courtenay](@courtenay-1429) and [Montargis](@montargis-1429) to [Gien](@gien-disbanded) on the Loire, where he dines on Wednesday 21 September.

At Gien the army that took the king to his crowning at Reims is disbanded. The Duke of Alençon goes home to his wife and his viscounty of Beaumont, and each of the other captains to his own frontier. Alençon asks to take Joan with him toward the borders of Brittany and Maine.\*\* Regnault de Chartres, Archbishop of Reims, Georges de La Trémoille, the king's favourite, and Raoul de Gaucourt, the three who govern the king, refuse. They would never let the two be together, writes Perceval de Cagny, a chronicler of Alençon's household. Alençon does not serve with her again.

The king's road on the map is approximate.

### Notes

\* The Journal of the Siege, a chronicle of Orléans, has Monday 12 September.

\*\* So Cagny. Ramsay names Maine alone.
