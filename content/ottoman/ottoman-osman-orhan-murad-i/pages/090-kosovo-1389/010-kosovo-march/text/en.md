After the defeats at Pločnik and Bileća, Murad demands that Prince Lazar accept Ottoman overlordship and pay tribute. Lazar refuses. He has made peace with King Sigismund of Hungary, and he wins the support of his son-in-law Vuk Branković, who holds Kosovo, and of King Tvrtko I of Bosnia. Tvrtko calls his army back from Dalmatia and sends it under Vlatko Vuković.

Murad brings troops from Anatolia, among them auxiliaries from the beylik of Candar at Kastamonu, and gathers his army at [Philippopolis](@philippopolis-1363). He marches to Ihtiman and then west by [Velbužd](@velbuzd) and [Kratovo](@kratovo), through the lands of his vassal Konstantin Dragaš. This road is longer than the one by [Sofia](@sofia-1382) and the Nišava valley to [Niš](@nis-1386), but it leads to Kosovo, a crossroads from which he can strike the lands of either Lazar or Vuk Branković. After a halt at Kratovo the army moves on by Kumanovo, Preševo and Gjilan and reaches [Priština](@pristina) on 14 June 1389.

Lazar gathers his troops near Niš. When he learns that Murad has reached Velbužd, he marches by Prokuplje to Kosovo field, where he can block every road the Ottomans might take. Vuk Branković’s troops and the Bosnian contingent join him there.
