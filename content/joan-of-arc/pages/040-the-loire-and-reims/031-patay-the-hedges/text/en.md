On 18 June, with Beaugency lost, the English march north from Meung for Janville: a vanguard under a knight with a white standard, then the train of artillery, victuals and merchants, then the main body under [Sir John Fastolf](@patay-the-hedges-fastolf), [Lord Talbot](@patay-the-hedges-talbot) and Sir Thomas Rempston, and last a rearguard of English. Within a league of [Patay](@patay-the-hedges-patay) they halt, warned by the runners of their rearguard that a great force is coming after them.

The captains send the vanguard and the train ahead to take post along the hedges near the village. The main body marches on to a narrow way between two strong hedges, where the French must pass. There Talbot dismounts with 500 picked archers to hold the passage until the main body and the rearguard have joined, and then he means to fall back on the main body along the hedges. The main body goes on north, and Fastolf, who has not dismounted, rides with it. Where the rearguard is after the halt is not known.

The French ride north after them, sixty or eighty of their best mounted men ahead as scouts. Behind them comes the vanguard under [La Hire](@patay-the-hedges-la-hire) and [Poton de Xaintrailles](@patay-the-hedges-la-hire), sent ahead to catch and hold the English so that the main body need not leave its pace. The main body follows under [the Duke of Alençon](@patay-the-hedges-alencon) and [the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois)](@patay-the-hedges-alencon). Joan rides with it, kept out of the van, to her anger. The French cannot yet see the English, and how far apart they are is not known.
