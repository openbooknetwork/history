In September 1348 the plague reaches the shores of Lake Geneva, in the lands of Amadeus VI, count of Savoy. The count’s officers arrest the Jews of the towns on the lake and hold them in the castle of [Chillon](@killing-chillon). Under torture they confess to poisoning wells. On 15 September Balavigny, a Jewish surgeon of Thonon, confesses that a rabbi from Toledo sent him poison and that he put it into a well at Thonon. On 10 October Agimet, a Jew of Geneva, confesses that Peyret, a rabbi of Chambéry, gave him poison for the wells of Venice, where he went to buy silks, and of southern Italy and Toulouse. Written copies of the confessions go from Chillon to other towns, among them [Strasbourg](@strasbourg-1348).

They pass from town to town, and the killings move north with them. Jacob Twinger von Königshofen, the chronicler of Strasbourg, wrote that Jews were tortured at [Bern](@bern-1348) and at Zofingen until they confessed to the same crime, and that Bern then wrote of it to Strasbourg, Freiburg and [Basel](@basel-1348). One account puts the first killings in a German-speaking town at Solothurn in November 1348. Over the winter towns across the Empire act on these letters, and a later chapter follows the burnings of 1349.

In France lepers and Jews were accused of poisoning wells in 1321, and the killings have a longer past. In 1096 crusaders killed the Jews of the Rhineland towns, among them [Mainz](@killing-mainz-1096). In 1298 bands led by a man called Rintfleisch killed the Jews of more than a hundred places in Franconia, starting at [Röttingen](@rottingen-1298). By one account bands called the Armleder killed Jews in Franconia and Alsace from 1336 to 1338. In the autumn of 1338 the Jews of [Deggendorf](@deggendorf-1338) in Bavaria were burned, and later chronicles give the cause as a charge of desecrating the host. In 1348 Jews have already been killed at [Toulon](@burning-toulon) on 13 April and at [Barcelona](@burning-barcelona) on 17 May.

Pope Clement VI answers the accusation in two bulls, in July and September 1348. The first page of the next chapter gives them.
