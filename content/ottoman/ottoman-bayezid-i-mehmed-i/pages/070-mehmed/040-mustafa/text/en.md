In 1416 a man who says he is Mustafa, the son of Bayezid lost at Ankara, appears in Wallachia, and Mircea backs him. Ottoman chronicles call him Düzme Mustafa, the false Mustafa, while many modern historians accept that he was Bayezid’s son. Cüneyd, whom Mehmed had made governor of [Nicopolis](@nicopolis-1416) on the Danube in 1414, joins him. By Loredan’s letter of June 1416, the Ottoman fleet at [Gallipoli](@gallipoli) was to sail to the Danube and stop Mustafa from crossing into Rumelia.

Mustafa and Cüneyd cross the Danube and gather followers in Macedonia and Thessaly, among them men of Sheikh Bedreddin. Mehmed brings his army from [Bursa](@bursa) by Gallipoli and beats them near Thessalonica. The two take refuge in the city, which Mehmed besieges, and its governor Demetrios Laskaris Leontares refuses to hand them over without the emperor’s consent.

Mehmed lifts the siege when Manuel II swears to keep both men in custody as long as Mehmed lives, and Mehmed agrees to pay 300,000 akçe a year for their keep. Mustafa and Cüneyd are held on the island of [Lemnos](@lemnos-1416). Mehmed then sends raiders into Wallachia for the help Mircea had given Mustafa.
