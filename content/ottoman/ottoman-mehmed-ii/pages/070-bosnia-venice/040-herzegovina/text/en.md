After the conquest of Bosnia in 1463 Mahmud Pasha marches south into the lands of Herceg Stjepan Vukčić Kosača, whose title of herceg, or duke, gives Herzegovina its name. He lays siege to [Blagaj](@blagaj-1463), the herceg’s seat on the Buna near the Neretva, and Stjepan agrees to a truce that gives the sultan all his lands north of Blagaj. Stjepan’s eldest son Vladislav has been at war with his father since spring 1462 and has asked Mehmed for help.

In December 1463 Stjepan accepts Matthias Corvinus as his overlord. In 1465 the Ottomans take most of the eastern part of his lands and make them the province of Hersek within the sanjak of Bosnia. Stjepan has handed the coast at [Makarska](@makarska-1465) to Venice, and in 1465 Matthias makes him take Hungarian garrisons into his forts on the Neretva.

@image radimlja-stecak-0527b4

Blagaj falls to the Ottomans in 1466. On 21 May 1466 Stjepan, old and ill, dictates his will at [Herceg Novi](@novi-1466) (then Novi), his winter seat on the coast, and blames Vladislav for bringing “the great Turk” into Bosnia, and he dies the next day. His sons divide what is left: Vladislav rules in the west, and Vlatko, the new herceg, holds the south-east around Novi.
