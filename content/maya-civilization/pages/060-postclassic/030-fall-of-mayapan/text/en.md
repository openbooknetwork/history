In 1441, the year the chronicles give, Ah Xiu Xupan of the Xiu lineage leads a revolt against the Cocom rulers of [Mayapan](@mayapan-1441), helped by mercenaries from elsewhere in Mexico. The rebels kill every Cocom in the city. One member of the family, away trading in Honduras, survives. Mayapan is sacked and burned. Excavators have found burnt roof beams in the ceremonial centre and a mass grave in the main plaza. No altars or burial cists are built after about 1461.

The great families leave to set up their own seats. The Xiu rule from [Maní](@mani-1441), the surviving Cocom from Tibolon in the province of [Sotuta](@sotuta-1441), and the Chel from Tecoh. Yucatán breaks into independent provinces, each called a kuchkabal in Yucatec Maya. Estimates of their number range from sixteen to twenty-four. Some have a single ruler, others are loose leagues of towns, and their borders shift with alliances and wars.

@image fall-of-mayapan-mani-map-d71cfa

Maní and Sotuta remain enemies. Cupul, with its capital at [Saci](@saci-1450), and Chikinchel fight for the salt beds of the north coast. Ecab holds the northeast, Cochuah the interior around Tihosuco, and Uaymil and [Chetumal](@chetumal-1450) the Caribbean coast in the southeast. Ah Canul, [Campeche](@campeche-1450) and Champotón lie along the Gulf coast. The wars between the provinces last until shortly before 1511, yet the first Spanish explorers still find wealthy coastal towns and busy markets.
