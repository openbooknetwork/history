In 879 a Viking army that has wintered at Fulham on the Thames crosses to the continent and makes its camp at Ghent. West Francia is weak after the deaths of Charles the Bald in 877 and Louis the Stammerer in 879, and Louis’s young sons Louis III and Carloman II are crowned together in September 879. The army takes Kortrijk in November 880, raids Arras and Cambrai in December and sacks Amiens and Corbie in 881.

On 3 August 881 Louis III and Carloman defeat the Danes at Saucourt in the Vimeu, west of Abbeville. The Annals of Fulda count 9,000 Danes killed, and the Old High German Ludwigslied celebrates the victory. The army turns east to the Meuse and makes a fortified camp at Asselt. Over the winter it burns Liège, Maastricht and Tongeren, sets Charlemagne’s palace at Aachen on fire, plunders Cologne, Bonn and the abbey of Prüm and on 5 April 882 takes Trier.

In the summer of 882 the emperor Charles the Fat leads an army from Worms against the camp at Asselt. After a siege of twelve days he negotiates. The Danish leader Godfrid is baptised with Charles as his godfather, marries Gisela, a daughter of Lothair II, and receives Frisia to hold as Charles’s vassal, and another leader, Sigfrid, is paid to leave. Godfrid holds Frisia until he is killed at a meeting at Spijk near Lobith in 885.

In 883 the rest of the army returns west, defeats Carloman on the Somme and winters at Amiens. In February 884 Carloman offers it 12,000 pounds of silver to leave, and in October it moves to Boulogne, where it divides between England and Leuven. Carloman dies in December 884, and the West Frankish nobles invite Charles the Fat to be their king.
