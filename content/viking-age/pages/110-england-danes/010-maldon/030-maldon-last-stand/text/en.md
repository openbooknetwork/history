The Vikings come west over the water carrying their shields and form up on the mainland, and the two shield walls close. Byrhtnoth is wounded by a spear and kills his attacker, but he is struck down and dies urging his men on and commending his soul to God. Godric son of Odda mounts Byrhtnoth’s horse and flees to the wood with his brothers Godwine and Godwig.

Many of the English recognise the horse by its trappings, take the rider for Byrhtnoth and run, and the levy breaks. The household troops stay and fight on around their lord’s body. Ælfwine, Offa, Leofsunu, Dunnere and the old retainer Byrhtwold speak in turn and fall one after another. The surviving text ends as a second Godric, son of Æthelgar, goes into the Viking ranks and is killed.

The monks of Ely take Byrhtnoth’s headless body to their abbey and bury it there. Byrhtferth of Ramsey writes that the Vikings lose so many men that they can hardly man their ships. On the advice of Archbishop Sigeric of Canterbury, King Æthelred pays them 10,000 pounds to leave, the first tribute to Viking raiders in generations.
