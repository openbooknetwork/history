[The bastille of Saint-Loup](@saint-loup) stands on the north bank east of Orléans, above the landing-place on the road to Chécy, a mile and a half to a league from the city.

On Wednesday 4 May, the eve of the Ascension, a few hours after the army from Blois has come in, about 1,500 French go out against it at about noon. By one account Joan leads them out with the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois); by another she is asleep at her lodging when it begins, calls for her armour and rides to the fort with her standard. With her coming the attack grows stronger.

While its garrison defends it hard, English come out to help, from [the bastille of Paris](@saint-pouair-sortie) or from the other bastilles. The city's belfry rings twice in warning, the French draw up to face them, and the English turn back to their forts. Towards vespers Saint-Loup is taken by force, burned and pulled down. The assault lasts three hours, or more than four or five. Some English who hide in the church tower in priests' vestments are spared at Joan's word and brought into the city.

Saint-Loup is the first English fort to fall that May. Its fall clears the approaches east of the city, and the French can turn to the forts across the river.
