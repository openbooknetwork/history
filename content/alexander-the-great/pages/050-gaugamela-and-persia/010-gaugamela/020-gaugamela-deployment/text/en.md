On the day before the battle Alexander's army comes down from the hills of the Jabal Maqlub and halts in sight of the Persians, who stand about 30 stades away on the plain that Darius has levelled for his chariots.\* The Macedonians camp there, and at dawn on 1 October 331 BC they deploy. Alexander has 7,000 horsemen and about 40,000 foot soldiers, Arrian says; the Persian army is far larger.\*\*

Darius's own written order of battle was captured afterwards. [Darius III](@gaugamela-deploy-darius) stands in the centre with his Kinsmen, his guard of spearmen with golden apples on their spear butts, and Greek mercenaries on either side of him. [Bessus](@gaugamela-deploy-bessus) holds the left with the Bactrians and the peoples of the east, and [Mazaeus](@gaugamela-deploy-mazaeus) the right with the Syrians, Mesopotamians and Medes. In front stand advance guards of horse and 200 scythed chariots, 100 of them on the left. The Persian line reaches far beyond the Macedonian right.

[Alexander](@gaugamela-deploy-alexander) leads the Companion cavalry on the right, the phalanx stands in the centre, and [Parmenion](@gaugamela-deploy-parmenion) commands the left with the Thessalian horse under Philip, son of Menelaus. Behind the phalanx a second line of infantry is ready to face about if the army is surrounded, and on each flank troops stand thrown back at an angle, so that the whole formation is, in Bosworth's words, roughly rectangular. The baggage stays in the [camp](@gaugamela-deploy-camp) under a Thracian guard.

Alexander then leads his army obliquely to the right, towards the edge of the levelled ground. The Persian left keeps pace with him, and the Saka horse ride along his line and engage his leading troops. Darius fears that his chariots will be useless on rough ground.

The sources give neither the sizes of the units nor the distances between them.

### Notes

\* Bosworth places the field in the plain between the Bumelus and the Jabal Maqlub. Tarn, after the archaeologist Aurel Stein, puts it on the plain between the Tigris and the Khazir, near Keramlais.

\*\* Arrian gives Darius 40,000 horse, 1,000,000 foot, 200 scythed chariots and about 15 elephants. Diodorus gives 800,000 foot and at least 200,000 horse, and Curtius 45,000 horse and 200,000 foot. Bosworth holds that the Persian advantage cannot be quantified.
