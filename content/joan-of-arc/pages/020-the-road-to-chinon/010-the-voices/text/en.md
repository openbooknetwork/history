On 22 February 1431 Joan told her judges at Rouen that she was about thirteen when she first heard a voice from God. She was in her father's garden at [Domrémy](@domremy-voices) about midday in summer; the voice reached her from the side of the church, on her right, and a light nearly always came with it. The year was 1424 or 1425.\*

Later, she said, the voice told her to leave home and go into France, and that she would raise the siege of Orléans. If she went to Robert de Baudricourt, the squire in command at [Vaucouleurs](@vaucouleurs-1428), he would find men to take her. Her father knew nothing of it.

Vaucouleurs is a walled town on the Meuse north of Domrémy, some twelve miles off by Lang's count and thirteen by Lowell's. Lowell writes that by midsummer 1428 no other town of eastern France still stood for Charles VII, the uncrowned king who held the lands south of the Loire.

In May 1428 Joan comes to Baudricourt and is sent home in the company of Durand Laxart, her cousin's husband, whom she called uncle. So Bertrand de Poulengy, a squire who was with the captain, remembered it at the retrial.\*\* Laxart recalled the captain's advice, given more than once: slap the girl and bring her home to her father.

In July Antoine de Vergy, governor of Champagne for the English, reviews his troops at [Saint-Urbain](@vergy-review-1428) and marches on Vaucouleurs. Within days the force is gone and Baudricourt still holds the town; how he kept it is not known. For dread of the Burgundians, Joan said, she went to [Neufchâteau](@neufchateau), a walled town of the Duke of Lorraine a few miles south, and stayed about a fortnight with an innkeeper called La Rousse. Her family and the other villagers go too, with their cattle; Lang and Lowell date this flight to Vergy's raid.\*\*\*

The map shows Vaucouleurs hemmed in by English, Bar and Lorraine land, her road there, Vergy's march and the flight. In this chapter borders and roads are approximate.

### Notes

\* Thurston puts the first voice in the summer of 1425, when she was thirteen and a half. Lowell finds 1424 in one of her answers and 1425 in another, and thinks neither was meant to be exact. Lang gives 1425 in one place and in another says the summer of 1424 is "even more probably" the time.

\*\* Lang accepts Poulengy's date. Thurston puts the journey a month later. Lowell doubts that she went to Vaucouleurs in 1428 at all, since neither she, nor Laxart, nor Jean de Metz ever spoke of such a visit. Lang cannot tell whether the captain's advice to Laxart belongs to this visit or the next.

\*\*\* Murray's note says the flight has no certain date: some put it in 1428, others at the raids of September 1426. Lowell calls its link with Vergy's raid not certain but "altogether probable". Lang notes that others who testified later made her stay at Neufchâteau four or five days, not a fortnight. Lang cites a compact of 22 July by which the town was to capitulate, and Lowell guesses that Baudricourt promised to surrender if no relief came by a fixed day.
