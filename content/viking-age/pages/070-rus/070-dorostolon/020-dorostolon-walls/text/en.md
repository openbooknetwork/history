On 23 April the Rus' wait for the Byzantines on the plain before Dorostolon. Leo the Deacon writes that they stand in a close wall of shields reaching to their feet, with their spears thrust forward. Tzimiskes puts his heavy infantry in the centre and his armoured cavalry on both wings, with archers and slingers behind, shooting over the front line.

The fighting lasts all day, and Leo writes that the advantage changes sides twelve times. Towards evening the emperor sends in all his cavalry, and the Rus' break and flee into the town.

The next day the Byzantines build a camp on a low rise near the town, with a deep ditch and a rampart topped with spears hung with shields. On 25 April the fleet comes up the Danube with its fire-bearing warships and grain transports and closes the river. Sviatoslav has his boats drawn up on the bank under the walls, out of reach of the Greek fire.
