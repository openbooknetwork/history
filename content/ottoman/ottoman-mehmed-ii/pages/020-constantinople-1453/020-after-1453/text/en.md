The Genoese colony of Galata, across the Golden Horn, has stayed neutral through the siege, and after the fall of the city it surrenders. In the account of Doukas its people bring the keys to Mehmed. Chalkokondyles writes that the podestà hands them to Zaganos Pasha before the Ottoman fleet arrives. On 1 June 1453 Mehmed gives Galata a charter that lets its people keep their houses, their churches and their trade.

Mehmed has his grand vizier Çandarlı Halil Pasha, who opposed the siege from the start, arrested on 1 June and executed on 10 July 1453. Zaganos Pasha becomes grand vizier in his place. Loukas Notaras, the last chief minister of the empire, is taken prisoner and at first spared, but on 3 June he is executed with his son and his son-in-law. Mehmed makes [Constantinople](@istanbul-1453) his capital in place of [Edirne](@edirne).

The monk Gennadios Scholarios, one of the leaders of those who opposed the union of the churches, becomes patriarch of the Orthodox Church on 6 January 1454. The Gattilusi of [Mytilene](@mytilene-1453) keep Lesbos, Enez (Ainos) and their other islands under the sultan, and in April 1454 Venice makes peace with Mehmed and keeps its trade in the city. Of the Byzantine lands only the Morea remains, held by Constantine’s brothers Thomas and Demetrios, who pay tribute to the sultan.
