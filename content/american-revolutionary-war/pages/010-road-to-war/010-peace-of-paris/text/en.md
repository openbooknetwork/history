The Seven Years’ War ends with the Treaty of Paris on 10 February 1763. France cedes Canada and all its land east of the Mississippi to Britain, except New Orleans. In a secret treaty of November 1762 it had already given Louisiana west of the river, with New Orleans, to Spain. Spain cedes Florida to Britain. Britain’s national debt has nearly doubled during the war, and the government means the colonies to share the cost of their defence.

Pontiac’s War breaks out at Fort Detroit in May 1763. Eight British forts fall, and Detroit and Fort Pitt are besieged.

On 7 October the Royal Proclamation reserves the land west of the Appalachians for the native nations and forbids colonial settlement there. It also creates the provinces of Quebec, East Florida and West Florida. Virginia’s Ohio Company and frontier settlers oppose the line.
