With his centre close to breaking, Napoleon orders Murat to charge with the cavalry reserve, about 11,000 horsemen in 80 squadrons. Murat’s squadrons ride through the Russian infantry near Eylau and divide. Grouchy’s dragoons on the right scatter the Russian cavalry attacking Saint-Hilaire’s division, then wheel left with Murat against the Russian cavalry in the centre.

D’Hautpoul’s cuirassiers break through the Russian guns and both lines of infantry and are stopped only about 2,500 yards on, in front of the Russian reserves. A second wave, the Guard cavalry and Grouchy’s dragoons, rides through the Russians as they try to form again, and the horsemen turn and charge back before retiring under cover of the Guard. Murat loses 1,000 to 1,500 troopers, but the Russian centre is held long enough for Davout to deploy.
