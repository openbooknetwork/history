Later in 329 BC the column that Alexander has sent from [Alexandria Eschate](@alexandria-eschate-329b) nears [Maracanda](@maracanda-329b) (today Samarkand). Spitamenes, the Sogdian noble who leads the rising, lifts his siege of the citadel and draws back west along the Polytimetus, today the Zeravshan, towards the desert. There 600 nomad horsemen join him, Arrian says. The column follows on worn-out horses. Arrian names its leaders as Pharnuches, a Lycian interpreter, and the officers Andromachus, Menedemus and Caranus.

@image zeravshan-panjakent-fa51f3

At the edge of the desert the horse archers ride around the Macedonians and shoot them down, and the column is destroyed in the battle of the Polytimetus, at a site no one knows.\* Most of its men are killed.\*\* Bosworth notes that the blame was laid on the officers, perhaps to clear the king of sending too few men.

Alexander hurries about 290 km with his best troops to Maracanda, which Spitamenes is besieging again, and Spitamenes escapes into the desert. The king buries the dead and lays waste the Zeravshan valley, killing the people in its forts, because he holds that they helped Spitamenes. Then he goes south to winter at [Bactra](@bactra-329b) (today Balkh).

@image castaigne-punishment-bessus-e913ea

There, in Arrian's account, he brings Bessus, the satrap who had claimed Darius's throne, before a council, has his nose and ears cut off and sends him to Ecbatana to be put to death before the Medes and Persians. Arrian himself condemns the mutilation as a barbaric custom.\*\*\*

The map follows Spitamenes and the column down the valley, and Alexander to Maracanda and south to Bactra.

### Notes

\* Arrian gives two accounts. In Ptolemy's, the column forms a square and makes for a wooded glen by the river. Caranus takes the cavalry across without telling Andromachus, the infantry follow him in panic and without orders, and the men are shot down on an island in the stream. In Aristobulus's, the nomads spring an ambush from a park while Pharnuches is trying to hand the command to officers who refuse it. Curtius tells a third story, in which Menedemus commands and the survivors are besieged on a hill.

\*\* Arrian puts the column at 60 Companions, 800 mercenary horse and 1,500 mercenary foot, and says that no more than 40 horsemen and 300 foot escape. Curtius gives it 3,000 foot and 800 horse, of whom 2,000 foot and 300 horse fall.

\*\*\* Curtius has Alexander give Bessus, as soon as he is brought in, to Darius's brother Oxathres, to have his nose and ears cut off and be crucified, and later send him from Bactra to Ecbatana. Diodorus says that Alexander hands Bessus to Darius's brother and kin, who cut him to pieces.
