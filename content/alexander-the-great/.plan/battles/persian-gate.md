# Battle of the Persian Gate, January 330 BC

Section 050 (gaugamela-and-persia). Card on `persian-gate`; three phase pages, `persian-gate-assault`,
`persian-gate-night-march`, `persian-gate-dawn`. Sides: `macedon` (Alexander's Macedonian column) and `persia`
(Ariobarzanes's army), the story's families.

The site is disputed (050.19). By the cloud ruling and the section's plan ("Choices"), the phases are drawn at
Bosworth's site after Stein: the gorge above the open ground of Mullah Susan, the Persian camp at its head below
the watershed towards the plain of 'Aliabad, the turning path over the Bolsoru pass. The whole drawing is a
`reconstruction` on that ground. Below, `sourced` means that Bosworth (for the ground) or an ancient author (for
the troops) states the place or the movement in the frame of that site. It does not mean the site itself is
certain. The texts name Speck's and Lendering's Tang-e Meyran and Hemati's Kopan–Yasuj site (050.19).

## Sources

## Ground

**Site.** The direct road from the plain of Fahlian up the Fahlian river and the Tang-i Layleh to Mullah Susan,
where it forks: "Towards the head of the valley, in an open space known as Mullah Susan, the route bifurcates;
one path continues east over the Bolsoru pass and onwards to Ardakan, another branches up a narrow gorge until
recently known as the Tang-i Mohammad Reza, rising to a watershed at 2167 metres and giving access to the plain of
'Aliabad. This gorge is probably what the Greek historians termed the Persian (or Susian) Gates" (Bosworth, C&E).
The Commentary calls the gorge the Tang-i Khas, "a narrow defile ascending with an easy gradient to a pass
(c.1,870 m) and leading into the Merv Dasht basin below" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.2). C&E note 191 withdraws that
name. `reconstruction` (the site, 050.19); the layout within it is `sourced` to Bosworth as below.

- **The Macedonian camp** at Mullah Susan, at the foot of the gorge: "Alexander's camp was established on the
  large open space at the entrance of the Tang-i Khas, known as Mullah Susan. It was here that Alexander
  retreated after his initial attack was beaten off" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.3); "Then, having encamped in a place
  open on all sides" (Curtius 5.4.1). `sourced`.
- **Length of the gorge**, camp to wall: about 30 stades. "There were thirty stadia which were retraced" (Curtius
  5.3.23); "The vulgate sources place this camp about 30 stades from the pass (Curt. v 3. 23; Polyaenus iv 3. 27;
  Diod. 68. 4 gives 300 stades), a distance perfectly compatible with the Mullah Susan" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.3).
  `sourced`. Drawn as 5.5 km, at about 185 m to the stade. That rate is the plan's and goes in no text.
- **The wall** across the gorge, below the watershed. Arrian: "had built a wall across the pass, and had pitched
  his camp there near the wall" (3.18.2). Where in the gorge: "probably across the narrows underneath the
  watershed" (Bosworth, C&E); "The wall blocked the top of the pass from side to side, and it served as defensive
  wall and advance fortification" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.2). The two readings differ by a few hundred metres. The
  plan follows C&E: the wall is in the narrows just below the crest. `sourced`.
- **The crest** (the watershed) a few hundred metres above the wall, held by an advance guard: "while an advance
  guard occupied the crest itself" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.2). `sourced`; the few hundred metres are
  `reconstruction`.
- **The Persian camp** beyond the crest, in open ground at the edge of the plain of 'Aliabad: "Stein located the
  Persian camp in the plain of 'Aliabad, about a kilometre from the head of the pass. The bulk of the army was
  concentrated below the crest in open ground" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.2); "The encampment will have extended back
  to the watershed and it was equipped with a series of outer fortifications" (Bosworth, C&E). The trench: "The
  camp itself had rudimentary defences, a surrounding trench and a series of outlying guardposts" (Bosworth, Comm.
  on 18.2); "At the very time he began to assault the trench" (Arrian 3.18.7). `sourced`.
- **The heights** on both sides of the gorge, held by the Persians: "his men commanded the heights on either side"
  (Bosworth, C&E); "shut in on all sides by steep and craggy rocks, on the summits of which the barbarians stood"
  (Curtius 5.3.17). `sourced`. The Kuh-i Rudian overlooks the gorge: "On the heights of the Kuh-i Rudian
  overlooking the gorge" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced`. That it lies on the north side, between the gorge and the
  Bolsoru path, is `reconstruction`: the turning force runs "along the base of the Kuh-i Rudian" from the Bolsoru
  pass to the camp (Bosworth, C&E).
- **The Bolsoru pass**, no more than 8 km from Mullah Susan: "This route began with an abrupt climb to the head of
  the Bolsoru pass (2,250 m) and then branched southwards over broken ground along an outlier of the Paskohak
  massif to 'Aliabad where the Persian army was encamped" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.4); "according to Stein, the
  distance from Mullah Susan to the pass is no more than 8 km" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.5). `sourced`. Its bearing
  from Mullah Susan (east-north-east, "one path continues east") is `reconstruction`.
- **Length of the turning march**: about 20 km in all. "Stein ... agrees that 12½ miles (20 km) is a fair estimate
  for Alexander's march" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.5; the OCR prints "124 miles (2okm)"). Arrian has 100 stades to the
  point of division, which Bosworth calls an over-estimate (on 18.5). `sourced`. The drawn path comes to about
  15 km; its winding is not drawn.
- **The river** of Persis (the Araxes, the Pulvar) lies far off the field, "more than 100 kilometres from the
  Persian encampment" (Bosworth, C&E). It is not drawn on the phase pages.

**Facing.** The Macedonians come up the gorge from Mullah Susan and face up it, towards the wall; the Persians at
the wall and in the camp face down it, towards Mullah Susan. `sourced` (Arrian 3.18.2–3; Bosworth, Comm. on
18.2–3). In the dawn phase the camp is attacked from behind, from the north-east: "He came down on the Persian
defences from the north-east" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced`.

**Width of the front.** `unknown`. No source read gives the width of the gorge, of the narrows or of the wall.
Dodge's "Barely three men could march abreast" belongs to his own site, and WP's "only a couple of meters wide"
has no footnote, so neither is used. For the drawing only, and in no text: the wall about 300 m long across the
narrows, and the Macedonian column about 200 m wide.

## Order of battle

### Macedon

The column that went through the mountains: "He himself took the Macedonian infantry, the Companion cavalry, the
light cavalry used for skirmishing, the Agrianians, and the archers, and made a forced march through the
mountains" (Arrian 3.18.1); "The king himself took his Macedonian troops, cavalry included, along with the
indispensable Agrianians and archers" (Bosworth, C&E). Parmenion's column (the baggage, the Thessalian cavalry,
the Greek allies, the mercenaries "and the rest of the more heavily armed soldiers", Arrian 3.18.1) is not on the
field. "Alexander had all the Macedonian phalanx troops at the Gates" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.6).

## Phases

The sources tell the three phases of the section's plan in the same order: the assault and the repulse; the
night march; the dawn attack. Curtius stretches the second over "two nights and a day", which Bosworth accepts
("probably over two nights", C&E), where Arrian has a single night.

### Phase 1: `persian-gate-assault`, "The assault in the defile"

**The moment drawn.** The Macedonian column halted in the narrows below the wall under the missiles. The retreat to
Mullah Susan, with which the phase ends, is a dashed arrow, and phase 2 opens with the army back in camp. The page
shows the moment of the check rather than the empty gorge after the retreat. That departs from "a unit's position
at the end of the phase is its block" and is the plan's choice, put to the overseer in the report.

**Opens.** The Macedonians encamped at Mullah Susan (camp unit at u 0, w −5,550), arrived on the fifth day: "on
the fifth day the pass which the Persians call the Susidan Gates" (Curtius 5.3.17); "Then indeed he pitched his
camp there" (Arrian 3.18.2). The Persians in position: P1 on the heights on both sides of the gorge (u ±350, w
−2,000 to −200); P2 at the wall (w 0); P3 and P4 on the crest (w +200 to +300); P5 and P6 in the camp (w +1,100);
P7 at their posts behind the camp. `sourced` as in Order of battle, the places within the gorge as in Ground.

**Moves.**

1. The column advances up the gorge to the wall: an arrow from the camp (w −5,550) up the gorge to just short of
   the wall (w −150). "but next day he marshalled his army, and led it up to the wall" (Arrian 3.18.2); "The king
   first thought to force his way through and advanced to the pass through narrow defiles in rough country, but
   without opposition" (Diodorus 17.68.2). `sourced`. Its block is one infantry column, M1 + M3 + M6 drawn as a
   single `pikes` block about 200 m wide and 1.8 km deep, its head at w −150 (u 0, w −1,050 at its centre),
   named "Macedonian infantry under Alexander". That the whole infantry went in is `sourced` ("marshalled his
   army", Arrian; "massed ranks of the Macedonians", Diodorus 17.68.2). Its length and its order are
   `reconstruction`. The cavalry (M2, M5, M7) and the light troops (M4) are not drawn. No source places them in
   this phase.
2. The missiles from the heights and the engines: two `fire` arrows from P1 (one from each side) onto the column,
   and the engines' fire from P3 shown by a clash at the column's head by the wall. "they rolled stones of huge size down
   the slopes of the mountains ... Stones also hurled from slings, as well as arrows" (Curtius 5.3.18–19); "the
   enemy assailing them with missiles from engines of war placed upon higher ground, which gave them an advantage
   over their assailants" (Arrian 3.18.3); "A rain of missiles from the heights above the Tang-i Mohammad Reza
   took a heavy toll on his infantry" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced`.
3. The retreat: a `dashed` arrow from the column back down the gorge to the camp at Mullah Susan. "he retreated to
   his camp" (Arrian 3.18.3); "having given the signal for retreat, he ordered his men to leave in close order,
   joining their shields over their heads. There were thirty stadia which were retraced" (Curtius 5.3.23); "he
   recalled the soldiers from the battle with a trumpet signal" (Diodorus 17.68.3). `sourced`.

**Not moving.** P2–P7 hold their places.

**Decides.** The frontal assault fails and the dead are left in the gorge: "he was forced to retreat, leaving his
dead in the defiles" (Bosworth, C&E); "It seemed to Alexander, however, discreditable to abandon his dead and
unseemly to ask for them" (Diodorus 17.68.4). Markers: Alexander (`user`) by the column; Ariobarzanes (`user`) by
the Persian camp; `persian-gate-assault-camp` (`flag`) at Mullah Susan, "Macedonian camp", note "Mullah Susan,
30 stades below"; `persian-gate-assault-wall` (`swords`), "Ariobarzanes's wall".

Units 9 (column, Macedonian camp; P1 ×2 blocks, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6, Persian camp; P7 may be left off this page to
stay near twelve). Arrows 4 (advance, two fire, retreat). Clashes 1.

Reconstruction share: about a third (the column's length and order, the spread of P1 along the gorge, P3's place
on the crest, P6 in the camp, the frame's bearing).

### Phase 2: `persian-gate-night-march`, "The night march over the mountain"

**Opens.** The Macedonians back in camp at Mullah Susan; the Persians as at the end of phase 1. The prisoners
show a path: "He was informed by the prisoners that they could lead him round by another route, so that he might
get to the other end of the pass" (Arrian 3.18.4); the guide is a Lycian herdsman in the vulgate (Curtius 5.4.4–12;
Diodorus 17.68.5; Bosworth, Comm. on 18.4). `sourced`.

**Moves.**

1. Craterus stays in the camp with M1 and M2, keeping the fires up: blocks at Mullah Susan, by the camp, facing up
   the gorge. "he left Craterus there at the camp with his own brigade and that of Meleager, as well as a few
   archers and 500 cavalry, with orders that when he perceived he had got right round and was approaching the
   camp of the Persians (which he could easily perceive, because the trumpets would give him the signal), he
   should then assault the wall" (Arrian 3.18.4); "purposely to order more fires than usual to be made, so that
   the barbarians might be more inclined to believe that the king himself was in the camp" (Curtius 5.4.14).
   `sourced`.
2. The turning force climbs to the Bolsoru pass by night: one arrow from Mullah Susan to the pass (u +5,100, w
   +600), shared by both groups up to the ridge. "Alexander crossed the Bolsoru pass under cover of darkness"
   (Bosworth, C&E); "Alexander himself in the third watch in silent march and not even having given the signal
   with the trumpet, went on to the route ... snow drifted by the wind made their progress wearisome" (Curtius
   5.4.17–18); "Alexander did make his way over the mountain at night struggling through deep snow" (Diodorus
   17.68.6). `sourced`.
3. The force divides on the ridge, and Philotas's column takes the right-hand road. M6 and M7 end the phase on the
   road from the ridge towards the camp, about u +2,600, w +900, facing south-west towards the camp. "At last they
   reached the summit. On the right was a road to Ariobarzanes himself; here the king left Philotas and Coenus with
   Amyntas and Polypercon in command of a light-armed band, advising them to advance slowly, since cavalry mixed
   with infantry were marching, and the soil was very rich and abounded in fodder" (Curtius 5.4.20); "the point
   where Alexander divided his forces, which was on the ridge extending from the Bolsoru Pass" (Bosworth, Comm. on
   18.5); "the rest under Philotas and three of the phalanx commanders followed by a slightly different route"
   (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced` for the division and its place. The course of the road and the end position are
   `reconstruction`: the "right" of a force heading south-east, so the side nearer the gorge, and short of the camp
   by dawn. Arrian sends this column "towards the plain, and to make a bridge over the river which one must cross
   to go into Persis" (3.18.6). Bosworth prefers Curtius ("But Curtius is more persuasive", Comm. on 18.9), and the
   drawing follows Bosworth. The texts tell both (050.17).
4. Alexander's group (M3, M4, M5) goes on south-east along the base of the Kuh-i Rudian by the steeper path,
   farther from the Persian posts. It ends the phase above the outer guard post, about u +3,400, w +3,000, facing
   south-west down on the camp. "Alexander himself with his guards and a company of what they called the agema,
   advanced with great trouble by a path which was steep, but more remote from the pickets of the enemy" (Curtius
   5.4.21); "instead of continuing to Ardakan he veered south-east along the base of the Kuh-i Rudian ... using the
   forest vegetation as a cover against Persian reconnaissance groups on the Kuh-i Rudian" (Bosworth, C&E).
   `sourced` for the direction. The end point is `reconstruction` from the next phase (he falls on the first
   guard post "before daylight", Arrian 3.18.6). The halt at midday and the march on "at the second watch"
   (Curtius 5.4.22–23) go in the text; they are not drawn.

**Not moving.** The Persians: P1 on the heights, P2–P4 at the wall and the crest, P5 and P6 in the camp, P7 at the
three posts. No source has them move or learn of the march: Curtius has the men fear the guards (5.4.19), and
Arrian has the camp fallen upon "without being observed" (3.18.7). `sourced` that they stay.

**Decides.** The Macedonians are behind and above the Persian position, undetected, by dawn. Curtius: "Curtius, however,
says explicitly that the march took two nights and a day" (Bosworth, Comm. on 18.6, on Curtius 5.4.22 f.);
Arrian: "implying that the whole turning movement took place in a single night" (the same note). The text
gives both with their authors.

Markers: Craterus (`user`) by M1, note "Keeps the camp fires burning"; Alexander (`user`) by M3; Philotas (`user`)
by M6/M7; `persian-gate-night-march-bolsoru` (`swords` or a plain point), "Bolsoru pass", note "The force divides
here"; Ariobarzanes by the camp. Units about 13: M1, M2, Macedonian camp, M3, M4, M5, M6, M7; P1 (one side may be
left off), P2, P5, P6, Persian camp, P7 (three small blocks under one id). The implementor may merge M3 with M4 and
M6 with M7, naming both parts, to stay near twelve. Arrows 2 or 3 (the climb to the ridge, Philotas's road,
Alexander's path). Clashes none.

Reconstruction share: about half (Philotas's road and its end, Alexander's halt above the posts, the bearing of
the Bolsoru path, the posts' places). The phase rests on sourced movements, but where they end is mostly drawn
from the next phase. Put to the overseer.

### Phase 3: `persian-gate-dawn`, "The dawn attack from behind"

**Opens.** Alexander's group above the outer guard post north-east of the camp; Philotas's column on its road
north of the camp; Craterus with M1 and M2 at Mullah Susan; the Persians at the wall, the crest, the camp and the
three posts. `sourced` from the end of phase 2.

**Moves.**

1. Alexander takes the three guard posts: the arrow of move 2 runs through them, and they get `skull` markers.
   "Falling upon the first guard of the barbarians before daylight, he destroyed them, and so he did most of the
   second; but the majority of the third guard escaped, not indeed by fleeing into the camp of Ariobarzanes, but
   into the mountains as they were, being seized with a sudden panic" (Arrian 3.18.6); "he cut down their first
   line and captured those who were stationed in the second position, then routed the third line" (Diodorus
   17.68.7); "destroying two advance fortifications and driving the occupants of a third into the mountains"
   (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced`. The posts' places are `reconstruction` (Ground table).
2. Alexander falls on the camp at dawn from the north-east: an arrow from above the posts down onto the north-east
   side of the Persian camp, with a clash there. "Consequently He fell upon the enemy's camp at the approach of dawn
   without being observed. At the very time he began to assault the trench, the trumpets gave the signal to
   Craterus" (Arrian 3.18.6–7); "He came down on the Persian defences from the north-east ... Now he attacked the
   Persian camp proper, sited around the watershed" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced`. M3–M5's end blocks at the camp's
   north-east edge.
3. Craterus storms the wall up the gorge: an arrow from Mullah Susan up the gorge to the wall, with a clash at the
   wall. "who at once attacked the advanced fortification" (Arrian 3.18.7); "his soldiers were led out to take
   possession of the pass in which they had been brought to a standstill the day before" (Curtius 5.4.29); "while
   Craterus led a second push up the defiles" (Bosworth, CAH). `sourced`. M1's end block just below the wall, M2
   behind it.
4. Philotas's column attacks by its own road: an arrow from its place north of the camp onto the camp's western
   part, towards the crest, with a clash. "At the same time Philotas, with Amyntas, Polypercon and Coenus, who had
   been ordered to take a different route, struck the barbarians with another fear" (Curtius 5.4.30); "Those who
   could make their retreat withdrew to the defences on the southern side of the camp complex, but they were
   vulnerable to the second assault led by Philotas, which compounded the chaos" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced` that
   it attacks. Where it strikes is `reconstruction`. Bosworth says the southern side; in this frame the side of
   the camp away from Alexander is the side towards the crest and the wall, and the arrow ends there. Arrian has no
   such attack (see phase 2, move 3).
5. The Persians break: a `dashed` arrow from the camp north-west towards Ptolemy's post north of the gorge (M8, a
   block at u +1,000, w −900), where they are cut down. "The enemy then, being in a state of confusion from being
   attacked on all sides, fled without coming to close conflict; but they were hemmed in on all hands ...
   Therefore most of them were compelled to wheel round and flee into the fortifications, which were already in
   the hands of the Macedonians. For Alexander, expecting the very thing which really occurred, had left Ptolemy
   there with three thousand infantry; so that most of the barbarians were cut to pieces" (Arrian 3.18.7–9); "To
   the north of the gorge Ptolemy had been left to deal with any stragglers who were able to circumvent Alexander's
   attack" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced` for the flight and Ptolemy. His place and the arrow's course are
   `reconstruction`. Curtius has the Persians fight "a memorable fight" instead of fleeing (5.4.31–32; Bosworth,
   Comm. on 18.8). The text tells both. The deaths over the cliffs ("hurling themselves over the precipices",
   Arrian 3.18.9) get a `skull` marker on the heights by the gorge.
6. Ariobarzanes escapes: a `dashed` arrow from the camp out east across the plain of 'Aliabad towards the edge of
   the bbox (to about u −900, w +3,900), P6's remnant at its end or off the page. "but Ariobarzanes himself, with a
   few horsemen, escaped into the mountains" (Arrian 3.18.9); "Ariobarzanes, closely attended by about forty
   horsemen and five thousand foot, burst through the centre of the Macedonians' line with much bloodshed on their
   own side and on their enemies', in haste to take possession of the city of Persepolis" (Curtius 5.4.33);
   "Ariobarzanes made his escape with a small body of cavalry and a somewhat larger infantry guard. He attempted to
   enter Persepolis" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced` that he escapes (050.20, disputed for his end). The direction,
   towards Persepolis, follows Curtius and Bosworth and is `reconstruction` on the ground. This is the sixth arrow:
   the implementor may fold move 1 into move 2's arrow (one arrow through the posts to the camp), giving five.

**Decides.** The Persian army is destroyed and the road to Persepolis is open: "The Persians were unable to meet
any of the three prongs of attack and were cut down en masse ... It was a complete and decisive victory for
Alexander" (Bosworth, C&E); "and won the pass, and killed most of the troops of Ariobarzanes" (Diodorus 17.68.7).
Ariobarzanes is shut out of Persepolis and killed (Curtius 5.4.34, which Bosworth accepts) or escapes into the
mountains (Arrian): the text tells both, and nothing off the field is drawn.

Markers: Alexander (`user`) by M3; Craterus (`user`) by M1 at the wall; Philotas (`user`) by M6/M7; Ptolemy
(`user`) by M8; `persian-gate-dawn-posts` (`skull`), "Guard posts", note "Two destroyed, one flees"; Ariobarzanes
(`user`) at the head of the escape arrow, note "Escapes with a few horsemen". Units about 11: M1, M2, M3 (with M4,
M5), M6, M7, M8; P2, P5 (broken, inside the camp), P6 (remnant), Persian camp, P1 (one block). Arrows 5–6. Clashes
4 (wall, camp north-east, camp west, Ptolemy's post).

Reconstruction share: about two fifths (the posts' places, Philotas's point of attack, Ptolemy's post and the
flight towards it, the escape's direction, the frame's bearing).

## Card

- **Sides**: `macedon` (Alexander's Macedonian column) and `persia` (Ariobarzanes's army).
- **Commanders**: Macedon: Alexander; Craterus, at the camp (Arrian 3.18.4; Curtius 5.4.14); Philotas, over the
  second column (Curtius 5.4.20, 30; Bosworth, Comm. on 18.6); Ptolemy (Arrian 3.18.9). Persia: Ariobarzanes,
  "the viceroy of Persis" (Arrian 3.18.2). Arrian alone gives him the title of satrap (Briant), and the card may
  say "satrap of Persis" after Arrian as the section's plan does.
- **Strengths**:
  - Macedon: `unknown`. No ancient source gives a total ("Arrian names the units, not a total", section plan).
    Engels: "All these probably amounted to about 17,000" (Engels, note 7). That is a modern estimate for the
    whole force Alexander took into Persis, built from Curtius's Uxian figures (5.3.3, 5.3.6) and with Thessalian
    cavalry that Arrian sends with Parmenion. The plan leaves it off the card and puts it to the overseer in the
    report. WP's "17,000 picked fighters" comes from Engels and Shahbazi.
  - Persia, `disputed` (050.18): Arrian 40,000 infantry and 700 cavalry (3.18.2); Diodorus 25,000 infantry and 300
    cavalry (17.68.1); Curtius 25,000 infantry (5.3.17).
- **Casualties**: `unknown` on both sides. No source gives a figure. Arrian: "most of the barbarians were cut to
  pieces" (3.18.9); Diodorus: "killed most of the troops of Ariobarzanes" (17.68.7); Curtius has about 40 horse
  and 5,000 foot break out with Ariobarzanes (5.4.33). The Macedonian dead of the first assault: "of his own force
  many were slain" (Diodorus 17.68.3), no figure.
- **Outcome**: Macedonian victory; the road to Persepolis opens. Ariobarzanes escapes into the mountains (Arrian
  3.18.9) or is killed outside Persepolis (Curtius 5.4.33–34; Bosworth), 050.20.
- **Date**: January 330 BC (Bosworth, C&E: Susa left "late December 331"); "ca. 20 January 330" (Lendering).

  `battle.yaml` only if the check asks for it.
