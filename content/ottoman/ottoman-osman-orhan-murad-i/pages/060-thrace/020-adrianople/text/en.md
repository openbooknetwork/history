By Ottoman tradition Lala Şahin defeats the governor (tekfur) of Adrianople at Sazlıdere, south-east of the city, and the governor escapes at night by boat down the Maritsa to [Ainos (Enez)](@ainos). The next morning the townspeople surrender on condition that they may stay and keep their goods. A Byzantine account says instead that most of the people, the metropolitan among them, are carried off as captives.

The date is disputed. Ottoman tradition, and historians who follow it such as Halil İnalcık, place the conquest in 1361 or 1362, as a chronicle records a solar eclipse in the year of the fall and one took place on 5 May 1361. Elizabeth Zachariadou, citing a poem that shows the city still Byzantine at Christmas 1366 and the Byzantine short chronicles, dates it to 1369, and most historians now follow her. Some think the city fell to independent Turkish frontier bands rather than to Murad’s army.

Adrianople, called Edirne by the Turks, becomes the seat of Lala Şahin as the first beylerbey of Rumelia and the main Ottoman base in the Balkans, while Murad’s court stays at Bursa and [Didymoteicho](@didymoteicho-1361). Turkmen from Anatolia cross to Europe in growing numbers and settle the plains of Thrace.
