[Joan of England](@joan-of-england), the fourteen-year-old daughter of Edward III, is travelling to Castile to marry Pedro, the son and heir of King Alfonso XI. The marriage is to tie Castile to England in its war with France, which has paused in a truce since the English took Calais in 1347. She leaves Westminster with her household in January 1348 and waits more than five weeks at [Plymouth](@portsmouth) for a north wind. On 21 March she sails with a fleet manned with archers, and on 31 March she reaches [Bordeaux](@bordeaux), the capital of English Gascony. She stays there while envoys ride ahead to Castile, and the wedding is set for 1 November.

The plague is in Bordeaux by June. On 25 July her household takes her out of the city to a village, probably Lormont, to escape it. Robert Bourchier, one of the leaders of her party, dies of the plague on 20 August, it is said. The plague follows Joan to the village, and on 2 September she dies, its first victim there. On 15 September Edward III writes to Alfonso XI that “destructive Death” has “snatched from both of us our dearest daughter, whom we loved best of all”, in Rosemary Horrox’s translation. In March 1350 Alfonso XI himself dies of the plague while he besieges Gibraltar.

From Bordeaux ships carry the plague onward, as one carried it to England. It moves up the coast to the land around [La Rochelle](@la-rochelle) by October 1348, and to Brittany in 1349. Normandy has the plague already: a chronicle of Normandy records it at [Rouen](@rouen) by 24 June 1348. With the plague in both kingdoms, England and France agree to several truces in 1348, and the war does not resume in full until 1355.
