After Süleyman’s death in 1357 Orhan sends his son Murad to command the frontier in Thrace, with his tutor (lala) Şahin. While Genoese pirates hold Orhan’s young son Halil, from 1357 to 1359, the Ottomans keep the peace with Byzantium. After Halil’s release Ottoman troops raid eastward from 1359, and the Florentine chronicler Matteo Villani reports Turkish raiders before the walls of Constantinople.

The Ottomans take Didymoteicho in 1359, lose it, and take it again in 1361, probably under Hacı İlbey, a frontier commander from Karesi. By the Ottoman chronicles Murad and Lala Şahin also take Çorlu (Tzurulon) and Lüleburgaz (Arkadiopolis) on the road to Constantinople, while Adrianople stays Byzantine.

Orhan dies in Bursa in March 1362, and Murad succeeds him. By the Ottoman chronicles the Byzantines then retake Çorlu, the akhis of Ankara drive out the Ottoman garrison, Murad’s half-brother İbrahim revolts and the Byzantines urge Halil, the youngest, to refuse him. Murad retakes Ankara, has his brothers put to death and makes Lala Şahin beylerbey, commander of the troops in Europe.
