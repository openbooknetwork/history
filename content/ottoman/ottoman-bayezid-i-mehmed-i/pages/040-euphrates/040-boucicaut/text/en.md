In 1399 Charles VI of France sends Marshal Boucicaut to help Manuel II, and Boucicaut sails from [Aigues-Mortes](@aigues-mortes-1399) with six ships and 1,200 men. From [Constantinople](@constantinople) he raids the Ottoman shore of Asia, but his small force achieves little. He persuades Manuel and John VII to end their quarrel. Manuel adopts John VII and leaves the city in his charge, so that he can go to the West himself to ask for help.

Manuel sets out with Boucicaut on 10 December 1399, with a suite of about 40, and leaves 300 French soldiers under Jean de Châteaumorand in the city. He leaves his wife and children with his brother Theodore in the Morea and reaches [Venice](@venice-1400) in April 1400. He travels on by Padua, Vicenza and Pavia to [Milan](@milan-1400), where he meets Duke Gian Galeazzo Visconti, and Charles VI receives him at Charenton outside [Paris](@paris-1400) on 3 June 1400. Henry IV of England meets him at Blackheath on 21 December and takes him to [London](@london-1400). Manuel stays at Eltham until the middle of February 1401 and receives £2,000. He then returns to Paris and stays in France until November 1402.

In Constantinople John VII refuses Bayezid’s demand to surrender the city. Bayezid keeps up the blockade, and many of the people slip out of the walls to give themselves up to the Ottomans. The Castilian traveller Clavijo writes a few years later that John had agreed to hand over the city if Bayezid defeated Timur, an agreement historians doubt.
