# Research for Joan of Arc

## 050 paris-and-compiegne

Coverage: primary (Perceval de Cagny's Chroniques, the household chronicle of Alençon; the Journal d'un bourgeois de Paris, from inside the walls; Monstrelet's Chronicles in Johnes's translation, book 2 ch. 66-86, Monstrelet being present at Compiègne; Chastellain and Jean Chartier and the Journal du siège in Quicherat 4; the letters of November 1429 to Clermont and Riom and the Duke of Burgundy's letter of 23 May 1430 in Quicherat 5; her own answers in the trial of 1431 in Barrett's translation; d'Aulon's and Thierry's depositions of 1456 in Murray) and scholarly (Lowell ch. 12-16, Lang ch. 16-20, Ramsay 1 ch. 27-28, Beaucourt 2 ch. 6, Champion's sketches in Barrett). The Bray check, Montépilloy and the attack on Paris have Cagny and the Burgundian chroniclers on each side; Saint-Pierre-le-Moûtier rests on d'Aulon alone for its course, with the letters of 7 and 9 November for its taking; La Charité has Cagny, the Berry herald and Jean Chartier in Quicherat 4, the letters, the Bourges levy of 24 November and the three lives; the Berry herald gives its length (about a month), an assault and the loss of the artillery, and no source read gives the day it was raised; Compiègne has Monstrelet and Chastellain from the Burgundian camp, Cagny, the Duke's letter and her own account. No source read gives a plan of the walls of Paris, the defenders' posts, the number of the garrison of La Charité or the losses at Saint-Pierre. The Wikipedia articles on Montépilloy (a commune stub), the siege of Paris, Saint-Pierre and La Charité give dates and figures that no source that counts carries (see 050.3, 050.21, 050.37, 050.41). France's Life (vol. 2 ch. 1-7) was not needed beyond these and was not quoted. No work by Pernoud, DeVries or Barker could be read; the Wikipedia article on the siege of Compiègne cites Pernoud and Clin and DeVries.
