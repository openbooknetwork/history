Sigismund’s army is about 50,000 men by the Old Czech Annals, 80,000 Hungarians and 12,000 Austrians by Eberhard Windecke, and three times the Hussites by Thomas Ebendorfer. On 21 December 1421 it surrounds Žižka’s Taborites and Praguers in the open before Kutná Hora. The battle turns on the wagon fort: chained wagons that horsemen cannot ride through, guns that keep them at a distance, and a fort that moves through the enemy lines and fires as it moves.

Sigismund sets out only at the start of October, with his Hungarian army and the Austrian troops of his son-in-law Albert V. About 25 October his Hungarians under Ladislav Blagay beat the Hussites of Moravia at Uherský Ostroh, and on 17 November most Moravian lords renounce the Four Articles at a diet in Brno. The army then marches by [Jihlava](@jihlava-1421) towards Kutná Hora, with a second column by [Litomyšl](@litomysl-1421), its core Hungarian under the Florentine Pippo Spano.

@image pippo-spano-e5ff33

Žižka reaches [Prague](@prague-1421) with the Taborites on 1 December and marches out on 8 December, the Praguers a day behind. Kutná Hora opens its gates to them. Žižka strengthens the garrison of Čáslav and returns to Kutná Hora, while the royal army comes on from Německý Brod by [Ledeč nad Sázavou](@ledec-1421), burning villages on the way, and reaches the town on 21 December.
