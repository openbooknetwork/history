Darius orders the horsemen at the front of his left to ride round Alexander's right before it leaves the levelled ground. [Bessus's](@gaugamela-break-bessus) Saka and Bactrians far outnumber the mercenary horse Alexander sends against them, and throw it back. Then [Aretes](@gaugamela-break-aretes) charges with the lancers and the Paeonians,\* and the Saka give way, until Bessus sends in the rest of the Bactrians. The fight sways to and fro, but the Macedonians hold.

In the middle of this fight the [scythed chariots](@gaugamela-break-chariots) charge Alexander. The javelin men and the Agrianians in front of the Companions bring down many of them and seize the reins; others pass through gaps the infantry opens for them and are overpowered behind the line.

As Persian horsemen leave their line to help the force riding round the right, a gap opens. [Alexander](@gaugamela-break-alexander) wheels into it with the Companions and the nearest battalions of the phalanx in a wedge and charges straight at Darius, while the phalanx presses on the Persian centre. [Darius III](@gaugamela-break-darius) leaves the field and flees towards Arbela.\*\*

On the other wing the Persian right and its chariots press hard on [Parmenion](@gaugamela-break-parmenion), whose horsemen are outnumbered.

The sources do not give where each unit stood at the end of this phase, how far the Persian centre shifted to its left, or the line of Darius's flight.

### Notes

\* Chinnock's translation of Arrian has Aristo, the commander of the Paeonians, lead this charge; Bosworth reads Aretes, whom Arrian's Greek names here.

\*\* Arrian writes that Darius was “the first to turn and flee”. Diodorus has his guard flee first, believing the king had fallen, and a Babylonian astronomical diary of the time says that his troops deserted him. Bosworth judges that Darius was hardly the first to run, but did not wait until Alexander came within reach of a spear.
