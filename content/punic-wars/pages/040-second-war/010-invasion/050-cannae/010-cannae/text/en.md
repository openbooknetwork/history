For 216 BC Rome elects the consuls Gaius Terentius Varro and Lucius Aemilius Paullus and gives them eight legions with as many allied troops, about 86,000 men. It is the largest army Rome has raised. The consuls command on alternate days.

In spring Hannibal leaves his winter quarters at Geronium and takes the Roman supply depot at Cannae in Apulia, placing himself between the Romans and their supplies. The consuls march south after him and find him on the bank of the river Aufidus after two days. They camp about 8 km away, and a skirmish on the approach goes in Varro’s favour.

Paullus camps two thirds of the army on one bank of the river and fortifies a smaller camp on the other bank, about 2 km away. On 1 August Hannibal offers battle and Paullus declines. Numidian cavalry then ride up to the smaller camp and attack the Romans fetching water from the river.
