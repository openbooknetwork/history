Early on Sunday 8 May the English leave their bastilles north of the river, setting fire to some of them, and draw up [in order of battle](@english-array) before the city. The French come out and draw up facing them. Joan, in a light coat of mail because of her wound, forbids them to begin a fight on a Sunday, though they may defend themselves if the English attack. The two armies stand face to face for a whole hour. Priests sing hymns in the field, and two masses are said before the army on a portable altar.

Then the English march away in good order. Of the English captains, Lord Talbot and Lord Scales go to [Meung](@meung-may-1429) and [Beaugency](@beaugency-may-1429), and Suffolk to [Jargeau](@jargeau-may-1429). The townspeople plunder the empty bastilles and pull them down, and their guns are hauled into the city.

Joan and the captains ride back in to give thanks, and Orléans holds a procession with a sermon. The siege is over. It has lasted about seven months, from 12 October 1428. The English still hold the Loire bridges of Meung, Beaugency and Jargeau, above and below the city. Joan then sets out to find Charles VII, with the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois) and the captains.
