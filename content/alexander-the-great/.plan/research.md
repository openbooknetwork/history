# Research for Alexander the Great

## 010 macedon-rising

Coverage: primary (Diodorus 16, Plutarch's Alexander 1-11, Justin 7-9, Arrian 1.1, 7.28) and scholarly (Ellis in the Cambridge Ancient History VI, Bosworth's Conquest and Empire). No source read gives the positions and movements of both lines at Chaeronea beyond Diodorus's two wings; Polyaenus, on whom the modern reconstructions of a feigned withdrawal rest, was not read. Hammond and Griffith's History of Macedonia II (Philip's reign) has no text file on archive.org and was not read. The Museum of Cycladic Art's Chaeronea catalogue (2024) was fetched, but its file holds only the front matter. No source read draws Macedon's border year by year; Ellis gives the annexations as a list of regions.

- 010.1 Event: Perdiccas III falls with his army against the Illyrians and his brother Philip takes a kingdom in danger, 360 or 359 BC (late 360 in Ellis; the archon year of Callimedes, 360/359, in Diodorus). agreed. "when he was defeated in a great battle by the Illyrians⁠9 and fell in the action, Philip his brother, who had escaped from his detention as a hostage, succeeded to the kingdom,⁠10 now in a bad way" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16A.txt, 16.2.4-5, primary) "The Macedonian army was overwhelmingly defeated. The king and 4,000 of his men were killed." (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 14, scholarly) "smooth continuity one imagines was earnestly desired by the embattled Macedonians in late 360 B.c." (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 14.III, scholarly) "When Callimedes was archon at Athens" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16A.txt, 16.2.1, primary)
- 010.2 Figure: Macedonian dead in Perdiccas's defeat, more than 4,000, Diodorus; Ellis gives the same number from him. single. "For the Macedonians had lost more than four thousand men in the battle" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16A.txt, 16.2.5, primary)
- 010.3 Event: Philip reorganises and drills the army and creates the Macedonian phalanx. disputed: Diodorus makes Philip the inventor of the phalanx; Bosworth holds the 12-cubit sarissa as Philip's innovation "highly probable", and Ellis sets expansion above any single invention. "Indeed he devised the compact order and the equipment of the phalanx, imitating the close order fighting with overlapping shields of the warriors at Troy,⁠15 and was the first to organize the Macedonian phalanx." (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16A.txt, 16.3.2, primary) "it is highly probable that the introduction of the 12-cubit sarisa as the fundamental offensive weapon was his innovation" (archive.org-onquest-20and-20Empire-20The-20Reign-20of-20Alexander-20the-20Great-20-281993-29-djvu.txt.txt, ch. 1, scholarly) "Of all innovations the most significant, and gradual, was simply expansion." (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 14, scholarly)
- 010.4 Figure: Philip's army against the Illyrians in 358, 10,000 foot and 600 horse, Diodorus, against about 30,000 foot and 4,000 horse in 334; Ellis takes both from Diodorus. single. "having no less than ten thousand foot-soldiers and six hundred horsemen" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16A.txt, 16.4.3, primary) "The levy of 10,000 foot and 600 horse in 359 (Diod. xvi.4.3) was certainly the maximum in the circumstances" (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 14, scholarly)
- 010.5 Event: Philip refounds Crenides as Philippi and works the gold mines of Mount Pangaeum, 356 BC. agreed. "he went to the city of Crenides,⁠50 and having increased its size with a large number of inhabitants, changed its name to Philippi" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16A.txt, 16.8.6, primary) "Under a new name, Philippi, the foundation took in a number of Macedonian settlers. This was the first of the long series of colonies founded by Philip and Alexander the Great." (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 14, scholarly)
- 010.6 Figure: the mines yield more than 1,000 talents a year, Diodorus; Ellis repeats it. single. "he increased their output so much by his improvements that they could bring him a revenue of more than a thousand talents" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16A.txt, 16.8.6, primary)
- 010.7 Battle: the Crocus Field, 352 BC, Philip and the Thessalians defeat Onomarchus and the Phocians; Thessaly comes under Philip. agreed. No source read gives positions. "Finally more than six thousand of the Phocians and mercenaries were slain, and among them the general himself; and no less than three thousand were taken captives." (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16B.txt, 16.35.6, primary) "Two or three months after the battle (‘the Battle of Crocus Field’, as it is often called, after the Crocion Plain, on which it was probably fought)" (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 14, scholarly)
- 010.8 Event: Olynthus taken by treachery and sacked, its people sold, and the Chalcidice made part of Macedon, 348 BC. agreed. "captured Olynthus through their treachery. 3 After plundering it and enslaving the inhabitants he sold both men and property as booty." (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16C.txt, 16.53.2-3, primary) "Olynthus was sacked, the captured Athenians imprisoned and the inhabitants sold into slavery. - 010.9 Event: Philip besieges Perinthus and Byzantium, 340 BC, while Alexander, sixteen, is regent in Macedon and founds Alexandropolis among the Maedi. single for the regency (Plutarch). "When Philip with much labour and hard fighting mastered the city wall, he found that the houses afforded a stronger one" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16D.txt, 16.76.3, primary) "While Philip was making an expedition against Byzantium,⁠13 Alexander, though only sixteen years of age, was left behind as regent in Macedonia and keeper of the royal seal" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Plutarch-Lives-Alexander-3.txt, ch. 9, primary)
- 010.10 Person: Alexander born in Hecatombaeon (about July) 356 BC, son of Philip and Olympias; he died aged 32 years and 8 months. agreed. "Alexander was born early in the month Hecatombaeon,⁠5 the Macedonian name for" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Plutarch-Lives-Alexander-3.txt, ch. 3, primary) "According to the statement of Aristobulus, he lived thirty-two years, and had reached the eighth month of his thirty-third year." (archive.org-download-theanabasisofale46976gut-46976-0.txt.txt, Arrian 7.28, primary)
- 010.11 Event: Aristotle teaches Alexander at the precinct of the Nymphs near Mieza. single (Plutarch). - 010.12 Event: Alexander tames Bucephalas, a Thessalian horse offered to Philip for thirteen talents. single (Plutarch); Arrian has the horse and its death at the Hydaspes, not the taming. "Once upon a time Philoneicus the Thessalian brought Bucephalas, offering to sell him to Philip for thirteen talents" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Plutarch-Lives-Alexander-3.txt, ch. 6, primary)
- 010.13 Battle: Chaeronea, August 338 BC (7 Metageitnion), Philip against Athens and Thebes; Philip wins, Alexander commanding one wing. agreed. Diodorus gives only the two wings: Alexander with seasoned generals on one, Philip with picked men on the other, the Boeotians and Athenians each holding a wing. "The armies deployed⁠87 at dawn, and the king stationed his son Alexander, young in age but noted for his valour and swiftness of action, on one wing" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16D.txt, 16.86.1, primary) "The sheer weight of Macedonia's cavalry, apparently led by Alexander, and the superior skill and tactics of the phalanx seem to have been decisive" (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 15, scholarly)
- 010.14 Battle: the course of Chaeronea, its positions and movements. open: Ellis calls the course virtually impossible to recover; the modern syntheses of a Macedonian wheel or feigned withdrawal rest on Polyaenus (Stratagems 4.2.2, 4.2.7, not read), whose passage the Wikipedia article on the battle calls of unclear reliability. "The course of the engagement, in August 338, on the Attic date 7 Metageitnion, is virtually impossible to recover." (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 15, scholarly)
- 010.15 Event: Alexander first breaks the Theban Sacred Band at Chaeronea. single (Plutarch); Diodorus has Alexander first to rupture the enemy line without naming the Band. "he is said to have been the first to break the ranks of the Sacred Band of the Thebans" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Plutarch-Lives-Alexander-3.txt, ch. 9, primary) "Then Alexander, his heart set on showing his father his prowess and yielding to none in will to win, ably seconded by his men, first succeeded in rupturing the solid front of the enemy line" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16D.txt, 16.86.3, primary)
- 010.16 Figure: Philip's army at Chaeronea, more than 30,000 foot and at least 2,000 horse, Diodorus; Bosworth repeats it. single. "His forces came to more than thirty thousand infantry and no less than two thousand cavalry." (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16D.txt, 16.85.5, primary)
- 010.17 Figure: Athenian losses at Chaeronea, more than 1,000 dead and 2,000 captured, Diodorus, who also quotes Lycurgus's speech with the same figures; Ellis repeats them. single. "More than a thousand Athenians fell in the battle and no less than two thousand were captured." (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16D.txt, 16.86.5, primary) "Athens lost 1,000 dead and 2,000 were taken prisoner" (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 15, scholarly)
- 010.18 Event: the congress at Corinth, 337 BC (delegates summoned in winter or spring, the synod constituted in spring or early summer, Ellis), elects Philip hegemon and votes war on Persia to punish the burning of the temples (the League of Corinth). agreed. "The Greeks elected him the general plenipotentiary of Greece, and he began accumulating supplies for the campaign." (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16D.txt, 16.89.3, primary) "Delegates of all states were summoned to Corinth in winter or spring" (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 15.IX, scholarly) "In spring or early summer (337) the appointed delegates gathered again at Corinth for the constitution of the general synod." (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 15.IX, scholarly) "A standing committee of proedroi was set up and a hegemon, Philip, elected. "opened the war with Persia by sending into Asia as an advance party Attalus and Parmenion" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16D.txt, 16.91.2, primary) "he sent forward three of his generals into that part of Asia which was under the power of the Persians, Parmenio, Amyntas, and Attalus" (tertullian.org-fathers-justinus-03-books01to10.txt, Justin 9.5, primary)
- 010.20 Event: Philip is killed at Aegae in 336 BC, entering the theatre at his daughter Cleopatra's wedding, by Pausanias, an Orestian of his bodyguard; the assassin is cut down. agreed. "There was a Macedonian Pausanias who came of a" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16D.txt, 16.93.3, primary) "a young man of the royal bodyguard, Pausanias, an Orestian with a personal grudge against him, at a moment when the guard had been ordered to stand back as Philip and the two Alexanders, son and son-in-law, entered the theatre, rushed forward and killed him" (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 15, scholarly)
- 010.21 Event: who stood behind Pausanias. disputed: Diodorus gives a private grudge against Attalus; Justin reports the belief that Olympias set him on and Alexander knew; Ellis says the rationalisations multiplied and the motive seemed inexplicable. So ration- alizations multiplied" (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 15, scholarly)
- 010.22 Event: Alexander, about twenty, is acclaimed king; Antipater presents him to the army. agreed. "his son Alexander,[16] being then about twenty years of age" (archive.org-download-theanabasisofale46976gut-46976-0.txt.txt, Arrian 1.1, primary) "Thus it was that at the age of twenty years Alexander received the kingdom" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Plutarch-Lives-Alexander-3.txt, ch. 11, primary) "Antipater quickly presented him to the army, which acclaimed him king." (archive.org-mbridge-ancient-history-vol.-6-The-20Cambridge-20Ancient-20History-2C-20Vol.-206-djvu.txt.txt, Ellis, ch. 15, scholarly)

- 010.24 Figure: Illyrian dead in Philip's victory over Bardylis, more than 7,000, Diodorus. single. "more than seven thousand Illyrians were slain in this battle" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-16A.txt, 16.4.7, primary)

## 020 greece-secured

Coverage: primary (Arrian 1.1-10, Diodorus 17.1-16, Plutarch's Alexander 11-13) and scholarly (Bosworth's Conquest and Empire, ch. 2). The sites of the Triballian battle, the Getic town and Pelium are placed by Bosworth only as uncertain; no source read fixes them. For the storming of Thebes Arrian and Diodorus differ on how the walls fell, and Bosworth calls the fight difficult to reconstruct.

- 020.1 Event: Alexander removes his rivals: Amyntas son of Perdiccas is killed, and Attalus, in Asia with the advance force, is murdered by Hecataeus. agreed for the killings; disputed for their timing: Bosworth has Amyntas dead by summer 335, probably before Alexander went south in 336, and Attalus gone within a few months of Philip's death; Hammond has Attalus killed in autumn 335 and Amyntas shortly before the crossing to Asia. disputed for Attalus's tie to Cleopatra, Philip's last wife: her brother in Diodorus, her uncle in Bosworth and Hammond. "Hecataeus, however, following the instructions of the king literally, had him killed by treachery" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-17A.txt, 17.5.2, primary) "Amyntas son of Perdiccas was accused of conspiring against Alexander, and killed." (archive.org-onquest-20and-20Empire-20The-20Reign-20of-20Alexander-20the-20Great-20-281993-29-djvu.txt.txt, ch. 2, scholarly) "Attalus was gone, within a few months of the king’s death" (archive.org-onquest-20and-20Empire-20The-20Reign-20of-20Alexander-20the-20Great-20-281993-29-djvu.txt.txt, ch. 2.I, scholarly) "In the event Hecataeus killed Attalus in autumn 335" (archive.org-20Griffith-20G.-20A-20history-20of-20Macedonia.-20V.3-2C-20336-167-20B.C.-201988-djvu.txt.txt, Hammond, ch. 1, scholarly) "he was put to death shortly before Alexander set out for Asia" (archive.org-20Griffith-20G.-20A-20history-20of-20Macedonia.-20V.3-2C-20336-167-20B.C.-201988-djvu.txt.txt, Hammond, ch. 1, scholarly) "A possible rival for the throne remained in Attalus, who was the brother of Cleopatra, the last wife of Philip" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-17A.txt, 17.2.3, primary) "Cleopatra, the sister of Hippostratus and niece of Attalus" (archive.org-onquest-20and-20Empire-20The-20Reign-20of-20Alexander-20the-20Great-20-281993-29-djvu.txt.txt, ch. 1, scholarly) "Attalus, the uncle of one of Philip’s" (archive.org-20Griffith-20G.-20A-20history-20of-20Macedonia.-20V.3-2C-20336-167-20B.C.-201988-djvu.txt.txt, Hammond, ch. 1, scholarly)
- 020.2 Event: Alexander marches into Greece and is given Philip's command of the war against Persia by the Greeks; Sparta refuses. agreed. "There he assembled all the Greeks who were within the limits of Peloponnesus,[18] and asked from them the supreme command of the expedition against the Persians, an office which they had already conferred upon Philip. He received the honour which he asked from all except the Lacedaemonians" (archive.org-download-theanabasisofale46976gut-46976-0.txt.txt, Arrian 1.1, primary) "How he razed Thebes to the ground and terrified the Greeks and was elected general plenipotentiary of Greece" (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-17A.txt, contents of book 17, primary)
- 020.3 Event: spring 335 BC, from Amphipolis Alexander crosses the Nessus and forces Mount Haemus, where the Thracians roll waggons down on the phalanx. single (Arrian). "Crossing the river Nessus,[23] they say he arrived at mount Haemus[24] on the tenth day." (archive.org-download-theanabasisofale46976gut-46976-0.txt.txt, Arrian 1.1, primary)
- 020.4 Event: Alexander crosses the Danube by night on boats, log canoes and stuffed hides, scatters the Getae and razes their town, then returns across. agreed (Arrian; Bosworth reads him, so one source for the crossing). "Those who crossed with Alexander amounted in number to 1,500 cavalry and 4,000 infantry." ## 040 issus-and-the-coast

Coverage: primary (Arrian 2.4-3.5, Diodorus 17.31-52, Curtius 3-4, Plutarch's Alexander 19-27, Polybius 12.17-22 on Callisthenes's account of Issus) and scholarly (Bosworth's Conquest and Empire ch. 2 and his Commentary on Arrian I). ## 090 legacy

Coverage: primary (Diodorus 18-21, Justin 13-15, Plutarch's Demetrius, Eumenes and Phocion) and scholarly (Hammond and Walbank's History of Macedonia III, Bosworth's Conquest and Empire, the Cambridge Ancient History VII of 1928, Bosworth's The Legacy of Alexander for the borders of 301 BC). The Diadochi wars rest mostly on Diodorus, who follows Hieronymus of Cardia; Hammond and Walbank follow the same texts, so most lines here are single for detail. The Alexandrias, koine and Gandhara rest on Bosworth's chapter on the foundations and the 1928 Cambridge Ancient History; Hellenistic art in Gandhara and Ai-Khanoum rest on Wikipedia alone (see Thin). The Babylonian Diadochi Chronicle, the contemporary record of the satraps' wars in Mesopotamia, was not fetched. After Ipsus the Taurus is the one line the sources give (Lysimachus north of it, 090.24); the other borders of 311-301 BC come as lists of lands (090.21-090.26), and the line between Antigonus and Seleucus in 306-301 and the new Seleucid-Mauryan border are given by no source read.

## Against the brief
010.14: the course of Chaeronea is 'virtually impossible to recover' (Ellis); the reconstructions rest on Polyaenus, not read, so the sources read give no positions for phase pages, and Chaeronea stays a card.

030.8: the Granicus has two ancient versions that cannot both stand: a forced crossing late in the day (Arrian) or an unopposed crossing at dawn (Diodorus). Phase pages can follow Arrian's sequence (030.9), and the text has to say that Diodorus differs.

040.11: the greatest of Darius's offers (the land to the Euphrates, 30,000 talents, a daughter) comes just before Gaugamela in every source but Arrian (Bosworth), so it belongs in 050, not with Tyre in 040.

040.19: whether Alexandria was founded before or after the visit to Siwa is disputed, and the brief's order (Memphis, Alexandria, Siwa) follows Arrian.

040.13: the sources give Tyre's sequence and both sides' movements (the mole, the fire ship, the fleets penning the harbours, the breach in the south), so phase pages are supported.

040.5: the Pinarus cannot be located, so the Issus field is a reconstruction (Kuru Çay is Bosworth's suggestion).

050.19: the site of the Persian Gate is disputed among the Tang-i Mohammad Reza (Bosworth, who says Stein misnamed it the Tang-i Khas), the Tang-e Meyran (Speck, Lendering) and Kopan-Yasuj (Hemati), so the phase pages draw a reconstruction.

050.15: Megalopolis was probably fought early in 330 (Bosworth, Badian), after the Persian Gate, not before the Uxian defile as the brief's order has it.

060.5: Bessus was mutilated in Bactria but sent to Ecbatana to be put to death, so his execution is not in Bactria.

060.13: Bosworth dates the Sogdian Rock to summer 328, before the death of Cleitus; the brief follows Arrian's spring 327, after proskynesis.

060.15: no source read calls Sogdiana 'the hardest war of the campaign'; Bosworth gives nearly two years of campaigning.

060.9: the Polytimetus, where Spitamenes destroyed a Macedonian column, is a battle the brief lacks.

070.17: Craterus's column left before the fleet reached Patala and the Ocean, so the army divided before the Ocean, the reverse of the brief's order.

070.9: the site of the Hydaspes crossing and battle is disputed (Jalalpur, Jhelum, or the plain of Gujrat), so the phase pages draw a reconstruction.

080.2: 'the worst losses of the campaign' in Gedrosia is disputed: Plutarch says not a quarter came back, Engels says a quarter escaped, and Bosworth says there was no military disaster.

080.12: the diary dates the death to between the evenings of 10 and 11 June 323; '11 June' rests on Depuydt, not read, through Lendering's note.

090.13: Ipsus was won by four kings, Ptolemy, Seleucus, Lysimachus and Cassander (090.15), so the brief's 'Ptolemies, Seleucids, Antigonids' does not describe 301 BC; no source read covers the Antigonids' later hold on Macedon.

090.4, 090.8, 090.10: Crannon (322), Gabiene (317/316) and Gaza (312) are battles the brief lacks; Diodorus gives positions for Paraetacene and Gabiene.

010.7, 070.2: the Crocus Field and Massaga are battles the brief lacks, and Pelium (020.7) is in the brief's text but not its battle list.

090: no source read covers 'every conqueror since' in the legend page.
