Velázquez sends a second fleet of four ships and about 240 men under his nephew Juan de Grijalva. Pedro de Alvarado, Francisco de Montejo and Alonso de Ávila captain the other ships, and Julián and Melchor come as interpreters. The fleet leaves Matanzas on 8 April 1518 and reaches Cozumel on 3 May, where the islanders flee inland.

Grijalva follows the east coast south past three large towns, one of them probably Tulum, to a bay he names Ascensión. He turns back north round the peninsula without landing. At Campeche the people refuse water, so the Spaniards fire on the town with small cannon and enter it after its people flee.

At Champotón the Spaniards land to avenge the defeat of the year before. Spanish accounts give one to seven Spaniards killed and fifty to sixty wounded, Grijalva among them with two arrow wounds and broken teeth. About 200 Maya die, among them Moch Couoh. Landa places this fight on the way west, Sharer and Traxler on the voyage home.

On 8 June the fleet reaches the mouth of a large river, which the crew names the Grijalva. At Potonchán the Chontal lord Tabscoob exchanges gifts with Grijalva, who gives him his green velvet doublet. Tabscoob speaks of a land called Culua to the west with much gold, and asks the Spaniards to leave. Grijalva sails on to San Juan de Ulúa, where envoys of Moctezuma meet him, and as far as the Pánuco River before returning to Cuba.
