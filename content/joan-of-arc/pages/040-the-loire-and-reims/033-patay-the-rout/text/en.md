Near [Patay](@patay-the-rout-patay) on 18 June the French vanguard has taken the English captain Lord Talbot and broken through the narrow way he held. [Sir John Fastolf](@patay-the-rout-fastolf), one of the English commanders, has stayed on his horse, and he rides north towards the English vanguard at the hedges to join it. Its men see him coming, think all is lost and the main body in flight, and leave the hedges with their captain and his white standard.

The French vanguard under [La Hire](@patay-the-rout-la-hire) and [Poton de Xaintrailles](@patay-the-rout-la-hire) presses into the English main body. The French main body under [the Duke of Alençon](@patay-the-rout-alencon) and [the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois)](@patay-the-rout-alencon) comes up and joins the rout. The French are by then so far into the main body that they take or kill whom they will. The fugitives run north-east for Janville, two leagues beyond Patay, and its townsmen shut the gates on them. Fastolf turns away with a small company and rides for Étampes, which he reaches about midnight.

Besides Talbot, the English captains Lord Scales, Sir Thomas Rempston and a Hungerford are taken. The French lose few men. The fight is over at about two in the afternoon, and that night the French lie at Patay.
