The accounts of Angiolello and Konstantin Mihailović suggest that Vlad III of Wallachia pays the sultan no tribute after 1459, but Tursun Beg writes that Vlad turns against Mehmed only in 1461, while the sultan is away at Trebizond. Vlad seeks an alliance with King Matthias Corvinus of Hungary. At the end of November 1461 he writes to Mehmed that his war with the Saxons of Transylvania has emptied his treasury and that he cannot leave Wallachia to come to Constantinople.

Mehmed sends his envoy Thomas Katabolinos to order Vlad to Constantinople, and he orders Hamza Bey of [Nikopol](@nikopol-1462) to seize Vlad when he has crossed the Danube. Vlad learns of the plan, rides south from [Târgoviște](@targoviste-1456), ambushes Hamza and his 1,000 horsemen in a narrow pass north of Giurgiu, and has Hamza and Katabolinos executed. He then rides to the Ottoman fortress of Giurgiu, calls to the guards in Turkish to open the gates, and takes and destroys the fortress.

In January and February 1462 Vlad’s men cross the frozen Danube and raid the Ottoman towns along it, from [Isaccea](@isaccea-1462) (Oblucița) near the mouth of the river up to [Oryahovo](@rahova-1462) (Rahova). On 11 February 1462 Vlad writes to Matthias that he has broken the peace with the sultan, and he lists 23,884 Turks and Bulgarians killed, without those burned in their houses: among them 6,414 at Giurgiu, 6,840 at [Silistra](@silistra-1462) (Durostor), 1,350 at [Enisala](@enisala-1462), 840 at [Hârșova](@harsova-1462), 630 at [Tutrakan](@tutrakan-1462), 410 at [Svishtov](@svishtov-1462), 1,138 at Nikopol and Gigen and 1,460 at Oryahovo. The Christian Bulgarians are spared, and many of them settle in Wallachia.
