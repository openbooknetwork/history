In 1340 the heirs of Genghis Khan rule most of Asia. The Golden Horde of Khan Özbeg holds the steppe north of the Black Sea and the Caspian Sea from its capital, [Sarai](@sarai-1340) on the lower Volga. The Chagatai Khanate holds Central Asia, and the Yuan emperors hold China. In Iran the Ilkhanate has broken up among rival khans since the death of Abu Sa’id in 1335.

The Florentine merchant Francesco Pegolotti, in a handbook for traders written about 1340, describes the caravan road to China. It starts at [Tana](@tana-1340) at the mouth of the Don and goes by ox wagon to Astrakhan at the mouth of the Volga, by river to Sarai and Saraichuk, and by camel wagon to [Urgench](@urgench-1340) and [Otrar](@otrar-1340). Pegolotti writes that the road is safe by day and by night, by the word of the merchants who have used it. His picture is that of the 1320s and 1330s. From 1340 the road grows worse as the rulers of the Chagatai Khanate fight among themselves, and by March 1345 Venetian envoys at Caffa call it ruined. Another road runs south to [Tabriz](@tabriz-1340), where Genoese merchants traded Persian silk until persecution drove them out in 1338.

At the western end of the roads, [Genoa](@genoa-1340) and [Venice](@venice-1340) keep trading ports. Genoa holds [Caffa](@caffa-1340) in the Crimea and Pera across the Golden Horn from [Constantinople](@constantinople-1340), and both republics have a quarter at Tana. There their merchants buy silk, spices, furs, wax and grain. They also buy young Turkic and Mongol slaves and sell them to the Mamluk sultans of Egypt, who make soldiers of them.

Europe has about 80 million people, by the estimate the historian Ole Benedictow uses.
