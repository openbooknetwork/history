In [Bursa](@bursa-yesil) Mehmed builds a complex on the hill east of the town, with a mosque, a medrese, a public kitchen and a bath. The Green Mosque, begun in 1412 under the vizier Hacı İvaz Pasha, is finished in December 1419 or January 1420 by the inscription over its portal. Its halls form a reversed T, with iwans and rooms for travelling dervishes around a domed court with a fountain. It takes its name from the green and blue tiles inside, which an inscription at the mihrab calls the work of the masters of Tabriz.

In [Edirne](@edirne-eski-cami), which Mehmed makes his main seat, the Old Mosque that his brother Süleyman began in 1402 is finished in 1414: a hall of nine domes on four pillars, built by the architects Hacı Alaeddin and Ömer bin İbrahim. At Didymoteicho, a favourite residence of the early sultans, the mosque begun under Bayezid and named after Mehmed is finished in 1420, and Mehmed founds a medrese at [Merzifon](@merzifon-medrese). His grand vizier Bayezid Pasha builds a mosque, a public kitchen and a medrese in his home town of Amasya, endowed in 1418.

Süleyman Çelebi, imam of Bayezid’s Ulu Cami in Bursa, finishes in 1409 his Vesîletü’n-Necât, known as the Mevlid, the first and best known Turkish poem on the birth of the Prophet. The poet Ahmedi, who dies in 1413, writes the İskendernâme, a book of Alexander in verse meant for Bayezid and after 1402 dedicated to Emir Süleyman. It holds the earliest account of Ottoman history that survives.
