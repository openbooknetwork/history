Antigonus has held Syria and Phoenicia since taking them from Ptolemy in 315 and 314 BC. In 312 BC Ptolemy, urged on by Seleucus, crosses the desert to [Gaza](@gaza-312). There they beat Antigonus's young son Demetrius.\* Ptolemy then gives Seleucus 800 foot and about 200 horse by Diodorus's count\*\*, and with them Seleucus marches through Mesopotamia and takes back [Babylon](@babylon-312), where he had been satrap.

In 311 BC the generals make peace. Cassander is to be general of Europe until Alexander IV comes of age, Lysimachus keeps Thrace, Ptolemy Egypt and the cities next to it in Libya and Arabia, and Antigonus the first place in all Asia. After the peace Glaucias, Cassander's officer, who guards the boy, kills Alexander IV and his mother Roxana in secret on his master's orders.\*\*\* With no heir left, each general holds his land, in Diodorus's words, "as if it were a kingdom won by the spear".

Seleucus goes east towards India, and by 306 or 305 BC he holds the upper satrapies of Iran as well as Babylonia. At a date that is not known he makes peace with Chandragupta, founder of the Mauryan Empire in India, and in Bosworth's words cedes him the eastern satrapies for 500 elephants; Hammond and Walbank name the lands as Gandhara, eastern Arachosia and Gedrosia. Antigonus founds [Antigonia on the Orontes](@antigonia-306), placed, Diodorus says, to watch Babylon and the upper satrapies.

In 306 BC Demetrius sails from Athens to Cyprus and defeats Ptolemy's fleet off [Salamis](@salamis-306). At the news Antigonus takes the diadem and the title of king, and gives the title to Demetrius too. Ptolemy follows in 305 BC, and the others soon after.

### Notes

\* Plutarch makes Demetrius twenty-two at Gaza. Diodorus gives him that age two years earlier, in 314 BC, as Geer notes.
