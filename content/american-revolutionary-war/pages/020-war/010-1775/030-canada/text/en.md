Congress sends two forces to take the province of Quebec and bring its inhabitants into the revolt. Richard Montgomery leads one north from Ticonderoga along Lake Champlain, takes Fort St Johns after a long siege and enters Montreal on 13 November. Benedict Arnold leaves Cambridge in September with about 1,050 men, up the Kennebec and through the Maine wilderness. After eight weeks of hunger and desertion, about six hundred reach the St Lawrence.

The two join before the walls of Quebec, and in a snowstorm on the last night of the year they assault the Lower Town. Montgomery is killed at the Près-de-Ville blockhouse, Arnold is wounded and over four hundred Americans are captured. The remaining troops keep up a blockade of the city through the winter.
