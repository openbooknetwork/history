In late 334 BC Alexander sends the men who married just before the campaign back from Caria to their wives in Macedon for the winter, and tells their officers to raise new troops there. Then he splits the army. Parmenion takes the allied troops and the baggage wagons by Sardis into Phrygia. Alexander marches into [Lycia](@lycia-333), where its main cities and, by Arrian's count, about thirty smaller towns surrender in turn. Why he chose this road is disputed.\*

By midwinter he is at the eastern end of the Lycian coast. Most of the army crosses [Mount Climax](@mount-climax-333) by a road that Thracian troops have cut, while Alexander takes his own column along the shore below. That beach, Arrian explains, is flooded in a south wind and passable only in a north wind. After strong southerlies the wind turns north, and the king and his men read it as a sign from the gods. In [Pamphylia](@pamphylia-333) most cities of the coastal plain submit. One that had come to terms breaks them and must then pay 100 talents instead of 50 and give hostages. A stronghold held by Greek mercenaries and local troops beats off his first assault, and he seems never to have renewed the siege.

Early in spring 333 BC Alexander turns north across [Pisidia](@pisidia-333), a land of warlike mountain peoples. He takes one town and other strongholds by storm or by agreement, but he seems, Bosworth notes, to have left no garrisons, and Pisidia was still independent when Alexander died. The map therefore leaves it outside his lands. At [Celaenae](@celaenae-333), the seat of the satrap of Phrygia, the garrison of the citadel promises to surrender if no help reaches it by a set day. Alexander leaves 1,500 soldiers there and makes Antigonus, a Macedonian general, satrap of Phrygia.

### Notes

\* Arrian says that Alexander meant to win control of the coast and so make the Persian fleet useless. Bosworth answers that for much of the way the route ran inland and left the Lycian coast open to Persian ships, and that Alexander had probably already decided to march east against the Great King.
