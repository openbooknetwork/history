Cardinal Giuliano Cesarini, the pope’s legate, comes to the diet at Nuremberg on 4 March 1431 and wins Sigismund and the princes for a new crusade. Sigismund names Frederick of Brandenburg its commander and stays away. The army gathers at [Weiden](@weiden-1431) in the Upper Palatinate, while Albert of Austria is to attack through Moravia. The Hussite unions and towns gather near Plzeň at the end of June under Prokop the Great, with Sigismund Korybut as a guest, but run short of food and fall back inland.

The crusaders cross the border on 1 August and lay siege to Tachov, burning the villages around it, and on 8 August turn to [Kladruby](@kladruby-1431). There they hear that the Hussites are gathering near Beroun, and they march south and lay siege to [Domažlice](@domazlice-town-1431). The Hussite army covers the 75 km from Beroun to [Chotěšov](@chotesov-1431) on 12 and 13 August and early on 14 August marches on Domažlice. The Old Czech Annals give the crusaders 130,000 men and the Hussites 40,000, and Bartošek of Drahonice counts 9,000 wagons with the crusade army.

@image domazlice-mathauser-b4246b

On 14 August the crusaders flee before the battle. The Old Czech Annals write that they run into the Bavarian forests with their wagons while the Hussites are still a mile away, before they have seen them. By the Czech accounts Frederick of Brandenburg pulls his troops back towards Kdyně to hold the road over the Všeruby pass, and the Saxons, who have not been told, take the move for flight and panic. Tradition adds that the crusaders run on hearing the Hussite hymn and the rumble of the wagons. A wagon fort of foot with Cesarini’s Italian guard holds the road by Rýzmberk castle for a while. The Hussites take about 2,000 wagons and 300 guns, and Cesarini escapes disguised as a soldier, leaving behind his hat, his gold cross and the papal bull.
