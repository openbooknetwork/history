After the Uxian defile, near the border of Persis, Alexander sends Parmenion with the baggage and the heavier troops by the main road. He himself takes the Macedonian infantry, the Companion cavalry, the light horse, the Agrianians and the archers by the direct road through the Zagros mountains. On the fifth day, Curtius says, he reaches the pass that Arrian calls the Persian Gates and Curtius the Susidan Gates. [Ariobarzanes](@persian-gate-assault-ariobarzanes), satrap of Persis, has built [a wall](@persian-gate-assault-wall) across it and camped behind it with a large army.\*

Where the pass lay is disputed.\*\* The field is drawn at the site the historian A. B. Bosworth accepts after the archaeologist Aurel Stein: a narrow gorge, which Bosworth calls the Tang-i Mohammad Reza, climbing from the open ground of Mullah Susan to a watershed, with the Persian camp just beyond it.

[Alexander](@persian-gate-assault-alexander) camps at Mullah Susan and the next day leads his infantry up the gorge to the wall. In the narrowest part the Persians on the heights on both sides roll down boulders and shower the column with sling stones and arrows, and Arrian adds engines of war on the higher ground. Many Macedonians are killed. Alexander has the trumpets sound the retreat, and the men fall back 30 stades to the [camp](@persian-gate-assault-camp) with their shields locked over their heads, leaving their dead in the pass.

The map shows the column at the moment it is halted below the wall. The sources do not give the direction of the gorge, the length or order of the column, where the troops on the heights stood, or where the engines were.

### Notes

\* Arrian gives Ariobarzanes 40,000 foot and 700 horse. Diodorus gives 25,000 foot and 300 horse, and Curtius 25,000 foot.

\*\* Stein placed the gorge above Mullah Susan, and Bosworth follows him; he notes that Stein had called it the Tang-i Khas, a gorge that in fact lies about 10 km further west. Henry Speck, whom Jona Lendering follows, puts the battle in the Tang-e Meyran north-east of Yasuj, and the linguist Abuzar Hemati between Kopan and Yasuj.
