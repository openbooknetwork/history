The English cross the bridge, form a line just short of the Norwegians, lock shields and charge. Snorri describes the Norwegian line as long and thin, with its wings bent back until they meet in a ring, and English horsemen riding at it again and again. Historians doubt the horsemen, since the English fought on foot, and treat the details of Snorri’s account, written down about 160 years later, with caution.

The fighting lasts for hours, and the Norwegians without mail suffer most. Harald is killed early in the battle, by Snorri’s account by an arrow in the throat. Tostig takes command under the king’s banner, Land-waster, and by Snorri’s account refuses his brother’s offer of quarter. He falls in the fighting that follows, and the shield wall begins to break up.
