The Battle of Hafrsfjord is fought between 872 and 900, by most scholars’ reckoning in the 880s. The traditional year 872 comes from a count back from the Battle of Svolder in Snorri Sturluson’s Heimskringla, made in the 1830s, and Norway celebrated a thousand years as a kingdom on that basis in 1872. The only source from Harald’s own time is Haraldskvæði, a poem attributed to his court poet Þorbjörn hornklofi, which tells of his victory at Hafrsfjord over Kjotve the Rich and Haklang.

Snorri, writing more than 300 years after the battle, gives the fuller story. The kings of Hordaland, Rogaland, Agder and Telemark gather ships and men against Harald, who sails south along the coast and collects forces from every district. The allies meet north of Jæren and go into Hafrsfjord, where Harald is waiting. After a long fight King Eirik of Hordaland and King Sulke of Rogaland are dead, the berserk Thor Haklang has fallen on his ship, and Kjotve the Rich flees.

In the sagas the battle completes Harald’s conquest of the south-west and makes him the first king of all Norway, and many of those who will not submit emigrate to Iceland. Most historians now see the unification of Norway as a process that took centuries, and some argue that the Harald Fairhair of the sagas did not exist. By the sagas’ account he dies about 932, and his sons Eric Bloodaxe and Haakon the Good follow him as kings.
