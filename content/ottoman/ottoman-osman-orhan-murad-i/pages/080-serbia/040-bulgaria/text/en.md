After Pločnik, Tsar Ivan Shishman of [Tarnovo](@tarnovo) renounces his vassalage and refuses to send troops to Murad. In 1388 the grand vizier Çandarlı Ali Pasha, Hayreddin Pasha’s son, crosses the eastern Balkan mountains with about 30,000 men and takes the Bulgarians by surprise. [Ovech (Provadia)](@provadia-1388), [Shumen](@shumen-1388), Madara and other towns fall, but [Varna](@varna) holds out, and Ali Pasha gives up its siege and turns north.

@image shumen-fortress-gate-c14543

Tutrakan on the Danube surrenders after Ali Pasha burns its fields. Shishman holds [Nikopol](@nikopol), and when Ali Pasha cannot take it, Murad comes with more troops, by the account of the Ottoman chronicler Sadeddin. Shishman asks for a truce and promises [Silistra](@silistra-1388), but then refuses to hand it over and strengthens its walls. Ali Pasha crosses the mountains a second time, takes Shumen, Cherven and Svishtov and besieges Shishman in Nikopol again. Shishman submits on harder terms: he hands over Silistra and accepts Ottoman garrisons in Shumen and Ovech.

@image nikopol-danube-4f95aa

Shishman keeps Tarnovo, the lands west of it and some castles on the Danube, and Varna and Kaliakra stay Bulgarian. Ivan Sratsimir of Vidin probably becomes an Ottoman vassal at this time and takes an Ottoman garrison.
