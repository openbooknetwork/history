Under the terms of Serbia’s vassalage, Lazar’s son Stefan Lazarević pays Bayezid a yearly tribute and sends troops to his campaigns. His mother Milica rules for him from [Kruševac](@krusevac). Stefan and his brother Vuk are to come to the sultan’s court each year, and they bring their sister Olivera to Bayezid at Bursa. In the summer of 1390 Serbian troops, joined by Ottoman detachments, retake [Borač](@borac-1390) and Čestin, which a Hungarian army took in November 1389.

Vuk Branković, Lazar’s son-in-law, rules [Priština](@pristina), Vučitrn and [Skopje](@skopje). King Sigismund of Hungary sends his palatine Nicholas Garay to him in July 1389, and in May 1390 Vuk asks Ragusa for refuge in case of war with Stefan. He holds out until early 1392, when the Ottomans take Skopje and settle Turkmen around it. Vuk becomes Bayezid’s vassal before the end of that year.

From 1390 akıncı raiders cross the Danube into Wallachia and the southern counties of Hungary. Sigismund leads an army against the Ottomans in the summer of 1392. He crosses the Danube near [Kovin](@kovin) and marches up the Mlava valley to Ždrelo, then turns back and tries to take [Golubac](@golubac-1392).
