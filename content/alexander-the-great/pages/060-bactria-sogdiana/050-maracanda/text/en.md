In spring 328 BC Alexander crosses the Oxus back into Sogdiana, where rebels still hold out in their forts. He splits his army into five columns, which take the forts one by one and meet at [Maracanda](@maracanda-328) (today Samarkand).

@image afrasiyab-walls-3f5a04

Spitamenes, the Sogdian noble who leads the rising, finds allies among the Massagetae, nomads of the Kyzyl Kum. With 600 of their horsemen he takes a fort in Bactria and rides up to [Bactra](@bactra-328) (today Balkh), where the small garrison rides out and is ambushed. Craterus, Alexander's leading general, drives the Massagetae back into the desert.

At Maracanda in the autumn Alexander holds a feast for the Dioscuri, the twin sons of Zeus. Flatterers rank him above the twins and his father Philip, and mock the generals who died at the Zeravshan the year before. Cleitus the Black, the cavalry commander who saved Alexander's life at the Granicus, praises Philip and taunts the king. Alexander seizes a spear from a guard and runs him through, then mourns him for three days. Bosworth sees the older Macedonians' anger at a king who claimed all the glory and turned to Persian ways.

@image de-blieck-cleitus-3a1dd1

For the winter Alexander goes south to [Nautaca](@nautaca-328), whose site is uncertain, and leaves his general Coenus to hold Sogdiana. With a garrison in every place, Arrian writes, Spitamenes is running out of ways to escape. He gathers 3,000 nomad horsemen at Gabae, on the desert's edge, and invades. [Coenus beats him](@coenus-328) at a place no one knows. Arrian counts over 800 of the enemy dead against 25 horse and 12 foot of Coenus's men. The Sogdians and Bactrians with Spitamenes go over to Coenus, and in winter 328–327 BC Spitamenes is killed and his head brought to Alexander.\*

### Notes

\* Arrian says that the Massagetae cut off his head and send it to Alexander to keep him from marching against them. Curtius says that his wife cuts it off while he sleeps and brings it herself.
