Itzamnaaj Bahlam III (Shield Jaguar) becomes king of Yaxchilan on 23 October AD 681. The city stands in a loop of the Usumacinta River, 40 km upriver from its old rival Piedras Negras. Inscriptions record his captures of Aj Sak Ichiy Pat in 689, of the lord of B'uktuun in 713 and of the lord of Lacanha in 729. Piedras Negras Stela 8 records the capture of one of his sajals, or subordinate lords, in 726.

In the last third of his reign the king rebuilds the centre of Yaxchilan with carved lintels, hieroglyphic stairways and stelae. Structure 23 belongs to his principal wife and aunt, Lady K'abal Xook. Its Lintel 24 shows her drawing a rope through her tongue while the king holds a torch above her, in a rite dated to 28 October 709. At times Lacanha and Bonampak, to the south, are under his rule.

Itzamnaaj Bahlam III dies on 15 June 742, in his nineties, and no accession is recorded at Yaxchilan for the next ten years. In 749 Yopaat B'alam II of Yaxchilan attends a ceremony for the king of Piedras Negras, and no record of his reign survives at Yaxchilan itself.

Yaxun B'alam IV (Bird Jaguar IV), son of Itzamnaaj Bahlam III and Lady Eveningstar of Calakmul, takes the throne on 3 May 752. He raises stelae that show him with his father, and Stela 11 credits him with at least 21 captives. He builds Structure 33 and dies in 768.
