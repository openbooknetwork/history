Early in August 1429 Charles VII, crowned at Reims on 17 July, is at Provins with his army, and his council decides to cross the Seine at [Bray](@bray-held-1429) and go back beyond the Loire. On the night before the crossing English troops come into Bray, though its townsmen have promised the passage. The captains who want war are glad of the check, and the army turns north to [Crépy](@crepy-1429). There on 11 August a challenge reaches the king from John, Duke of Bedford, regent of France for Henry VI.

On Sunday 14 August the two armies meet at [Montépilloy](@montepilloy), about two leagues from [Senlis](@senlis-1429). The French lodge by a hedge in the fields. Bedford camps by the abbey of [Notre-Dame-de-la-Victoire](@victoire-1429) on the little river Nonette, and overnight his men dig in behind stakes, ditches and carts. Joan and La Hire are at the head of the French skirmishers. They skirmish that evening and all the next day, but Bedford will not come out. About 300 men die, and which side loses more is not known. On the 16th Bedford falls back by Senlis to Paris.

Now the towns come over to the king. [Compiègne](@compiegne-1429) sends him its keys, and on 18 August he rides in and makes Guillaume de Flavy its captain. Senlis yields by treaty, and [Beauvais](@beauvais-1429) drives out its bishop, Pierre Cauchon. Creil, Pont-Sainte-Maxence, Choisy, Gournay-sur-Aronde, Aumale, Blangy and Lagny on the Marne follow. Bedford leaves Paris to the Burgundians and to Louis of Luxembourg, Henry VI's chancellor, and goes into Normandy. On 28 August, at Compiègne, a truce with Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, is signed.

These towns are now the king's.
