The traveller Ibn Battuta of [Tangier](@tangier) has been on the road since 1325 when the plague finds him in Syria. In 1348, at [Damascus](@damascus), he learns that his father died 15 years earlier. After a journey north to Aleppo he is back at Damascus in July, when the plague there is at its worst, and he goes on through Jerusalem and an emptied Gaza to [Cairo](@cairo), where he is told of 21,000 dead in a single day.

From Cairo he travels through Upper Egypt to the Red Sea port of Aydhab, takes ship to Jeddah and reaches [Mecca](@mecca) on 16 November 1348. He fasts there through Ramadan and makes the pilgrimage of early 1349, by one account his last, and then goes back with the Syrian caravan by Medina, Jerusalem and Gaza to Cairo. Pilgrims carry the plague to Mecca itself in 1349.

In the spring of 1349 Ibn Battuta sails from Egypt on a small Tunisian ship, lands on the island of Jerba and goes on to [Tunis](@tunis-1349), where he stays 36 days. Catalan sailors take him by way of Sardinia to the coast of Algeria, and he goes on overland by Tlemcen to Taza. There the news meets him: “In the town of Taza I learned the news of my mother’s death of the plague, may God the Most High have mercy on her.” He reaches [Fez](@fez-1349) in November 1349, presents himself to the Marinid sultan Abu Inan and settles in his realm. Then he goes to Tangier to visit his mother’s tomb.
