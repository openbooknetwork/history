On 11 April 1453 the Ottomans set up their guns in batteries before the weakest stretches of the walls: three before the palace of Blachernae, two before the [Charisian gate](@charisian-gate), four before the [gate of St Romanos](@st-romanos-gate) and three before the [gate of Pege](@pege-gate). Orban’s great gun first stands before the [Kaligaria gate](@kaligaria-gate) and is then moved to the Mesoteichion. In the first days of the siege Ottoman troops also take the castle of Therapia on the Bosporus and a smaller castle at the village of Studios near the Sea of Marmara.

The guns open fire on 12 April. A great gun takes two to three hours to load, so the fire is slow, and the defenders repair much of the damage after each shot. Each night they fill the breaches in the outer wall with a stockade of timber, stones and barrels of earth, and [Giustiniani](@constantinople-guns-giustiniani), who now holds the Mesoteichion with the emperor, leads the work.

On the evening of 18 April Mehmed sends janissaries and other troops against the stockade in the Lycus valley. They fill the moat in front of it and fight in the dark for four hours, and the defenders throw them back.
