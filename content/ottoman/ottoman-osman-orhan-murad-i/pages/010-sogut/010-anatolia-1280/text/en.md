The Seljuk Sultanate of Rûm has been subject to the Mongols since its defeat at Köse Dağ in 1243, and to the Ilkhanate, the Mongol state in Iran, since 1256. Sultan Kaykhusraw III reigns in [Konya](@konya), while the Ilkhan’s governors and the sultan’s regents hold the power. In 1277 the Mamluk sultan Baibars defeats the Mongols at Elbistan, and the Karamanids hold Konya for a short time. In 1284 the Ilkhan Ahmad Tekuder has Kaykhusraw executed and makes Mesud II sultan.

The marches (uç) on the western edge of the plateau face the Byzantine border. Turkmen nomads graze their herds there, and their beys raid Byzantine land and name the sultan in the Friday sermon. The strongest are the Germiyan at Kütahya, whom the Seljuks settled there to hold the other Turkmen in check. Ertuğrul’s people keep their pastures at [Söğüt](@sogut), on the march that faces Bithynia.

Michael VIII Palaiologos has ruled from [Constantinople](@constantinople) since his troops took the city back from the Latins in 1261. In Asia the empire holds Bithynia with Nicaea, the valleys of the Hermos and the Maeander, and the Aegean coast. In 1280 his son and co-emperor Andronikos rebuilds [Tralles](@tralles) in the Maeander valley. Further east lie the Empire of [Trebizond](@trebizond) on the Black Sea, the Armenian kingdom of Cilicia with its capital at [Sis](@sis), and the Mamluk sultanate in Syria and Egypt.
