The two armies meet on a narrow coastal plain between the sea and the Amanus foothills, cut by the river Pinarus with its steep banks. Which stream the Pinarus was is not known; the historian A. B. Bosworth suggests the Kuru Çay.\*

[Darius III](@issus-lines-darius) forms his line on the north bank while a screen of cavalry and light infantry covers him from across the river. He sets 30,000 Greek mercenaries opposite the Macedonian phalanx, with the Persian infantry called Cardaces beside them, and takes his place with his guard to the left of the centre.\*\* On his left the infantry runs on into the foothills, and some troops on the hill stand behind Alexander's right. Then he recalls the screen and masses most of his cavalry by the sea under [Nabarzanes](@issus-lines-nabarzanes), where the ground suits horsemen.

Alexander comes north up the plain, opening his column into line as it widens. [Alexander](@issus-lines-alexander) holds the right with the Companion cavalry, the phalanx stands in the centre, and [Parmenion](@issus-lines-parmenion) commands the left, with orders not to let go of the sea; [Craterus](@issus-lines-craterus) leads its infantry. Seeing the Persian horse gather against Parmenion, Alexander sends the Thessalian cavalry to him behind the phalanx, out of the enemy's sight. On the right he brings up archers, Agrianians and Greek mercenaries, so that his line now reaches past the Persian left. A charge of Agrianians and archers drives the troops on the hill up the slope, and [300 horsemen](@issus-lines-flank-guard) are left to watch them.

No source gives the places of the Thessalians and the allied horse within the left, or how far the Persian cavalry and the mass behind the line extended.

### Notes

\* Callisthenes, a historian with Alexander's army, put the plain at less than 14 stades, about 2.6 km, from sea to hills. Bosworth gives the plain at the Kuru Çay as about 4 km wide, and in his Commentary on Arrian he calls a plain of that width only a very rough match for Callisthenes's figure. Other modern writers place the battle at the Payas or the Deli Çay.

\*\* Arrian puts Darius in the centre of the whole army, and Callisthenes half way down the line. Curtius puts him on the left wing with 3,000 horsemen and 40,000 foot; Bosworth prefers Curtius and notes that, flanked by his own troops, Darius could still be seen as roughly central.
