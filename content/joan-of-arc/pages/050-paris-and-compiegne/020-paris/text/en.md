On 23 August 1429 Joan and the Duke of Alençon leave Charles VII at Compiègne, and on 25 August they take [Saint-Denis](@saint-denis-1429), north of Paris.\* For two weeks they skirmish at the city's gates. Paris is held for Henry VI by its garrison and townsmen under Louis of Luxembourg, Bishop of Thérouanne, and the Burgundian captain L'Isle-Adam. The king reaches Saint-Denis on 7 September, and that evening Joan and the captains lodge at [La Chapelle](@la-chapelle-1429), halfway to the city.

On Thursday 8 September, the feast of the Nativity of Our Lady,\*\* the army draws up in the pig market outside the [Porte Saint-Honoré](@porte-saint-honore). Joan, Marshal de Rais and Raoul de Gaucourt attack the gate, and the barrier and earthwork before it are burned and taken. Alençon waits behind a hill in case the defenders sally from the [Porte Saint-Denis](@porte-saint-denis-1429).\*\*\* Past the first ditch and the bank beyond it, the second ditch holds water too deep to cross. Joan sounds it with her lance and has wood thrown in, but it cannot be filled before night. Late in the day a crossbow bolt goes through her thigh, and her standard-bearer is shot dead.\*\*\*\* She will not leave the ditch, saying the place would have been taken, until she is carried back to La Chapelle. At her trial she said she was well again in five days.

Next morning, as Perceval de Cagny, a chronicler of Alençon's household, tells it, she wants to attack again, and the Baron of Montmorency rides out of Paris with fifty or sixty gentlemen to join her. Then René, Duke of Bar, and Charles, Count of Clermont, bring the king's order to come back to Saint-Denis.\*\*\*\*\*

The gates stand on the line of the old city wall; their places, like the army's road, are approximate.

### Notes

\* Cagny has them lodge there on Friday 26 August.

\*\* Monstrelet alone sets the assault on Monday 12 September, with the king leading the army from between Montmartre and Paris.

\*\*\* The Journal of the Siege, a chronicle of Orléans, has the Dukes of Alençon and Bourbon lie in wait there. Ramsay posts Alençon there with the Marshal de Boussac.

\*\*\*\* The bolt strikes her thigh in Cagny and the Journal of the Siege, and her leg in the Bourgeois of Paris, who was inside the walls, and in Lowell. Cagny has Gaucourt and others carry her back, and the Journal has Alençon send for her.

\*\*\*\*\* That night the bridge Alençon had thrown over the Seine near Saint-Denis is taken apart: by the king's order in Cagny, by some of the council without Alençon's knowledge in Lowell.
