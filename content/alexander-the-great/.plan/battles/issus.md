# Battle of Issus, November 333 BC

Section 040 (issus-and-the-coast). Card on `issus` (page `020-issus/010-issus`); three phase pages, `issus-lines`,
`issus-river`, `issus-rout`. The phases follow Arrian 2.8–11 (claims 040.6, 040.7), with Bosworth's reading where
Arrian is silent. Sides: `macedon` (Alexander's army) and `persia` (Darius's army and its Greek mercenaries), the
story's families; the Thessalian cavalry takes `thessaly` on the phase pages (section plan, "Battles").

**The site is not located** (040.5, `open`). By the cloud ruling the field is drawn at the Kuru Çay, Bosworth's
suggestion, and the text says the river is not located. So every place below is relative to the river, the sea and
the foothills, which the sources do give; its position on the map is a `reconstruction` throughout, and the
basis marks below speak only of the place of a unit relative to the others.

## Sources

- **Arrian 2.8–11**, primary, from Ptolemy and Aristobulus (Chinnock's note 255). The fullest order of battle
  of both sides: the Macedonian line from each end to the centre (2.8.3–4), the Persian line, its screen across the
  river, the force on the hill and the levy behind (2.8.5–8), the cavalry moved to the Persian right (2.8.10), and
  Alexander's changes: the Thessalians to the left, the right extended, the hill cleared (2.9). The sequence of the
  fight (2.10–11.1), the cavalry fight by the sea (2.11.2–3), Darius's flight and the pursuit (2.11.4–6), the dead
  and the camp (2.11.8–10).
- **Polybius 12.17–22**, primary, gives Callisthenes, an eyewitness. The ground: under 14 stades from sea to
  foothills, the river across it with steep banks, torrent clefts in the plain (12.17.4–5, 12.20.4). The Persian
  line from the sea: cavalry, mercenaries, peltasts to the mountains (12.17.7). Darius "half way down the line"
  (12.18.9); the Persian cavalry charge across the river (12.18.11). Alexander's army and its deployment 32, 16 and
  8 deep (12.19); the line kept clear of the foothills, the crescent against the hill (12.21.5–6). Polybius's own
  reckonings (12.18.4–8, 12.21.7) are his inferences, not Callisthenes's facts.
- **Curtius 3.8.27–3.11.27**, primary (vulgate). The Persian order with commanders: Nabarzanes and the cavalry on the
  right, Thymondas and 30,000 Greek mercenaries, Aristomedes and 20,000 foot on the left, Darius on the left with
  3,000 horse and 40,000 foot (3.9.1–5). The Macedonian order (3.9.7–10). The Persian cavalry's opening charge on
  the left, the Thessalians sent round (3.11.1–3); the fight about Darius's chariot (3.11.7–12); the Thessalians'
  fall-back and counter-charge (3.11.13–15); the flight and the losses (3.11.16–27).
- **Diodorus 17.33–36**, primary (vulgate), told without the ground (Welles, note 56: "his narrative is very
  conventional"). Alexander's charge at Darius, Oxathres's stand, the chariot, the cavalry's flight through the
  defiles (17.33.5–34.9); the losses (17.36.6). His cavalry in front of the phalanx (17.33.1) is not used.
- **Plutarch 20.8–9**, primary: Alexander "extended it past the enemy's left, got on their flank".
- **Bosworth, C&E ch. 2.VI**, scholarly. The site and width, the Persian line, the Macedonian deployment and the
  battle in three movements: the charge and the phalanx's crisis, Darius's flight, the general rout. Gives the
  end state of the middle phase in one sentence (see phase 2).
- **Bosworth, Comm. on 2.7.1–2.11.4**, scholarly. The candidates for the Pinarus (Payas, Deli Çay, Kuru Çay); the
  phalanx order; Darius's place; the gamma of the Persian left; the Thessalians' move; the extension of the right
  and the gap in the Persian left; the troops Alexander charged; the Cardaces left out of Arrian; the cavalry fight
  on the left.
- **Bosworth, CAH VI**, scholarly. The same frame: position north of the Pinarus, 4 km "compatible with the 14
  stades"; Alexander charges the Cardaces; Darius's guard falls; the phalanx of mercenaries "folded from the right".
- **Engels, Appendix 2**, scholarly, for distances: "7.75 miles from the Pillars to the Payas River"; 14 stades
  "(2,832 yards)"; the Payas plain "about 3,828 yards". He holds the Pinarus to be the Payas.
- **Tarn pp. 24–27**, scholarly, a different reading: the Pinarus "probably the Deli", Darius "behind the centre",
  the hypaspists wheel on the Greeks. Used only for the type of the hill force and in the report's differences.
- **WP, Battle of Issus**, for the coordinates (36.7525 N, 36.1923 E, the infobox) and the modern candidates (Deli,
  Payas: Hammond and Devine). Its battle narrative (Alexander on foot with the hypaspists, the phalanx forced back
  across the river) follows modern writers not read here and is not used.

## Ground

**The site.** "The Persians had established a defensive position south of the Pinarus, one of the many streams
discharging into the sea from the Amanus. Its exact location is a mystery which, given the changed hydrography of
the area, may never be resolved. It may be that the battle was fought in the vicinity of the Kuru Cay, some 15 km
north of the Pillar of Jonah, where the plain is relatively narrow, about 4 km wide" (Bosworth, C&E). The Kuru Çay
"lies 3 km north of the Payas, and the banks apparently are steep enough to give some justification to
Callisthenes' description and at the same time permit a cavalry charge" (Bosworth, Comm. on 7.1). `reconstruction`
(cloud ruling; 040.5 `open`). Bosworth's "south of the Pinarus" is a slip: his own CAH text has the Persians "took
up position north of the river Pinarus", Arrian puts them "beyond the river" from Alexander's side (2.9.2), and
Welles has "a fortified line along the north bank of the river Pinarus" (Diodorus, note 56). The plan puts them on
the north bank.

- **Frame origin**: the mouth of the Kuru Çay. The article's coordinates, 36.7525 N, 36.1923 E (WP infobox), lie on
  the shore by the Payas (WP: Hammond and Devine's Pinarus); 3 km north of them (Bosworth) the coast that harita
  draws (`harita coast Turkey 36.10 36.70 36.30 36.90`: `[36.204,36.769] [36.200,36.785]`) gives about
  **[36.2015, 36.7795]**. `reconstruction`. The implementor fits the point to the drawn coast.
- **Frame bearing**: u inland along the river, about **80°**, square to the shore, which harita draws running about
  345° there. w runs 170°, from the river towards the Macedonians. `reconstruction`: Callisthenes has the river
  "running obliquely across this space" (Polybius 12.17.5), Bosworth paraphrases "at right angles to both" (Comm.
  on 7.1); no file gives the Kuru Çay's course. If the relief on the map shows the stream's valley, the implementor
  follows it.
- **The river**: `water`, a path from the mouth (0, 0) inland to the foothills, through about (1,000, 30),
  (2,000, −20), (3,000, 40), (4,000, 0) and on to (4,800, −150) into the hills; width about 40 m for legibility.
  Steep banks: "the river running obliquely across this space, with gaps in its banks just where it issues from
  the mountains, but in its whole course through the plain as far as the sea passing between steep hills difficult
  to climb" (Polybius 12.17.5); "upon the bank of the river, which was in many parts steep and precipitous"
  (Arrian 2.10.1). `sourced` for the banks, `reconstruction` for the line. Width of the stream: `unknown`.
- **The sea**: west of u = 0, the coast as harita draws it. "This general had been ordered not to abandon the sea"
  (Arrian 2.8.4). `sourced`.
- **The foothills of the Amanus**: east of about u = 4,000. "towards the mountain on the right and towards the sea
  on the left" (Arrian 2.8.2). The bay in the hills on the Macedonian right: "the mountain near which they were
  posted in one part sloped a great way back and formed a sort of bay, like a bay in the sea, and afterwards bending
  forwards caused the men who had been posted at the foot of it to be behind Alexander's right wing" (Arrian
  2.8.7). `sourced` for the shape, `reconstruction` for the place: the hill force stands at about (4,700, +1,500).
  Not drawn as land; the map's relief shows the range.
- **The stockade**: "in certain places, where it seemed more easy to ascend, he extended a stockade along it"
  (Arrian 2.10.1). Its places are not given, and Bosworth holds it "may be an elaboration by Ptolemy himself"
  (Comm. on 10.1). Not drawn; a text may mention it.
- **Ravines**: "the torrents descending the mountains have formed so many clefts in the plain that most of the
  Persians in their flight perished in such fissures" (Callisthenes in Polybius 12.20.4). No places: not drawn.

**Facing.** The Macedonians come back north from the Pillar of Jonah (Arrian 2.8.1–2; Bosworth, C&E) and face north
across the river: `f.face(270)`, about 350°. The Persians stand on the north bank and face south: `f.face(90)`,
about 170°. `sourced` (Arrian 2.9.2: "the main body of Persians beyond the river"). The Persian right and the
Macedonian left rest on the sea (west, low u); the Persian left and the Macedonian right on the foothills (east,
high u). `sourced` (Arrian 2.8.4, 2.8.7, 2.8.10).

**Width of the front.** `sourced`, two figures. "The distance, he says, from the sea to the foot of the hills is
not more than fourteen stades" (Callisthenes in Polybius 12.17.4), "14 stadia (2,832 yards)" (Engels), about 2.6
km. At the Kuru Çay "the plain is relatively narrow, about 4 km wide" (Bosworth, C&E), "a distance compatible with
the 14 stades given by Callisthenes" (Bosworth, CAH). The drawing uses Bosworth's **4 km**, the width at the site it
is placed on; a text may give Callisthenes's 14 stades with his name. The Persian left goes on into the foothills
beyond that (Bosworth, C&E: "continued the line into the foothills of the Amanus").

**Distance between the lines** before the advance: `unknown`. Callisthenes has Alexander deploy "at a distance of
about forty stades from the enemy" (Polybius 12.20.1), the vulgate Darius's camp "a mere 30 stades from the defiles"
(Bosworth, Comm. on 8.3). For the drawing only, the Macedonian front halts about 700 m south of the river, out of
missile range ("he caused them to rest for some time, and then led them forward", Arrian 2.10.1).

**Bbox.** One for all three phases: u −800 to +5,800, w −3,100 to +1,900 (about 6.6 × 5.0 km), in the story's
`max_zoom` 14.

## Order of battle

### Macedon, from the sea (left, west) to the foothills (right, east)

"First, upon the right wing near the mountain he placed his infantry guard and the shield-bearers, under the
command of Nicanor, son of Parmenio; next to these the regiment of Coenus, and close to them that of Perdiccas.
These troops were posted as far as the middle of the heavy-armed infantry to one beginning from the right. On the
left wing first stood the regiment of Amyntas, then that of Ptolemy, and close to this that of Meleager. The
infantry on the left had been placed under the command of Craterus; but Parmenio held the chief direction of the
whole left wing" (Arrian 2.8.3–4). Arrian groups the battalions "from each extremity to the centre" (Bosworth,
Comm. on 8.3); Curtius gives the same order from the right: "Nicanor ... next to him stood Coenus, Perdiccas,
Meleager, Ptolemaeus, and Amyntas" (3.9.7).

No source gives a strength for any Macedonian unit at Issus. Diodorus's figures (17.17.3–4) are of the army that
crossed in 334 BC and are not carried to Issus: garrisons and reinforcements changed it (Tarn p. 25: "Many of the
allies had been left with Calas"). Every unit strength below is `unknown`.

### Persia, from the sea (right, west) to the foothills (left, east)

"Of the heavy armed infantry, he placed first the 30,000 Greek mercenaries to oppose the phalanx of the
Macedonians, and on both sides of these he placed 60,000 of the men called Cardaces, who were also heavy-armed
infantry ... He also posted 20,000 men near the mountain on their left and facing Alexander's right. Some of these
troops were also in the rear of Alexander's army ... The remaining multitude of Darius's light-armed and heavy-armed
infantry was marshalled by nations to an unserviceable depth and placed behind the Grecian mercenaries and the
Persian army arranged in phalanx" (Arrian 2.8.6–8). "Cavalry were massed by the sea to the right; mercenary
infantry occupied the centre, while the Persian national infantry continued the line into the foothills of the
Amanus, extending forward almost at right angles to envelop the Macedonian flank. The rest of the army was massed
in depth to the rear of the line" (Bosworth, C&E). Every Persian figure below is an ancient figure and goes with

## Phases

Positions are (u, w) in metres in the frame above: u from the river mouth inland, w from the river towards the
Macedonians (south positive). Sizes are for the drawing only and appear in no text.

### Phase 1: `issus-lines`, "The lines on the Pinarus"

**Opens.** The Macedonians come down from the Pillar of Jonah and open from column into line as the plain widens:

2.10.1). `sourced`.

of the Pinarus, his left thrust back to its own bank, and the phalanx stationary in the stream bed" (Bosworth, C&E).

### Phase 3: `issus-rout`, "Darius flees"

**Opens.** The blocks of phase 2. `sourced` as there.

**Two accounts of Darius's flight**, `disputed`. Arrian: "as soon as the left wing of Darius was terrified and routed
by Alexander, and the Persian king perceived that this part of his army was severed from the rest, without any
further delay he began to flee in his chariot along with the first" (2.11.4). The vulgate: Oxathres "interposed the
cavalry which he commanded directly before the chariot of Darius" (Curtius 3.11.8), and Darius fled only when his
horses, "pierced with spears and frantic from pain, had begun to toss the yoke" (Curtius 3.11.11; Diodorus
17.34.2–7). Bosworth: "Darius did resist more stubbornly than Arrian suggests, but, even so, the engagement did not
last long" (C&E); "The Persian guard fell in defence of their king, who escaped capture by flight" (CAH). The
drawing shows what both have: Alexander's horse reaches the guard, the guard breaks, Darius flees. The text tells
the two accounts.

## Card

- **Name and date**: Battle of Issus, November 333 BC: "in the month Maimacterion, when Nicostratus was archon of the
  Athenians" (Arrian 2.11.10); "perhaps in November, 333 B.C. ... or somewhat earlier" (Welles, Diodorus note 56).
- **Sides**: `macedon` (Alexander's army) and `persia` (Darius's army with its Greek mercenaries).
- **Commanders**: Macedon: Alexander; Parmenion, over the whole left; Craterus, over the infantry of the left (Arrian
  2.8.4). Persia: Darius III; Nabarzanes, over the cavalry of the right ("Nabarzanes with the cavalry guarded the
  right wing", Curtius 3.9.1; "the cavalry were commanded by Nabarzanes, the Persian chiliarch", Bosworth, Comm. on
  8.5). Nabarzanes is in names.md; the section plan has the other Persian commanders `unknown` and should take him.
  Thymondas and Aristomedes (Curtius 3.9.2–3) are not in names.md and stay off the card.
- **Strengths** (as the section plan):
  - Macedon, `single` (040.27): 40,000 foot and 4,500 horse at the crossing to Asia, joined by 5,000 foot and 800
    horse before Cilicia (Callisthenes, in Polybius 12.19.1–2); Polybius reckons "forty-two thousand foot and five
    thousand horse" present (12.19.3). The card says it is higher than Arrian's and Diodorus's counts of the army
    that crossed (030.3). Arrian gives no figure for Issus.
  - Persia, `disputed` (040.8): about 600,000 fighting men (Arrian 2.8.8; Plutarch 18); 400,000 foot and 100,000
    horse (Diodorus 17.31.2); 30,000 cavalry and 30,000 mercenaries (Callisthenes, in Polybius 12.18.2); Curtius
    250,000 foot and 62,000 horse (3.2.4–9, through Bosworth, Comm. on 8.8). Bosworth: "it is impossible to give
    even an educated guess at the reality".
- **Casualties** (040.9):
  - Persia: about 100,000 with more than 10,000 cavalry (Arrian 2.11.8); more than 100,000 infantry and not less
    than 10,000 cavalry (Diodorus 17.36.6); 100,000 foot and 10,000 horse (Curtius 3.11.27).
  - Macedon, `disputed`: 300 foot and 150 horse (Diodorus 17.36.6); about 4,500 wounded, 302 foot missing and 150
    horse killed (Curtius 3.11.27, Rolfe's text); Ptolemy, son of Seleucus, and about 120 Macedonians of note killed
    in the centre (Arrian 2.10.7). Bosworth: "According to the sources Alexander had lost some 500 men, the
    Persians 100,000. These are propaganda figures" (C&E).
- **Outcome**: Macedonian victory. Darius flees, and his camp is taken with his mother, wife, son and two daughters
  (Arrian 2.11.9; 040.10).
- **max_zoom**: the field is about 6 km across, within the story's 14; nothing to set in `battle.yaml` unless the
  check asks.
