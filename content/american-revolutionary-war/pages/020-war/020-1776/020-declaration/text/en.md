By spring the colonies are electing new governments and instructing their delegates. On 7 June Richard Henry Lee of Virginia moves “that these United Colonies are, and of right ought to be, free and independent States”. Congress appoints a committee of five to draft a declaration. Thomas Jefferson writes it, and Adams and Franklin revise it.

On 28 June the fort of palmetto logs on Sullivan’s Island, at the entrance to Charleston harbour, beats off a British fleet.

Lee’s resolution passes on 2 July with twelve colonies in favour and New York abstaining. On 4 July Congress adopts the amended text. It lists the king’s offences, from the dissolved assemblies to the Quebec Act and the hired German troops, and declares the political connection with Great Britain dissolved.
