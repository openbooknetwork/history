When Alexander has recovered, the fleet goes on down the Acesines into the [Indus](@indus-325). The Malli and the land down to the sea are put under Macedonian governors, and the peoples along the lower river are forced to submit; those who rebel are crushed.

Before the fleet reaches the delta, Alexander divides the army for the journey west. In Arrian's account Craterus takes the battalions of Attalus, son of Andromenes, Meleager and Antigenes, the elephants and the men no longer fit to serve. He is to lead them through [Arachosia](@arachosia-325) and [Drangiana](@drangiana-325) to [Carmania](@carmania-325), where the two armies will meet. Bosworth thinks Alexander, who meant to take the harder road along the coast himself, could spare men who had had enough of hardship. The column's route on the map, by the Bolan Pass, is approximate: Bosworth names the Bolan or the Mulla Pass.

When Alexander reaches [Patala](@patala-325), at the head of the delta, he finds the city and the fields empty. Bosworth notes that most scholars place Patala at the ruins of Bahmanabad, north-east of Hyderabad, and after Aristobulus he puts the army's arrival in the middle of July 325 BC.

Alexander has a harbour and docks built, then sails down the Indus with his fastest ships. The ships are battered by a storm and then left stranded by the tide, which the Macedonians had never seen. At last he passes the mouth of the river into the open sea, the [Indian Ocean](@indian-ocean-325). For the Greeks this was the Ocean, the outer sea they believed ran all round the earth. There he sacrifices bulls to Poseidon and casts golden vessels into the sea, asking safe passage for the fleet that is to sail on to the Persian Gulf.
