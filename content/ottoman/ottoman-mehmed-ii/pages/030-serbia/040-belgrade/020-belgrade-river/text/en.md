When news comes that Hunyadi is gathering a relief army, Mehmed holds a council. [Karaca Pasha](@belgrade-river-karaca), the beylerbey of Rumelia, asks to cross the Danube with part of the army and stop Hunyadi there. The commanders of Rumelia oppose him, and the council decides to keep the whole army before the fortress. Hunyadi camps across the river without hindrance.

On 14 July 1456 [Hunyadi](@belgrade-river-hunyadi) comes down the Danube with his boats, about 200 of them and only one a galley, against the Ottoman fleet that closes the river above the city. About 40 boats from the fortress join the attack. After five hours of fighting the Christians have sunk three large Ottoman galleys and taken four large boats and 20 smaller ones.

With the river open, Hunyadi brings his troops and food into the fortress, and he keeps bringing in men by boat. Before he came the fortress held about 6,000 soldiers and 2,500 crusaders by one account. The defenders gain heart, and the Ottomans now besiege a fortress that they cannot cut off.
