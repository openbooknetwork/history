# The long road home

## Header
- Years: summer 325 BC to June 323 BC (the death between the evenings of 10 and 11 June 323 BC, 080.12)
- Opens: Alexander's empire runs from the League of Corinth, Thessaly and Macedon (Sparta outside) to the Indus
  delta and the Ocean, the brief's `macedon-325a`, with Gedrosia and Ora still outside it; Taxiles and Porus hold their kingdoms
  as his allies, the Saka stay beyond the Jaxartes. The army stands at Patala, the fleet is fitted out under
  Nearchus, and Craterus's column with three brigades, the elephants and the unfit has gone west by Arachosia and
  Drangiana (070.17). The reader has been told of the refusal at the Hyphasis, the arrow in the Mallian town and
  the Ocean reached.
- Tells:
  - summer to autumn 325 BC: the army leaves Patala for Ora and crosses the Gedrosian desert to Pura in sixty
    days, with heavy losses whose scale the sources dispute (080.1, 080.2)
  - autumn 325 to winter 325–324 BC: Nearchus brings the fleet along the coasts of Ora, Gedrosia and the
    Fish-eaters to Carmania; Craterus's column rejoins; Alexander meets Nearchus; Cleander and Sitalces are
    executed with 600 of their men, the first of a series of purges (080.3, 080.4, 070.17)
  - winter 325–324 to spring 324 BC: Alexander goes by Persis to Susa, Hephaestion with the main army nearer the
    coast, the fleet on to the Tigris and Susiana; the purges go on; the Susa weddings; the 30,000 Iranian youths
    trained in Macedonian arms arrive (080.4, 080.5, 080.6)
  - spring to summer 324 BC: from Susa to the Tigris and up it to Opis; the Opis mutiny, the executions, the
    Persian commands, the feast of reconciliation; about 10,000 veterans go home under Craterus to replace
    Antipater (080.7, 080.8)
  - autumn 324 BC: Hephaestion dies of a fever at Ecbatana; Perdiccas takes the body to Babylon (080.9)
  - spring to June 323 BC: Babylon: the fleet readied for Arabia; the fever from 18 Daesius; the death between
    the evenings of 10 and 11 June 323 BC; the cause not known; the memoranda of the last plans read and laid
    aside after the death; Roxana has Stateira and her sister killed (080.10–080.14)
- Introduces: people: Nearchus, Stateira, Parysatis, Drypetis, Medius of Larissa, Cassander, Cyrus the Great,
  Semiramis; Engels as the holder of a view. Places: Ora, Gedrosia, Pura, Salmus, Harmozeia, Opis, Libya, Carthage,
  the Pillars of Heracles, Arabia. Peoples: the Fish-eaters, the Carthaginians, the Epigoni.
- Battles: none (brief); no phase pages
- Hands on: Alexander dies at Babylon with the empire whole, from Greece to the Indus, and no heir named: Roxana
  is pregnant, Arrhidaeus is at Babylon. Craterus and the 10,000 veterans have got no further than Cilicia (080.19),
  Antipater still holds Macedon under orders to bring fresh troops, the fleet for Arabia stands ready at Babylon
  and the army lays the last plans aside. Unsettled: who rules, and what killed him (080.13, open).

## Pages

**010 gedrosian-desert** — late summer and autumn 325 BC. The army marches from Patala to the land of Ora and
then through the Gedrosian desert to Pura, the capital of Gedrosia, in sixty days from Ora; it turns down to the
coast for seven days to find water, and the flash flood, the heat and the hunger destroy much of the baggage
train and the camp followers. Why: Nearchus, through Arrian, says Alexander meant to outdo Semiramis and Cyrus
and to bring supplies near the fleet; Engels reads the march as planned to supply the fleet and wrecked when the
monsoon held the fleet in the Indus. Map change: the army's route runs west from Patala through Ora and the
Makran to Pura, and Gedrosia and the coast of Ora come into the empire (`macedon-325a` gives way to
`macedon-325`).
Rests on: 080.1 (single for the motive: the text names Nearchus, through Arrian, and gives Engels's reading as a
second view), 080.2 (disputed: three accounts, each with its author: Plutarch, not a quarter of the fighting
force brought back from 120,000 foot and 15,000 horse; Engels, about a quarter of the land army escaped;
Bosworth, Plutarch's base figure is exaggerated, at least 18,000 Macedonian infantry reached Opis and there was
no military disaster; the text says the losses were heavy and their scale is disputed, per the cloud ruling),
080.15 (agreed for the line, which the text calls approximate; disputed for where the worst suffering fell: the
text gives Engels, in the Kolwa depression before Turbat, and Bosworth, the Kolwa and Kech valleys still able to
yield a surplus and the hardship growing west of Pasni, each with its author), 070.17 (the army divided before the
march; Craterus's column is not in the desert).

**020 nearchus-carmania** — autumn 325 to winter 325–324 BC. When the monsoon winds drop, Nearchus sails the
fleet from the Indus along the coasts of Ora, Gedrosia and the Fish-eaters and puts in on the inhabited coast of
Carmania; Alexander, marching on from Pura, is joined in Carmania by Craterus's column and by the generals from
Media, has Cleander and Sitalces, Parmenion's killers, put to death with 600 of their men on their subjects'
charges, and meets Nearchus, who is sent back to sail on to Susiana and the Tigris. Why: the fleet waited for the
season (Arrian 6.21); the executions were meant to warn every governor (Arrian 6.27), and Bosworth makes them the
first of a series of purges of satraps, mostly Iranian. Map change: the fleet's route along the coast to
Harmozeia, the army's from Pura into Carmania, and Craterus's column arriving from Arachosia and Drangiana.
Rests on: 080.3, 080.4, 080.17 (single, Bosworth: the landfall at Harmozeia, told with his name), 080.20 (single,
Bosworth: the march across the Jaz Murian into the Halil Rud valley, where Craterus and the convoys join), 070.17
(single, Arrian: Craterus's column and its route). Where the meeting took place is not known: Diodorus puts Salmus
by the sea, Arrian has Nearchus go up into the interior, and Bosworth places the Carmanian capital inland near
Khanu (080.20); the text says the site is unknown.

**030 susa-weddings** — winter 325–324 to spring 324 BC. Alexander goes with a light force by Pasargadae to
Persepolis while Hephaestion takes the main army and the elephants by a route nearer the coast; the united army
goes on to Susa, where Nearchus waits with the fleet. The purges go on in Persis and at Susa. At Susa Alexander
marries Stateira, the eldest daughter of Darius, and, by Aristobulus, Parysatis, daughter of Artaxerxes III;
Hephaestion marries Drypetis; Companions take Persian and Median brides in the Persian rite; more than 10,000
Macedonians with Asian wives receive gifts; and the 30,000 Iranian youths trained in Macedonian arms arrive, to
the Macedonians' resentment. Why: the weddings bound the Achaemenid house to the conquerors (Bosworth ch.
XVIII), and the youths were formed as a counterpart of the phalanx (Diodorus). Map change: Alexander's and
Hephaestion's routes from Carmania through Persis to Susa, and the fleet's from Harmozeia along the Persian Gulf
to Susiana.
Rests on: 080.4 (the purges go on: Bosworth's "series of purges"), 080.16 (single, Bosworth's conjecture: the
text says the exact routes cannot be traced and names no town on them), 080.17 (single, Bosworth: the fleet by the
mouth of the Euphrates to the bridge on the Pasitigris near Susa), 080.5 (agreed for the weddings; disputed for
the number of Companions: the text gives Arrian's eighty besides the named grooms and Briant's ninety-one, each
with its author), 080.6.

**040 opis-mutiny** — spring to summer 324 BC. Alexander sails from Susa down the Karun to the sea and along the
coast to the mouth of the Tigris, Hephaestion leads the infantry overland to the Gulf, and the reunited army
goes up the Tigris to Opis, breaking the Persian weirs. At Opis, told that the unfit will be sent home, the
Macedonians demand to go all together and mock his father Ammon; Alexander has thirteen ringleaders executed,
shuts himself away and gives the commands to Persians; the army yields, and at a feast of reconciliation he prays
for harmony and partnership in rule between Macedonians and Persians. About 10,000 veterans go home under
Craterus, with a talent each beyond their pay, Craterus to replace Antipater in Macedon. Why: the Epigoni, the
foreign horsemen among the Companions and Alexander's Persian dress, which the reader met in 060, had long
offended the army (Arrian 7.6, 7.8). Map
change: the routes from Susa to the Tigris and up it to Opis, and the veterans' column setting out west.
Rests on: 080.7 (agreed for the mutiny and the reconciliation; the 9,000 at the feast is told as Arrian's
"common account", and the text does not give Plutarch's 9,000, which belong to the Susa wedding feast; the place,
Opis, is Arrian's, and Diodorus tells the mutiny before Alexander leaves Susa without naming Opis), 080.8, 080.19
(single, Bosworth: Craterus sent first to Cilicia to oversee the armaments for the western campaign; told with his
name), and 060.27 (agreed: Alexander's Persian court dress); the text tells the dress as 060 tells it, as one grievance among the Epigoni and the
foreign horsemen, with Arrian 7.6 and 7.8 for the army's anger at it.

**050 death-of-hephaestion** — summer to autumn 324 BC. The court moves from Opis up into the Zagros by
Bagistane and the plain of the royal horses to Ecbatana; during the games and drinking there Hephaestion falls
ill of a fever and dies on the seventh day; Alexander's grief is great by every account, and Perdiccas takes the
body to Babylon for a funeral pyre. Why: the court went to the Persian kings' summer capital from the heat of the
plain (Bosworth ch. XIX); Diodorus and Plutarch tie the illness to drink. Map change: the route from Opis to
Ecbatana, and the body's route from Ecbatana to Babylon.
Rests on: 080.9, 080.18 (single, Bosworth: the court moves up by easy stages with a halt at Bagistane).

**060 death-in-babylon** — winter 324–323 to June 323 BC. Alexander comes down to Babylon, where Nearchus's
fleet has come up the Euphrates from the Gulf and warships carried in sections from Phoenicia to Thapsacus have
come down the river; a fleet is readied for Arabia. After drinking with Medius of Larissa he falls ill on 18 Daesius, and
the Royal Journals follow the fever day by day; he dies on 29 Aiaru by the Babylonian diary, between the evenings
of 10 and 11 June 323 BC, aged 32 years and 8 months by Aristobulus. The cause is not known: the Journals give a
fever, and the story that Antipater sent poison by Cassander is a rumour that Arrian and Plutarch report.
After his death Perdiccas finds his memoranda, with a thousand warships for a war against Carthage and the west,
a road along Libya to the Pillars of Heracles, temples and a tomb for Philip, and the army lays them aside; and,
by Plutarch, Roxana, pregnant, has Stateira and her sister killed with Perdiccas's knowledge. Why: the Arabian
expedition was the first step of the western plans (Bosworth ch. XVIII–XIX). Map change: the routes of the king,
of Nearchus's fleet and of the Phoenician warships converging on Babylon, and Arabia named as the fleet's goal.
Rests on: 080.10 (single for the list of memoranda: the text names Diodorus and says they were read after the
death, and may add Bosworth's judgement that the western project, though attested by sources of dubious
authenticity, has no cogent reason to be dismissed as apocryphal; agreed for the Arabian fleet), 080.11, 080.12 (written "between the evenings of 10 and 11 June 323 BC" in the
text and "June 323 BC" in labels, per the cloud ruling; not "11 June"), 080.13 (open: the text says the cause is
not known, gives the Journals' fever and the poison story as a rumour reported by Arrian and Plutarch, and
Plutarch's word that no one suspected poison at first), 080.14 (single: told as Plutarch's account).

road home".

All routes and markers are the family `macedon` (the king's army, Craterus's column and the fleet are all
Macedonian), except the marker `arabia-323`, which no family holds and takes no `color`. Markers carry a year in
their id so that they cannot clash with the ids of other sections. Each id has one note in
`shared/markers.yaml` (step 7 ruling): `patala-325b` is this section's Patala, apart from 070's `patala-325`;

Ecbatana, Persepolis or Thapsacus with the same note, the cartographer may take its marker in place of mine and
say so; where the note differs, mine stays under its own id.

Zones: the section opens on `macedon-325a`, 070's last zone (the Indus valley to Patala and the Ocean, July 325
BC; Gedrosia and Ora left out), which no page of 080 shows. `macedon-325` is the brief's zone for page 010 on
(`macedon`, `macedon-325a` with Gedrosia and the coast of Ora, late 325 BC; reconstruction from 070.15–070.17,

these maps: Thessaly, the League and Sparta lie west of every bbox, Taxila and Porus north-east of page 010's.

## Choices
- The desert march (010) and Nearchus's voyage (020) get a page each though they cross the same Makran in the
  same autumn, which the rule of shared pages would merge: the brief gives them two lines, they rest on separate
  accounts (Arrian 6.21–26 and Bosworth ch. XVII for the land, Arrian 6.28, Diodorus 17.106 and Bosworth ch.
  XVIII for the sea), and two long routes on one coast would bury each other's labels. The voyage page carries
  the reunion in Carmania, where both routes end.
- The brief's line "the reckoning with the satraps; the Susa weddings" is spread over two pages: the executions
  in Carmania on 020, where they happened, and the purges going on in Persis and at Susa on 030 with the weddings.
- The Epigoni (080.6) go on the weddings page, where Arrian and Diodorus have them arrive; the veterans' return
  (080.8) goes on the Opis page, where it follows the feast.
- Roxana's killing of Stateira (080.14) closes 060 as Plutarch's account, rather than waiting for 090, since the
  digest gives it to this section.
- The Babylon page frames Mesopotamia, the Gulf and the Levant, where the last plans were set moving, not the
  whole empire; 090's first page shows the whole empire at the settlement.
- Each route stops where the sources leave it: Craterus's veterans in Cilicia, the Arabian fleet unsailed (no
  route, a label for its goal), the western plans unmapped (Carthage and the Pillars of Heracles lie west of the
  story's extent).
- Nearchus's voyage is drawn with no coastal station between the Indus and Harmozeia (decisions).
- `macedon-324`, the Map part's `zones.yaml` entry, serves pages 030–060: the outline of `macedon-325` unchanged.
- Events in the sources read with no digest line are not told: Calanus's death, the tomb of Cyrus, Harpalus's
  flight and the Exiles' Decree, the Cossaean campaign of winter 324–323 BC, the Pallacopas canal and the last
  Alexandria, Leonnatus's battle in Ora, Peucestas's 20,000 Persians in the phalanx. The route from Ecbatana to
  Babylon is drawn without the Cossaean detour.
- Page dates are given by season: Engels (Patala in mid-July, Pura in late October, the reunion in mid-January
  324) and Bosworth (Ora left in early October, the reunion in the winter of 325–324) disagree on the months.
