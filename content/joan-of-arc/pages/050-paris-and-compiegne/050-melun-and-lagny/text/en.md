The truce with Burgundy runs out at Easter, 16 April 1430, and Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, takes the field on the Oise as soon as it is past. At the end of March Joan leaves the court at [Sully](@sully-1430) without the king's leave, as if riding out for pleasure, and rides north with her little household of soldiers.

[Melun](@melun-1430), held by the English and then the Burgundians since Henry V took it, drives out its garrison about Easter and comes over to Charles VII. Whether Joan is there before it rises is not known. In Easter week, in the trenches at Melun, her voices, which she names as Saint Catherine and Saint Margaret, tell her she will be taken before Saint John's Day, 24 June. They tell her so nearly every day after, but never say when.

She rides on to [Lagny](@lagny-1430) on the Marne, whose garrison harries the English of Paris. Near the town she and the garrisons' men meet Franquet d'Arras, a Burgundian captain of partisans, back from a raid with about 300 men. His archers, on foot, hold off her first attacks until the garrison of Lagny comes up with culverins and crossbows. His men are killed or taken, and he is captured.

Joan wants to exchange Franquet for the keeper of an inn in Paris, and gives him up to justice when she hears the innkeeper is dead. He is tried for a fortnight before the bailli of Senlis and a jury of Lagny men, confesses to murder, theft and treason, and is beheaded. The Burgundians blame her for his death.

Melun is the king's.
