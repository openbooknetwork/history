Once Rouen is his, Charles VII has the trial that condemned Joan in 1431 looked into. In February 1450\* he commissions Guillaume Bouillé, a doctor of theology, who hears seven witnesses at [Rouen](@rouen-1456) on 4 and 5 March, and the matter goes no further. In late April and May 1452 Cardinal Guillaume d'Estouteville, papal legate in France, opens a second enquiry at Rouen with Jean Bréhal, Inquisitor of France; some twenty witnesses are heard.\*\*

@image nullification-trial-manuscript-bfb664

On 11 June 1455 Pope Calixtus III authorises a review of the case. His judges are Jean Juvénal des Ursins, Archbishop of Reims, Guillaume Chartier, Bishop of Paris, and Richard Olivier de Longueil, Bishop of Coutances, and they take Bréhal with them. The court holds its first sitting in [Notre-Dame](@notre-dame-1455) in Paris on 7 November 1455. Joan's mother, Isabelle Romée, in mourning, comes before the judges with her sons Jean d'Arc and Pierre d'Arc, shows them the pope's letter and, weeping, presents her petition. Men of Orléans, where she has lived for fifteen years by Lowell's account, come with her. A second public sitting follows on 17 November.

@image isabelle-romee-retrial-miniature-2b4164

The witnesses are questioned where they live: in January and February 1456 at [Domrémy](@domremy-1456) and Vaucouleurs, in February and March at [Orléans](@orleans-1456), in April and May at Paris, and at Rouen in December and again in May. On 28 May her squire, Jean d'Aulon, gives his evidence at [Lyon](@lyon-1456).\*\*\*

The judges give their verdict at Rouen, in the great hall of the archbishop's palace, at eight o'clock on Wednesday 7 July 1456; the Archbishop of Reims reads it out. The trial of 1431 and its sentences, with the abjuration and the execution, are declared "null, non-existent, without value or effect", in Murray's translation. The sentence is proclaimed that day at Saint-Ouen and the next in the Old Market, where a cross is put up.

On the map only Calais is English. Bar and Lorraine keep their approximate edges of 1429, since no source here gives them for 1456.

### Notes

\* Murray dates the commission 13 February, Beaucourt 15 February.

\*\* Whose step the enquiry of 1452 was is told three ways. Beaucourt says the legate acted on his own authority. Murray says he acted at the formal request of Isabelle Romée. Lowell says the petition was pressed in Charles VII's name for five years, and was brought in the name of the mother and her two sons only in 1455.

\*\*\* The witnesses are counted differently. Murray gives 34 at Domrémy and Vaucouleurs, 41 at Orléans, 20 at Paris and 19 at Rouen, besides d'Aulon. Lowell gives considerably more than a hundred, besides those heard in 1450 and 1452. The bull of canonisation of 1920 gives 123.
