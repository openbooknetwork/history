Caracol, on the Vaca Plateau in present-day Belize, lies 76 km southeast of Tikal and is at first a client of the larger city. Altar 21 at Caracol records that Yajaw Te' K'inich II takes the throne there in April AD 553 under the authority of the Tikal king Wak Chan K'awiil. In 556 Tikal defeats Caracol in an "axe" war. Caracol then changes sides and accepts the Kaanul king of Dzibanche as its overlord.

In April 562 Caracol and the Kaanul kingdom defeat Tikal in a "star war", named after a glyph that shows a star pouring liquid on the earth. Martin and Grube name the Kaanul king as Sky Witness, later studies of Dzibanche as K'ahk' Ti' Ch'ich'. Wak Chan K'awiil is probably captured and sacrificed, which ends his line on the throne of Tikal.

Tikal is not sacked, but its power is broken. Its Stela 17, raised by Wak Chan K'awiil, is the last monument set up in the city for more than a century, and much of its public sculpture is deliberately damaged. This gap, the Tikal hiatus, divides the Early from the Late Classic in the usual chronology.

Caracol grows quickly in the following decades, and Yajaw Te' K'inich II rebuilds Caana, its largest building. The account of these wars survives on the damaged Altar 21, which his son K'an II sets in the centre of the Caracol ballcourt in 633.
