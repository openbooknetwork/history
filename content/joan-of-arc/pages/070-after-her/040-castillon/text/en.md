In 1451 Charles VII's armies take Gascony, the English duchy of the south-west. [Bordeaux](@bordeaux-1453) agrees terms on 12 June and is handed over to the French commander Dunois on 30 June.\* [Bayonne](@bayonne-1451), besieged from 6 or 7 August, is entered on 20 August.\*\* On the mainland the English keep only Calais.

Bordeaux calls them back. John Talbot, Earl of Shrewsbury, a veteran captain whom Henry VI made his lieutenant in Aquitaine on 2 September 1452, lands in the Médoc on 17 October. The country round Bordeaux rises for him, and in October Bordeaux opens its gates and takes the French seneschal, Coëtivy, prisoner.\*\*\* The map shows this English Gascony of 1452 and 1453, from Bordeaux and the Médoc up the Dordogne to Castillon, approximately, from the places the sources name.

On 13 or 14 July 1453 the French lay siege to Castillon. Jean Bureau and his brother, who direct the French artillery, keep the guns in a camp ringed with ditches beside the Lidoire, a stream east of the town, while the Bretons hold the Mont Horable, a height to the north-east.

Talbot marches from Bordeaux to relieve the town\*\*\*\* and arrives at daybreak on 17 July; Ramsay supposes he crossed the Dordogne at Branne, and the map's line follows that guess. He drives French archers out of an abbey west of the town and attacks the camp. For about an hour the English fight at the palisades while the French guns fire into them. Then the Breton men-at-arms under the lord of Montauban and the lord of La Hunaudaye come down on the English left, and the French charge out of the camp.\*\*\*\*\* Talbot is killed, and his son Viscount Lisle with him.\*\*\*\*\*\* Monstrelet's continuator says four or five hundred English dead were buried.

Castillon surrenders the next day. Bordeaux, blockaded from 1 August, agrees terms early in October, and on 19 October 1453 Charles VII's officers take possession of the city. For Ramsay the Hundred Years' War ends here. Of all the lands the English held in France, Calais is left.

### Notes

\* Oman has Bordeaux fall in August 1451.

\*\* Beaucourt has the surrender signed on the 20th and Dunois enter on the 21st.

\*\*\* Ramsay, from the English constable's accounts, has Bordeaux occupied on 20 October. Beaucourt has the 23rd.

\*\*\*\* The forces are given differently. For Talbot, Ramsay takes as most likely the 500 men-at-arms and 800 archers of Aeneas Sylvius; Beaucourt gives about 7,000; Monstrelet's continuator gives 800 to 1,000 horse followed by four to six thousand foot; and Oman gives the army Talbot brought in 1452 as fewer than 3,000. For the French, Beaucourt gives about 1,800 lances with a body of francs-archers, and Monstrelet's continuator 1,600 to 1,800 men-at-arms and archers. Ramsay and Monstrelet's continuator give the Bureau brothers 700 sappers and gunners. Oman says, as a report, that the French had more than a hundred guns; Beaucourt says three hundred.

\*\*\*\*\* Oman names no Bretons: in his account the English column is blown apart by the French guns.

\*\*\*\*\*\* Talbot's death is told four ways. Oman has him mortally wounded by a cannon-ball. Ramsay has a cannon-ball bring down his small horse and shatter his knee, and Talbot trodden underfoot. Beaucourt has him hit in the leg by a culverin shot and finished by French archers. Monstrelet's continuator has his horse struck down by a culverin-ball and Talbot killed as he lies under it.
