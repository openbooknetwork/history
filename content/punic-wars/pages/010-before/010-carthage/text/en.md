Phoenician settlers from Tyre found Carthage on the coast of present-day Tunisia, by tradition in 814 BC. By the 3rd century BC it is the leading power of the western Mediterranean. It holds the African coast from Cyrenaica to the Atlantic, western Sicily, Sardinia, Malta, the Balearic Islands and trading towns in southern Spain.

Two suffetes elected each year, a council of elders and a citizen assembly govern the city. Its wealth comes from sea trade and from the farmland of its African hinterland. The navy is the largest in the western Mediterranean. The army is made up mostly of mercenaries and subject peoples: Libyans, Numidians, Iberians, Gauls and Balearic slingers.

For more than two centuries Carthage fights the Greek cities of Sicily, led by Syracuse, for control of the island. A series of treaties with Rome divides the trade of the two cities. Polybius dates the first to 509 BC. The last, in 279 BC, joins them against Pyrrhus of Epirus.
