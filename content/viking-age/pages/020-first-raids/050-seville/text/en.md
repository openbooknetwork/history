In 843, after Lambert II and his Breton allies have defeated and killed Renaud, the count whom Charles the Bald had set over Nantes, a Viking fleet sacks Nantes on the Loire and kills its bishop. The Vikings have held a base on the island of Noirmoutier, off the mouth of the Loire, since about 824.

In 844 a fleet from Francia sails south. It plunders Asturias but loses heavily at A Coruña, where King Ramiro I defeats it by the Tower of Hercules. In August or September it takes Lisbon and holds it for 13 days, then raids Cádiz, Medina Sidonia and Algeciras.

On 25 September the fleet sails up the Guadalquivir and camps on Isla Menor in the marshes. It beats the local Muslim forces on 29 September and takes Seville by storm on 1 or 3 October, but the citadel holds out. Emir Abd ar-Rahman II sends his hajib Isa ibn Shuhayd, who musters the governors’ troops at Córdoba and sets up his headquarters on the Aljarafe hills, and Musa ibn Musa, lord of the Banu Qasi, joins him.

After several clashes the Muslim army wins at Talyata, now Tablada, on 11 or 17 November. Muslim writers give 500 to 1,000 Vikings killed, 30 ships burned and at least 400 captured and hanged from palm trees, details that Ann Christys thinks later authors may have added. The survivors trade their plunder and captives for food and clothes and leave Spain. Abd ar-Rahman builds an arsenal at Seville and walls round its towns, and the Viking raids of 859 and 966 fail.
