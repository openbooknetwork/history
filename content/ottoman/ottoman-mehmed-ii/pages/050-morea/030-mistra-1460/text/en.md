In January 1459 Thomas rises against the treaty to win back the fortresses that he gave up. With 300 Italian foot soldiers paid by Pope Pius II and the Duchess of Milan he takes the lower town of [Patras](@patras-1459), but the citadel holds, and he withdraws when an Ottoman relief force approaches. He also takes [Kalamata](@kalamata-1459) and the Mani from Demetrios’s commanders. Mehmed sends Hamza Zenevisi, who relieves Patras and routs Thomas’s army at [Leontari](@leontari-1459), and the brothers make peace at Kastritza near Sparta, but they soon fight again.

Mehmed sets out from Edirne in April or early May 1460 and reaches Corinth 27 days later. Demetrios sends Matthew Asen in his place, and Mehmed has Asen arrested and sends Mahmud Pasha ahead to [Mistra](@mistra-1460), which Demetrios hands over on 30 May 1460, or 29 May by some accounts, seven years after the fall of Constantinople. Mehmed arrives on 31 May, receives Demetrios with honour and promises him lands in Thrace, and then he marches west. At [Gardiki](@gardiki-1460), where the people of Leontari have taken refuge, 6,000 people are said to be massacred after the castle surrenders, and the other castles then surrender and about 10,000 of their people are sent to Constantinople.

Thomas leaves Kalamata for Venetian [Navarino](@navarino-1460), sails with his family to [Corfu](@corfu-1460) in July and goes on to Italy, taking the relics of Saint Andrew with him. [Monemvasia](@monemvasia-1460) refuses to surrender and on 12 September 1460 places itself under Pope Pius II, and Venice keeps Modon, Coron, Nauplia and Argos. Zaganos Pasha takes Kalavryta and Chlemoutsi in the north-west, and Mehmed attacks Salmeniko in person: the town surrenders when the janissaries cut its water, but Graitzas Palaiologos holds out in the citadel. In July 1461 he breaks out with his men to Venetian [Lepanto](@lepanto-1461), and the Morea becomes an Ottoman province.
