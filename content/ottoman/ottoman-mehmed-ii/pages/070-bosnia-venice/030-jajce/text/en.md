While Mehmed is in Bosnia in 1463, Ali Bey Mihaloğlu raids southern Hungary to draw off its forces. He is driven out of Syrmia, turns east and is beaten near [Timișoara](@timisoara-1463) (Temesvár) by John Pongrácz, voivode of Transylvania. Matthias Corvinus makes peace with Emperor Frederick III on 19 July 1463 and turns south, and at [Petrovaradin](@petrovaradin-1463) on 12 September he signs his alliance with Venice.

In autumn 1463 Matthias leads his army into northern Bosnia, takes more than 60 fortified places and the town of Jajce, and lays siege to its citadel. The Ottoman garrison surrenders on 16 December 1463, and about 400 of its men join the Hungarian army. Matthias makes northern Bosnia into two Hungarian frontier provinces, the banates of Jajce and [Srebrenik](@srebrenik-1463), and Herceg Stjepan Vukčić Kosača accepts him as overlord.

In July 1464 Mehmed returns to Bosnia and lays siege to Jajce, but when Matthias gathers his army on the Sava, the sultan lifts the siege on 24 August. Matthias crosses the Sava, takes [Srebrenica](@srebrenica-1464) and lays siege to [Zvornik](@zvornik-1464), and he withdraws to Hungary when a large Ottoman army under Mahmud Pasha arrives. Pope Pius II, who has called a crusade and means to lead it in person, waits at [Ancona](@ancona-1464) for the fleet that is to carry it and dies there on 15 August 1464, and the crusade ends with his death.
