No one fights on Thursday 5 May, the feast of the Ascension. A council resolves to clear the south bank instead, taking the Tourelles and the forts around them, so that food can reach the city from Berry once more. Joan sends the English a last summons tied to an arrow.

On Friday 6 May about 4,000 men cross the Loire in boats from between Saint-Loup and the Tour Neuve, the tower at the east end of the walls. They come to an island, [the Île Saint-Aignan](@ile-saint-aignan), and from it they cross the other arm of the river on a bridge of two boats. William Glasdale, captain of the Tourelles, empties and burns [the bastille of Saint-Jean-le-Blanc](@saint-jean-le-blanc) and draws his men back to [the Augustins](@augustins), a fort in a ruined convent near the bridge.

Before the Augustins the French fall back towards the island, on a cry that the English of Saint-Privé are coming, or by the captains' decision. The English come out after them. Joan and the Gascon captain La Hire turn with lances couched and drive them back, and before night the French take the Augustins by storm. Joan has it burned so that the men will not stop to plunder, and she is hurt in the foot by a caltrop, a spiked iron.

That night the French lie before the Tourelles, and the townspeople bring them bread and wine. Joan is taken back into the city to sleep, or stays before the fort all night. In the dark English take to the river at [Saint-Privé](@saint-prive), and by morning its fort is empty.
