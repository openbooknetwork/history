[Sir John Fastolf](@patay-the-rout-fastolf), who has not dismounted, rides north towards the English vanguard at the hedges to join it. Its men see him coming, think all is lost and the main body in flight, and leave the hedges with their captain and his white standard. So Wavrin, who rode with Fastolf, tells it.

The French vanguard under [La Hire](@patay-the-rout-la-hire) and [Poton de Xaintrailles](@patay-the-rout-la-hire) presses into the English main body. The French main body under [the Duke of Alençon](@patay-the-rout-alencon) and [the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois)](@patay-the-rout-alencon) comes up and joins the rout. In Wavrin the French are by then so far into the main body that they take or kill whom they will.\* The fugitives run north-east for Janville, two leagues off, and its townsmen shut the gates on them. Fastolf turns away with a small company and rides for Étampes, which he reaches about midnight.

The English captains Lord Talbot, Lord Scales, Sir Thomas Rempston and a Hungerford are taken.\*\* The French lose few men, by Wavrin and Monstrelet, who give no figure. Monstrelet has the fight over at about two in the afternoon, and that night the French lie at [Patay](@patay-the-rout-patay).\*\*\*

### Notes

\* Ramsay has the main body keep together under Fastolf and reach Étampes after midnight, and says that what became of the rearguard is not stated.

\*\* Hungerford is a lord in Monstrelet and Sir Walter Hungerford the younger in Ramsay. The English losses are given by author: about 1,800 dead and 100 to 120 taken in Monstrelet; about 2,000 dead and 200 taken in Wavrin; about 2,200 dead in Gruel, by the heralds' count, and in the Journal of the Siege; 2,000 to 3,000 dead and 400 to 500 taken besides the lords in Perceval de Cagny; more than 4,000 killed and taken in Dunois's deposition at the retrial; and a third or more of the army in Oman.

\*\*\* Where the battle was fought is disputed: near the village of Patay in Cagny and the Journal of the Siege, two leagues from Janville in Wavrin, half a league short of the village in Monstrelet, near Coinces, two or three miles short of it, in Ramsay, and on the edge of the Lignerolles wood between Saint-Péravy and Patay in Anatole France. The map takes one place, on the road north into Patay, and every position on it is approximate.
