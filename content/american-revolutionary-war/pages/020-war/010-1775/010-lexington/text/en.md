On the night of 18 April 1775 Gage sends about seven hundred men across the Charles to seize the militia stores at Concord. Riders warn the towns ahead of them, and church bells and alarm guns call out the militia. At dawn the column finds 77 militiamen drawn up on Lexington green. Someone fires, and eight of the militia are killed.

At Concord the troops find little, and at the North Bridge they are driven back. The march back becomes a running fight, with militia firing from walls and houses and thousands more converging on the road. Reinforced by Lord Percy’s brigade at Lexington, the column reaches Charlestown by evening with 300 casualties. The next morning about 15,000 militia hold a line around Boston from Chelsea to Roxbury, and the siege of Boston begins.
