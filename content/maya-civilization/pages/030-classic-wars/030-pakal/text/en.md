K'inich Janaab Pakal, born in March AD 603, takes the throne of Palenque in July 615 at the age of twelve. His mother Sak K'uk', who has ruled since 612, remains a strong force at court until her death in September 640. In March 626 he marries Lady Tz'akbu Ajaw of Uxte'k'uh, and their sons K'inich Kan Bahlam II and K'inich K'an Joy Chitam II are born in 635 and 644.

Under Pakal, Palenque extends its power in the west. In 628 Piedras Negras captures one of Pakal’s officials, and six days later Palenque captures Nuun Ujol Chaak, lord of Santa Elena, which becomes its tributary. In 659 Pakal takes six prisoners, among them Ahiin Chan Ahk of Pipa', a place usually identified with Pomona. During Tikal’s hiatus at least one ruler of Tikal takes refuge with Pakal.

From 647 Pakal builds on a large scale, first the temple now called El Olvidado, then a great enlargement of the Palace. He adds House E, painted white, Houses B and C in 661 and House A in 668, whose walls show prisoners taken in 662. The east court of the Palace marks his military victories.

Pakal dies on 29 August 683 at the age of 80, after a reign of 68 years and 33 days. He is buried under the Temple of the Inscriptions in a carved stone sarcophagus, with a jade mask on his face. His son K'inich Kan Bahlam II succeeds him.
