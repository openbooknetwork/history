Henry V, King of England, revives the old claim of Edward III to the crown of France, and the civil war in France gives him his chance. On 13 August 1415 his fleet comes to anchor in the estuary of the Seine, close to the Chef de Caux, and from the next day his army comes ashore unopposed between [Harfleur](@harfleur-1415) and Honfleur.

Battered by cannon and mined, Harfleur surrenders on 22 September, when no help has come from the French army at Rouen. The siege costs Henry a fifth of his men, more to camp fever than to fighting, and about 5,000 sick are sent home besides.

Against most of his council, Henry leaves on 8 or 9 October and marches for the English port of [Calais](@calais-1415). On 13 October he finds the ford of [Blanche-Taque](@blanche-taque-1415) on the Somme held against him and has to go up the river. He crosses on 19 October at [Béthencourt](@bethencourt-1415), above Péronne, while the French gather at Bapaume in far greater numbers.

On Friday 25 October the French under the Constable d'Albret bar the road to Calais between the woods and enclosures of Agincourt and Tramecourt. The ploughed fields are soaked by a week of rain. Henry draws up his army in a single line, with Edward, Duke of York, leading the right, Lord Camoys the left and the king himself the centre, and sharpened stakes planted before the archers. Henry moves forward until the French are within bowshot. Their horsemen are shot down or turn back through their own ranks, and the men-at-arms on foot, struggling through the mud, are beaten. At an alarm Henry has the prisoners killed.

The Constable d'Albret, John I, Duke of Alençon, Edward, Duke of Bar, and the Count of Nevers die, and Charles of Orléans, John, Duke of Bourbon, and Marshal Boucicaut are taken to England. Henry reaches Calais on 29 October, leaving Harfleur in English hands.
