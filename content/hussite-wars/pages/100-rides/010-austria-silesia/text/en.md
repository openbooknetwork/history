On 12 March 1427 the Taborites and the Orphans under Prokop lay siege to Zwettl in Lower Austria. On 25 March by most accounts an Austrian relief army under Reinprecht of Walsee and Leopold of Krayg attacks them, perhaps on the vineyard hill by the town, and after four hours puts them to flight. While the Austrians plunder the abandoned wagon fort, the Hussites rally and attack again, and the Austrians lose 4,000 men by Andreas of Regensburg, 9,000 by the Czech accounts, before they reach the shelter of the town walls. The Hussites plunder [Altenburg](@altenburg-1427) abbey and go home by Horn.

In mid May the field armies ride into Upper Lusatia and Silesia, round Zittau to Lubań, Lwówek and Jawor, and return by Trutnov and Náchod with gold, silver and cattle. After the crusade Prokop takes [Kolín](@kolin-1427) from Diviš Bořek of Miletínek on 16 December 1427 and leads Taborites, Orphans, Praguers and Moravian Hussites into Upper Hungary. They plunder the suburbs of [Pressburg](@pressburg-1428) on 18 February 1428 and turn back by Šintava and Nové Mesto nad Váhom to Uherský Brod.

From there the army turns north through the Moravian Gate into Silesia. The people of Ostrava and Osoblaha flee, Głogówek is taken, Duke Václav of Opava makes a truce and Bolko of Opole an alliance. On 18 March 1428 the Hussites beat the army of Bishop Konrad of [Wrocław](@wroclaw-1428), led by Půta of Častolovice, outside [Nysa](@nysa-1428). Its peasant foot breaks after a short fight, and about 2,000 are said to be killed or drowned in the river, but Půta holds the town. They raid across Silesia, take ransom from the towns, burn the suburbs of Wrocław on 1 May and go home by Kłodzko.
