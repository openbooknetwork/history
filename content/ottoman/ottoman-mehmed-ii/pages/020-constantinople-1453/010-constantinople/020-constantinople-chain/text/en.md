On 2 April 1453 the defenders close the Golden Horn with a chain made by Bartolomeo Soligo. It rests on wooden floats and runs from the [tower of Eugenius](@tower-of-eugenius) below the Acropolis to the [Kastellion](@galata-kastellion), a tower on the shore of [Galata](@galata-1453). Behind it lie 26 ships under the Venetian [Alviso Diedo](@constantinople-chain-diedo): five from Genoa, five from Venice, three from Venetian Crete, one each from Ancona, Aragon and France, and about ten of the emperor’s.

Baltaoğlu Süleyman Bey brings the Ottoman fleet from Gallipoli to the [Double Columns](@double-columns) on the Bosporus shore. The eyewitnesses count 110 to 430 ships, and a modern estimate gives about 110, most of them galleys and large rowing boats. In the first days of the siege part of the fleet probably takes the Princes’ Islands in the Sea of Marmara.

@image breydenbach-galley-075805

In mid April [Baltaoğlu](@constantinople-chain-baltaoglu)’s galleys attack the ships behind the chain and try to break it. The Christian ships stand higher in the water than the galleys, and their crews beat off the attack. The fleet returns to the Double Columns, and the Horn stays closed to it.
