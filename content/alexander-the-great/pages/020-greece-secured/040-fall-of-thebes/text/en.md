In the late summer of 335 BC, while Alexander is fighting in Illyria, Theban exiles slip back into Thebes by night. They kill two officers of the Macedonian garrison and call on the people to win back their freedom. Alexander has sent no news for a long time, and the exiles’ claim that he has been killed in Illyria is believed. The Thebans revolt and shut the garrison inside their citadel, the Cadmeia, behind a double stockade.

Alexander marches from [Pelium](@pelium-335b) through Eordaea and Elimiotis, reaches [Pelinna](@pelinna-335) in Thessaly on the seventh day, and enters Boeotia on the sixth day after that. The Thebans, Arrian says, learn he is past [Thermopylae](@thermopylae-335) only when his army is near their city.

Diodorus gives Alexander more than 30,000 foot and at least 3,000 horse. The Thebans refuse his terms, and late in September the city is stormed.\* The garrison comes out of the Cadmeia to join the attackers, and in Arrian’s account Phocians, Plataeans and other Boeotians, Alexander’s allies, do much of the killing. Diodorus and Plutarch put the Theban dead at more than 6,000 and the captives at more than 30,000. Diodorus gives more than 500 Macedonian dead.

Alexander leaves the city’s fate to the allies who fought with him. They vote to raze Thebes and sell its people as slaves, sparing the priests and the guest-friends of the Macedonians, and to rebuild [Orchomenus](@orchomenus-335) and [Plataea](@plataea-335). By one report in Arrian, Alexander himself spares the house and descendants of the Theban poet Pindar. Diodorus and Plutarch say that Alexander meant the razing as a warning to the other Greeks. In Bosworth’s judgement it ends the unrest in southern Greece, and Alexander returns to Macedon to prepare the invasion of Asia.

### Notes

\* The sources tell the storming in two ways, and Bosworth calls the battle difficult to reconstruct. In Arrian, the battalion of Perdiccas, one of Alexander’s commanders, attacks the stockade without waiting for orders. The Thebans drive it back, Alexander’s phalanx routs them, and the Macedonians reach the gates on the heels of the fleeing Thebans, before these can be shut. In Diodorus, the Thebans hold firm until Alexander sees a postern gate its guards have left and sends Perdiccas through it.
