Bayezid rides over the field after the battle and finds the hostages from Rahova whom the crusaders killed the night before. On 26 September the captives are brought before him, and the French knight Jacques de Helly, who once served Murad I, points out the lords to be held for ransom, among them Nevers, Coucy and Eu. Boucicaut, already among the men led out to die, is spared when Nevers pleads for him. Those judged to be under 20, among them the 16-year-old Johann Schiltberger, are made slaves, and the rest are killed until Bayezid stops the executions in the afternoon, between 300 and 3,000 men by different counts.

The captives are marched on foot to [Gallipoli](@gallipoli), and after two months there the lords are taken on to [Bursa](@bursa). Coucy dies at Bursa in February 1397, and Eu dies in captivity in June. In June 1397 the ransom is set at 200,000 florins, and on 24 June the prisoners are freed on a first payment of 75,000 and their promise to wait in Venice for the rest. Nevers returns to France in February 1398.

Sigismund, fearing treachery from the Wallachians, sails down the Danube to the Black Sea and goes home by Constantinople, the straits and the Adriatic. Bayezid marches on [Vidin](@vidin-1397) and takes it at the end of 1396 or early in 1397, and Ivan Sratsimir is taken to Bursa, where he is probably strangled. Most historians count him as the last ruler of medieval Bulgaria. Ottoman raiders cross into Syrmia and plunder Zemun and [Mitrovica](@mitrovica-1396), and Schiltberger has them reach [Pettau](@pettau-1396) (Ptuj) in Styria, a raid that some historians doubt.
