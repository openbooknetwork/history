The lands of the Bohemian Crown are the kingdom of Bohemia, the margraviate of Moravia, the duchies of Silesia and the margraviates of Upper and Lower Lusatia. Charles IV joins them under the crown of Bohemia in 1348, with [Prague](@prague-1400) as their capital. The king of Bohemia is one of the seven electors who choose the King of the Romans. By some estimates the Church holds a third of the land in Bohemia.

Charles IV’s son Wenceslas IV is King of the Romans from 1376 and King of Bohemia from 1378. On 8 May 1394 the great lords and his cousin Jobst of Moravia take him prisoner at Králův Dvůr. On 20 August 1400 the four Rhenish electors meet at [Lahnstein](@lahnstein-1400), depose him for idleness and neglect and elect the Count Palatine Rupert the next day. In 1402 his half-brother Sigismund, King of Hungary, takes him prisoner again and hands him to the dukes of Austria in [Vienna](@vienna-1402).

@image sigismund-portrait-641514

Sigismund’s Hungarian troops take Kutná Hora in December 1402 and leave Bohemia in the summer of 1403. Wenceslas escapes from Vienna on 11 November 1403, travels by Mikulov and Kutná Hora and enters Prague at Christmas. His neighbours are the margraves of Meissen to the north, the dukes of Bavaria to the west, the dukes of Austria to the south, Sigismund in Hungary to the south east and Władysław II Jagiełło, King of Poland, to the north east. Wenceslas has no children, and Sigismund is his heir.
