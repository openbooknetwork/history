From Phrada the army goes south in late 330 BC to winter by the lakes where the Helmand ends. Bessus, the Bactrian satrap who calls himself king, is still free beyond the mountains. Early in 329 BC Alexander marches up through Arachosia, in deep snow and short of food. Behind him Satibarzanes, the rebel satrap of Areia, returns there with 2,000 of Bessus's horsemen, and in the spring Alexander's general Erigyius kills him.

In late March the army reaches the Kabul valley. There, with the passes of the Hindu Kush still deep in snow, Alexander founds [Alexandria in the Caucasus](@alexandria-caucasus-329), near today's Begram. Bessus lays waste the land below the mountains, but when Alexander comes over the range to [Drapsaca](@drapsaca-329) (perhaps today's Kunduz), Bessus gives up Bactria. He crosses the great river to the north, burns his boats and withdraws to [Nautaca](@nautaca-329) in Sogdiana. His Bactrian horsemen ride home. Bosworth explains that he could not unite the Bactrian nobles.

[Bactra](@bactra-329) (today Balkh) falls to Alexander. In midsummer heat the army crosses about 75 km of waterless desert to [the Oxus](@oxus-329) (today the Amu Darya). The river is too wide and fast to bridge, so the soldiers stuff their leather tent covers with chaff and sew them tight. On these floats, Arrian says, the army is across in five days, at a point no source records. Before crossing, Alexander sends home his oldest Macedonians and the Thessalians who had stayed on as volunteers.

Two Sogdian nobles, Spitamenes and Dataphernes, offer to hand Bessus over. Bosworth judges that they chose to buy Alexander's favour with his capture. Alexander sends Ptolemy, one of his officers, ahead, and Bessus is brought in naked and bound.\*

Arachosia and Bactria up to the Oxus join the empire, and Bessus's kingdom shrinks to Sogdiana.

### Notes

\* Arrian gives two accounts of the handover. In Ptolemy's own, he rides hard through Sogdiana and takes Bessus from captors who are having second thoughts. In Aristobulus's, Ptolemy only escorts the party, and Spitamenes and Dataphernes give Bessus up to Alexander themselves. Bosworth thinks Aristobulus's account the likelier.
