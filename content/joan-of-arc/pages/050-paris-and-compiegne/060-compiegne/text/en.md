In May 1430 Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, takes the castle of [Choisy](@choisy-1430), held by Louis de Flavy for his brother Guillaume, the captain of [Compiègne](@compiegne-1430). He razes it and bridges the river. [Soissons](@soissons-1430), held for Charles VII by a captain the duke has bought, shuts its gates on Joan and the Archbishop of Reims.\* By 21 or 22 May the besiegers lie across the Oise from the town: the duke at [Coudun](@coudun-1430), John of Luxembourg, a Burgundian captain, at [Clairoix](@clairoix-1430), Baudot de Noyelle at [Margny](@margny-1430), at the end of the causeway from the bridge, and the English under Lord Montgomery at [Venette](@venette-1430).

Joan hears of the siege at [Crépy](@crepy-1430). Perceval de Cagny, a chronicler of Alençon's household, has her ride through the night to see her good friends of Compiègne and come in at sunrise.\*\* Late in the afternoon of 23 May\*\*\* she rides out over the bridge against the post at Margny. By her own account, she drove the Burgundians back twice to their camp and a third time halfway, and then the English cut her off from the bridge.\*\*\*\* As the enemy reach the bridge, Guillaume de Flavy has it raised and the gate shut for fear of losing the town, leaving Joan outside with a few of her men. Neither the chronicles nor the older histories accuse Flavy of treason, though the question is still debated.\*\*\*\*\*

An archer pulls her from her horse by her cloth-of-gold tunic, says Chastellain, a Burgundian chronicler, and she gives her faith to the Bastard of Wandonne, a man-at-arms of John of Luxembourg's company. The duke comes too late for the fight and speaks with her. The chronicler Monstrelet, who was there, could not remember what was said. That evening she is given into John of Luxembourg's keeping.

### Notes

\* Soissons is sold to Burgundy some weeks later. Lang dates the refusal 18 May and, after a document of Champion's, names the captain Guichard Bournel and the price 4,000 saluts.

\*\* Chastellain has her enter by night and rest two nights before the sortie. Her company is 300 to 400 fighting men in Cagny, and in Lowell and Lang the band of about 200 of the Italian captain Bartolomeo Baretta.

\*\*\* This is the day of the duke's own letter to the town of Saint-Quentin, written at Coudun that evening, and of Cagny and the Bourgeois of Paris; Lowell, Lang and Beaucourt follow it. Monstrelet and Chastellain, who were in the Burgundian camp, have the eve of the Ascension, 24 May, and Ramsay follows them while noting the letter's date.

\*\*\*\* Cagny tells it otherwise: a Burgundian ambush laid behind the hill of Clairoix rides between her and the bridge, and five or six men lay hands on her, to whom she answers that she has given her faith to another. Lowell gives the Bastard of Wandonne the first name Lionel.

\*\*\*\*\* So the Wikipedia article on the siege of Compiègne reports. Cagny, Champion and Ramsay find no treason in the shutting of the gate.
