Early in August 1429 Charles VII, crowned at Reims on 17 July, is at Provins with his army, and his council decides to cross the Seine at [Bray](@bray-held-1429) and go back beyond the Loire. On the night before the crossing, writes Jean Chartier, the king's chronicler, the townsmen of Bray let in a company of English and Burgundians.\* The captains who want war are glad of the check, and the army turns north to [Crépy](@crepy-1429). There on 11 August a challenge reaches the king from John, Duke of Bedford, regent of France for Henry VI.

On Sunday 14 August the two armies meet at [Montépilloy](@montepilloy), about two leagues from [Senlis](@senlis-1429). The French lodge by a hedge in the fields. Bedford camps by the abbey of [Notre-Dame-de-la-Victoire](@victoire-1429) on the little river Nonette, and overnight his men dig in behind stakes, ditches and carts. Lowell puts Joan and La Hire at the head of the French skirmishers. They skirmish that evening and all the next day, but Bedford will not come out. Monstrelet, a Burgundian chronicler, counts about 300 dead and cannot say which side lost more.\*\* On the 16th Bedford falls back by Senlis to Paris.

Now the towns come over to the king. [Compiègne](@compiegne-1429) sends him its keys, and on 18 August he rides in and makes Guillaume de Flavy its captain. Senlis yields by treaty, and [Beauvais](@beauvais-1429) drives out its bishop, Pierre Cauchon. [Creil](@creil-1429), [Pont-Sainte-Maxence](@pont-sainte-maxence-1429), [Choisy](@choisy-1429), [Gournay-sur-Aronde](@gournay-1429), [Aumale](@aumale-1429), [Blangy](@blangy-1429) and [Lagny](@lagny-1429) on the Marne follow. Bedford leaves Paris to the Burgundians and to Louis of Luxembourg, Henry VI's chancellor, and goes into Normandy. On 28 August, at Compiègne, the king's envoys sign a truce with Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy.
