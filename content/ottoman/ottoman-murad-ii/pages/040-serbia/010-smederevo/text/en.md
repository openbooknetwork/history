Ottoman attacks in 1437 and 1438 force Đurađ to give up Braničevo and then Ždrelo and Višesav. In the spring of 1439 Murad himself marches against Serbia, and in May Đurađ flees to Hungary to ask for help, leaving his son Grgur and his wife’s brother Thomas Kantakouzenos to defend Smederevo. Murad’s army surrounds the city in early June and bombards it with cannon, the first fired in Serbia.

King Albert calls the nobility of Hungary to arms against the Ottomans, but few armed noblemen gather at [Titel](@titel-1439). One of them is John Hunyadi, who raids the besiegers and wins several small fights. In Kosovo the men of [Novo Brdo](@novo-brdo-1427) march out against the Ottomans and are beaten at Gračanica on 6 August 1439.

After nearly three months, hunger forces the garrison of Smederevo to surrender on 18 August 1439. The Serbian Despotate comes to an end, and the Ottomans hold all of Serbia except Novo Brdo, which holds out, and Zeta on the coast. Murad keeps Đurađ’s sons Grgur and Stefan as prisoners, and Albert makes Hunyadi and his brother bans of Severin. Albert dies of dysentery on 27 October 1439.
