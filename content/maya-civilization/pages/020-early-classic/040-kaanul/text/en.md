The Kaanul dynasty rules from Dzibanche, in the south of present-day Quintana Roo. The city’s ancient name, Kaanu'l, means "place of snakes", and its kings take the title k'uhul Kaanu'l ajaw, "holy lord of Kaanu'l", with a snake head as their emblem glyph. The earliest dated use of this glyph is on a hieroglyphic stairway at Dzibanche from AD 495, which shows bound captives with the name of the king Yuknoom Ch'een I. A text dated 529 at El Resbalón shows that city under Kaanul rule.

In the 6th century the Kaanul kings build a network of vassals and allies around Tikal. In 537 Yaxchilan, on the Usumacinta, captures a vassal of the Kaanul king Tuun K'ab Hix. In 546 Tuun K'ab Hix oversees the enthronement of Aj Wosal as king of Naranjo, 42 km east of Tikal, and takes the city out of Tikal’s alliance.

At Tikal Wak Chan K'awiil, known as Double Bird, takes the throne on 27 December 537, probably after a time in exile. In 553 he oversees the accession of Yajaw Te' K'inich II at Caracol, 76 km southeast of Tikal, as Caracol Altar 21 records. Caracol has been a client of Tikal since the Early Classic. He succeeds his father K'an I, king from 531.
