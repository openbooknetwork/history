After about an hour Cortés and the horsemen come up behind the Chontal army. Caught between the horse and the foot, the warriors break and flee. The Chontal have never seen horses, and Bernal Díaz writes that they take horse and rider for a single creature.

Bernal Díaz counts more than 800 dead on the field. Two Spaniards are killed and about seventy wounded. The Spaniards bury their dead and sear the wounds of the men and the horses.

In the following days envoys of Tabscoob bring gold, jade, turquoise, feathers and food, and twenty young women. One of them is Malintzin, a Nahua woman sold to the Chontal as a slave, who speaks Nahuatl and Chontal Maya. Baptised Marina, she becomes Cortés’s interpreter together with Aguilar. On 25 March Cortés founds Santa María de la Victoria at Potonchán, the first Spanish town in Mexico, named for Our Lady and the victory. In April the fleet sails on to San Juan de Ulúa.
