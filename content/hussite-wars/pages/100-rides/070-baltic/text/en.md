In 1432 the Orphans agree at Pabianice to help King Władysław II Jagiełło of Poland against the Teutonic Order, for pay and for an alliance with Poland. At the end of April 1433 Jan Čapek of Sány leads about 7,000 foot, 700 horse and 350 wagons north through Silesia and crosses the Oder at Głogów in mid May. With the Polish army they take Santok and Strzelce Krajeńskie in the Neumark, which the Order holds, and besiege Gorzów in vain.

From early July to 15 August the Polish and Hussite army besieges Chojnice, and its assault on 22 July fails. They march north along the Vistula, burn the abbey of Pelplin and take Tczew on 29 August. On 1 September they camp before Gdańsk, and on 4 September they ravage the abbey of Oliwa and reach the sea. By the Czech accounts the soldiers fill bottles with sea water to take home, and some are knighted on the shore.

The army turns back by Starogard, and on 13 September 1433 the Order agrees to the truce of Jasieniec. The Polish king pays the Orphans their wages and gives Čapek gifts, among them a camel. The Orphans march home and join the siege of Plzeň in the autumn.
