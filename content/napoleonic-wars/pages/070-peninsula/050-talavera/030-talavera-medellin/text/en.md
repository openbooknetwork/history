At dawn on 28 July 30 French guns on the Cascajal, the hill across the Portiña from the Medellín, open fire, and Wellesley pulls his infantry back behind the crest. Ruffin’s division attacks the Medellín again, its three regiments side by side, each about 160 files across and nine ranks deep.

As the columns near the top, the British rise from cover in lines two deep that overlap them, and the 29th and 48th Foot fire and charge with the bayonet. Hit from front and flank, with only their front ranks able to fire, the French columns break and run. A cannonade follows until noon, when a truce of two hours begins.
