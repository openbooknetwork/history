Parliament punishes Massachusetts. The Boston Port Act closes the harbour from 1 June 1774 until the tea is paid for. The Massachusetts Government Act of 20 May replaces the elected council with one appointed by the crown and limits town meetings to one a year unless the governor calls more. Royal officials may be tried in Britain or another colony when the governor judges that a fair trial is impossible in Massachusetts.

General Gage arrives as governor in May, and by 1775 about 3,000 British troops are in Boston. A new Quartering Act allows soldiers to be housed in unoccupied buildings. The colonies call these laws the Intolerable Acts, and colonies as far away as South Carolina send Boston supplies.

In June the Quebec Act extends the province of Quebec south and west over the Ohio country to the Mississippi, taking in part of the Indian reserve of 1763. It keeps French civil law, gives the province no elected assembly and tolerates the Catholic church. Virginians and Pennsylvanians with claims beyond the mountains, and Protestant New Englanders, see it as part of the same policy.
