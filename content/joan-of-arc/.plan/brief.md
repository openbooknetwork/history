# Joan of Arc

The subject is Joan of Arc and the war she turned: a France split between an English king, the Duke of Burgundy
and a disinherited Dauphin, the peasant girl from Domrémy who reached him in 1429, the relief of Orléans, the
march to Reims and the coronation, the failure before Paris, her capture at Compiègne, her trial and burning at
Rouen in 1431, and the retrial that annulled the sentence in 1456. 1407 to 1456, from the murder that opened the
civil war between Armagnacs and Burgundians to the verdict of nullification, with one closing page on what she
became afterwards, to her canonisation in 1920. It tells a war, one campaign and
its aftermath seen through one life, so there is no chapter on how a people lived. The Hundred Years' War
before her, which the reader may know little of, gets the opening section, and no more of it than her story
needs.

At the end the reader knows why France had two kings in 1429 and who held which lands, how a seventeen-year-old
with no rank came to ride with a royal army, what she did at Orléans and on the Loire in ten weeks and why the
coronation at Reims mattered more than any battle, why the advance stopped at Paris, how she was taken and sold,
what she was tried for and by whom, and how the war ended and the court that condemned her was overturned
within the lifetime of her mother.

What is in doubt is said as doubt. Her voices are told as she and the witnesses told them, in the trial's own
words where they survive, and the story takes no side on what they were. A number of men, a date or a place
that the chronicles give differently names its author.

## Sections

### 010 france-divided, 1407 – 1424

**Tells:**
- The murder of the Duke of Orléans in Paris opens the civil war of Armagnacs and Burgundians
- Domrémy on the Meuse, a village of the king's party on the Burgundian border, where Joan is born about 1412
- Henry V lands: Harfleur and Agincourt, then the conquest of Normandy
- The murder of John the Fearless on the bridge of Montereau drives Burgundy to England; the Treaty of Troyes
  disinherits the Dauphin
- Two kings in 1422: the infant Henry VI in Paris, the Dauphin Charles south of the Loire, the "King of Bourges"
- Baugé, 1421: the Dauphin's Scots and French beat the English and kill Clarence; Cravant, 1423, and Verneuil,
  1424, break that army

**Battles:** Agincourt (card), Baugé (card), Verneuil (card); Cravant is told in the text, with no card

**Pages:** 6

### 020 the-road-to-chinon, 1425 – April 1429

**Tells:**
- The voices in her father's garden, about 1425, and what she said they asked of her
- The English close on the Loire: Salisbury's campaign and the siege of Orléans begun in October 1428
- Vaucouleurs: Robert de Baudricourt sends her home in May 1428, holds back when she returns in January 1429 and
  then sends her; the Battle of the Herrings, 12 February 1429. The days she left Vaucouleurs and reached Chinon
  differ between authors and are told with their names
- Eleven days across Burgundian country to Chinon
- Chinon and Poitiers: the Dauphin receives her, the clergy examine her, she is armed and given a banner

**Battles:** the Herrings, at Rouvray (card)

**Pages:** 5

### 030 orleans, 29 April – 8 May 1429

**Tells:**
- The convoy from Blois and her entry into the city
- The bastille of Saint-Loup is stormed
- Saint-Jean-le-Blanc is abandoned by the English and the Augustins taken, across the river
- The Tourelles: she is wounded and the fort falls
- The English march away on 8 May

**Battles:** the relief of Orléans (card, no phase pages: no source read gives a plan of the bastilles, and the
section's own pages show each fort on the day it fell, from the Journal of the Siege and the witnesses of 1456)

**Pages:** 6

### 040 the-loire-and-reims, May – July 1429

**Tells:**
- The Loire towns: Jargeau stormed, the bridge at Meung, Beaugency surrendered
- Patay: the English archers ridden down before they are set, Talbot taken
- The march into Burgundian Champagne with no siege train: Auxerre passed by under a truce, Troyes yielding as
  the assault is readied, Châlons opening its gates
- Reims opens its gates; Charles VII is anointed on 17 July with Joan beside him
- What the coronation changed: the towns that came over in the weeks after

**Battles:** Jargeau (card), Patay (card, no phase pages)

**Pages:** 6

### 050 paris-and-compiegne, August 1429 – May 1430

**Tells:**
- The armies face each other at Montépilloy and do not fight; the king treats with Burgundy
- The assault on Paris at the Porte Saint-Honoré fails, and she is wounded
- The army is disbanded: Saint-Pierre-le-Moûtier taken, La Charité not
- Spring 1430, with a small company and no royal army: Melun, Lagny
- Compiègne: the sortie, the gate closed behind her, an archer pulls her from her horse and she yields to the
  Bastard of Wandonne, a man of John of Luxembourg

**Battles:** Paris (card), Compiègne (card)

**Pages:** 6

### 060 rouen, May 1430 – May 1431

**Tells:**
- A prisoner of John of Luxembourg: Beaulieu, the leap from the tower of Beaurevoir
- Sold to the English by John of Luxembourg for ten thousand livres (francs in Ramsay, who names the Duke of
  Burgundy as the seller); the road north round the coast to Rouen
- The court of Pierre Cauchon: who sat on it, who paid for it and what it had to prove
- The questioning: the voices, the sign given to the king, the man's dress
- The cemetery of Saint-Ouen: the abjuration, and the relapse four days later
- The Old Market, 30 May 1431

**Battles:** none

**Pages:** 6

### 070 after-her, 1431 – 1456, and a closing page to 1920

**Tells:**
- Henry VI is crowned in Paris, and Burgundy changes sides at Arras in 1435
- Paris opens its gates to Charles VII in 1436
- Normandy won back: Rouen, and Formigny
- Castillon ends English Gascony, and the war
- The retrial: Charles VII's enquiry of 1450 once Rouen is his, the legate d'Estouteville's of 1452, her mother's
  petition and the papal rescript of 1455, the witnesses of Domrémy, Orléans and Rouen, and the sentence annulled
  at Rouen on 7 July 1456
- Afterwards, the closing page: how she was remembered, at Orléans each 8 May and in France at large, and her
  canonisation in 1920. It tells what is recorded and claims nothing of what she means

**Battles:** Formigny (card), Castillon (card)

**Pages:** 6

## Size

41 pages in seven sections. Her campaigns fill thirteen months in one corner of France, so the story is short
beside the book's others, and the map is close in: a page shows towns and river crossings where another story
shows provinces.

## Sources to look for first

For the survey, not a ruling: the record of the trial of condemnation of 1431 and the record of the nullification
of 1455 to 1456, both in translation; the Journal of the Siege of Orléans; the chronicles of Monstrelet, of
Perceval de Cagny and of the Bourgeois of Paris; and among modern scholars Régine Pernoud, Kelly DeVries on her
as a soldier, Juliet Barker on the English in France, and Helen Castor.
