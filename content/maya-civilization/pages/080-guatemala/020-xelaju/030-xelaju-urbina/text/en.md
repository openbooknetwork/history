Beyond the pass the army comes out onto open ground near Cantel. By local tradition the K'iche' army meets it here, on the Llanos de Urbina. Alvarado writes to Cortés that the Spanish wait until the K'iche' come close enough to shoot their arrows and then charge into them. The K'iche', who have never seen horses, are afraid, and he writes that many of them die.

In the same letter Alvarado reports that one of the four lords of Q'umarkaj, the captain general of the country, is killed. He gives neither the lord’s name nor the manner of his death. Local tradition names him Tecun Uman and places his death on the Llanos de Urbina. The Título K'oyoi already describes the fight in terms close to the later legend.

In the legend Tecun Uman wears quetzal feathers and kills Alvarado’s horse, then falls to Alvarado’s lance. His spirit companion, a quetzal, lands on his chest and is stained red by his blood. Another account gives the killing blow to one of Alvarado’s men, a soldier named Argueta.

The cavalry scatters the K'iche', and the army crosses the valley to Xelajú. It finds the town deserted.
