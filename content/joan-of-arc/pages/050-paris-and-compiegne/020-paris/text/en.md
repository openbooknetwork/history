On 23 August 1429 Joan and the Duke of Alençon leave Charles VII at Compiègne, and on 25 August they take [Saint-Denis](@saint-denis-1429), north of Paris. For two weeks they skirmish at the city's gates. Paris is held for Henry VI by its garrison and townsmen under Louis of Luxembourg, Bishop of Thérouanne, and the Burgundian captain L'Isle-Adam. The king reaches Saint-Denis on 7 September, and that evening Joan and the captains lodge at [La Chapelle](@la-chapelle-1429), halfway to the city.

On Thursday 8 September, the feast of the Nativity of Our Lady, the army draws up in the pig market outside the [Porte Saint-Honoré](@porte-saint-honore). Joan, Marshal de Rais and Raoul de Gaucourt attack the gate, and the barrier and the boulevard, the earthwork before it, are burned and taken. Alençon waits behind a hill in case the defenders sally from the [Porte Saint-Denis](@porte-saint-denis-1429). Past the first ditch and the bank beyond it, the second ditch holds water too deep to cross. Joan sounds it with her lance and has wood thrown in, but it cannot be filled before night. Late in the day a crossbow bolt goes through her thigh, and her standard-bearer is shot dead. She will not leave the ditch, saying the place would have been taken, until she is carried back to La Chapelle. She is well again in five days.

Next morning she wants to attack again, and the Baron of Montmorency, who has always held for the other side, rides out of Paris with fifty or sixty gentlemen to join her. Then René, Duke of Bar, and Charles, Count of Clermont, bring the king's order to come back to Saint-Denis.
