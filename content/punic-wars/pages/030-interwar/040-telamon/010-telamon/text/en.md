In 232 BC Rome divides former Gallic land in Picenum among its poorer citizens, which alarms the Boii and Insubres of the Po valley. In 225 BC these two peoples hire Gaesatae, warriors from beyond the Alps, under the kings Concolitanus and Aneroëstes. Polybius connects the Ebro Treaty with Hasdrubal to Rome’s fear of this war.

Rome calls up its Italian allies. The consul Lucius Aemilius Papus stands at Ariminum with four legions and 32,000 allies, and a force of Sabines and Etruscans guards the border of Etruria. The other consul, Gaius Atilius Regulus, is in Sardinia with an army of the same size.

The Gauls cross the Apennines into Etruria and march towards Rome. Near Clusium they slip away at night to Faesulae and defeat the Roman army that follows them, killing 6,000. When Papus arrives they turn back along the coast with their plunder, and he follows without risking a battle. Regulus lands at Pisae and marches south, and near Telamon his scouts meet the Gallic vanguard head on.
