In July the Howe brothers assemble 32,000 troops on Staten Island, among them thousands of Hessians, carried by a fleet of more than 400 ships. On 27 August they outflank Washington on Long Island and drive him into the Brooklyn lines. Two nights later he ferries nine thousand men across the East River in fog without losing one. New York City is occupied on 15 September, and on the night of 20 to 21 September a fire destroys part of it.

Washington is pushed north to White Plains, then across the Hudson. Fort Washington falls on 16 November with its garrison of 2,800, and Fort Lee four days later. What is left of the army, fewer than five thousand men fit for duty, retreats across New Jersey and crosses the Delaware into Pennsylvania in early December, taking the boats along the river with it. Enlistments run out at the end of the year. New York and Long Island stay British until the end of the war.
