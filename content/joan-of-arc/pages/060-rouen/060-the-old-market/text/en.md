Early on Wednesday 30 May 1431, Martin Ladvenu, a Dominican friar sent by Pierre Cauchon, the Bishop of Beauvais, comes to Joan's cell in [the castle of Rouen](@castle-last-morning-1431). He tells her she is to die that day and hears her confession, and she is allowed to take communion.\* When Cauchon arrives she says to him, by the account of Jean Toutmouillé, who was with Ladvenu, "Bishop, I die through you."

She is led through the streets to [the Old Market](@old-market-1431). Jean Massieu, the usher of the court, who walked beside her, put her guard at more than 800 soldiers with axes and swords; Lowell writes of several score English soldiers. At about nine, near [the church of Saint-Sauveur](@saint-sauveur-1431), she is set on one of three scaffolds, and Nicolas Midi preaches. The sentence read to her then declares her a relapsed heretic, cut off from the Church and handed over to the secular power.

@image vieux-marche-pyre-97466c

The bailli of Rouen, who stands for the secular power, should now pass sentence, and he does not. Laurent Guesdon, who stood in for him that day, said at the retrial that the executioner seized her the moment the church's sentence was read, before he or the bailli had given any sentence, and he held this to be wrong.

She asks for a cross. One of the English standing by fashions a small one for her out of a stick, and Massieu has the cross of the church brought for her to look at. In the fire she calls on Jesus to the end. The Bourgeois of Paris heard that once her dress had burned, the fire was drawn back so that the crowd could see her body, and that the executioner then burned it to ashes. Her ashes are thrown into [the Seine](@seine-1431).

### Notes

\* Whether she said that morning that her voices had deceived her is disputed. An inquiry the judges held on 7 June 1431, added to the record, has her say so. Lowell sees no reason to think it was altered in the writing down. Thurston says a doubt must always be felt, and gives the word of Guillaume Manchon, the recorder of the trial, that to the last she declared her voices came from God.
