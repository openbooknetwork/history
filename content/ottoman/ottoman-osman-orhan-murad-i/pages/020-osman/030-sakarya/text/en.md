According to the chronicles, the tekfurs of [Bursa](@bursa), [Adranos](@adranos), Kestel and Kete gather their men at Bursa in the spring of 1303 and march on [Yenişehir](@yenisehir). Osman meets them at the pass of Dimbos. His nephew Aydoğdu and the tekfur of Kestel fall, and a charge by Orhan’s horsemen drives the Byzantines back.

Osman catches the tekfur of Kete before he reaches [Ulubat](@ulubat) and has him put to death before its walls, and the fort surrenders, followed by Kestel. Dimbos is known only from the Ottoman chronicles, and historians hold that Aşıkpaşazade built part of his account from reports of Bapheus.

Osman also extends his land north along the Sakarya. [Lefke](@lefke), Mekece, Akhisar and Köprühisar pass to him, the first three through Köse Mihal, and his raiders reach the Black Sea coast. By 1308 Ottoman forts hold the roads into [Nicaea](@nicaea) from the east.
