After Arras the English lose town after town round Paris and in Normandy. Ramsay gives the list: [Dieppe](@dieppe-1435) on 29 October 1435, and soon all of the Pays de Caux except Caudebec; then, early in 1436, [Pontoise](@pontoise-1436), Charenton, Brie-Comte-Robert, Saint-Germain-en-Laye, Corbeil and Vincennes. Paris is cut off.

From Pontoise, Monstrelet says, comes a French and Burgundian force under Arthur de Richemont, Constable of France for Charles VII, with the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois) and L'Isle-Adam, a Burgundian captain now in Charles VII's service. In April 1436 they storm [Saint-Denis](@saint-denis-1436). Near Épinay, just beyond it, they rout a sortie of the English garrison of Paris under Sir Thomas Beaumont and take him prisoner.\* Those of his men who reach the Tour du Velin at Saint-Denis are killed once hunger drives them out.

On Friday 13 April 1436 Richemont's men come before [the Porte Saint-Jacques](@porte-saint-jacques-1436), the gate of the left bank. With the gates shut, townsmen lower ladders to them, and L'Isle-Adam goes up first, bearing Charles VII's banner and the king's pardon for Paris. The gate is opened, and the constable enters [Paris](@paris-1436).

@image paris-1436-recovery-miniature

The English troops, under Lord Willoughby and Louis of Luxembourg, Bishop of Thérouanne, shut themselves up in [the Bastille Saint-Antoine](@bastille-1436). They come to terms with the constable, and on 17 April they march out under his safe-conduct while the Parisians jeer at them. By Monstrelet's account they go on to Rouen by land and by water. Paris, Henry VI's capital since 1422, is now Charles VII's.

The map shows what the English hold after 13 April 1436, drawn approximately from the places the sources name: Normandy without Dieppe and the Caux, most of Maine, Calais and Gascony, and, round Paris, Meaux, Creil, Montereau and Mantes, with Beaumont-sur-Oise and Crépy, which the English won back in 1434. Chartres, which the Bastard of Orléans took in April 1432, and the bridge of Meulan, lost in September 1435, are the king's. The constable's march runs only from Pontoise to Saint-Denis, since no source gives his road.

### Notes

\* Ramsay puts Beaumont's defeat on 10 April. Monstrelet puts it on the Thursday before the French entered Paris.
