On 30 May 1434 about 10,700 men of the Taborite and Orphan field armies with 360 to 480 wagons meet about 13,500 to 14,000 men of the lords’ league with 660 to 720 wagons, by Petr Čornej’s estimates. František Šmahel puts the field armies at no more than 6,000 to 8,000. Both sides fight from wagon forts in the Hussite way, and the battle turns on a feigned retreat that draws the field armies out of theirs.

The league chooses Diviš Bořek of Miletínek as its commander and lays siege to the Orphan town of [Český Brod](@ceskybrod-1434) on 27 May. The next day it learns that the field armies are coming from Kouřim to relieve the town, and marches out to meet them. The field armies draw up their wagons at the foot of Lipská hora near the village of Lipany, and the league on the plain north-east of the village of Hřiby.

For two days the sides negotiate. The field armies ask for things to return to how they stood before 5 May, which would give them back the New Town of Prague and the siege of Plzeň. The league answers that the brotherhoods must first give up everything they have taken in the war. On Sunday 30 May the talks end.
