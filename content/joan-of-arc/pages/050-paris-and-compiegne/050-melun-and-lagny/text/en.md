The truce with Burgundy runs out at Easter, 16 April 1430, and Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, takes the field on the Oise as soon as it is past. At the end of March\* Joan leaves the court at [Sully](@sully-1430) without the king's leave, as if riding out for pleasure, says Perceval de Cagny, a chronicler of Alençon's household. Lowell has her ride north with her little household of soldiers.

[Melun](@melun-1430), held by the English and then the Burgundians since Henry V took it, drives out its garrison about Easter and comes over to Charles VII. Whether Joan was there before it rose is not known, Lowell writes. At her trial she said that in Easter week, in the trenches at Melun, her voices, Saint Catherine and Saint Margaret, told her she would be taken before Saint John's Day, 24 June. They told her so nearly every day after, but never said when.

She rides on to [Lagny](@lagny-1430) on the Marne, whose garrison harries the English of Paris. Near the town\*\* she and the garrisons' men meet Franquet d'Arras, a Burgundian captain of partisans, back from a raid with about 300 men. His archers, on foot, hold off her first attacks until the garrison of Lagny comes up with culverins and crossbows. His men are killed or taken, and he is captured.\*\*\*

At her trial she said she had wanted to exchange Franquet for the keeper of an inn in Paris, and gave him up to justice when she heard the innkeeper was dead. He was tried, she said, for a fortnight before the bailli of Senlis and a jury of Lagny men, and confessed to murder, theft and treason; he was beheaded. Monstrelet and Chastellain, both Burgundian chroniclers, blame her for his death.

On the map her road is approximate, and Melun is the king's.

### Notes

\* Moranvillé, Cagny's editor, puts it between 28 March and 1 April. Lowell has the very end of March or the first days of April.

\*\* The date of the fight is given differently: early May in Monstrelet, May in Champion, and the end of April in Lowell, who takes a raid of 21 April that the Bourgeois of Paris records for the same affair.

\*\*\* Monstrelet gives her 400 men. Cagny has 300 to 400 English put to death.
