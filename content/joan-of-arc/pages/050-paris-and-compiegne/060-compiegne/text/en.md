In May 1430 Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, takes the castle of [Choisy](@choisy-1430), held by Louis de Flavy for his brother Guillaume, the captain of [Compiègne](@compiegne-1430). He razes it and bridges the Oise. Soissons, held for Charles VII by a captain the duke has bought, shuts its gates on the army of Joan and the Archbishop of Reims. By 21 or 22 May the besiegers lie across the Oise from the town: the duke at [Coudun](@coudun-1430); John of Luxembourg, a Burgundian captain, at [Clairoix](@clairoix-1430); Baudot de Noyelle at [Margny](@margny-1430), at the end of the causeway from the bridge; and the English under Lord Montgomery at [Venette](@venette-1430).

Joan hears of the siege at Crépy. She rides through the night to see her good friends of Compiègne and comes in at sunrise. Late in the afternoon of 23 May she rides out over the bridge against the post at Margny. She drives the Burgundians back twice to their camp and a third time halfway, and then the English cut her off from the bridge. As the enemy reach the bridge, Guillaume de Flavy has it raised and the gate shut for fear of losing the town, leaving Joan outside with a few of her men. Whether it is treason is disputed.

An archer pulls her from her horse by her cloth-of-gold tunic, and she surrenders and gives her word to the Bastard of Wandonne, a man-at-arms of John of Luxembourg's company. The duke comes too late for the fight and speaks with her; what they say is not known. That evening she is given into John of Luxembourg's keeping.
