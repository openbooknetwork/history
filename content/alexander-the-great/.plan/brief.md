# Alexander the Great

The subject is Alexander III of Macedon: the kingdom his father built, the conquest of the Persian Empire in
eleven years, the great battles, the return, his death, and the Hellenistic world his successors carved from
the empire. 360 to 301 BC, from Philip II's accession (late 360 or 359) to the battle of Ipsus. It tells a war — one king's
campaigns — so there is no chapter on how a people lived; the Achaemenid Empire, which the reader may know
little, gets a context page before the invasion.

At the end the reader knows how a Balkan kingdom became master of Greece, how its army crossed into Asia and
broke the Persian Empire at the Granicus, Issus and Gaugamela, how far east the conquest ran and why it
stopped, how Alexander died and why the empire could not outlive him whole, and what the Hellenistic world he
left behind was.

## Sections

### 010 macedon-rising, 360 – 336 BC

**Tells:**
- Philip II takes a broken kingdom and rebuilds its army: the sarissa phalanx and the Companion cavalry
- Macedon expands: Thessaly, Thrace, the Chalcidice, the gold of Philippi
- Alexander's youth: Aristotle at Mieza, Bucephalus
- Chaeronea breaks Athens and Thebes; the League of Corinth makes Philip hegemon of Greece
- Philip is assassinated at Aegae; Alexander takes the throne at twenty

**Battles:** Chaeronea (card; no phase pages — its course cannot be recovered from the sources read, 010.14)

**Pages:** 6

### 020 greece-secured, 336 – 335 BC

**Tells:**
- The accession: rivals removed, the League of Corinth renewed under Alexander
- The Danube campaign against the Triballi and the Getae
- Illyria: Pelium and the escape
- Thebes revolts on a rumour of his death; the city is stormed and razed, Greece falls silent

**Battles:** Thebes (card)

**Pages:** 4

### 030 asia-minor, 334 – 333 BC

**Tells:**
- The Achaemenid Empire of Darius III: its satrapies, roads and armies (context page)
- The crossing of the Hellespont and the spear in the soil of Asia; Troy
- The Granicus: the first Persian army broken
- Sardis and the Ionian cities; the siege of Miletus; the fleet dismissed
- The siege of Halicarnassus; Memnon's war at sea
- Caria, Lycia and Pamphylia; winter march to Gordium and the knot

**Battles:** Granicus (phase pages, after Arrian's sequence; the text says Diodorus differs, 030.8–9), Miletus
(card), Halicarnassus (card)

**Pages:** 7

### 040 issus-and-the-coast, 333 – 332 BC

**Tells:**
- Cilicia; Darius brings the royal army and cuts the line behind Alexander
- Issus: the king against the King, Darius flees, his family captured
- Darius's first peace letter after Issus and its refusal (the greatest offer comes in 050, 040.11)
- The seven-month siege of Tyre: the causeway and the fleets
- Gaza taken; Egypt submits without a fight
- Memphis, the founding of Alexandria, the oracle at Siwa (Arrian's order; the text says the order is
  disputed, 040.19)

**Battles:** Issus (phase pages, drawn as a reconstruction since the Pinarus is not located, 040.5), Tyre
(card), Gaza (card)

**Pages:** 7

### 050 gaugamela-and-persia, 331 – 330 BC

**Tells:**
- Darius's greatest offer and its refusal; the march through Mesopotamia; Darius assembles the empire's last
  great army
- Gaugamela: the decisive battle on chosen ground
- Babylon and Susa open their gates; the treasuries
- The Uxian defile and the Persian Gate; Persepolis
- Agis III's rising in Greece is crushed at Megalopolis, probably early in 330 (050.15)
- The burning of the palace at Persepolis
- The pursuit of Darius; his murder by Bessus; the Greek allies sent home

**Battles:** Gaugamela (phase pages), Persian Gate (phase pages: the assault in the defile, the night march
over the mountain path, the dawn attack from behind; drawn as a reconstruction since the site is disputed,
050.19), Megalopolis (card)

**Pages:** 6 (Persepolis and the burning share a page, step 6 ruling)

### 060 bactria-sogdiana, 330 – 327 BC

**Tells:**
- Hyrcania and Areia; the Philotas affair and Parmenion's death
- Over the Hindu Kush after Bessus; Bactria taken, Bessus handed over and sent to Ecbatana for execution (060.5)
- Spitamenes raises Sogdiana; the Jaxartes; the column destroyed at the Polytimetus; nearly two years of
  campaigning (060.9, 060.15)
- The death of Cleitus at Maracanda; proskynesis and the pages' conspiracy
- The Sogdian Rock; marriage to Roxana (Arrian's order; the text says Bosworth dates the Rock to 328, 060.13)

**Battles:** Jaxartes (card), Polytimetus (card), Sogdian Rock (card)

**Pages:** 6

### 070 india, 327 – 325 BC

**Tells:**
- The Cophen campaign and the rock of Aornos
- Taxila: an ally beyond the Indus
- The Hydaspes: Porus and the elephants; Bucephalus dies
- East to the Hyphasis; the army refuses; the turn back
- The fleet down the Indus; the Mallian town and the arrow that nearly killed him
- Craterus's column leaves for the west; Patala and the Ocean reached (070.17)

**Battles:** Aornos (card), Hydaspes (phase pages, drawn as a reconstruction since the site is disputed,
070.9), Mallian town (card)

**Pages:** 7

### 080 the-long-road-home, 325 – 323 BC

**Tells:**
- The Gedrosian desert: heavy losses, whose scale the sources dispute (080.2)
- Nearchus's fleet along the coast to Susa
- The reckoning with the satraps; the Susa weddings
- The Opis mutiny and the reconciliation feast
- Hephaestion dies at Ecbatana
- Babylon: the last plans, the fever, death between the evenings of 10 and 11 June 323 BC (080.12)

**Battles:** none

**Pages:** 6

### 090 legacy, 323 – 301 BC and after

**Tells:**
- Babylon settlement: two kings, one regent, the generals take the satrapies
- Greece rises and falls: the Lamian War, ended at Crannon
- The wars of the Successors: Perdiccas, Antigonus, Eumenes's end at Gabiene, the kingdoms form
- Ipsus: Antigonus is killed and the empire is divided among Ptolemy, Seleucus, Lysimachus and Cassander
  (090.13, 090.15)
- The Alexandrias: Greek cities and koine from the Nile to Bactria
- The legend: the Alexander Romance

**Battles:** Crannon (card), Gabiene (card), Ipsus (card)

**Pages:** 7 (the Successors' wars take three pages, step 6 ruling)

## Count

56 section pages. Phase pages: Granicus, Issus, Gaugamela, the Persian Gate and the Hydaspes carry them, two
or three each — 12 to 15. Chaeronea and Tyre stay cards. About 68 to 71 pages in all, beside Maya at 70.

## Map

### Extent
`[18, 23.5, 77, 46]` (west, south, east, north). West: Pelium in Illyria, Epirus and the Peloponnese, with the
heel of Italy as context. South: Siwa, the Gedrosian coast and Patala on the Indus delta. East: the Hyphasis
(Beas), where the army turned back. North: the Getae beyond the Danube and the Saka beyond the Jaxartes. Zones
are clipped to the land from the Balkans and Greece through Asia Minor, the Levant, Egypt and Cyrenaica (Libya),
Mesopotamia, the Caucasus, Iran and Central Asia to the Indus and the Punjab (India). The countries around them
(Italy, the Arabian peninsula, Sudan, Ukraine, Russia, China and Nepal) are drawn with borders and get no zones.
Country labels stay off.

### Zones
Two families are cut back stage by stage. `persia-336` is the empire at its height, and each later `macedon-*`
zone sits over it with the lower priority number, so a page's Persian Empire is what Macedon has not yet taken.
`indians-327` is cut back the same way. The build cuts a zone once for the whole story, so each stage is its own
`zones.yaml` entry on the base outline, shown with its Macedon zone (see the note on cutting below the zone table). From macedon-356 to macedon-325, each `macedon-*` zone holds
all the land of the one before it. Pisidia is the exception: it stays out of macedon-333 and of every later
`macedon-*` zone, because Bosworth ch. 2.V says it was not conquered ("still stubbornly independent"; a cloud
ruling for the careful side). Where a page shows an outline already drawn, under another name or year, the
cartographer writes a `shared/zones.yaml` entry rather than a copy: macedon-322, macedon-323 and macedon-324 are
such entries. The Family column gives each zone's priority in brackets, which is its family's.

No source read draws a line on the ground. The digest's "Border" lines are 030.21 (the satrapies, reconstructed
from the settlement lists), 090.2 and 090.7 (the settlements as lists of regions) and 090.15 (the partition after
Ipsus, given only as "carved up" plus Coele-Syria). 030.2 is the Royal Road, a route rather than a zone. 040.5,
050.19 and 070.9 are battle sites. So every zone is a `reconstruction`, and each section's text says once that its
borders are approximate. Where a source does name a river (the Indus, Hydaspes, Acesines, Hyphasis, Oxus or
Jaxartes), the zone follows it.

No zone in `content/shared/zones/` covers these lands in these years: the shared zones are all of 1356–1481. The
Punic Wars zones `macedon-192` and `seleucid-192` show other years and other land. So no row names a shared zone.

The build cuts a zone wherever a zone of a lower priority number shares any page with it, not page by page. So persia-336 is not drawn once and cut back: each stage of the conquest has its own zones.yaml entry on persia-336's outline (persia-334a, persia-334, persia-333, and one more per later stage), and a page shows the stage of its date. The same holds for league-337, which gives league-335 on the page of Thebes razed.

Zones of this table also stand on pages outside the sections first planned for them. macedon-340 is on 030's
first pages, before any land in Asia. porus-326 is on the pages of 327 BC. thessaly-352, league-337 and
sparta-337 are on 090 pages, where they show Greece as it stands before the Lamian War. Each row's Sections
column includes these.

The Royal Road (030.2) is a route on the context page. The satrapies of 030.21 are not zones, because they are
provinces of one state. Markers can name them.

### Tally
None. The brief follows no single figure from page to page. The armies and the dead are given battle by battle
on the cards, and the sources dispute them (030.3, 040.8, 050.9, 080.2), so a running figure would have no basis.
