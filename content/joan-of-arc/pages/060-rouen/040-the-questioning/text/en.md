At eight in the morning of Wednesday 21 February 1431, Joan comes before her judges for the first time, in the chapel royal of [the castle of Rouen](@castle-sessions-1431). From the next day they sit in the Robing Room at the end of the great hall, and from 10 March a few of them question her in her cell: twelve days of questioning up to 17 March.

The judges press her on her voices. She says that a voice from God first came to her when she was thirteen, about noon one summer in her father's garden, and that she has seen Saint Michael with angels, and Saint Catherine and Saint Margaret. Of the sign she gave Charles VII, she tells on 13 March of an angel who brought a crown, which was given to the Archbishop of Reims in the king's presence. She took the man's dress at God's command, she says, and will not give it up unless He allows it.

On 27 March Thomas de Courcelles, a Paris bachelor of theology, reads her the charges drawn up by the promoter, or prosecutor, Jean d'Estivet: seventy articles. Nicolas Midi cuts them to twelve. On 5 April these go to the doctors and then to the University of Paris, which condemns her. Admonished in private on 18 April, while ill, and in public on 2 May, she does not submit.

On 9 May she is taken into [the great tower](@great-tower-1431), the keep of the castle, and shown the instruments of torture, with the torturers beside them. The judges put the torture off, and on 12 May they decide against it.
