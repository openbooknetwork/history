Joan rides out of [Vaucouleurs](@vaucouleurs-departure) by the Gate of France in men's clothes, with six companions: the squires Jean de Metz and Bertrand de Poulengy, their servants Julien and Jean de Honnecourt, a king's messenger named Colet de Vienne, and Richard the Archer.

The day she leaves is told three ways: about 12 February 1429, the day of the Battle of the Herrings near Orléans, about the 13th, or the 23rd.

As far as the Loire the road runs through country that owns Henry VI as king. They travel mostly by night, to stay clear of the Burgundian and English troops on the roads, and hear mass only twice. They sleep the first night at the abbey of [Saint-Urbain](@saint-urbain) on the Marne. At [Auxerre](@auxerre-1429), a Burgundian city, she hears mass in the principal church. At [Gien](@gien-1429), on the Loire about forty miles above Orléans, they reach a town that holds for Charles VII, and word of her goes ahead to Orléans. Then they ride on south of the river across the Sologne.

At [Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois](@fierbois-letter) she hears mass three times in one day and writes to the king for leave to come. She has ridden a hundred and fifty leagues to help him, she says. The road takes eleven days. She reaches [Chinon](@chinon-arrival) towards noon, on 23 February or on 6 March.

Her road runs across English and Burgundian land into the king's. Where she crosses the Loire is not told.
