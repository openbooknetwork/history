The peace conference opens at Lausanne in November 1922. İsmet Pasha leads the Turkish delegation. Talks break off in February and resume in April, and the treaty is signed on 24 July 1923. It replaces Sèvres, sets most of the borders of today's Türkiye and abolishes the capitulations. A separate convention signed there in January 1923 provides for an exchange of populations with Greece.

The status of Mosul is left to later negotiation, and Alexandretta remains part of French Syria. The Allied occupation of İstanbul ends in early October 1923, and Turkish troops enter the city on 6 October.
