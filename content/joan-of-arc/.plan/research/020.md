# Research for Joan of Arc

## 020 the-road-to-chinon

Coverage: primary (the trial of 1431 in Barrett's and Murray's translations, which are one record and count as one source; the depositions of 1455-1456 in Murray: Laxart, the Le Royers, Jean de Metz, Poulengy, Dunois, Gaucourt, Pasquerel, Seguin; the Journal du siège and the French Continuator of Guillaume de Nangis in Quicherat 4; Monstrelet A; the Bourgeois; Cagny; the Chronique de la Pucelle; the letter of 22 March) and scholarly (Lowell, Lang, Ramsay 1, Oman, Beaucourt 2, Thurston, Champion's sketches in Barrett). The Journal du siège and the Chronique de la Pucelle share their text and count as one account; Monstrelet's Herrings is Wavrin's (Ramsay's note). France's Life was read for where to look and no line rests on it alone: 020.63 cites it beside Lowell and Lang, and its reading of the crossing at Gien is marked single. The Herrings: the Journal, Monstrelet, the Bourgeois, Ramsay and Oman give the laager and the course of the fight, but none read gives the laager's size or a plan of the field; Cagny's editor sets the fight between Janville and Saint-Péravy, and the Wikipedia article reports, without a footnote, S. Cooper's case for Rouvray-Sainte-Croix (Cooper unread). No record of the Poitiers examination survives (Thurston, Lowell); what was said there rests on the witnesses of 1456. How Baudricourt kept Vaucouleurs in July 1428, and why he let her go in February 1429, are not known (Lang, Lowell).

- 020.1 Event: at about thirteen Joan first heard a voice, towards noon in summer, in her father's garden at Domrémy, on her right towards the church, and seldom without a light, as she told her judges on 22 February 1431. single (the trial record; Barrett and Murray translate one record). "declared that at the age of thirteen she had a voice from God to help her and guide her. And the first time she was much afraid. And this voice came towards noon, in summer, in her father's garden … She heard the voice on her right, in the direction of the church; and she seldom heard it without a light." (sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu-basis-joanofarc-trial.asp.txt, public examination of 22 February 1431, lines 1162-1166, primary) "The first time that I heard this Voice, I was very much frightened ; it was mid-day, in the summer, in my father's garden." - 020.3 Event: what she said the voice bade her: to leave home and go into France, to raise the siege of Orléans, and to go to Robert de Baudricourt at Vaucouleurs, who would give her an escort; her father knew nothing of it. agreed (her answers of 1431; Seguin's account in 1456 of what she told the examiners at Poitiers in 1429; Jean de Metz and Laxart report the same of her in 1429). "this voice told her once or twice a week that she should leave and come to France, and that her father knew nothing of her leaving … the voice told her again that she should raise the siege of the city of Orleans … she, Jeanne, should go to Robert de Baudricourt, in the town of Vaucouleurs of which he was captain, and he would provide an escort for her." - 020.10 Event: in July 1428 Antoine de Vergy, governor of Champagne for the English, marched against Vaucouleurs (his army reviewed at Saint-Urbain on 16-17 July, Lowell; marching about 17-18 July, Lang); the force left within days and Baudricourt kept the town. agreed (Lang; Lowell). How he kept it is open: Lang cites a compact of 22 July for a capitulation and says nothing more is known; Lowell guesses at a conditional surrender. "About July 17 or 18, 1428, the Governor of Champagne, Antoine de Vergy, marched a smaller force than he had expected to raise, for the purpose of reducing the region of Vaucouleurs to the English allegiance. … Vaucouleurs was not surrendered. The force which approached it departed within five days at most." (archive.org-download-maidoffrancebein00languoft-maidoffrancebein00languoft-djvu.txt.txt, ch. 5 and ch. 7, lines 3162-3165, 3853-3855, scholarly) "At any rate, the Anglo-Burgundian force withdrew, and Baudricourt kept Vaucouleurs for Charles VII. … On July 16-17 Antoine de Vergy passed the attacking army in review at St. Urbain" (archive.org-download-joanofarc00lowe-joanofarc00lowe-djvu.txt.txt, ch. 3 and note, lines 1599-1601, 1620-1621, scholarly)
