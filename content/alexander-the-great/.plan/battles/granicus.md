# Battle of the Granicus, May 334 BC

Section 030 (asia-minor). Card on `granicus`; three phase pages, `granicus-vanguard`, `granicus-bank`,
`granicus-mercenaries`. The phases follow Arrian's sequence (claim 030.9; decisions.md, the cloud ruling on the
Granicus). Diodorus's dawn crossing (030.8) is the other account: the overview's text tells it, and no phase draws
it. Sides: `macedon` (Alexander's army) and `persia` (the satraps' army and its Greek mercenaries), the story's
families.

## Sources

- **Arrian 1.12–16**, primary. The only full order of battle of the Macedonian army (1.14.1–3), from the extreme
  right to the centre and then from the extreme left to the centre (Bosworth, Comm. on 14.1). The Persian
  cavalry along the bank and the mercenaries behind (1.14.4). The sequence: the vanguard of Amyntas, son of
  Arrhabaeus, and Socrates's squadron; Alexander's oblique crossing; the fight on the bank; the break of the
  Persian centre and wings; the mercenaries surrounded (1.14.6–16.2). Losses (1.16.2–4). No Persian order of
  battle beyond the dense squadrons on their left (Welles, note 13: Arrian "does not give the Persian order of
  battle").
- **Plutarch 16**, primary. Parmenion's objection that it is late in the day; Alexander crosses "with thirteen
  troops of horsemen"; the duel with Rhoesaces and Spithridates; the phalanx crosses while the cavalry fight;
  the mercenaries' stand on "a certain eminence" and their request for quarter; losses. No order of battle.
- **Diodorus 17.17–21**, primary, the other account. The strengths of the army that crossed (17.17.3–4). The
  dawn crossing (17.19.3), which is not drawn. The only Persian order of battle, left to right (17.19.4), which
  this plan uses for the order of the Persian cavalry line (see Order of battle). Parmenion's wing engaged
  (17.19.6); the Thessalian cavalry praised (17.21.4); losses (17.21.6).
- **Bosworth, C&E ch. 2.III**, scholarly. The ground: the plain of Adrasteia, the foothills around Dimetoka, the
  banks three to four metres high, the river scarcely more than a metre deep, the gravel slopes, the foothills
  1½ km east of the river. A reading of Arrian's movements: Amyntas's detachment placed between the infantry and
  the Companions, Alexander's diagonal movement downstream to the left, the mercenaries enveloped on the wings.
  His warning that "the facts are probably lost for ever".
- **Bosworth, C&E "C. I. The invasion army of 334 B.C."**, scholarly. The units: eight Companion squadrons
  1,800 strong at the Hellespont, the prodromoi armed with the sarissa at the Granicus, the hypaspists "perhaps"
  three chiliarchies, the Thracian cavalry beside the allied Greek cavalry at the Granicus.
- **Bosworth, Comm. on 1.13–16**, scholarly. The site (Strabo: the plain of Adrasteia above the confluence with
  the Koçabaş Dere, two miles north of Biga); the vertical clay banks and the gravel beds; the hills 1–2 km from
  the river; the place of the archers and Agrianians; the two places Arrian gives Craterus's battalion; the
  infantry left out of Arrian's line; the oblique crossing. He judged Diodorus's version the more plausible
  (on 13.2).
- **Bosworth, CAH VI**, scholarly. Repeats the ground (the OCR prints "some 5 to 4 metres" for three to four, and
  "13 km" for 1½ km) and the vanguard crossing "diagonally across the Granicus, making use of the gravel slopes".
- **WP, Battle of the Granicus**, for the coordinates of the site (40.3167, 27.2811) and Hammond's location of
  the ford between Gümüşçay (formerly Dimetoka) and Çeşmealtı (Hammond 1980, through WP). Its order of battle
  follows Hammond and Devine and is not used where Arrian is read directly.
- Not used: Dodge (`archive.org-download-cu31924071184281-cu31924071184281-djvu.txt.txt`, ch. XIX) places
  artillery on Parmenion's flank and a separate oblique advance by Parmenion with no ancient source given;
  Tarn, vol. I (`archive.org-li.pahar.2881-...`) has the cavalry "on the steep bank of the lower Granicus" and
  adds nothing to the positions.

## Ground

**The river.** The Granicus is the Biga Çayı (names.md), which "rises in Mount Ida, and falls into the
Propontis near Cyzicus" (Arrian, Chinnock's note 121). At the battle it flows from south to north, towards the
Propontis: sourced, since Bosworth's "downstream to the left" for an army facing east puts downstream to the north
(Bosworth, C&E: "This movement naturally took him downstream to the left"), and Priapus lies "north-west of the
Granicus mouth" (Bosworth, Comm. on 12.8). The implementor draws its line as `water` from the river's real course
at the site; no source file gives that course point by point.

- Site: "the battle was fought in the plain of Adrasteia, which opens out above the confluence with the Koçabaş
  Dere, 2 miles north of the modern town of Biga" (Bosworth, Comm. on 13.1, after Strabo 13.1.11); "the Persian
  army in position on the east bank of the Granicus, occupying the foothills around the modern town of Dimetoka"
  (Bosworth, C&E). Hammond "has placed the ford between the modern villages Gümüşçay (formerly called Dimetoka)
  and Çeşmealtı" (WP, "Location"). Frame origin: the article's coordinates, lon 27.2811, lat 40.3167 (WP
  infobox), moved onto the river's course if they fall off it. `sourced` for the site, `reconstruction` for the
  exact ford.
- Banks: "The river Granicus was sunk in a channel below steeply rising banks, which are now some three to four
  metres high" (Bosworth, C&E); "almost vertical clay banks 3-4 m high in its lower reaches; but Janke reported
  the existence of several gently sloping gravel beds on both sides of the river" (Bosworth, Comm. on 13.4).
  `sourced`. Not drawn as a separate feature: the emblem has no bank piece; the text carries it.
- Depth: "the depth of the river, scarcely more than a metre in early spring, would not have been a serious
  obstacle" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced`. Arrian has Parmenion say "many parts of the stream are deep" (1.13).
- Width of the river: `unknown`. The implementor picks a water width for legibility.
- The higher ground: Arrian's mercenaries stand behind the cavalry "for the ground above the bank was steep and
  commanding" (1.14.4), "which can only have been the range of foothills 1½ km east of the river" (Bosworth, C&E).
  The Commentary gives "the hills running parallel to the river bank at an interval of 1-2 km" (on 13.2) and "the
  foothills, which rise some 150 m above the plain" (on 14.4). Plutarch: "These made a stand at a certain
  eminence" (16.13). `sourced`: the mercenaries' line stands about 1.5 km east of the river.

**Facing.** The Macedonians come from the west, from Arisbe and Hermotus (Arrian 1.12), and stand on the west
bank facing east (bearing about 90°, or square to the river's local course); the Persians "had taken up their
position on the other side of the Granicus" (Arrian 1.13) on the east bank, facing west. `sourced`. The
Macedonian right is upstream (south), the left downstream (north): `sourced`, from Alexander's oblique line "in
the direction in which the stream flowed" (Arrian 1.14.7) read with Bosworth's "downstream to the left" (C&E).
The Persian left is therefore the south end of their line, opposite Alexander: "where they observed Alexander
himself advancing against their left wing" (Arrian 1.14.4). `sourced`.

**Width of the front.** `unknown`. No source gives it, and no two sourced places fix its ends. For the drawing
only, and in no text: a Macedonian front of about 3.5 km along the river, the Persian cavalry line matching it,
and the mercenaries' line about 1.5 km long on the high ground. A suggested frame: `frame([27.2811, 40.3167], b)`
with `b` the river's local bearing downstream (about 0°, to be fitted to the real course), so u runs north along
the river (Macedonian right at negative u) and w runs east, from the Macedonians towards the Persians:
`f.face(90)` for the Macedonians, `f.face(270)` for the Persians. River at w = 0; Macedonian front a few hundred
metres west of it; Persian cavalry on the east bank top, w just above 0; mercenaries at w ≈ 1,500. One bbox for
all three phases, about u −2,600 to +2,700 and w −900 to +2,200 (some 5 × 3 km).

## Order of battle

### Macedon, from the extreme right (south, upstream) to the extreme left (north, downstream)

Arrian lists "the Macedonian units in lateral order, first from the extreme right to the centre and then from the
extreme left to the centre" (Bosworth, Comm. on 14.1). Alexander commands the right, Parmenion the left: "he sent
Parmenio to command upon the left wing, while he led in person on the right" (Arrian 1.14.1).

No source gives a Macedonian strength on the field. The figures below are of the army that crossed to Asia a few
days before (Diodorus 17.17.3–4) and are marked so; the unit names on the map leave them out unless a text gives

## Phases

### Phase 1: `granicus-vanguard`, "The vanguard in the river"

**Opens.** Both armies drawn up on their banks, the river between them, as in Order of battle: M1–M8 on the
west bank from south to north, facing east; P1–P4 along the top of the east bank, facing west, the dense
squadrons of P1 opposite M1 and M3; P5 on the high ground about 1.5 km behind. "Both armies stood a long time at
the margin of the river, keeping quiet from dread of the result; and profound silence was observed on both sides.
For the Persians were waiting till the Macedonians should step into the water, with the intention of attacking
them as they emerged" (Arrian 1.14.4–5). `sourced`.

**Moves.**

1. M3, the vanguard, goes straight across the ford and up the far bank into P1. "He then commanded Amyntas, son
   of Arrhabaeus, to make the first rush into the river at the head of the skirmishing cavalry, the Paeonians,
   and one regiment of infantry; and in front of these he had placed Ptolemy, son of Philip, in command of the
   squadron of Socrates, which body of men indeed on that day happened to have the lead of all the cavalry force"
   (Arrian 1.14.6). Arrow from M3's place east across the river, ending at the bank top in P1. The direction
   (straight across, on a gravel slope) is `reconstruction`: "we may assume that Amyntas' force took the easiest
   route, crossing the river without difficulty and continuing at speed up the opposite slope" (Bosworth, C&E);
   "This group forged diagonally across the Granicus, making use of the gravel slopes" (Bosworth, CAH).
2. P1 meets it with missiles from the bank and then comes down to the water. "The Persians began the contest by
   hurling missiles from above in the direction where the men of Amyntas and Socrates were the first to reach
   the bank; some of them casting javelins into the river from their commanding position on the bank, and others
   stepping down along the flatter parts of it to the very edge of the water" (Arrian 1.15.1). A `fire` arrow
   from P1 at the crossing point, and a clash at the east bank. `sourced`.
3. M3 is cut down and thrown back to Alexander: a `dashed` arrow from the east bank back west into the river
   towards M1. "the Macedonians who first came into conflict with the Persians, though they showed great valour,
   were cut down, except those who retreated to Alexander, who was now approaching" (Arrian 1.15.2–3); "It was
   badly mauled and forced back towards the river" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced`. M3's end block: a thin remnant in
   the river bed by the west bank, `reconstruction` from "retreated to Alexander".
4. Alexander leads the right wing, M1 with M2, into the ford in an oblique line downstream: an arrow from M1
   east and a little north (downstream), towards M3's crossing point. "He himself led the right wing with
   sounding of trumpets, and the men raising the war-cry to Enyalius. He entered the ford, keeping his line
   always extended obliquely in the direction in which the stream flowed, in order that the Persians might not
   fall upon him on the flank as he was emerging from the water" (Arrian 1.14.7); "This movement naturally took
   him downstream to the left, and he kept his forces in a diagonal line" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced`. The angle
   and the end point (the head of M1 at the east bank by M3's crossing) are `reconstruction`. The direction is
   disputed among modern writers (WP, "Account of Arrian": Badian a sideways move to the left in midstream,
   Hammond upstream to the right); the plan follows Arrian's words and Bosworth.

**Not moving.** M4–M8 and P2–P5 stand where they were. Arrian's "one rank of the Macedonians after another" comes
in phase 2. Plutarch's "thirteen troops of horsemen" (16.3) is one more sign of a cavalry crossing and moves no
other unit.

**Decides.** The first rush fails but draws the Persian charge on itself: "But it had achieved its main task. The
momentum of the Persian charge was absorbed and there was free ground for Alexander to make a counter-charge"
(Bosworth, C&E). About 25 Companions fall in this onset: "about twenty-five of the Companions were killed at the
first onset" (Arrian 1.16.4); "Amyntas' men had been virtually sacrificed to stem the Persian charge" (Bosworth,
Comm. on 16.4).

Units 13 (M1–M8, P1–P5), arrows 4, clashes 1–2. Markers: Alexander, Parmenion, Amyntas, son of Arrhabaeus (by
M3), Memnon (by P1), Arsites (by P2).

Reconstruction share: about a third (the crossing route of M3, the angle and end of Alexander's line, M3's
remnant, the Persian order of P2–P4 carried over from Diodorus, Craterus's battalion, the length of P5).

### Phase 2: `granicus-bank`, "The fight on the bank"

**Opens.** M1 (with M2 mixed in) at the east bank by the crossing point, in contact with P1, the remnant of M3
behind it; M4–M8 still on the west bank; P2–P4 on the bank top; P5 on the high ground. `sourced` from the end of
phase 1.

**Moves.**

1. Alexander's charge into the mass of the Persian horse and its leaders: clash between M1 and P1 at and above
   the east bank. "He made his first assault upon the Persians at the place where the whole mass of their horse
   and the leaders themselves were posted; and around him a desperate conflict raged, during which one rank of
   the Macedonians after another easily kept on crossing the river" (Arrian 1.15.3). `sourced`.
2. The fight pushes the Persians off the bank: a short arrow from M1 east onto the plain, P1 forced back. "the
   Macedonians striving to drive the Persians entirely away from the bank and to force them into the plain, and
   the Persians striving to obstruct their landing and to push them back again into the river. At last
   Alexander's men began to gain the advantage, both through their superior strength and military discipline, and
   because they fought with spear-shafts made of cornel-wood, whereas the Persians used only darts" (Arrian
   1.15.4–5). `sourced`. The duels are markers, not arrows: Alexander unhorses Mithridates, Rhoesaces breaks his
   helmet, Cleitus the Black cuts off the arm of Spithridates (Arrian 1.15.6–8); Plutarch has Spithridates crack
   the helmet and Cleitus run him through (16.8–11) (030.10, disputed). Spithridates `skull` by P2/P1, Cleitus
   the Black `user` by M1. `sourced`.
3. More squadrons cross along the river: "Meantime the horsemen, as many as were able, kept on securing a
   landing all down the river, and were joining Alexander's forces" (Arrian 1.15.8); "The bridgehead grew larger
   and, as more squadrons pressed over, the Persian cavalry was forced inexorably back" (Bosworth, C&E).
   `sourced` for the right wing. Parmenion's cavalry (M7, M8) crossing on the left is `reconstruction`: "If
   Diodorus (XVII. 19.6) may be trusted, the same operation took place on the left of the line, where the
   Thessalian cavalry under Parmenion established the bridgehead" (Bosworth, C&E), resting on "the command of
   Parmenion gallantly met the attack of the troops posted opposite them" (Diodorus 17.19.6) and "next to him the
   Thessalian cavalry won a great reputation for valour" (Diodorus 17.21.4), both from the other account. One
   arrow for M8 (with M7) across the river into P4.

### Phase 3: `granicus-mercenaries`, "The mercenaries cut down"

**Opens.** The Macedonian army across the river on the east bank (end of phase 2). The Persian cavalry gone. P5
alone on the high ground about 1.5 km east of the river: "the Greek mercenaries, the main body of whom was still
remaining where it was posted at first. This they did rather from amazement at the unexpected result of the
struggle than from any steady resolution" (Arrian 1.16.2); "they held their position in the foothills, no doubt
hoping for an armistice" (Bosworth, C&E); "These made a stand at a certain eminence, and asked that Alexander
should promise them quarter" (Plutarch 16.13). `sourced`.

**Moves.**

1. The phalanx (M4–M6) advances frontally on P5: an arrow from the centre east to P5's front, clash. "Leading the
   phalanx against these, and ordering the cavalry to fall upon them from all sides in the midst, he soon cut them
   up" (Arrian 1.16.2); "The luckless mercenaries had to face the phalanx frontally while they were enveloped on
   the wings by the cavalry" (Bosworth, C&E). `sourced`.
2. The cavalry envelops the wings: one arrow round P5's south flank (M1 with M3, Alexander's horse) and one round
   its north flank (M8 with M7, Parmenion's horse), each ending on P5's flank or rear; clashes on both flanks.
   `sourced` that the cavalry falls on them "from all sides" and on "the wings"; which cavalry takes which flank
   is `reconstruction` from their places in the line. Alexander charges first and loses his horse there: "he,
   influenced by anger more than by reason, charged foremost upon them and lost his horse ... and most of the
   Macedonians who were slain or wounded fought or fell there" (Plutarch 16.14). Alexander's marker stays by the
   southern arrow; `reconstruction` for the side.

**Decides.** The mercenaries are cut down where they stand, about 2,000 taken: "so that none of them escaped
except such as might have concealed themselves among the dead bodies. About 2,000 were taken prisoners" (Arrian
1.16.2); "nine tenths of them were cut down where they stood, the remaining two thousand taken as
prisoners-of-war" (Bosworth, C&E). P5's end block: the mercenaries' square, surrounded, with a `skull` marker
("cut down; about 2,000 taken"). Off the field, Arsites flees into Phrygia and kills himself (Arrian 1.16.3) and
the 300 panoplies go to Athens (Arrian 1.16.7; Plutarch 16.17): text only, not drawn. Arrian's Macedonian
losses (1.16.4) and the other authors' figures go in the text with their authors (030.12).

## Card

- **Sides**: `macedon` (Alexander's army) and `persia` (the satraps' army and its Greek mercenaries).
- **Commanders**: Macedon: Alexander; Parmenion, on the left wing (Arrian 1.14.1). Persia: Arsites, satrap of
  Hellespontine Phrygia, and Spithridates, satrap of Lydia and Ionia (Arrian 1.12); Arsames (Arrian 1.12;
  Diodorus 17.19.4 as Arsamenes); Memnon of Rhodes (Arrian 1.15.2; Diodorus 17.19.4). Arrian's Rheomithres,
  Petines and Niphates (1.12) are not in names.md and stay off the card.
- **Strengths**:
  - Macedon: no source gives the number on the field. The army that crossed to Asia, `disputed` (030.3): Arrian
    "not much above 30,000 foot" and more than 5,000 horse; Diodorus 32,000 foot and 4,500 horse (17.17.3–4).
  - Persia, `disputed` (030.11): Arrian "about 20,000" horse and Greek mercenary foot "a little short of the same
    number" (1.14.4); Diodorus "more than ten thousand" horse and "not fewer than one hundred thousand" foot
    (17.19.4–5).
- **Casualties**, `disputed` (030.12):
  - Macedon: Arrian about 25 Companions "at the first onset", "over sixty" other horse, "about thirty" foot
    (1.16.4); Aristobulus through Plutarch "thirty-four dead in all, of whom nine were footmen" (16.15).
  - Persia: Arrian "only about 1,000" horse killed and the mercenaries cut down but for "about 2,000" taken
    (1.16.2); Plutarch "twenty thousand footmen ... and twenty-five hundred horsemen" (16.15); Diodorus infantry
    "more than ten thousand", cavalry "not less than two thousand", and "upwards of twenty thousand" taken alive
    (17.21.6). Bosworth notes Gronovius's emendation of Arrian's 1,000 horse to 2,000 and reads Diodorus's
    20,000 prisoners as "probably a slip" for 2,000 (Comm. on 16.2); the card gives the ancient figures with
    their authors and not the emendations.
- **Outcome**: Macedonian victory. Spithridates killed (Arrian 1.16.3); Arsites flees into Phrygia and kills
  himself (Arrian 1.16.3); 030.6.
