On Holy Saturday, 22 March 1421, the Dauphin's army of Scots and French under the Earl of Buchan lies at Baugé in Anjou, having come up by Le Lude the day before. Thomas, Duke of Clarence, Henry V's brother, hears of it at dinner at Beaufort-en-Vallée and rides out at once with his mounted men-at-arms, without waiting for his archers. The fight is beyond the bridge over the Couasnon, on a low hill by the church of Vieil-Baugé. Clarence is killed, and the Earls of Somerset and Huntingdon are taken. The victors are the army that Cravant and Verneuil will break.

Henry V besieges [Meaux](@meaux-1422) on the Marne from October 1421 until May 1422, and there he catches the dysentery that kills him. He dies in the castle of [Vincennes](@vincennes-1422) on 31 August 1422. Henry VI, his son by Catherine of Valois, is a baby of nine months, and Henry V's brother John, Duke of Bedford, governs France for him as regent. Charles VI dies in [Paris](@paris-1422) on 21 October. At his burial at [Saint-Denis](@saint-denis-1422) on 11 November a herald cries Henry VI King of France and England, and Bedford rides back to Paris with the king's sword carried before him.

The Dauphin Charles has not waited: on 30 October he has already taken the title of King at [Mehun-sur-Yèvre](@mehun-1422), as Charles VII. His enemies call him the "King of Bourges", because he stays so long at [Bourges](@bourges-1422).

So France has two kings. Henry VI holds the north: by 1428, Paris and its country, Normandy, Perche, Alençon and most of Maine and Champagne. Charles VII is obeyed south of the Loire, between the Garonne and the Rhône, and in Dauphiné.
