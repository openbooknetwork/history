In 1407 the King of France is Charles VI, mad at intervals since 1392, as Lang dates it. Two princes of his blood fight over the power he cannot hold: his only brother, Louis, Duke of Orléans, and his cousin John the Fearless, Duke of Burgundy, who rules the trading cities of Flanders to the north and the duchy and county of Burgundy to the east.

On Sunday 20 November 1407 the two dukes make peace and take communion together. On the evening of Wednesday 23 November, Louis of Orléans leaves the house where he has dined with the queen, Isabeau of Bavaria, and rides off on his mule down the rue Vieille-du-Temple in [Paris](@paris-1407). Near the Porte Barbette armed men fall on him in the dark and cut him down. Monstrelet, a chronicler of the Burgundian side, counts about eighteen of them, who had lodged at an inn near the gate and had planned the killing for days.

John the Fearless soon admits to two of the princes that he gave the order, and then says so openly in the council. He withdraws to [Lille](@lille-1407), in his own lands, and later, Lowell writes, the great men of the kingdom gather with him to hear a priest praise the murder.

The killing turns the quarrel of two dukes into civil war. In 1410 the young Charles of Orléans, Louis's son, marries the daughter of Bernard, Count of Armagnac, and the count takes the lead of the Orléans party, whose men become known as the Armagnacs. Their enemies are the Burgundians, and the two parties divide France for the rest of this story.
