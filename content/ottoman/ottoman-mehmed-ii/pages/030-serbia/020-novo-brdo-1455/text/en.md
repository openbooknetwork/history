In spring 1455 Mehmed marches against southern Serbia and its silver mines. His army comes from [Edirne](@edirne) by Sofia and lays siege to Novo Brdo, the richest mining town of the despot. The Serbs cannot face the Ottoman army in the open, so they hold their towns and the peasants flee into the castles and the forests. After 40 days of fire from heavy guns Novo Brdo surrenders on 1 June 1455.

Konstantin Mihailović, a young man of the town, describes what follows. The sultan stands at the small gate of the castle and has the men of rank beheaded. About 700 young women are given to his soldiers and commanders, and 320 boys, Mihailović and his two brothers among them, are taken to be trained as janissaries.

The army takes the mines of [Trepča](@trepca-1455) and the other towns around Novo Brdo, and [Prizren](@prizren-1455) surrenders on 21 June. Southern Serbia becomes Ottoman. On his way home Mehmed visits the [tomb of Murad I](@murad-tomb-1455) on Kosovo field. Đurađ asks Hungary for a new crusade and comes back to Smederevo with nothing, and early in 1456 he accepts a peace that leaves the south to the sultan.
