Stephen the Great of Moldavia takes the Danube port of [Kiliya](@kilia-1465) (then Kilia) on 25 or 26 January 1465, and on 7 March 1471 he defeats Radu, the Ottoman vassal in Wallachia, at Soci. In 1473 he stops paying his tribute to the sultan. While Mehmed is at war with Uzun Hasan, Stephen marches from [Suceava](@suceava-1475) into Wallachia, defeats Radu again in a battle of three days at [Râmnicu Sărat](@ramnicu-1473) from 18 to 20 November 1473, takes [Bucharest](@bucharest-1473) and makes Basarab Laiotă prince of Wallachia. Radu soon returns with Ottoman help, and by late 1474 Basarab too has gone over to the Ottomans.

Mehmed orders Hadım Süleyman Pasha, the beylerbey of Rumelia, to take his army from the siege of Shkodër by [Sofia](@sofia-1474) into Moldavia. Süleyman crosses the frozen Danube and rests in Wallachia, where Basarab joins him with 17,000 men, and he enters Moldavia in December 1474. Stephen lays waste to the land before him and waits at the high bridge over the Bârlad near Vaslui, in a marshy valley with wooded hills on every side.

In fog on the morning of 10 January 1475 Stephen's guns and archers fire on the Ottomans from three sides, the wooden bridge breaks under their horsemen, and Moldavian buglers hidden behind the Ottoman lines spread confusion. Süleyman's army breaks, and Moldavian and Polish horsemen pursue it for three days as far as [Isaccea](@isaccea-1475) (then Oblucița) on the Danube. The Wallachians leave the field without fighting, and Pope Sixtus IV calls Stephen a true champion of the Christian faith.
