The legionaries serving as marines and the tight Roman formation make boarding difficult, but the Carthaginians gain ground as the day goes on. In the end Roman discipline breaks, and several crews run their ships aground on purpose and flee inland.

Pulcher breaks out to the south with 30 ships, the only ones to survive. The Carthaginians capture 93 Roman ships, an unknown number are sunk, and 20,000 men are killed or captured. It is Carthage’s greatest naval victory of the war.
