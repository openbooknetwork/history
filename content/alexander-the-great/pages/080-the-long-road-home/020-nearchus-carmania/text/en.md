As the monsoon winds begin to drop in the autumn of 325 BC, Nearchus takes the fleet out of the Indus below [Patala](@patala-325c). He sails west along the coasts of Ora and Gedrosia and then of the Fish-eaters, poor fishing people who have little food to spare for his crews. By his own account he loses one ship on the way, and he puts in at [Harmozeia](@harmozeia-325), the chief port of Carmania, near today's Bandar Abbas. Bosworth reckons the voyage at about 1,300 km.\*

Meanwhile Alexander rests his army for a few days at [Pura](@pura-325b), then marches west through the low basin of the Jaz Murian and up the Halil Rud into Carmania. There Craterus joins him with the elephants and the rest of the army, whom he has led by Arachosia and Drangiana, and convoys of food and camels arrive from the provinces to the north.

The generals Cleander and Sitalces also come down from Media. They are the officers who killed Parmenion at Ecbatana in 330 BC. Their subjects and their own soldiers accuse them of robbing temples and tombs and of abusing the people they rule. Alexander has them put to death, and with them, Bosworth says, 600 of their men. Arrian writes that he meant it as a warning to every governor. For Bosworth it opened a run of purges that fell mostly on Iranian satraps.

Nearchus reports his voyage to the king at a town Diodorus calls [Salmus](@salmus-325), which Bosworth takes for the Carmanian capital; where it stood is not known.\*\* Alexander then sends Nearchus back to the fleet to sail on to Susiana and the mouth of the Tigris.

### Notes

\* Bosworth puts the voyage at around sixty days, Engels at about seventy-five.

\*\* Diodorus places Salmus by the sea: Alexander is holding a dramatic contest in its theatre when the fleet sails into the harbour. Arrian has Nearchus travel up from the coast with a few men, and Bosworth puts the capital about five days from the sea, probably on the western side of the Halil Rud valley.
