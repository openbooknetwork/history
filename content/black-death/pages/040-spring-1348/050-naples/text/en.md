In November 1347 King Louis I of Hungary sets out from Visegrád to avenge his brother Andrew, who was murdered at Aversa in 1345. Louis blames Andrew’s wife, Queen Joanna I of Naples. His army enters her kingdom on 24 December, and on 15 January 1348 Joanna sails for her county of Provence. At Aversa, on 23 January, Louis has Charles of Durazzo, her cousin, beheaded where Andrew was killed. In February he enters [Naples](@naples).

The plague reaches Naples in the second half of March 1348. When Louis returns to the city in April from a tour of the kingdom, some of his barons are already dead, and the chronicler Matteo Villani writes that he fears for his men. He leaves 1,200 German horsemen under Conrad Wolf to hold the castles and crosses to [Barletta](@barletta) on the Adriatic. At the end of May he sails with a few companions to [Vrana](@vrana) on the Dalmatian coast and rides on to [Buda](@buda), which he reaches by early June.

No source of the time says that Louis’s men carry the plague into Hungary, and his army stays in Naples for the rest of 1348. The Dalmatian ports have had the plague since the turn of the year. Its first trace in Hungary comes from Transylvania in December 1348.

Joanna meanwhile is in Provence and needs money to win back her kingdom. On 9 June 1348 she sells [Avignon](@avignon-1348), which she holds as countess of Provence, to Pope Clement VI for 80,000 florins. When she hears that Louis has gone, she sails home. She reaches Naples on 17 August 1348. Louis’s garrisons soon lose most of what he won.
