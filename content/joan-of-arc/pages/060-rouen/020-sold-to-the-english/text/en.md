While Joan is held at Beaurevoir, the English raise the money to buy her in Normandy. In August 1430 the Estates of Normandy, meeting at [Rouen](@rouen-1430), grant Henry VI 120,000 livres tournois, ten thousand of them for the price of Joan, "said to be a witch". John of Luxembourg, the Burgundian lord who holds her, sells her for that sum.

[Compiègne](@compiegne-relief-1430) holds out under Guillaume de Flavy, and on 25 and 26 October it is relieved. The besiegers leave their guns behind.

In the latter part of October the Burgundians take her from [Beaurevoir](@beaurevoir-october-1430) to [Arras](@arras-1430), where the Duke of Burgundy seems to be holding his court. There Jean de Pressy, a knight of Artois, and many others ask her to put on a woman's dress, and she will not. She spends a night at the castle of [Drugy](@drugy-1430) on the way to [Le Crotoy](@le-crotoy-1430), a fortress at the mouth of the Somme that has been English since 3 March 1424. There, or at Arras or somewhere between, the English take her and pay. A receipt of 6 December names the ten thousand livres tournois the king paid to have her, a "prisoner of war". A fellow captive at Le Crotoy, Nicolas de Queuville, chancellor of the church of Amiens, hears her confession, and she hears him say Mass.

On 21 November the University of Paris writes to Henry VI and to Pierre Cauchon, Bishop of Beauvais. It complains of the delay and wants her tried in [Paris](@paris-university-1430).

She is brought to Rouen late in 1430 and shut in a tower of the castle. Her road from Le Crotoy to Rouen is not known.
