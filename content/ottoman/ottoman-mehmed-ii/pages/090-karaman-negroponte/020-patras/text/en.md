In February 1466 Venice elects Vettore Cappello, the loudest voice for the war, captain general of the sea for the second time. He leaves Venice in April, strengthens the walls of Modon, Lepanto and [Chalcis](@negroponte) (then Negroponte), and takes 25 galleys into the northern Aegean. There he takes the islands of [Gökçeada](@imbros-1466) (Imbros), [Thasos](@thasos-1466) and [Samothrace](@samothrace-1466) from the Ottomans.

On 12 July 1466 Cappello lands at Piraeus and marches on [Athens](@athens-1466), the main Ottoman base in the region. His men take the lower town without much fighting, but the Acropolis holds. In early August he gives up and sails for Patras, which the Venetian provveditore of the Morea, Jacopo Barbarigo, is besieging with 2,000 men.

A few days before Cappello arrives, with the town about to fall, Turahanoğlu Ömer Bey comes with 12,000 horsemen and routs Barbarigo’s force. Six hundred Venetians are killed and a hundred taken prisoner, and Barbarigo is killed and his body impaled. Cappello attacks to avenge him and loses 1,200 men, and he takes what is left of his army back to Chalcis, where he dies on 13 March 1467.
