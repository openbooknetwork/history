# Asia Minor

## Header
- Years: 336 BC (Darius III's accession, on the context page) to summer 333 BC (Memnon's death off Mytilene);
  the campaign itself runs from spring 334 to spring 333 BC
- Opens: Alexander holds Macedon with Thrace to the Haemus, is archon of Thessaly and hegemon of the League of
  Corinth, with Sparta outside it; Thebes is razed and Greece is quiet (020.13, 020.14). The Persian Empire holds
  all of Asia Minor but the crossing of the Hellespont: Philip's advance force, pushed back by Memnon and the
  satraps in 335, holds only the crossing points on the Asian shore, Abydus among them (Bosworth ch. 2.II; claim
  030.25). The reader has been told that the League voted war on Persia to punish the burning of the
  temples (010.18), that Philip sent an advance force into Asia under Parmenion and Attalus in 336 (010.19), that
  Attalus was murdered (020.1), and that the Greeks gave Alexander Philip's command against Persia (020.2).
- Tells:
  - 336 BC: Bagoas poisons Artaxerxes III and kills Arses, and Darius III takes the throne (who made him king is disputed, 030.1); Darius makes Bagoas
    drink his own poison (030.1). The empire of some twenty satrapies (030.21) and the Royal Road from Sardis to
    Susa (030.2); its armies, and the Greek picture of them that Briant rejects (030.24)
  - spring 334 BC: Alexander leaves Macedon and Greece to Antipater and marches by Amphipolis to Sestos, twenty days from home (Arrian 1.11), in
    twenty days (030.4); the army that crosses, by Arrian's and Diodorus's figures (030.3)
  - spring 334 BC: the crossing of the Hellespont, the spear cast into the shore, the tombs of the heroes at Troy
    (030.5)
  - spring 334 BC: the satraps' council at Zeleia overrules Memnon's advice to avoid battle (030.22)
  - May 334 BC: the battle of the Granicus against the satraps' army (030.6–030.12); Arsites flees and kills
    himself; 300 panoplies sent to Athens (030.13)
  - summer 334 BC: Calas made satrap of Hellespontine Phrygia, Parmenion takes Dascylium, Mithrenes surrenders
    Sardis, democracies at Ephesus and in the Ionian cities (030.14)
  - summer 334 BC: the siege of Miletus; the fleet of Nicanor, the fleet commander, holds Lade against some 400 Persian ships; the city is
    stormed (030.15); Alexander disbands his fleet (030.16)
  - autumn 334 BC: the siege of Halicarnassus against Memnon, now commander of the fleet and coast; the defenders
    burn the city and hold the citadels and Cos; Alexander razes it and gives Caria to Ada (030.17, 030.18)
  - winter 334 – spring 333 BC: Parmenion by Sardis into Phrygia; Alexander through Lycia, the beach below Mount Climax,
    Pamphylia, across Pisidia (a passage, not a conquest) to Celaenae; Antigonus made satrap of Phrygia; the
    purpose of the march disputed (030.19, 030.23)
  - spring 333 BC: Gordium and the knot (030.20)
  - spring to summer 333 BC: Memnon takes Chios and Lesbos, besieges Mytilene and dies; who took the city is
    disputed; Pharnabazus and Autophradates carry on (030.18)
- Introduces:
  - People: Darius III, Artaxerxes III, Arses, Bagoas, Memnon of Rhodes, Arsites, Spithridates, Arsames,
    Rhoesaces, Amyntas, son of Arrhabaeus, Socrates, son of Sathon, Philotas (Parmenion's son,
    commanding the Companion cavalry on the right at the Granicus, Arrian 1.14), Cleitus the Black, Calas, Mithrenes, Nicanor,
    the fleet commander, Nicanor, son of Parmenion (in the phase texts), Hegesistratus, Orontobates, Ada, Antigonus, Pharnabazus, Autophradates; the writer Herodotus, and the historians
    Briant, Badian and Jacobs where a text says who holds a view
  - States and peoples: the Persian Empire, the Achaemenids, the Persians, the Carians, the Lycians
  - Places: Asia Minor, the Royal Road, Susa, Sestos, Troad, Troy, Zeleia, Granicus, Hellespontine Phrygia,
    Dascylium, Lydia, Sardis, Ionia, Ephesus, Miletus, Lade, Caria, Halicarnassus, Salmacis, Arconnesus, Cos,
    Chios, Lesbos, Mytilene, Antissa, Methymna, Pyrrha, Eressus, Lycia, Mount Climax, Pamphylia, Pisidia, Phrygia,
    Celaenae, Gordium. The Hellespont is introduced in 010; the satrapy labels of the context page (Egypt,
    Babylonia, Persis, Media, Bactria) stand on its map but are introduced in 040 and 050
- Battles: the Granicus, 3 phase pages; the siege of Miletus, card only; the siege of Halicarnassus, card only
- Hands on: Alexander stands at Gordium in spring 333 BC with his army reunited, master of western Asia Minor:
  Hellespontine Phrygia under Calas, Lydia, Ionia, Caria under Ada, Lycia, Pamphylia, and Phrygia under Antigonus.
  Left unsettled: Pisidia, crossed but not conquered, stays independent; Memnon's fleet holds Chios and Lesbos
  and the sea, and after his death in summer 333 Pharnabazus and Autophradates carry on the war; Paphlagonia,
  Cappadocia and Cilicia lie ahead of the army, and Darius is mustering the royal army at Babylon (040.2, told
  in 040).

## Pages

**achaemenid-empire**, 336 BC. Darius III comes to the throne in 336 BC, the same year as Alexander, after the
eunuch Bagoas has poisoned Artaxerxes III and killed Arses; Darius then makes Bagoas drink his own poison. This
is the context page the brief asks for: the empire the League has voted war on, its twenty or so satrapies, the
Royal Road that joins Sardis to Susa, and its armies, which Greek writers painted as unable to stand without
Greek mercenaries. Why: the reader meets Persia here before the invasion, as `agents/
outline.md` asks for a war against a people the reader knows little about. Map change: the Persian Empire enters
the story whole, from the Aegean to the Hindu Kush, with its satrapies named and the Royal Road drawn.
Rests on: 030.1, 030.2, 030.21, 030.24. 030.2, 030.21 and 030.24 are `single`: the distance and the ninety
days are given as Herodotus's figures through Briant, with Briant's warning that Herodotus's account of the roads
is fragmentary; the satrapies are Jacobs's reconstruction from the settlement lists, and the text says no source
draws their bounds; the armies are told as Briant's reading, named as his: the Greek picture of a Great King who
could not field a real army and leaned on Greek mercenaries goes back to Ephorus through Diodorus, and Briant
holds it untenable; the 20,000 mercenaries at the Granicus may be given as the ancient figure he cites. The
30,000 mercenaries before Issus is left to 040. The text does not credit the road to Darius I: in 030.21 only the Wikipedia quote says so.

**hellespont**, spring 334 BC. Alexander leaves Macedon and Greece to Antipater and marches from Amphipolis to
Sestos in twenty days; Parmenion ferries the army across the Hellespont while Alexander crosses from the tip of
the Chersonese to the Troad, casts his spear into the shore, leaps out first, taking Asia as spear-won, and
honours the tombs of Achilles and the heroes at Troy. In the temple of Athena at Troy he dedicates his own armour
and takes in exchange arms kept from the Trojan War, which are carried before him into battle (070 recalls them,
when Peucestas covers him with the shield from Troy in the Mallian town). The advance force sent in 336, pushed
back by Memnon and the satraps in 335, has kept only the crossing points on the Asian shore, Abydus among them,
and the army lands under its cover. Why: the war of the League against Persia, voted in 337
and given to Alexander in 336 (010.18, 020.2), and Greece silenced by the fall of Thebes (020.14). Map change:
the Macedonian army marches across Thrace and crosses into Asia.
Rests on: 030.3, 030.4, 030.5, 030.25 (the advance force driven back to the Hellespont, Abydus held; the
researcher is adding the line, from Bosworth ch. 2.II). The arms from Athena's temple rest on 030.5 as told by
Arrian 1.11 and Bosworth ch. 2.III, both in files 030.5 cites. 030.3 is `disputed`: the text gives Arrian's "not much above 30,000 foot" and
more than 5,000 horse and Diodorus's 32,000 foot and 4,500 horse, each with its author, with Diodorus's 12,000
foot and 1,500 horse left with Antipater; Plutarch's range may follow in one clause.

**granicus** (overview of the battle group), May 334 BC. The army marches east from the Troad along the
Hellespont; the satraps of Asia Minor, Arsites of Hellespontine Phrygia and Spithridates of Lydia with Arsames
and Memnon of Rhodes, have gathered their cavalry and Greek mercenaries and wait on the far bank of the
Granicus. Why: at their council at Zeleia Memnon of Rhodes urged the satraps to avoid battle, burn the fodder
and crops and carry the war to Europe; Arsites would not see a house of his satrapy burned, the Persians sided
with him, and they marched to the Granicus to fight. Map change: the Macedonian march from Arisbe to the Granicus
and the satraps' army moving west from Zeleia to meet it.
Rests on: 030.22, 030.6, 030.7, 030.8, 030.11. 030.22 is `agreed` and told plainly. 030.8 is `disputed`: the text tells Arrian's and Plutarch's version
(Alexander rejects Parmenion's advice and forces the river late in the day against cavalry lined on the bank)
and Diodorus's (an unopposed crossing at dawn), each with its author, says the phase pages follow Arrian, and
gives Bosworth's two judgements: Diodorus's version the more plausible in his Commentary (1980), the weight of
evidence for Arrian in Conquest and Empire (1988), where he adds that the facts are probably lost for ever.
030.11 is `disputed`: Arrian's about 20,000 horse and nearly as many Greek mercenaries, Diodorus's more than
10,000 horse and at least 100,000 foot, each with its author.

**granicus-vanguard** (phase), May 334 BC. The two armies face each other across the river; Amyntas, son of
Arrhabaeus, and Socrates's squadron lead into the ford and are mauled by the best of the Persian horse, Memnon
and his sons among them; Alexander follows with the right wing, its line drawn obliquely downstream. Map change:
the first crossing. Rests on: 030.9.

**granicus-bank** (phase), May 334 BC. The cavalry fight on the bank; Alexander unhorses Mithridates, Rhoesaces
breaks his helmet, Cleitus the Black cuts off Spithridates's arm; the Persian centre gives way and then the
wings. Map change: the Persian horse broken and in flight. Rests on: 030.9, 030.10. 030.10 is `disputed` in
detail: Arrian has Cleitus sever Spithridates's arm after Rhoesaces struck the helmet; Plutarch has Spithridates
crack the helmet and Cleitus run him through. The text gives both with their authors.

**granicus-mercenaries** (phase), May 334 BC. The phalanx and the cavalry surround the Greek mercenaries, who
have stood apart on the higher ground, and cut them down; about 2,000 are taken. Arsites flees into Phrygia and
kills himself; Alexander sends 300 panoplies to Athens with the dedication from all the Greeks except the
Lacedaemonians. Map change: the mercenary infantry destroyed. Rests on: 030.9, 030.6, 030.12, 030.13. 030.12 is
`disputed`: the text gives Arrian's losses (about 1,000 Persian horse; of the Macedonians about 25 Companions,
over 60 other horse and about 30 foot), and names Plutarch's and Diodorus's far larger Persian figures and
Aristobulus's 34 Macedonian dead (through Plutarch), each with its author.

**miletus**, summer 334 BC. Calas takes Arsites's satrapy, Parmenion occupies Dascylium, Mithrenes surrenders
Sardis, and at Ephesus and the Ionian cities Alexander sets up democracies and remits the tribute. At Miletus the
garrison commander Hegesistratus resists, counting on the Persian fleet; the 160 ships of Nicanor, the fleet
commander, reach Lade three days before some 400
Persian ships, the walls are breached and the city stormed. Then Alexander disbands his fleet. Why: after the
Granicus no Persian army stands in the field in Asia Minor (030.12, 030.14), and Miletus holds out because the
Persian fleet is near (Arrian 1.18). Map change: Hellespontine Phrygia, Lydia and Ionia with Miletus pass to
Alexander; the two fleets meet off Miletus.
Rests on: 030.14, 030.15, 030.16. 030.14 is `single` (Arrian) and told plainly with Arrian named once for the
democracies and the remitted tribute. 030.15 is `single` for the fleet figures: the 160 and the 400 name Arrian.
030.16 is `disputed` for the reason: Arrian's (no money, a fleet no match for the Persian, the plan to beat the
fleet from the land) and the view Diodorus reports (to cut off the army's hope of flight), each with its author.

**halicarnassus**, autumn 334 BC. Alexander marches down the coast into Caria and besieges Halicarnassus, held by
Memnon, whom Darius has named commander of the fleet and the whole coast, with the Carian satrap Orontobates.
After weeks of sorties and breaches the
defenders burn the city and withdraw to the citadels of Salmacis and Arconnesus and to Cos; Alexander razes the
city, leaves the citadels blockaded and gives Caria to Ada. Why: the Persian forces and mercenaries driven from
the coastal garrisons had gathered at Halicarnassus (Arrian 1.20, Diodorus 17.23.4). Map change: Caria passes to
Alexander under Ada, while the citadels and Cos stay Persian.
Rests on: 030.17, 030.18 (the appointment only, which is `agreed`). 030.17 is `agreed`.

**lycia-pamphylia**, winter 334 – spring 333 BC. Alexander sends the newly married men home for the winter and splits his
army: Parmenion goes by Sardis into Phrygia with the allies and the baggage, while Alexander marches through
Lycia, takes his own column along the beach below Mount Climax when the north wind holds back the sea, passes
through Pamphylia and north across Pisidia to Celaenae, where he makes Antigonus satrap of Phrygia. Pisidia is
crossed, not conquered: Alexander takes a Pisidian town by storm but leaves no garrison, and the region stays independent
(Bosworth ch. 2.V), so the map leaves it out of Alexander's lands. Why: disputed, see below. Map change: Lycia,
Pamphylia and Phrygia added, Pisidia left outside; two Macedonian columns cross Asia Minor.
Rests on: 030.19, 030.23. 030.19 is `single` (Arrian): told plainly, with Arrian named for the north wind.
030.23 is `disputed`: Arrian says Alexander meant to command the coast and make the Persian fleet useless;
Bosworth says the route left the coast unblockaded and that Alexander had probably already decided to march east
against the Great King. The text gives both, each with its author. The passage through Pisidia rests on Bosworth
ch. 2.V (the brief's cloud ruling on macedon-333), named as his.

**gordium**, spring to summer 333 BC. Alexander reaches Gordium, where Parmenion joins him, and looses the knot of
the wagon whose loosener would rule Asia. Meanwhile Memnon, commander of the fleet and coast, carries the war to
the islands, meaning to move it into Macedon and Greece: Chios is surrendered to him, the towns of Lesbos go over,
and he blockades Mytilene, and dies of illness; Pharnabazus and Autophradates carry on. Why:
Gordium was the meeting point of the two columns (Arrian 1.29); Memnon's aim is in the quote of 030.18. Map
change: the army reunited at Gordium; the Persian fleet sweeps up the Aegean islands behind it.
Rests on: 030.20, 030.18. 030.20 is `disputed`: most writers say he cut the cord with his sword, Aristobulus
that he drew out the pin of the pole, and Arrian will not decide; the text gives both and Arrian's refusal.
030.18 is `agreed` for the campaign and Memnon's death and `disputed` in detail on who took Mytilene: Diodorus
(17.29.2) has Memnon storm it before he dies, Arrian (2.1) has it surrender to Pharnabazus after his death. The
text gives both, each with its author, and the map marks the city as blockaded and taken without saying by whom.

## Battles

**granicus**, May 334 BC (the month Daesius; 030.7). Card on `granicus`; 3 phase pages.
- Sides: `macedon` (Alexander's army) against `persia` (the satraps' army and its Greek mercenaries).
- Commanders: Alexander, with Parmenion on the left wing (Arrian 1.14); for Persia the satraps Arsites and
  Spithridates, Arsames, and Memnon of Rhodes (030.6). Arrian also names Rheomithres, Petines and Niphates (1.12),
  who are not in names.md and stay off the card.
- Strengths: Macedonian, the army that crossed to Asia, `disputed` (030.3): Arrian not much above 30,000 foot and
  more than 5,000 horse; Diodorus 32,000 foot and 4,500 horse. No source gives the number on the field.
  Persian, `disputed` (030.11): Arrian about 20,000 horse and a little fewer Greek mercenaries; Diodorus more than
  10,000 horse and at least 100,000 foot.
- Casualties, `disputed` (030.12): Macedonian, Arrian about 25 Companions, over 60 other horse, about 30 foot;
  Aristobulus (through Plutarch) 34 dead, 9 of them foot. Persian, Arrian about 1,000 horse killed and the
  mercenaries cut down but for about 2,000 taken; Plutarch 20,000 foot and 2,500 horse; Diodorus more than 10,000
  foot and at least 2,000 horse dead and over 20,000 taken.
- Outcome: Macedonian victory; Spithridates killed, Arsites's suicide (030.6).
- Phases: 3, following Arrian's sequence (030.9; decisions.md, Granicus ruling): the vanguard in the river, the
  fight on the bank, the mercenaries. Positions and movements of both sides: Arrian 1.13–16 in
  `.references/archive.org-download-theanabasisofale46976gut-46976-0.txt.txt`; Bosworth, Conquest and Empire
  ch. 2.III "First victory" in `.references/archive.org-onquest-20and-20Empire-20The-20Reign-20of-20Alexander-20the-20Great-20-281993-29-djvu.txt.txt`
  (the ground: the east bank by Dimetoka, banks three to four metres high, gravel slopes, the mercenaries on the
  foothills about 1.5 km back); Bosworth's Commentary I on 1.13–16. Diodorus 17.19–21 is the other version and is
  not drawn. Names: Arrian's hypaspist commander at the Granicus is written "Nicanor, son of Parmenion" at every
  mention (names.md: three Nicanors, each with a fixed description).

**miletus**, summer 334 BC. Card on `miletus`; no phase pages (the brief gives it a card).
- Sides: `macedon` against `persia` (the garrison and the Persian fleet).
- Commanders: Alexander, with Nicanor, the fleet commander, at the head of the Greek fleet (030.15). Persian:
  Hegesistratus, commander of the garrison (Arrian 1.18; names.md); the fleet's admirals are not named.
- Strengths: fleets, Macedonian 160 ships, Persian about 400 ships, both Arrian (030.15, `single`). Land forces
  `unknown`.
- Casualties: `unknown` (no figure in the sources read; Arrian 1.19 says most defenders were killed in the city
  and about 300 mercenaries on an islet were taken into service, which the card may give as a note).
- Outcome: Miletus stormed; the Persian fleet withdraws without a battle.

**halicarnassus**, autumn 334 BC. Card on `halicarnassus`; no phase pages.
- Sides: `macedon` against `persia` (Persian troops and Greek mercenaries).
- Commanders: Alexander; Memnon of Rhodes (030.17, 030.18), with Orontobates, satrap of Caria (Arrian 1.23;
  names.md).
- Strengths: `unknown`.
- Casualties: `unknown` for the siege as a whole; Arrian gives figures for single sorties only (1.20, 1.22).
- Outcome: the city burned by its defenders and razed by Alexander; the citadels Salmacis and Arconnesus stay in
  Persian hands, blockaded; Caria given to Ada (030.17).

### 020-hellespont
- Title: "The crossing of the Hellespont"
- `date`: "Spring 334 BC"; `when`: none
- Edges: west, Amphipolis and the Chalcidice; east, the Troad and the shore of the Propontis; north, the Hebrus
  valley in Thrace; south, Lesbos. About `[23.5, 39.4, 27.6, 41.6]`.
- Zones: `macedon-340`, macedon (Macedon and Thrace, before any land in Asia), reconstruction as in the brief;
  `thessaly-352`, thessaly, reconstruction from 010.7; `league-337`, league, reconstruction from 010.18, 020.2;
  `thracians-340`, thracians, the free Thracians of the Haemus, reconstruction from 020.3; `triballi-335`,

- Routes (all `macedon`, in the order travelled):

  - `crossing-parmenion-334`, from Sestos across the strait to the Asian shore opposite (Abydus in Arrian 1.11

  - `troy-arisbe-334`, from the Achaeans' Harbour by way of Troy to the army's camp in the plain of Arisbe;

- Markers:
  - `amphipolis-334`, Amphipolis, `macedon`: where the march sets out
  - `sestos-334`, Sestos, `macedon`: the crossing point on the European shore
  - `hellespont-334`, Hellespont, label: the strait, "no more than seven stades wide" between Sestos and the Asian

## Choices
- The context page is dated 336 BC and stands first, before the section's years of 334–333, because Darius's
  accession and the empire he took over come before the invasion; it uses the Philip-era zones `macedon-340`,
  `league-337`, `sparta-337` and `thessaly-352` for Greece.
- `macedon-340` serves the section's first three pages (context, Hellespont, Granicus overview), since no land in
  Asia is held before the Granicus; the brief lists it for 010 and 020 only, so its sections widen to 030. No new
  zone is needed for that.
- The zone `macedon-334a` (summer 334: Hellespontine Phrygia, Lydia, Ionia with Miletus) stands on the Miletus
  page, because `macedon-334` is the end of 334 with Caria under Ada, which is not yet taken while Miletus is
  besieged. It was proposed by this plan and is now in the Map part.
- Dascylium, Sardis, Ephesus and the Ionian cities share the Miletus page: one campaign of one summer on the same
  coast. Miletus and Halicarnassus keep pages of their own, 60 km and a few weeks apart, because each carries its
  own siege card and a page carries one battle.
- Memnon's war at sea is told on the Gordium page, not with Halicarnassus as the brief's line pairs them: his
  offensive opens in spring 333 and he dies in summer 333 (Arrian 2.1, Bosworth ch. 2.V: "When Alexander was at
  Gordium, Memnon's offensive was at its height"), after the winter march. The Halicarnassus page names his
  appointment only.
- The Granicus gets 3 phases following Arrian's sequence of 030.9, one per step of it; the losses, Arsites's
  suicide and the panoplies close the third phase instead of an aftermath page.
- The Halicarnassus citadels share one marker with the city, as they stand on it at the page's scale.
- The Aegean islands stay out of every zone of the section, so Memnon's gains are shown by markers and his
  fleet's route, not by a zone that would need a family or outline of its own.
- Not drawn, though Arrian tells them: the night attempt on Myndus, the newly married men sent home and their
  return with reinforcements to Gordium, Pharnabazus's move to Tenedos, Proteas's raid at Siphnos, and the arrest
  of Alexander, son of Aeropus. None is a digest claim and none changes the map of its page.
- Abydus gets a marker on the Hellespont page, since the text names it as the advance force's foothold.
- The citadels of Halicarnassus are not carried as an open thread: no later section takes them up, and their
  fall in 332 is in no digest claim, so the halicarnassus page leaves them blockaded and Hands on drops them.
- Markers and labels use names.md forms only. Zeleia has a marker on the Granicus overview, since 030.22 tells
  the council there. Elaeus, Arisbe, Mycale, Samos, Phaselis, Perge, Termessus, Sagalassus, Aspendus and
  Side stand only as points of routes, without markers; route names avoid them.
- Pisidia is shown as a label on Persian-coloured land on the winter-march page, as land crossed and not held
  (the brief's cloud ruling); the page's text says so in one clause.
- On the context page six of the twenty or so satrapies are shown by label: Hellespontine Phrygia, which the
  section enters first, and five that later sections reach and introduce (Egypt, Babylonia, Persis, Media, Bactria), chosen to
  show the empire's spread; Lydia is shown by its seat, Sardis. Jacobs names them all.
