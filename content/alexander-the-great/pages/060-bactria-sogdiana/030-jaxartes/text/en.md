With Bessus taken, Alexander marches north in summer 329 BC to [Maracanda](@maracanda-329) (today Samarkand), Sogdiana's royal seat, and on to the Jaxartes (today the Syr Darya), the northern edge of the Persian Empire. He and his staff take it for the Tanais, the Greeks' boundary of Asia and Europe. Near today's Khujand he plans a city, Alexandria Eschate, to guard against the nomads beyond, Arrian says.

@image syr-darya-khujand-e5fd4b

The towns along the river kill their Macedonian garrisons, and most of Sogdiana and part of Bactria follow Spitamenes and Dataphernes, the nobles who had given up Bessus. Bosworth notes that the local lords distrusted an assembly Alexander had called at Bactra. Spitamenes besieges the garrison in the citadel of Maracanda.

Alexander takes five of the seven towns by the river in two days, killing the men and enslaving the rest. At [Cyropolis](@cyropolis-329), the largest, whose site is uncertain, he leads a few men in along a dry stream bed under the wall, and a stone strikes him on the head and neck. He sends a column south to relieve Maracanda and fortifies the new city in twenty days.

@image khujand-fortress-remains-ec7d5b

Across the river Saka horsemen jeer at him. Catapults clear the far bank and the army crosses on stuffed skins. The nomads circle and wound the first squadrons, but when Alexander mixes archers and javelin men among his horsemen and charges, they flee. In this battle of the Jaxartes Arrian counts 1,000 Saka dead, among them the chief Satraces, and 150 taken. Curtius puts the Macedonian dead at 60 horsemen and about 100 foot, with 1,000 wounded. Alexander chases them some 15 km, Bosworth reckons, until bad water makes him too sick to go on. The Saka king then sends envoys to apologise.

On the map Sogdiana joins the empire and at once rises.
