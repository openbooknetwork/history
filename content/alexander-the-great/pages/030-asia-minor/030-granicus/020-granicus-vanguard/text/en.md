Late in the day the two armies face each other across the Granicus.\* On the west bank [Alexander](@granicus-vanguard-alexander) commands the right wing with the Companion cavalry, and [Parmenion](@granicus-vanguard-parmenion) the left with the Thessalian, Thracian and allied Greek horse. The phalanx and the hypaspists hold the centre. On the steep east bank the Persian horsemen stand in one long line, with the Greek mercenaries on the higher ground behind them. Seeing Alexander opposite their left, the Persians mass their best squadrons there, with [Memnon of Rhodes](@granicus-vanguard-memnon) and his sons. [Arsites](@granicus-vanguard-arsites) and Spithridates stand next to them. For a long time, Arrian writes, both armies wait in silence.

Alexander sends a vanguard in first: [Amyntas, son of Arrhabaeus](@granicus-vanguard-amyntas), with the lancers, the Paeonians and one infantry unit, led by the squadron of Socrates, son of Sathon. The Persians throw javelins down from the bank, and some ride down to the water's edge. The vanguard is outnumbered and cut down, and the survivors fall back on Alexander. Arrian counts about 25 Companions killed in this first onset.\*\* Bosworth judges that it took the force of the Persian charge on itself.

Meanwhile Alexander leads the right wing into the river to the sound of trumpets, keeping his line slanted downstream so that the Persians cannot fall on its flank as it comes out of the water.

The sources do not say where the vanguard crossed, how far Alexander's line slanted, or where the mercenaries stood along the river. The order of the Persian squadrons beyond their left is taken from Diodorus.

### Notes

\* This is Arrian's account, which Plutarch shares and these pages follow. Diodorus has Alexander cross unopposed at dawn and fight the Persian horse on the far side, with no fight in the river. Bosworth judged Diodorus's version the more plausible in 1980. In 1988 he held that the weight of evidence favours Arrian and Plutarch on the hour of the attack, but called Arrian's cavalry drawn up at the water's edge "a military absurdity" and thought the facts of the fight "probably lost for ever".

\*\* Aristobulus, as Plutarch cites him, gives only 34 Macedonian dead for the whole battle. The last page of the battle gives both counts.
