At the first sign of spring in 327 BC, Arrian says,\* Alexander leaves winter quarters at [Nautaca](@nautaca-327) for a rock in Sogdiana. Many Sogdians have fled there, among them the wife and daughters of Oxyartes, a Bactrian noble in revolt. If the rock falls, Arrian writes, the rebels will have nothing left. Offered terms, the defenders jeer that only soldiers with wings could take it.

Three hundred practised climbers go up the cliff by night, driving iron tent pegs into the frozen snow and hauling up on ropes. About thirty fall to their deaths.\*\* At dawn the rest wave linen flags from the heights, and the defenders surrender to the "winged men" at the Sogdian Rock, whose site is unknown. Alexander marries Oxyartes's daughter Roxana.\*\*\* Her good treatment wins her father over, and to Bosworth the marriage marks the king's claim to the conquered land.

In late spring, with the army gathered at [Bactra](@bactra-327) (today Balkh), Alexander tries to bring in proskynesis, the bow or prostration due to a Persian king. Bosworth reads it as one ceremony meant for all his subjects alike. Callisthenes, the Greek historian with the army, refuses it, and the ceremony is dropped.

@image persepolis-treasury-relief-c4c817

Hermolaus, a royal page whom Alexander had flogged, plots with other pages to kill the king in his sleep. Bosworth thinks the quarrel over proskynesis may have helped drive them to it. The plot is betrayed and the pages are stoned to death. Callisthenes, close to Hermolaus, is arrested; how he died is disputed.\*\*\*\*

After nearly two years of fighting by Bosworth's reckoning the risings are over, and Sogdiana is wholly Alexander's. Alexander leaves Amyntas, son of Nicolaus, as satrap of Bactria and Sogdiana with 10,000 foot and 3,500 horse, and at the end of spring sets out for India.

### Notes

\* Bosworth thinks the evidence favours summer 328 BC, the date of the tradition Curtius follows, which puts the siege before the death of Cleitus the Black.

\*\* Arrian says 30. Curtius says 32, names the rock's lord as Ariamazes and gives him 30,000 armed men.

\*\*\* Arrian has Alexander fall in love with Roxana when she is taken captive on the rock. Plutarch says that he first saw her dancing at a banquet.

\*\*\*\* Alexander's officer Ptolemy says that Callisthenes was racked and hanged. Aristobulus says that he was carried along with the army in fetters and died a natural death, and Chares of Mytilene, the court chamberlain, that he died in fetters seven months later.
