In spring 1481 Mehmed crosses to Üsküdar and gathers an army for a new campaign. He keeps its goal secret, and Rhodes, southern Italy and the Mamluk sultanate of Egypt have all been named as possible targets. He falls ill on the march and dies in his camp near [Gebze](@gebze-1481) on 3 May 1481, aged 49. Gout is the usual explanation, and Babinger argues that his physician Yakub Pasha poisoned him, while İnalcık finds no support for this in the other sources.

Karamani Mehmed Pasha, the grand vizier, keeps the death secret, has the body brought to [Constantinople](@istanbul-1481) and sends messengers to the two princes: Bayezid, governor at [Amasya](@amasya-1473), and Cem, governor of Karaman at [Konya](@konya-1474), who is nearer. The janissaries, who favour Bayezid, learn of it, march into the city and kill the grand vizier, and they plunder houses and shops. The former grand vizier İshak Pasha names Bayezid’s son Korkut regent and calls Bayezid to come at once, and the messenger to Cem is stopped on the road.

Bayezid learns of his father’s death on 7 May, reaches Üsküdar on 21 May and on 22 May takes the throne in Constantinople as Bayezid II, on the day Mehmed is buried beside his mosque. At Mehmed’s death the Ottoman lands reach from Bosnia, Albania and the Morea to the Euphrates and Trebizond, and from the Danube to the Mediterranean coast of Anatolia. The khan of the Crimea is the sultan’s vassal, and an Ottoman garrison holds [Otranto](@otranto-1481) in Italy.
