Seven months after Joan's death at Rouen, Henry VI, the ten-year-old king of England, is crowned King of France. On Sunday 16 December 1431 his great-uncle, Cardinal Beaufort, crowns him in [Notre-Dame](@notre-dame-1431) in Paris.\* Paris holds for him, and the Burgundians are still the allies of the English.

@image henry-vi-paris-coronation-miniature

That alliance breaks at [Arras](@arras-1435), a town of Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy. A congress meets there in the summer of 1435 to make peace. No general peace can be agreed, and the English envoys leave on 6 September. The envoys of Charles VII, the king crowned at Reims in 1429, and the Duke then settle a peace of their own, which the Duke seals in his letters of 21 September in the abbey of Saint-Vaast. By Ramsay's account an apology for the murder of the Duke's father, John the Fearless, at Montereau in 1419 is offered and accepted. The Duke swears "good peace" with Charles VII. Cardinals sent to Arras release him from his oaths to England.

@image arras-peace-1435-miniature

While the congress sits, the regent of France for Henry VI, John, Duke of Bedford, dies at Rouen in the night of 14 to 15 September. The news reaches Arras on the 16th.

Now the English stand alone against France and Burgundy, and Ramsay dates the last stage of the Hundred Years' War from the Treaty of Arras. Saint-Denis, which the French took on 1 June 1435, is besieged by the English and Burgundians, and on 4 October its garrison marches out with the honours of war, by Ramsay's dates.

The map changes in allegiance, not in land: the Duke's lands now belong to an ally of Charles VII, and the English still hold Paris, the country round it and Normandy. The borders on this chapter's maps are approximate, since the sources give these holdings town by town, never as a line.

### Notes

\* The Bourgeois of Paris and Ramsay give Sunday 16 December. Monstrelet has Henry VI go to Notre-Dame for his crowning on the 17th.
