Before daylight Alexander falls on the three Persian [guard posts](@persian-gate-dawn-posts) behind the camp. He destroys the first, most of the second, and drives the third into the mountains. At dawn [Alexander](@persian-gate-dawn-alexander) attacks the Persian camp from the north-east without being seen, and as his men reach the trench the trumpets give the signal to [Craterus](@persian-gate-dawn-craterus), who storms the wall from below. From a third side the column of [Philotas](@persian-gate-dawn-philotas), Parmenion's son, strikes the camp.\*

Attacked from all sides, the Persians break. Arrian says that they fled without coming to blows; hemmed in, most turned back to the wall, already in Macedonian hands, where [Ptolemy](@persian-gate-dawn-ptolemy), left there by Alexander, cut them down, and others fell to their deaths [over the cliffs](@persian-gate-dawn-cliffs).\*\* Bosworth calls it a complete and decisive victory, which opens the road to Persepolis. No source gives the losses of either side.

[Ariobarzanes](@persian-gate-dawn-ariobarzanes) breaks out of the camp.\*\*\*

The sources do not give the places of the guard posts, where Philotas's column struck the camp, where Ptolemy stood, or which way the Persians fled.

### Notes

\* Curtius tells of Philotas's attack, with Coenus, Amyntas, son of Andromenes, and Polyperchon, and Bosworth makes it the third prong of the assault. Arrian has no such attack: in his account this column was sent to bridge the river on the road into Persis.

\*\* Arrian gives Ptolemy 3,000 foot. Bosworth thinks Ptolemy's part probably exaggerated, and his force much smaller. Curtius has the Persians fight a memorable battle instead of fleeing.

\*\*\* Arrian says that Ariobarzanes escaped into the mountains with a few horsemen. Curtius has him break through the Macedonian line with about 40 horsemen and 5,000 foot, find Persepolis shut against him and die fighting, and Bosworth accepts this account.
