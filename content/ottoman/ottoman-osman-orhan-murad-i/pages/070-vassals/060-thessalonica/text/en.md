In 1381 John V names Andronikos IV his heir again, passing over his second son Manuel. In November 1382 Manuel sets himself up as emperor in Thessalonica and acts against the Ottomans, extending his rule into Thessaly and Epirus, at least in name. Murad sends his grand vizier, Çandarlı Kara Halil Hayreddin Pasha. The Ottomans take [Serres](@serres-1383) on 19 September 1383 and enslave its people, and in October they reach the walls of Thessalonica.

@image manuel-ii-family-57cf74

Hayreddin demands the city’s surrender. Manuel calls the people to the main square and urges them to resist, but many would rather surrender than see the city plundered. The Ottomans have no fleet, and they change from siege to blockade. No help reaches the city by sea. Hayreddin dies at Serres on 22 January 1387. Afraid for their lives and their trade, the townspeople force Manuel to leave on 6 April 1387, and three days later they open the gates.

@image thessaloniki-walls-1712f8

The city is spared. It keeps its privileges in return for a tax and takes a small Ottoman garrison. Evrenos Bey makes Serres his base for raids deeper into Macedonia. Manuel makes his peace with the sultan, and John V sends him to live on Lemnos.
