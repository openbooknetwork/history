The Mam of the western highlands have their main town at Xinabahul, now [Huehuetenango](@huehuetenango-1525). Lovell writes that the K'iche' resent the execution of their kings for a plan that the Mam king Kayb'il B'alam had suggested, and propose a march on the Mam, which the Spanish quickly take up.

In July 1525 Pedro de Alvarado’s brother Gonzalo de Alvarado y Contreras leaves the Spanish camp at [Tecpán](@tecpan-1525) with 40 Spanish cavalry, 80 Spanish infantry and some 2,000 Mexican and K'iche' allies. He makes [Totonicapán](@totonicapan-1525) his supply base and turns north to [Momostenango](@momostenango-1525), slowed by heavy rain, and takes the town after a four-hour battle. The next day, on the road to Huehuetenango, a Mam army from [Malacatán](@malacatan-1525) bars his way.

After that battle the army rests for a few days and then finds Huehuetenango deserted. Kayb'il B'alam has withdrawn to the fortress of Zaculeu, and he leaves Gonzalo’s offer of terms unanswered. Zaculeu stands on a plateau above the Selegua River, with deep ravines on the south and east. The only way in is a narrow neck of land on the north, guarded by a fortified structure across three quarters of its width, and walls and ditches defend the approach.
