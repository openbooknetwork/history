Sigismund, King of Hungary and King of the Romans, claims Bohemia as the heir of his brother Wenceslas IV. The Moravian lords accept him at Brno at Christmas 1419, but the Hussites will take him only if he allows communion in both kinds, and he refuses. On 1 March 1420 Pope Martin V issues the bull Omnium plasmatoris domini, which calls a crusade against the Wycliffites and Hussites, and on 17 March the papal legate proclaims it at [Breslau](@breslau-1420), where the princes of the Empire are meeting. Two days earlier Sigismund has had the Hussite Jan Krása executed in Breslau.

At the end of April the crusade army leaves Silesia by Kladsko. [Hradec Králové](@hradec-kralove-1420) surrenders without a fight on 3 May, and Sigismund makes [Kutná Hora](@kutna-hora-1420), a mining town of mostly German Catholics, his base. On 7 May Čeněk of Vartenberk hands [Prague Castle](@prague-castle-1420) to the king’s men. In the middle of May Sigismund rejects the terms of the Praguers’ envoys, and Prague calls the Hussites of the country to its aid.

Žižka and the Taborites march north, beat Petr of Šternberk’s horse and foot before [Benešov](@benesov-1420) and reach Prague on 20 May, and the men of Žatec, Louny and Slaný arrive three days later. While they are away, Oldřich of Rožmberk besieges [Tábor](@tabor-1420), until about 350 horsemen under Mikuláš of Hus ride back from Prague and scatter his army at the end of June. The crusaders reach Prague on 30 June. By 10 July their army, about 30,000 men by modern estimates and 80,000 by the chroniclers, stands in camps on the left bank of the Vltava, from Prague Castle to the village of Bubny.
