Since her capture at [Compiègne](@compiegne-summons-1430) in May 1430, Joan has been the prisoner of John of Luxembourg, the Burgundian captain besieging the town, whose follower the Bastard of Wandonne took her. She is kept three or four days at [Clairoix](@clairoix-beaulieu-1430) and then sent to John of Luxembourg's castle of [Beaulieu](@beaulieu-1430), near [Noyon](@noyon-1430).

There she slips out "between two pieces of wood" and would have locked her keepers in the tower, but the porter catches sight of her.\* She is moved to his castle of [Beaurevoir](@beaurevoir-1430), near [Saint-Quentin](@saint-quentin-1430), and kept in one of its towers for about four months.

His aunt the Demoiselle of Luxembourg and his wife the Lady of Beaurevoir urge her to dress as a woman. She answers that she does not have God's leave, and that it is not yet time. The Demoiselle begs her nephew not to hand her to the English.

Pierre Cauchon, Bishop of Beauvais, comes to the siege of Compiègne on 14 July. In Henry VI's name and his own he summons the Duke of Burgundy, John of Luxembourg and the Bastard of Wandonne to give her up to him for trial, and offers her captors up to 6,000 francs, or else 10,000, the sum the king could pay for a captive king or prince.

Then she hears that she is sold, that the English are coming for her, and that the people of Compiègne, all of them down to the age of seven, will be put to fire and sword when the town falls. Against her voices she throws herself from the tower, trusting to God and Our Lady. She is picked up senseless and cannot eat or drink for two or three days.

### Notes

\* As Joan told her judges at Rouen in March 1431.
