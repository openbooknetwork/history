From Campeche the fleet sails on for six days in good weather and four in a storm. The casks leak, and the water runs out again. The ships stop at Champotón, where the Champotón River meets the sea. Its Maya name is Chakán Putum, and its lord is Moch Couoh, of the Couoh lineage. Shallow water keeps the ships well out, so the Spaniards go ashore in the boats.

A landing party finds fresh water at wells near the shore and fills the casks. Armed warriors come out of the town, and both sides try to talk by signs until night falls. In the dark the Spaniards hear large numbers of warriors gathering around them. They judge a retreat to the boats at night too risky, post guards and wait for the dawn.

The expedition has about 110 men, not all of them soldiers, armed with swords, crossbows and muskets. The warriors of Champotón fight with bows and arrows, darts, stones, spears and clubs, and wear padded cotton armour.
