Soult’s IV Corps and Murat’s cavalry reach the plateau before Eylau at about 2 p.m. on 7 February. Bagration’s rearguard holds a position about a mile in front of the town and throws back the first French attack, gaining time for Bennigsen’s heavy artillery to pass through Eylau. As Augereau’s corps and the Imperial Guard come up, giving Napoleon about 45,000 men, Bagration falls back in order to the main army.

A second rearguard under Barclay de Tolly holds the town itself. The French attack it in the evening, by Napoleon’s later account on his orders, though other evidence suggests a skirmish that got out of hand. The fight in the streets and around the churchyard goes on long after dark and costs each side about 4,000 men. Barclay is shot in the arm, and at 10 p.m. Bennigsen gives up the town and draws his line back a short distance. Most of both armies spend the freezing night in the open without food.
