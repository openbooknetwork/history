While Murad is in the Balkans, his son-in-law Alaeddin Ali Bey of Karaman attacks the towns Murad had bought from Hamid and takes Beyşehir. Murad winters at Bursa and then marches on Konya with Bayezid and with Serbian troops sent by his vassals. The Ottoman chronicles date the campaign to 1386, while other accounts give 1387.

The armies meet at Frenkyazısı on the plain of Konya, and the Karamanid army is beaten. Bayezid is said to earn the name Yıldırım, the Thunderbolt, in this battle. Alaeddin shuts himself up in Konya and offers peace, and Çandarlı Ali Pasha advises the sultan to press on until Alaeddin submits. During the siege Serbian soldiers who plunder against the sultan’s orders are executed, and some of the Serbian vassal lords turn toward Lazar.

Alaeddin’s wife Nefise asks her father Murad to spare him. By the Ottoman chronicles Murad pardons Alaeddin on condition that he comes to kiss the sultan’s hand. Beyşehir returns to the Ottomans, and the peace between the two beyliks holds until Bayezid’s reign.
