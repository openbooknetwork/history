# Gaugamela and Persia

## Header
- Years: spring 331 BC – July or August 330 BC
- Opens: Alexander holds Macedon and Thrace, Asia Minor, Cyprus, the Levant to Gaza, and Egypt to Paraetonium and
  Siwa (`macedon-331`). Darius III holds the empire east of the Euphrates (`persia-336` as cut back) and is
  mustering a new army at Babylon. Antipater governs Macedon and Greece; Sparta stands outside the League of
  Corinth (`sparta-337`). The reader has been told the Granicus, Issus and the capture of Darius's family, Darius's
  first letter after Issus and its refusal (040.11), the sieges of Tyre and Gaza, the submission of Egypt, the
  founding of Alexandria and the journey to Siwa.
- Tells:
  - spring 331 BC: Alexander leaves Memphis for Tyre
  - summer 331 BC: Darius's last and greatest offer, the land west of the Euphrates, a ransom and his daughter;
    Alexander refuses (040.11)
  - summer 331 BC: Darius marches north from Babylon over the Tigris to Arbela and Gaugamela and levels the plain
  - July–August 331 BC: Alexander crosses the Euphrates at Thapsacus and marches east through northern Mesopotamia
  - September 331 BC: he crosses the Tigris unopposed; the lunar eclipse of 20 September 331 BC
  - 1 October 331 BC: the battle of Gaugamela; Darius flees by Arbela into Media
  - October 331 BC: Babylon opens its gates (late October); Mazaeus is made its satrap
  - summer 331 – winter 331/330 BC: Agis III raises the Peloponnese and besieges Megalopolis (told on its page)
  - December 331 BC: Susa and its treasury, already surrendered to an officer sent ahead, are taken over
  - December 331 – January 330 BC: the Uxian defile is forced
  - January 330 BC: the battle of the Persian Gate
  - late January 330 BC: Persepolis taken and plundered, its treasury seized; Pasargadae
  - early 330 BC, probably early spring: Antipater defeats Agis III at Megalopolis; Agis falls
  - spring (May) 330 BC: the palace of Persepolis burns
  - May–July or August 330 BC: Alexander pursues Darius from Persis through Media to Rhagae and the Caspian Gates; Darius
    leaves Ecbatana; the Greek allies are paid off and sent home
  - July 330 BC (Arrian; August in Bosworth): Bessus, Nabarzanes and Barsaentes arrest Darius and stab him as Alexander overtakes them; Darius
    dies short of Hecatompylus
- Introduces:
  - People: Mazaeus, Bessus, Abulites, Ariobarzanes, Agis III, Thaïs, Xerxes (as the Great King who
    burned Athens), Nabarzanes, Barsaentes; among the minor commanders on the
    Gaugamela card and pages only Simmias, Aretes, Polyperchon, Attalus (the Agrianians) and Philip, son of Menelaus
    (the Thessalians), as the battle plan rules; Philotas on the Persian Gate pages; the scholars Stein, Speck, Lendering, Hemati and San José Campos where a text says
    who holds a view
  - States and peoples: the Medes, Bactrians, Parthians, Arachosians, Dahae, Saka and Scythians, Indians,
    Armenians, Cappadocians, Syrians and Mesopotamians of Darius's line; the Babylonians; the Uxians
  - Places: the Tigris, Mesopotamia, Gaugamela, the Bumelus, Arbela, the Lycus, Susiana, the Uxian
    defile, the Persian Gate, Persis, the Araxes, Persepolis, Pasargadae, Media, Ecbatana, Rhagae, the Caspian
    Gates, Hecatompylus, Megalopolis, Crete, Bactria
- Battles: Gaugamela, 3 phase pages; the Persian Gate, 3 phase pages; Megalopolis, a card and no phase pages
- Hands on: Alexander holds Mesopotamia, Babylonia, Susiana, Persis and Media and the road east to where Darius
  died (`macedon-330`); Parmenion sits at Ecbatana with the treasure of Persis. Darius is dead and his body sent to
  Persis for royal burial. Bessus and his fellow conspirators have fled east, Bessus to Bactria, and the eastern
  satrapies are still unconquered (`persia-336`, its last page). Greece is quiet after Megalopolis; the Spartans'
  fate has been referred to Alexander. The Greek allies have gone home. Left unsettled: Bessus in Bactria, and
  how far east Alexander means to go.

## Pages
1. `gaugamela`, spring – 1 October 331 BC. Alexander leaves Egypt, refuses Darius's last offer, crosses the
   Euphrates at Thapsacus and the Tigris unopposed, and finds Darius waiting on levelled ground at Gaugamela. Why:
   Darius, beaten at Issus in narrow ground, chose open ground for his cavalry and chariots and offered terms to
   avoid the battle; Alexander would accept nothing short of the whole empire. Map: Alexander's march from Memphis
   by Tyre and Thapsacus across northern Mesopotamia to the Tigris and Gaugamela, and Darius's march from Babylon to
   Gaugamela, meet on one field; the battle card.
   Rests on: 040.11 (disputed: the offer is told in summer 331 with Bosworth, Diodorus and Plutarch, with one
   clause that Arrian sets it at Tyre in 332; the ransom is given as 30,000 talents in Diodorus and Bosworth,
   10,000 in Arrian and Plutarch); 050.1; 050.2; 050.3; 050.28 (single: "the empire's last great army" is told as
   Badian's judgement, with his name); 050.9 (disputed: on the card each figure with its author,
   and in the text that Bosworth holds the numbers beyond reckoning); 050.10 (single: Arrian named).
2. `gaugamela-deployment`, phase, 30 September – 1 October 331 BC. Alexander halts 30 stades off on 30
   September; at dawn on 1 October the two lines deploy and Alexander moves to the right, off the levelled ground. Rests on: 050.4; 050.5; 050.6.
3. `gaugamela-breakthrough`, phase, 1 October 331 BC. The cavalry fight on the right flank, the scythed chariots
   speared or let through, the gap in the Persian line, the wedge at Darius, and Darius's flight. Rests on: 050.6;
   050.8 (disputed: the Greek and Roman writers have Darius flee first, Arrian says he was the first to turn; the
   Babylonian diary, as Lendering reads it, says his troops deserted him; Bosworth holds he was neither first nor
   last; both accounts told with their authors).
4. `gaugamela-left-wing`, phase, 1 October 331 BC, afternoon and evening. Persian and Indian horse break through to
   the baggage; Mazaeus presses Parmenion; the returning Companions meet the fleeing Persian horse; the Thessalians
   drive off the Persian right; the pursuit begins (its route to the Lycus is drawn on `babylon-and-susa`). The
   text says that Diodorus and Bosworth put the raid on the camp early in the battle, where Arrian tells it after
   the break-in. Rests on: 050.6; 050.7 (disputed: Arrian and Plutarch
   have Alexander turn back on Parmenion's call; Diodorus and Curtius have the messengers fail to reach him, which
   Bosworth thinks true; both told with their authors); 050.11 (disputed: on the card).
5. `babylon-and-susa`, October – December 331 BC. Darius flees by Arbela over the Zagros into Media; Alexander
   takes Arbela with Darius's baggage, marches to Babylon, which opens its gates in late October, makes Mazaeus its
   satrap, and in December takes over Susa and its treasury. Why: after Gaugamela no Persian army stood west of the
   Zagros, and Darius left the lowland capitals to buy time (Arrian 3.16, Diodorus 17.64). Map: `macedon-331b`
   takes in Mesopotamia, Babylonia and Susiana; Darius's flight to Ecbatana, Mazaeus's withdrawal to Babylon and
   Alexander's march to Babylon and Susa.
   Rests on: 050.8 (disputed, for the flight: drawn after Arrian and Bosworth; the text may add that the diary has
   his troops flee "to the land of the Guti"); 050.12; 050.13 (disputed: 50,000 talents in Arrian, more than 40,000
   of bullion in Diodorus, each named).
6. `persian-gate`, December 331 – January 330 BC. From Susa Alexander forces the Uxian defile, then splits the army:
   Parmenion takes the baggage and heavy troops by the carriage road, Alexander takes the Macedonians through the
   mountains and finds Ariobarzanes walled into the Persian Gate; the battle card. Why: Persis was the heartland of
   the empire, and Alexander chose to cross the Zagros in winter rather than meet a stronger defence in spring
   (Bosworth). Map: Alexander's route from Susa over the Pasitigris to the Uxian defile and the Gate, Parmenion's
   route by the carriage road towards the plain of Persepolis.
   Rests on: 050.16 (single: Arrian named for the Uxian tribute); 050.17; 050.18 (disputed: on the card); 050.19
   (disputed: the text says the site is disputed, names Bosworth's site, where the map draws it:
   the gorge above Mullah Susan (the Tang-i Mohammad Reza, Bosworth, Conquest and Empire ch. 2.X and its note; Stein's "Tang-i Khas", a name Bosworth corrects, since the real Tang-i Khas runs parallel some 10 km further west), and Speck's and Lendering's Tang-e Meyran north-east of Yasuj; it says once that the drawing is a
   reconstruction).
7. `persian-gate-assault`, phase, January 330 BC. The advance into the defile, the wall, the missiles and stones
   from the heights, the retreat to camp. Rests on: 050.17; 050.19.
8. `persian-gate-night-march`, phase, January 330 BC, night. Craterus, the phalanx commander whom 040 introduced,
   left at the camp; Alexander led by prisoners
   round the pass by a mountain path. Rests on: 050.17 (the guide is "prisoners" in Arrian, a Lycian herdsman in
   the vulgate: both named); 050.19.
9. `persian-gate-dawn`, phase, January 330 BC, dawn. The three outposts destroyed, the camp attacked from behind at
   dawn, Craterus storming the wall at the trumpet signal, Philotas's column striking as a third prong, Ptolemy
   cutting off the retreat; Ariobarzanes escapes. Rests on: 050.17; 050.19; 050.29 (the researcher's new line
   on Philotas's column; disputed: Curtius 5.4.30 and Bosworth, Conquest and Empire ch. 2.X, "three prongs of
   attack", have Philotas's men join the assault on the camp; Arrian 3.18.6 sends Amyntas, Philotas and Coenus to
   bridge the river on the way to Persis; the page draws Curtius's and Bosworth's attack and the text gives Arrian's
   account beside it, each with its author); 050.20 (disputed: Arrian has Ariobarzanes escape into the mountains; Curtius has him
   killed outside Persepolis, which Bosworth accepts; both told).
10. `persepolis`, late January – May 330 BC. Alexander crosses the river into the plain, takes Persepolis before its
    treasury can be plundered, gives the city to his soldiers but spares the palaces, seizes 120,000 talents and the
    treasury of Pasargadae, sends much of the bullion to Susa, and after four months the palace burns. Why: Persepolis
    was the city of Darius I and Xerxes, and Alexander presented its sack as the revenge of the Greeks (Arrian,
    Diodorus 17.70, Bosworth). Map: `macedon-330a` takes in the Uxian hills and Persis; Alexander's road
    from the Gate to Persepolis and Pasargadae; the treasure convoy to Susa.
    Rests on: 050.20 (disputed: Ariobarzanes's end, one clause, if not told on the phase page); 050.21 (single for
    the figure: "Diodorus gives 120,000 talents, Curtius the same, from the same tradition"); 050.25 (single:
    the convoy to Susa told after Diodorus, named); 050.22 (disputed:
    three accounts, Arrian's deliberate revenge against Parmenion's advice, Diodorus's and Plutarch's drunken
    feast and Thaïs's torch, and San José Campos's reading of the excavations as a planned fire, each with its
    author).
11. `megalopolis`, summer 331 – spring 330 BC. The page opens by going back to summer 331 BC, in Greece, while
    Alexander marched on Gaugamela. Agis III, with the mercenaries who escaped Issus and Persian money,
    brings his army from Crete, raises most of the Peloponnese and besieges Megalopolis; Antipater comes south with
    40,000 and Agis falls in the battle near the city; the League refers the Spartans to Alexander. Why: Antipater
    was drawn north by a revolt in Thrace, and Sparta, outside the League, took its chance for Persia (Diodorus
    17.62, Bosworth). Map: `sparta-331`, Sparta and the Peloponnesian states that joined Agis; Agis's crossing
    from Crete and Antipater's march from Macedon to Megalopolis; the battle card.
    Rests on: 050.14; 050.26; 050.27 (single for the list of allies: Bosworth named or the list told as his;
    Athens's holding aloof told plainly); 050.15 (disputed: the date is told as disputed, "probably early in 330 BC" with Bosworth and
    Badian, with one clause that others put it in autumn 331 BC (Welles after Curtius; San José Campos); Antipater's losses 3,500 in Diodorus, not more than
    1,000 in Curtius, both named; 5,300 Spartan and allied dead in both).
12. `death-of-darius`, May – July or August 330 BC. Alexander marches north from Persepolis to catch Darius in Media; Darius
    leaves Ecbatana for the Caspian Gates and the east; Alexander sends Parmenion to Ecbatana with the treasure,
    pays off the Greek allies, rides through Rhagae and the Caspian Gates and across the desert, and overtakes the
    Persian column as Bessus's party stabs the king. Darius dies before Alexander sees him; Alexander sends the body
    to Persis. Why: Darius meant to draw back into the eastern satrapies and raise a new army there; his satraps,
    led by Bessus, would not let him fall into Alexander's hands (Arrian 3.19–21, Bosworth). Map: `macedon-330`
    takes in Media and the road east to where Darius died; Alexander's pursuit from Persepolis to Hecatompylus,
    Darius's flight from Ecbatana, Parmenion's road to Ecbatana.
    Rests on: 050.23 (disputed for date and place: Arrian puts the discharge at Ecbatana before the chase;
    Plutarch has the Thessalians sent home when Darius's arrest was known; Diodorus and Curtius after Darius's
    death, at Hecatompylus in Curtius, which Bosworth follows; each told with its author; the bonus as 2,000
    talents in Arrian, Plutarch and Bosworth); 050.24 (the date of Darius's death is disputed: July 330 BC in
    Arrian, August in Bosworth; both told with their authors).

## Battles

### Gaugamela (`gaugamela`)
- Date and place: 1 October 331 BC (the Babylonian astronomical diary; Arrian's month Pyanepsion), the plain by the
  Bumelus (Gomel) below the Jabal Maqlub, about 600 stades from Arbela (Arrian 3.8; Bosworth).
- Sides: `macedon`, Alexander, with Parmenion commanding the left; `persia`, Darius III, with Bessus leading the
  Bactrians on the left and Mazaeus commanding the right. Every unit of Alexander's side, the Thessalians
  included, is drawn as `macedon` (thessaly has the same colour). Minor commanders named on the card and pages:
  only Simmias, Aretes, Polyperchon, Attalus (the Agrianians) and Philip, son of Menelaus (the Thessalians).
- Strength, Macedonian: 7,000 cavalry and about 40,000 infantry (Arrian).
- Strength, Persian: Arrian, 40,000 cavalry (Chinnock 3.8.6 and Bosworth's Commentary; the "400,000" of Bosworth's
  Conquest and Empire is taken as a misprint), 1,000,000 infantry, 200 scythed chariots and about 15 elephants;
  Diodorus, 800,000 infantry and at least 200,000 cavalry; Curtius, 45,000 cavalry and 200,000 infantry. Bosworth:
  the Persian advantage "cannot be quantified".
- Casualties, Macedonian: Arrian, about 100 killed and more than 1,000 horses lost; Diodorus, about 500 killed;
  Curtius, fewer than 300.
- Casualties, Persian: Arrian, about 300,000 killed; Diodorus, more than 90,000; Curtius, 40,000. Bosworth: losses
  impossible to estimate.
- Outcome: Alexander's victory; Darius flees into Media.
- Phases: 3. Positions and movements of both sides: Arrian 3.11–15 with Darius's captured order of battle
  (archive.org-download-theanabasisofale46976gut-46976-0.txt.txt), Bosworth, Conquest and Empire ch. 2.IX
  (archive.org-onquest-20and-20Empire-20The-20Reign-20of-20Alexander-20the-20Great-20-281993-29-djvu.txt.txt),
  Diodorus 17.57–61 (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-17C.txt), Curtius 4.12–16
  (archive.org-ufus-2C-20History-20of-20Alexander-2C-20Loeb-20TWO-IN-ONE-20VOLUME-20-28Rolfe-29-djvu.txt.txt).
  Proposed split, which the battle-coordinator may refine: the deployment and the march to the right; the flank
  fight, the chariots, the wedge and Darius's flight; the break-in to the baggage, the pressure on Parmenion, the
  last cavalry fight and the start of the pursuit. The battle-coordinator's plan is
  `content/alexander-the-great/.plan/battles/gaugamela.md`.

### The Persian Gate (`persian-gate`)
- Date and place: January 330 BC (Lendering: about 20 January), a walled defile in the Zagros on the direct road
  from Susiana into Persis. Site disputed (050.19); drawn as a reconstruction at Bosworth's site: the
  gorge above the open ground of Mullah Susan, Alexander's camp (the Tang-i Mohammad Reza in Bosworth's Conquest and
  Empire; Stein and Bosworth's Commentary call it the Tang-i Khas, a name Bosworth later corrects), with the Persian camp at its head below the
  watershed towards the plain of 'Aliabad, and the turning path over the Bolsoru pass (Bosworth, Commentary on
  3.18.2–5, and Conquest and Empire ch. 2.X).
- Sides: `macedon`, Alexander, with Craterus at the camp, Ptolemy (Arrian) and Philotas (Curtius, Bosworth);
  `persia`, Ariobarzanes, satrap of Persis.
- Strength, Macedonian: `unknown` (Arrian names the units, not a total). Engels's estimate of about 17,000 stays off
  the card, as the battle plan rules.
- Strength, Persian: Arrian, 40,000 infantry and 700 cavalry; Diodorus, 25,000 infantry and 300 cavalry; Curtius,
  25,000 infantry.
- Casualties: `unknown` on both sides (Arrian: most of the Persians cut down; no source read gives a figure).
- Outcome: Alexander's victory; the road to Persepolis opens.
- Phases: 3, as the brief gives them: the assault in the defile (the column drawn halted in the narrows), the night
  march over the mountain path, the dawn attack from behind with Philotas's column as the third prong (050.29).
  The battle-coordinator's plan is `content/alexander-the-great/.plan/battles/persian-gate.md`. Positions and movements of both sides: Arrian 3.18
  (archive.org-download-theanabasisofale46976gut-46976-0.txt.txt), Curtius 5.3–4 (the Loeb file above), Diodorus
  17.68 (penelope.uchicago.edu-Thayer-E-Roman-Texts-Diodorus-Siculus-17D.txt), Bosworth's Commentary I on 3.18
  (archive.org-History-20of-20Alexander-3B-20Volume-20I-20Books-20I-III-20-20A.-20B.-20Bosworth-djvu.txt.txt) and
  Conquest and Empire ch. 2.X, and Speck's ground in Lendering's web version
  (livius.org-articles-battle-persian-gate-330-bce.txt), which a text may set beside Bosworth's.

### Megalopolis (`megalopolis`)
- Date and place: early 330 BC, probably early spring (Bosworth; Badian: early 330); others put it in autumn 331 BC
  (050.15). Hilly ground near Megalopolis, Agis probably holding the road south into Laconia (Bosworth).
- Sides: `sparta`, Agis III, with Sparta, Elis, the Achaeans but Pellene, and the Arcadians but Megalopolis;
  `macedon`, Antipater, with the Macedonians and the loyal Greeks.
- Strength, Spartan side: not less than 20,000 infantry and about 2,000 cavalry (Diodorus; Bosworth repeats it).
- Strength, Antipater: not less than 40,000 (Diodorus; Bosworth repeats it).
- Casualties: Spartans and allies, more than 5,300 killed (Diodorus; Curtius 5,300). Antipater, 3,500 killed
  (Diodorus) or not more than 1,000 (Curtius).
- Outcome: Antipater's victory; Agis III killed; the rising ends.
- No phase pages: no source read gives the course of the battle beyond Curtius's rhetoric (digest coverage line).

## Choices
- The greatest offer opens the section, as decisions.md rules, told in summer 331 BC on the Gaugamela page rather
  than on a page of its own: it changes nothing on the map and belongs to the march that ends at Gaugamela.
- Alexander's march from Memphis to Tyre in spring 331 BC is drawn here, as the first leg of the road to
  Gaugamela; section 040 ends in Egypt.
- Darius's offer, the march through Mesopotamia and the approach to the field share the Gaugamela campaign page, as
  the Punic Wars "road to Cannae" page does.
- The Uxian defile and the Persian Gate share one page: the same mountains within weeks, and the brief's one line.
- The fall of Persepolis and the burning of the palace share one page: the same place within four months, and the
  burning moves no border. This gives six section pages where the brief expected seven.
- Megalopolis comes after the Persepolis page. Its battle falls in early spring 330, while Alexander was at
  Persepolis, and Badian has Alexander waiting there for news of Agis's war; the burning is told before it on the
  Persepolis page, and the Megalopolis text places its battle "while Alexander wintered at Persepolis".
- The Persian Gate is drawn at Bosworth's site (the gorge above Mullah Susan, which Bosworth's Conquest and Empire
  calls the Tang-i Mohammad Reza and Stein named the Tang-i Khas; the Persian camp below the watershed, the turning path over the Bolsoru pass), because Bosworth gives the ground in two works read in full,
  and the phase pages need one site; the texts name Speck's and Lendering's Tang-e Meyran and Hemati's site.
- Thapsacus is placed at Dibse after Bosworth, not at Carchemish after Engels.
- The Tigris crossing is placed slightly north of Mosul after Bosworth; Engels's Abu Wijam ford is in the same
  stretch.
- Darius's flight after Gaugamela is drawn by Arbela and Ruwandiz after Arrian and Bosworth, not by the Diyala,
  Lendering's reading of the diary's "land of the Guti".
- Alexander's pursuit is drawn turning north-east short of Ecbatana, after Bosworth; Arrian says he entered
  Ecbatana. The page shows the allies sent home from Ecbatana with a marker, because a route to the Syrian coast
  would run off the map.
- The Uxian page tells Arrian's action against the mountain Uxians (050.16). The vulgate's action against Madates,
  which Bosworth holds a separate encounter, is not in the digest and is left to a clause at most, from Bosworth's
  Commentary.
- Left without a page and not in the digest: Amyntas's reinforcements at Susa, the death of Darius's wife (Plutarch 30), the
  thirty-day campaign against the Mardi from Persepolis (Bosworth), and Alexander's acclamation as king of Asia
  after Gaugamela (Bosworth).
- Zone `macedon-330a` for Persis taken and Media not yet, January–May 330 BC, asked for by this plan and now in
  the Map part.
- Phase page ids and titles are proposals; the battle-coordinator may retitle them but keeps the ids and the count.
