On a night of wind and rain in 326 BC, in April or May by Arrian's dating,\* Alexander's striking force waits behind a [wooded headland](@hydaspes-crossing-headland) 150 stades upstream of his camp, with boats carried there in pieces and hides stuffed with hay. Craterus holds the camp opposite Porus. Between the two, [Meleager, Attalus, son of Andromenes, and Gorgias](@hydaspes-crossing-mercenaries) wait along the bank with the Greek mercenaries, under orders to cross once they see the battle joined.

Towards daybreak the storm dies down. The cavalry goes over on the hides and part of the infantry in the boats. They pass the wooded island unseen, and [Porus's scouts](@hydaspes-crossing-scouts) gallop off to warn him only when the boats are nearly across. [Alexander](@hydaspes-crossing-alexander) lands first, but the shore turns out to be a second island, cut off by a channel that the night's rain has swollen. When a ford is found at last, the men wade across breast-deep, and the horses nearly swim.

Arrian puts the force across at about 6,000 foot and 5,000 horse. Alexander orders the foot to follow slowly and rides ahead with the cavalry, the horse archers in front, while Tauron hurries his archers after them. Porus's son comes up with horsemen and chariots.\*\* The horse archers ride at them and Alexander charges with his squadrons, and the Indians give way. Arrian says that 400 of their horsemen fall, [Porus's son](@hydaspes-crossing-son) among them, and that the [chariots](@hydaspes-crossing-chariots), heavy and stuck in the clay, are taken. The survivors carry the news to Porus.

The sources do not give where the islands lay or where the fight with Porus's son took place.

### Notes

\* Arrian dates the battle to the Athenian month Munychion, April to May 326 BC, but also links it with the summer solstice. Bosworth keeps May, with its spring thunderstorms. Diodorus places it in the Athenian year that began in July 326 BC, which his translator Welles takes to mean about July.

\*\* Arrian follows Ptolemy, who gives the son 2,000 horse and 120 chariots and says that he came after the last crossing. Aristobulus, in Arrian, says that he came with about 60 chariots while Alexander was still crossing from the island, and rode past instead of attacking. Plutarch, quoting a letter of Alexander's, gives 1,000 horse and 60 chariots, and Curtius 4,000 horse and 100 chariots under Porus's brother.
