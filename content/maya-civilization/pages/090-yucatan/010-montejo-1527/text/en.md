Charles V names Francisco de Montejo, a veteran of the Grijalva and Cortés expeditions, adelantado of Yucatán on 8 December 1526. Montejo sails from Spain in June 1527 with four ships, 400 men by Sharer and Traxler’s count, 250 soldiers besides the crews by Chamberlain’s. In late September he reaches Cozumel, where the lord Aj Naum Pat receives him in peace.

On the coast of Ekab Montejo founds Salamanca de Xelha, the first Spanish town on the peninsula, and leaves 40 men there under Alonso Dávila and 20 at Pole. As food runs short he burns his ships. With 125 men he marches north through Xamanha and Belma to Conil, a town of 5,000 houses, where he halts for two months.

In spring 1528 the people of Chauaca leave their town by night, attack the Spanish the next morning and are beaten. At Aké, 16 km north of Tizimín, the Spanish kill more than 1,200 Maya in a large battle, and the neighbouring lords submit. Montejo comes back to Xelha by Sisia and Loche with 60 men. He finds 12 of the 40 at Xelha alive and the post at Pole wiped out.

A supply ship from Santo Domingo takes Montejo south to Chaktumal, capital of the Chetumal province, where the shipwrecked sailor Gonzalo Guerrero refuses to rejoin the Spanish. Montejo sails on to the Ulúa River in Honduras and back to Xamanha, where Dávila has moved the colony. Late in 1528 he leaves for New Spain.
