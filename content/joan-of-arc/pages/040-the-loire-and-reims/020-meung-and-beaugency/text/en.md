On Wednesday 15 June the army leaves [Orléans](@orleans-15-june-1429) and storms the fortified bridge of [Meung](@meung-bridge-1429), leaving the town and castle to the English. On Thursday 16 June it comes before [Beaugency](@beaugency-1429). Matthew Gough, a Welsh captain and Lord Talbot's lieutenant, and Sir Richard Gethin, bailli of Évreux, have left the town for its castle and bridge, with some hundreds of men. Men hidden in the houses fall on the French as they settle in.

On Friday 17 June Arthur de Richemont, Constable of France, comes with 500 or 600 fighting men. Charles VII, urged by his favourite Georges de La Trémoille, has forbidden it, and the Duke of Alençon, who commands the army, has orders not to receive him.

Meanwhile an English relief from [Paris](@paris-fastolf-1429) under Sir John Fastolf has halted at [Janville](@janville-1429) on the news that Jargeau has fallen, and Talbot rides in from Beaugency to join it. Fastolf advises going no further, and Talbot swears to fight with his own men alone if he has to.

On 17 June the English come within about a league of Meung and Beaugency and find the French drawn up on a small hill. They dismount, and two heralds offer three knights to fight anyone who comes down. It is late, the French reply: find quarters for tonight, and tomorrow we shall see you nearer. The English turn back, take the town of Meung and pound its bridge all night.

In that night Gough agrees terms. On the morning of Saturday 18 June his men ride off through the Beauce with their lives, horses and harness, and the French enter.

Where the hill stands is not known.
