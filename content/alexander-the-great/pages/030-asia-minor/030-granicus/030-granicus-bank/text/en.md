[Alexander](@granicus-bank-alexander) charges where the mass of the Persian horse and their leaders stand, and a hard cavalry fight breaks out at the edge of the river while more Macedonian squadrons cross behind him. In the press of horses each side tries to shove the other back, the Persians down into the river, the Macedonians up and away from it. In Arrian's view the Macedonians win because they are stronger and better trained, and because their cornel-wood spears outmatch the Persians' javelins.

Alexander is in the thick of it. Two Persian nobles, Rhoesaces and Spithridates, attack him and his helmet is split. [Cleitus the Black](@granicus-bank-cleitus), commander of the royal squadron, saves his life, and [Spithridates](@granicus-bank-spithridates) is killed in the fight round him.\*

While the cavalry fight, the phalanx crosses the river. The Persian line gives way first where Alexander fights, then in the centre, and then both wings break and flee. Alexander does not pursue them far. [Memnon of Rhodes](@granicus-bank-memnon) escapes.

Arrian says nothing of the left wing. Diodorus has [Parmenion's](@granicus-bank-parmenion) men hold the enemy facing them and praises the Thessalian cavalry, and Bosworth suggests the left crossed as the right did. That crossing, the places where the phalanx crossed and the direction of the Persian flight are not given by the sources.

### Notes

\* In Arrian, Rhoesaces strikes Alexander's helmet, and as Spithridates raises his sword behind the king, Cleitus cuts off his arm. In Plutarch, Spithridates cracks the helmet and Cleitus runs him through with a spear. In Diodorus, Alexander kills Spithridates himself, and Cleitus cuts off the arm of Rhoesaces as he strikes again at the king's split helmet.
