In winter 1428–1429 Durand Laxart, her cousin's husband, brings Joan from his village of Burey back to [Vaucouleurs](@vaucouleurs-1429). She lodges with Henri Le Royer, a cartwright, and his wife Catherine, about three weeks in all, as Catherine said in 1456.\*

Joan said that the captain, Robert de Baudricourt, sent her away twice before he heard her out and promised an escort, just as her voice had foretold. Catherine Le Royer saw him come to the house with the curé, Jean Fournier, and had it from Joan that the priest then exorcised her.

Charles II, Duke of Lorraine, sends her a safe-conduct and has her brought to [Nancy](@nancy-1429), his capital. On the way Laxart and Jacques Alain, a man of Vaucouleurs, take her on pilgrimage to the shrine of [Saint-Nicolas-de-Port](@saint-nicolas-de-port). The duke asks about his health; she asks for his son, his son-in-law René of Anjou in Lowell's reading, and for men to take her into France. He gives her four francs, and she goes back.

Before she left for Nancy, Jean de Metz, a squire, had sworn at Vaucouleurs to take her to the king, and he and another squire, Bertrand de Poulengy, had offered to escort her at their own cost, which the king's treasury later repaid. The townspeople buy her men's clothes for the road. Laxart said that he and Jacques Alain paid twelve francs for her horse; Jean de Metz put it at about sixteen.\*\*

Baudricourt gives way, though with doubt. He gives her a sword, makes her escort swear to take her well and safely, and, as Lang has it, says "Go, come what may." His reasons are not known, Lowell and Lang agree. The Journal of the Siege tells that on the day of the Herrings, 12 February 1429, she knew of the defeat and told Baudricourt.\*\*\*

### Notes

\* Lang dates her stay at Burey to January 1429, with the move into the town early in February. Poulengy has her come back towards the beginning of the fast before Easter.

\*\* Jean Morel, her godfather, said by hearsay that the duke gave her a black horse, which Lowell doubts.

\*\*\* Lowell finds the evidence that she announced the defeat insufficient, though Baudricourt came to believe she had. Lang dates the chronicles that tell it to about 1467, long after, and does not take it as certain. Thurston dates her announcement 17 February.
