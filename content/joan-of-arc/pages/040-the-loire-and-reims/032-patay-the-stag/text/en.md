On 18 June, on the road north to [Patay](@patay-the-stag-patay), the French scouts ride on beside the woods without sight of the English, who are marching from Meung for Janville. A stag breaks out of the woods and runs towards the village, into the ranks of the English main body, and the English raise a loud shout. The scouts now see the English and send word back to their captains.

[La Hire](@patay-the-stag-la-hire) and [Poton de Xaintrailles](@patay-the-stag-la-hire) gallop up the road with the vanguard and ride into the narrow way between two hedges where [Lord Talbot](@patay-the-stag-talbot) stands with 500 archers, before the English have joined at the hedges. They surround and overwhelm him. Talbot is taken, by the archers of Xaintrailles, and all his men are killed, before they can plant their stakes.

[Sir John Fastolf](@patay-the-stag-fastolf) and the main body hurry north to join the vanguard at the hedges near the village, and do not reach them: the French vanguard is through the narrow way and on them. Behind it [the Duke of Alençon](@patay-the-stag-alencon) and [the Bastard of Orléans (later Count of Dunois)](@patay-the-stag-alencon) gallop up with the main body. Where the French scouts, the bodies on the road behind them and the English rearguard are at this hour is not known.
