Yik'in Chan K'awiil, son of Jasaw Chan K'awiil I, becomes king of Tikal on 8 December AD 734. A carved altar at Tikal, probably made between 733 and 736, shows a bound lord of Calakmul and possibly names its king Yuknoom Took' K'awiil. Calakmul’s allies still surround Tikal: El Perú to the west, Naranjo to the east and Dos Pilas to the south-west.

The king builds Temple IV, 70 m from the plaza floor to the top of its roof comb and the tallest temple at Tikal. Its carved lintels of sapodilla wood carry a date of 741. A causeway links it to the Great Plaza, about 750 m to the east.

In 743 Yik'in Chan K'awiil defeats El Perú, which becomes a vassal of Tikal. He takes an image of a celestial serpent, possibly the patron god of El Perú, back to Tikal. Lintel 3 of Temple IV celebrates the victory and shows him seated on a palanquin.

In 744 Tikal defeats Naranjo and captures its king, Yax Mayuy Chan Chaak, who is probably sacrificed during the victory celebrations. Yik'in Chan K'awiil dedicates Stela 5 the same year. The defeats of El Perú and Naranjo end the alliance network of Calakmul.
