Early on Wednesday 30 May 1431, Martin Ladvenu, a Dominican friar sent by Pierre Cauchon, the Bishop of Beauvais, comes to Joan's cell in [the castle of Rouen](@castle-last-morning-1431). He tells her she is to die that day and hears her confession, and she is allowed to take communion. When Cauchon arrives she says to him, "Bishop, I die through you."

She is led through the streets to [the Old Market](@old-market-1431) under a guard of English soldiers. At about nine, near [the church of Saint-Sauveur](@saint-sauveur-1431), she is set on one of three scaffolds, and Nicolas Midi, a doctor of Paris, preaches. The sentence read to her then declares her a relapsed heretic, cut off from the Church and handed over to the secular power.

The bailli of Rouen, who stands for the secular power, should now pass sentence, and he does not. The executioner seizes her the moment the church's sentence is read, before the bailli has given any sentence.

She asks for a cross. One of the English standing by fashions a small one for her out of a stick, and Jean Massieu, the usher of the court, has the cross of the church brought for her to look at. In the fire she calls on Jesus to the end. Once her dress has burned, the fire is drawn back so that the crowd can see her body, and the executioner then burns it to ashes. Her ashes are thrown into [the Seine](@seine-1431).
