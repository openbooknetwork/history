Timur learns that Bayezid is about 16 km away and lifts the siege of [Ankara](@ankara-1402). He crosses the Çubuk stream, draws up his army on the plain north-east of the town and fortifies his [camp](@ankara-timur-camp) by the water. Bayezid leaves his first camp on the wet ground near [Ravlı](@ankara-ravli) (Akyurt) and moves to the low hills [near Melikşah](@ankara-bayezid-camp) on the west side of the plain, and the outposts skirmish through the night.

The early Ottoman chroniclers place the battle only at Ankara, Doukas in a plain at Ankara, and Timur’s letter of victory east of Ankara, half a fersah away. Kemalpaşazade, early in the 16th century, is the first to name the Çubuk plain. Ömer Halis Bıyıktay in 1934 placed the fighting along the Kızılcaköy stream, which joins the Çubuk from the west, and the last stand on a hill he calls Çataltepe, and most later historians follow him. Halil Çetin argues instead for Culuk near Haymana, south-west of Ankara.

David Nicolle puts Timur’s army at about 140,000 men, most of them horsemen, with 32 war elephants, and Bayezid’s at no more than 85,000. Ibn Arabshah gives Timur 800,000 men, and Timur’s letter of victory gives the Ottomans 70,000 horse and foot. Sharaf ad-Din Ali Yazdi dates the battle to Thursday 20 July 1402, Ibn Arabshah to 28 July.
