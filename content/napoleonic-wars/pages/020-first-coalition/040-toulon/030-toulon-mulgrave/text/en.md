During the night of 17 to 18 December, in heavy rain, Dugommier launches the general assault. The main column attacks Fort Mulgrave while La Poype strikes the eastern defences on Mont Faron. The rain spoils the muskets, and much of the fighting is with bayonets. The first Republican troops to enter the works are thrown back before reinforcements overwhelm the garrison, and Bonaparte receives a bayonet wound in the thigh.

The French then occupy l’Éguillette and Balaguier and bring up guns that can fire into both roadsteads. La Poype’s troops take key positions on Mont Faron. At a council on board Hood’s flagship Victory on 18 December, the Allied commanders decide that the fleet can no longer stay in the harbour and that the garrison cannot hold the city without it.
