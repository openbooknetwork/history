Joan is born about 1412 at [Domrémy](@domremy), a village on the upper Meuse that makes one parish with its neighbour Greux. When her judges at Rouen asked her age on 21 February 1431, she said she thought she was nineteen, and Lowell and Lang both say that the exact year cannot be known. Only one source gives her a birthday: a letter that Perceval de Boulainvilliers wrote to the Duke of Milan in 1429, which puts it on the night of the Epiphany, 6 January.

At the same hearing she named her father, Jacques d'Arc, and her mother, Isabelle, and said she was baptised in the church of Domrémy. In 1455, when witnesses were questioned in the village for the retrial, her godfather Jean Morel said the same and called the church Saint-Remy. Jacques came from Ceffonds in Champagne, and Isabelle, called Romée, from Vouthon. A widow of Domrémy, Beatrix Estellin, described them as labourers and good Catholics, honest folk, but not rich.

Joan told the court that her mother taught her the Paternoster, the Ave Maria and the Credo, and that at sewing and spinning she "feared no woman in Rouen". Whether she also kept the animals in the fields is told two ways.\*

A little way down the river stands [Vaucouleurs](@vaucouleurs-1412), a walled town and castle held for the king's party. Lang calls its district, with Domrémy and Greux, an island of loyalty in country that was Anglo-Burgundian or outside France altogether. Across the Meuse the people of [Maxey](@maxey-1412) were Burgundians, Joan said, and in Domrémy itself she knew only one. The boys of the two villages fought each other in the meadows as Armagnacs and Burgundians, a quarrel that Lowell takes for an old feud between neighbours.

The map shows the upper Meuse, where the kingdom, with Vaucouleurs, meets the duchies of Bar and Lorraine, and the county of Burgundy lies to the south. Domrémy stands on the edge of the kingdom and Bar, and who was its lord is disputed.\*\* The line through the village follows a small stream, the Three Fountains brook, and is approximate.

### Notes

\* On 22 February 1431 Joan told the court that while she lived at home she did the work of the house and did not go to the fields to look after the sheep and other animals. Jean Morel said in 1455 that until she left her parents she followed the plough and sometimes minded the cattle. Thurston, writing in 1910, calls the popular picture of a child alone in the pastures with the flocks unfounded.

\*\* Lowell puts the part of Domrémy south of the Three Fountains brook in the duchy of Bar, so that Joan was born a subject of the Duke of Bar and only at one remove of the King of France. Lang has her father's house divided from Lorraine by the brook, or even on the Lorraine side of it, and notes that the villagers spoke of "going into France".
