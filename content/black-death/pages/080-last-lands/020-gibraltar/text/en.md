[Alfonso XI](@alfonso-xi) of Castile has spent his reign fighting the Marinid sultans of Morocco and the emirs of Granada for the Strait of Gibraltar. He beat them at the Río Salado in 1340 and took Algeciras in 1344 after a siege of 20 months. In August 1349 he lays siege to Gibraltar, in Muslim hands since 1333. His army of about 20,000 digs ditches across the isthmus north of the town, while his son and heir Pedro stays in [Seville](@seville-1350).

The plague has been in Iberia since spring 1348. It reached Portugal in April or May 1348 and [Lisbon](@lisbon) by early summer. Soon after New Year 1350 Castilian soldiers in the camp begin to die of it. Alfonso refuses to leave, though his nobles and generals plead with him. The chronicle of his reign has him answer that it would be “a greatly shameful thing if because of fear of death he left it as it was”. He falls ill with a plague swelling and dies in the night of 25 to 26 March 1350, aged 38. He is the highest-ranking European monarch to die of the plague.

His death ends the siege. Yusuf I of Granada, who has been gathering a force to relieve the town, lets the Castilians leave in peace and forbids his border commanders to touch the procession taking the king’s body to Seville. The chronicle has everyone in Gibraltar come out of the town and watch it leave in silence. At Seville his son, aged 15, becomes king as Pedro I. Alfonso is later buried at Córdoba, and Castile does not take Gibraltar until 1462.
