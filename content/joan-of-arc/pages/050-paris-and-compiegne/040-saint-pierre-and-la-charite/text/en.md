In October 1429 Joan stays about three weeks at [Bourges](@bourges-1429) in the house of Marguerite La Touroulde, as her hostess told the retrial in 1456. The king's council at [Mehun-sur-Yèvre](@mehun-1429) resolves to take La Charité, and Saint-Pierre-le-Moûtier first: towns of the upper Loire that soldiers of fortune hold against the king, like [Cosne](@cosne-1429). [La Charité](@la-charite) belongs to Perrinet Gressart, who serves Burgundy and took it from the French in 1423.\*

Charles d'Albret, half-brother of Georges de La Trémoille, the king's favourite, leads the expedition with Joan. At the end of October\*\* they besiege [Saint-Pierre-le-Moûtier](@saint-pierre-le-moutier). Only her squire Jean d'Aulon told how it was taken, at the retrial. When the first assault fails, d'Aulon, wounded in the heel, rides to her and asks why she stays alone, with four or five men about her. She is not alone, she answers: fifty thousand of her own are with her, and the town must fall before she goes. She has wood and hurdles brought to bridge the ditch, and the town falls. On 9 November she and Albret write from [Moulins](@moulins-1429) that it is taken.\*\*\*

Short of powder, Joan and Albret write to Clermont-Ferrand (then Clermont) and Riom for more. From about 10 November they lie before La Charité in the cold. The Berry herald, a royal chronicler, records an assault in which a baron of Dauphiné is killed. Joan told her judges that she had an assault made there. The king sends neither food nor money. After about a month, the herald says, the siege is raised and the artillery lost.\*\*\*\* The day is not known; Cagny's editor Moranvillé puts it at the end of November or early in December. Gressart keeps La Charité for Burgundy.

In December\*\*\*\*\* at Mehun-sur-Yèvre Charles VII ennobles Joan and her family. The roads on the map are approximate.
