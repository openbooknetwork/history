Parliament looks to the colonies to pay for the garrison it keeps in America. The Sugar Act of April 1764 lowers the duty on molasses but tightens customs enforcement and sends smuggling cases to vice-admiralty courts without juries. The Stamp Act of March 1765 requires a tax stamp on newspapers, legal documents, licences and playing cards. It is the first direct tax Parliament has laid on the colonies.

In August 1765 a Boston crowd destroys the stamp distributor’s office and sacks the lieutenant governor’s house. Crowds force stamp agents to resign in one colony after another, and from November groups calling themselves Sons of Liberty organise the resistance. In October delegates from nine colonies meet in New York as the Stamp Act Congress and declare that only their own assemblies may tax them.

Merchants stop importing British goods. Parliament repeals the act in March 1766 and at the same time passes the Declaratory Act, which asserts its right to legislate for the colonies “in all cases whatsoever”.
