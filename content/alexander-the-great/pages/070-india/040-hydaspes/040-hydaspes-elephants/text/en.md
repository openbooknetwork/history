The elephant drivers turn their beasts on the Macedonian cavalry, and the phalanx advances against them. The foot soldiers aim at the drivers and wound the beasts, but where an elephant can turn, it breaks the line. The Indian horse rallies once more and is driven back again among the elephants. By now [Alexander's](@hydaspes-elephants-alexander) cavalry has massed into a single body, and it cuts down the Indians cooped up in the narrow space. Most of the drivers have been killed, and the [elephants](@hydaspes-elephants-beasts), wounded or left without riders, trample friend and foe alike, then back slowly away.

Alexander rings the whole Indian line with his cavalry and has the foot lock shields and press in. The Indian cavalry is almost wiped out and the infantry with it, and all who can flee through the gaps between Alexander's squadrons. Seeing the victory, [Craterus](@hydaspes-elephants-craterus) and the other officers left on the bank cross the river, and their fresh troops take over the pursuit. In Bosworth's account [Meleager, Attalus, son of Andromenes, and Gorgias](@hydaspes-elephants-mercenaries) bring their mercenaries over at the same time, as their orders were.

[Porus](@hydaspes-elephants-porus) fights on while any body of his men holds together. Wounded in the right shoulder, he turns his elephant away. Alexander sends [Taxiles](@hydaspes-elephants-taxiles), the king of Taxila and Porus's old enemy, to call on him to stop; Porus turns to strike him with a javelin, and Taxiles rides off out of reach. Other messengers follow, and at last Porus dismounts and is led to Alexander.\* Both sides' losses are disputed.\*\*

The sources do not give the line of the Indian flight, where Craterus crossed, or where Porus gave himself up.

### Notes

\* This is Arrian's account. Diodorus has Porus charge with forty elephants and fight on until, fainting from his many wounds, he falls from his elephant. In Curtius he slides from his beast, and his driver, thinking that he is dismounting, makes the elephant kneel.

\*\* Arrian puts the Indian dead at nearly 20,000 foot and 3,000 horse, two sons of Porus among them, and Alexander's at about 80 foot, 10 horse archers, about 20 Companions and about 200 other horsemen. Diodorus gives more than 12,000 Indians killed and more than 9,000 taken with 80 elephants, and 280 horsemen and more than 700 foot soldiers dead on the Macedonian side.
