[Alexander](@issus-river-alexander) leads the line forward at a measured pace, so that the phalanx keeps its front. When it comes within range of missiles, he and the troops of the right charge into the river at a run. As soon as the fighting is hand to hand, the Persian left gives way and flees, and Alexander's horse push on towards [Darius](@issus-river-darius).

The centre cannot keep up. The banks are steep in many places, and the phalanx loses its line in the stream bed, opening a gap towards the right. The Greek mercenaries in Persian pay attack the gap and try to drive the Macedonians back into the river. In this fight [Ptolemy, son of Seleucus](@issus-river-ptolemy), one of the battalion commanders, falls with about 120 Macedonians of rank.

By the sea the Persian cavalry does not wait on its bank. It crosses the river and charges the Thessalians, who are forced back.\* The historian A. B. Bosworth writes that the phalanx "was more closely challenged than at any other time in the reign", and that the weight of the Persian horse by the sea was almost too much to withstand. As this phase ends, the Macedonian line is broken and slanted: Alexander has crossed the river, the Thessalians have been pushed back to their own bank, and the phalanx is stuck in the stream bed.

[Parmenion's](@issus-river-parmenion) left holds on. The sources do not say exactly where the gap opened, where the Cardaces stood while the left broke, or which way the beaten Persian left fled.

### Notes

\* Arrian puts this charge during the struggle in the river. Callisthenes and Curtius put it at the opening of the battle, and Curtius says that one squadron of Thessalians was ridden down in the first onset.
