When word comes that the left is hard pressed, [Simmias](@gaugamela-left-simmias) halts his battalion instead of joining the advance, and a gap opens in the phalanx. Persian and Indian horsemen ride through it to the baggage, where the prisoners rise to join them. The officers of the second line turn their men about, fall on the raiders from behind and cut many of them down at the [camp](@gaugamela-left-camp).

On the left, [Mazaeus's](@gaugamela-left-mazaeus) horsemen ride round [Parmenion's](@gaugamela-left-parmenion) flank, and a detachment rides on to the camp; the map draws this raid together with the break-in there.\* Parmenion sends to Alexander for help.\*\*

[Alexander](@gaugamela-left-alexander) and the Companions, coming back from the pursuit, meet a mass of fleeing Persian, Parthian and Indian horse riding in deep formation. In the hardest cavalry fight of the day about 60 Companions fall, but Alexander's men prevail, and only those Persians who can force their way through escape. By then the Persian right is giving way: as news of Darius's flight spreads, the Thessalian cavalry throw it back, and Mazaeus leads his men off behind the Macedonian army.

Alexander rides on after Darius until he crosses the river [Lycus](@gaugamela-left-lycus), about 32 km away, while Parmenion takes the Persian camp with its baggage, elephants and camels. Both sides' losses are disputed.\*\*\*

The sources do not give the places of the fights in this phase or where the units stood at its end.

### Notes

\* Arrian tells only of Persian and Indian horse breaking through the gap. Diodorus and Bosworth put Mazaeus's raid on the camp early in the battle. Diodorus says that Mazaeus sent 2,000 Cadusians and 1,000 Scythian horsemen round the flank to the camp, Curtius tells of 1,000 horsemen, and Plutarch also tells of the raid. Bosworth holds that the attack on the camp is surely historical.

\*\* Arrian says Alexander turned back from the pursuit at once. Diodorus says the riders found him far off in pursuit and came back without reaching him, which Bosworth thinks surely true. Plutarch and Curtius tell the episode in yet other ways.

\*\*\* For the Macedonians, Arrian gives about 100 killed and more than 1,000 horses lost, Diodorus about 500 killed and Curtius fewer than 300. For the Persians, Arrian gives about 300,000 killed and more taken, Diodorus more than 90,000 and Curtius 40,000. Bosworth holds that the losses on either side cannot be estimated.
